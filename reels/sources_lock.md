# Sources: lock (how a pin tumbler lock works)

Every claim was checked against a fetched page.

| Script line | Source (fetched) | Supporting text |
|---|---|---|
| The key turns a cylinder called the plug | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pin_tumbler_lock | "an outer casing has a cylindrical hole in which the plug is housed"; the plug rotates to open |
| The plug sits in an outer casing; where they meet is the shear line | same | "The point where the plug and cylinder meet is called the shear point." (US 5,697,239 uses "shear line") |
| Holes drilled in the plug, typically five or six | same | "A series of holes, typically five or six of them, are drilled vertically into the plug." |
| Key pins are rounded so the key slides over them, various lengths | same | "key pins of various lengths, which are rounded to permit the key to slide over them easily" |
| Above each key pin, a spring-loaded driver pin | same | "Above each key pin is one or more spring-loaded driver pins." |
| Springs push the pins down into the plug | same | "the pins are pushed down into the plug by the springs" |
| No key: pins straddle the shear line, plug can't turn | same | "the pins straddle the shear point, preventing the plug from rotating" |
| Right key lifts pins to align exactly at the shear line | same | "the pins will rise causing them to align exactly at the shear point" |
| Plug turns, lock opens | same | "This allows the plug to rotate, thus opening the lock." |
| Wrong key: pins too high or too low, stay misaligned, plug stays locked | same | "If the wrong key is used, the pins remain misaligned, preventing the plug from turning"; "lifting the pins too much or too little prevented opening" |

Note: Wikipedia's "too much or too little" sentence is written about double-acting locks; the general wrong-key sentence covers the claim on its own.
