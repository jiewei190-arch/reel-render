# Sources: extinguisher (how a fire extinguisher puts out fire)

All pages fetched with WebFetch on 2026-10-27 and quoted below.

| Script line | Source (fetched) | Quote |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ barely cools a fire; it pushes the air away (hook) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_extinguisher | "Carbon dioxide extinguishes fire mainly by displacing oxygen." "It was once thought that it worked by cooling, although this effect on most fires is negligible." |
| Fire needs four things at once: fuel, heat, oxygen, the chemical reaction | https://www.osha.gov/etools/evacuation-plans-procedures/emergency-standards/portable-extinguishers/about | "For fire to exist, the following four elements must be present at the same time: oxygen ... heat ... fuel ... the chemical reaction that is fire." |
| Take away any one and the fire can't exist | same OSHA page | Inference from "must be present at the same time" (flagged as inference). |
| First recorded extinguisher patented in England in 1723 | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_extinguisher | "The first fire extinguisher of which there is any record was patented in England in 1723 by Ambrose Godfrey." |
| Handheld cylinder built to hold pressure | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_extinguisher | "Typically, a fire extinguisher consists of a hand-held cylindrical pressure vessel." |
| Most common design: pushing gas shares a chamber with the agent | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_extinguisher | "Stored pressure fire extinguishers are the most common type." "In stored-pressure units, the expellant is stored in the same chamber as the firefighting agent itself." |
| Squeeze the lever, pressure drives the agent out | https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/fire-extinguishers/ ; Wikipedia (expellant) | "Squeeze the lever slowly and evenly." Expellant = the gas that pushes the agent out. |
| Water cools burning wood and fabric | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_extinguisher ; OSHA | "Water cools burning carbonaceous material and is very effective against fires in furniture, fabrics, etc." |
| Foam blankets the fuel so oxygen can't reach it | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_extinguisher | "a frothy blanket or seal over the fuel, preventing oxygen reaching it." |
| Dry powder smothers the fuel and breaks the chemical reaction | OSHA page | Dry chemical powder "smothers the fuel and disrupts the chemical reaction." |
| CO₂ once thought to cool; mostly displaces oxygen; cooling negligible on most fires | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_extinguisher | quotes above |
| PASS: pull, aim low, squeeze, sweep | https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/fire-extinguishers/ | "Pull the pin ... Aim low. Point the extinguisher at the base of the fire ... Squeeze the lever slowly and evenly ... Sweep the nozzle from side-to-side." |
| Class A wood, cloth, paper; Class K cooking oils and fats | USFA page | "Class A: ordinary materials like cloth, wood and paper." "Class K: vegetable oils, animal oils and fats in cooking appliances." |
| Every extinguisher takes away heat, oxygen, or the reaction | OSHA page | Extinguishers "either cool burning fuel, displace or remove oxygen, or stop the chemical reaction." |
