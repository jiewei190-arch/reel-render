# Sources: seatbelt (how a seatbelt locks)

Every claim was checked against a fetched page. Rewritten from the earlier draft so the reel explains the locking mechanism.

| Script line | Source (fetched) | Supporting text |
|---|---|---|
| Pull slowly it slides, yank it and it locks | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seat_belt | "The belt can be pulled from the reel only slowly and gradually"; "A sudden rapid pull of the belt ... causes the reel to lock" |
| Belt stored on a spring-loaded reel called a retractor | same | "Seat belts are stowed on spring-loaded reels, called retractors, equipped with inertial locking mechanisms" |
| Spring keeps the belt taut, you can move around | same | "The vehicle occupant can move around with relative freedom while the spring tension of the reel keeps the belt taut" |
| Ratchet teeth on each end of the spool | https://patents.google.com/patent/US4371128A/en | spool "having ratchet-like teeth 24 on the periphery of each spool end" |
| A locking pawl engages the teeth; belt can't come out | same | "a locking pawl which engages ratchet teeth upon a spool that stores the safety belt"; "locks the seat belt retractor mechanism 10 to prevent the further protraction of the belt" |
| Two sensors | Wikipedia Seat belt | "There are two main types of inertial seat belt locks."; dual-sensing retractors use both |
| Pendulum swings from plumb under hard braking or rollover, reel locks | Wikipedia Seat belt; patent US4371128 (pawl-mounted pendulum, "an inertia mass which responds to a crash or sudden deceleration") | "A vehicle-sensitive lock is based on a pendulum swung away from its plumb position by rapid deceleration or rollover" |
| Centrifugal clutch; slow pull pays out, jerk locks | Wikipedia Seat belt | "A webbing-sensitive lock is based on a centrifugal clutch activated by the rapid acceleration of the strap" |
| Pretensioner: a small charge tightens the belt to remove slack | Wikipedia Seat belt | "In a crash, a small charge quickly tightens the belt to remove slack." |
