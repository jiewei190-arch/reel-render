# Sources: Does turkey really make you sleepy? (midcentury presenter, myth-bust)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Turkey is not special: all meat has tryptophan at comparable levels, and cheddar cheese has more per gram | Live Science (Christopher Wanjek): https://www.livescience.com/health/071120-bad-turkey-sleep.html |
| Turkey doesn't have more tryptophan than chicken or beef; some foods like nuts and cheeses have even more | Texas Health Resources: https://www.texashealth.org/areyouawellbeing/Eating-Right/The-Turkey-Tryptophan-Connection-Is-It-Really-Whats-Behind-the-Thanksgiving-Snooze |
| Carbs (potatoes, stuffing, bread, pie) trigger insulin; insulin pulls most other amino acids into muscle, leaving tryptophan free to reach the brain, where it is used to make serotonin, which helps regulate sleep | Live Science (above) |
| Carbs raise blood sugar then a "sugar crash" can cause tiredness | Texas Health (above) |
| Eating a lot of food at one time adds to tiredness | Texas Health (above) |
| Alcohol is known to make you feel drowsy | Texas Health (above) |
| The real cause is the meal (big, carb-heavy, with alcohol), not turkey itself | Live Science + Texas Health (above) |
| NOT CLAIMED: exact tryptophan numbers (milligrams), because the pages fetched gave none |
