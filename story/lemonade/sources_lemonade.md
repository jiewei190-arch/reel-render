# Sources: lemonade ("Two lemonade stands in a price war", the prisoner's dilemma)

The two stands, their prices and their costs are a made-up story. The economics the story illustrates is sourced below.

| Claim in the script | Source | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Two sellers of identical lemonade undercut each other until the price reaches cost and neither earns anything ("Exactly what the lemons and sugar cost", "And two stands earning nothing", "Zero profit") | MIT OpenCourseWare 14.27 Economics and E-Commerce (Fall 2014), Lecture 2 Review, Prof. Sara Ellison: the Bertrand Paradox. With "identical products, so consumers buy from producer with lowest price", the equilibrium is P1* = P2* = c, "so both firms price at marginal cost and make no profit" (https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-27-economics-and-e-commerce-fall-2014/) | university course |
| Whatever the rival does, cutting is better for each stand ("If Teal held its price, cutting won. If Teal cut, cutting still beat being undercut") | Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Prisoner's Dilemma" (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/prisoner-dilemma/): "whatever the other does, each is better off confessing than remaining silent" | academic encyclopedia |
| Each side's best move leaves both worse off; game theorists call it the prisoner's dilemma | SEP (same entry): "the outcome obtained when both confess is worse for each than the outcome they would have obtained" had both stayed silent; the entry lists "price setting for duopolistic firms" among the situations it models | academic encyclopedia |
| Real shops escape it by being different (better lemonade, a shady spot, a loyal regular) | MIT OCW 14.27 Lecture 2: firms escape the Bertrand Paradox through product differentiation (its examples include coffee shops), which it covers with the Hotelling model | university course |

## Softened / left out
- The 30-cent cost (20¢ lemons + 10¢ sugar) and the price steps are story numbers, not data.
- The story's ending (a better product, a better spot, a loyal customer) shows product differentiation. The other escape routes the sources name (repeated games where cutting is punished, search costs) are left out for length.
- Price-fixing deals between rivals are not shown as a way out (illegal for real firms in most places).
