# HOOKS v2: the first 3 seconds of a short science explainer

Written Oct 28 2026 by worker H3. This extends HOOKS.md and does not replace its data. The 30-Short title study and the two fully captured Zack D. Films openers stay in HOOKS.md.

## How much to trust each source

Every claim below is tagged:

- **[data]**: measured by a platform or a study.
- **[report]**: a secondary write-up of someone else's data or document.
- **[opinion]**: a creator's or strategist's advice.
- **[ours]**: measured on our own reels.

No statistic here is invented. If a figure only appears in blogs and I couldn't trace it to a primary source, it is either labelled [report] or left out. An example: the "67% of top ads put the message in 3 s" number is left out, because TikTok's own guide says 63%. Zack D. Films, Kurzgesagt and Veritasium have no published interviews about Shorts hooks that I could find. For them, only titles and openers we observed ourselves are used, and those are in HOOKS.md.

What the evidence says, in short:

1. **The swipe decision is made in the first second or two.** YouTube's only public per-Short measure of this is "Viewed vs. swiped away" in Studio. In Paddy Galloway's study of 5,400 Shorts from 33 channels, the best performers sat at 70-90% "viewed", and Shorts below about 60% rarely did well. [report] He also warned against obsessing over the number. [report]
2. **The key message early wins.** TikTok's own creative guide says that over 63% of the highest-CTR auction ads put their key message in the first 3 seconds. In the same guide, 40% of the highest view-through ads used concise text overlays, and 1 in 3 addressed the viewer directly. [data, ads, not organic videos]
3. **Curiosity is a knowledge gap the viewer can feel.** Loewenstein's information-gap theory says curiosity arises "when attention becomes focused on a gap in one's knowledge", and it needs some prior knowledge to work. [data/theory] In practice: the hook must touch something the viewer already knows (a toilet, a cat, the moon) and then reveal the gap.
4. **The opening must confirm the promise.** The leaked MrBeast production handbook says the first minute is where most viewers are lost. It says the opening must show that the video delivers what the thumbnail and title promised, and it adds "re-engagement" moments later in the video. [report: Gigazine, Passionfruit] For a Short, the "first minute" is the first 2-3 seconds, but that last step is our extrapolation.
5. **Legit bait beats click traps.** Veritasium's Derek Muller separates enticing but accurate packaging from misleading packaging, and argues the first kind is necessary. [opinion, via summaries]
6. **Speaking speed has room.** Normal speech is about 150 wpm. A UCLA study found that comprehension of audiovisual lectures held up at 1.5x and even 2x speed (about 234-332 wpm), because the visuals help. [data, one study] Our af_heart narration runs about 185-220 wpm while speaking and about 165-190 wpm including pauses. First lines run 190-312 wpm (eclipse is the fastest). [ours] So speed is not our problem, and clarity of the first noun is.
7. **Many people watch muted.** 69% of US adults surveyed watch video with the sound off in public places, and 80% of caption users aren't deaf or hard of hearing. [data, self-reported survey: Verizon Media/Publicis 2019] So frame 0 plus the burned-in caption must work without the voice.

## (1) The hook patterns that fit how-it-works topics (ranked)

Each pattern has two lines written for topics we've already made. Every line has to be true and sourced in that reel's sources file. Lines marked "needs source" need a new source before they can be used.

**1. Mid-action, "When you…" / "If you…"** (Zack D. Films' format; the two most-viewed explainers in our 30-Short study open this way [data, HOOKS.md])
- flush: "When you flush, the water doesn't get pushed. It gets pulled."
- match: "When you strike a match, the fire starts on the box."
- Why it works: the viewer is the subject, and the action is already happening at 0.0 s.

**2. Contradiction of what the viewer thinks** ("It doesn't X. It Y.") [opinion: Kallaway's "contrarian snapback"; matches the HOOKS.md titles]
- airfryer: "An air fryer doesn't fry anything."
- catseyes: "Cats' eyes don't glow. They bounce your light back."
- Needs a true contradiction, or the comments will punish it (HOOKS.md rule 9).

**3. Show the impossible-looking result first, explain second** [opinion: vidIQ, "show the payoff not the setup"; data: TikTok's "key message in 3 s"]
- magnet: "Cut a magnet in half, and you get two magnets."
- foxfire: "This mushroom glows in the dark, with no battery."
- On screen it must be the result itself (both halves snapping to N/S, the glowing cap), not a title.

**4. A specific question the viewer can picture but not answer**
- harvestmoon: "Why does the October moon look so huge?"
- submarine: "How does a submarine sink on purpose, and come back up?"
- Specific beats vague. "Have you ever wondered…" isn't a question, it's a delay.

**5. A test the viewer can try** (second person, concrete, often a re-hook too)
- harvestmoon: "Hold your fingernail up to the moon tonight. It covers it."
- flush: "Pour a bucket in fast, and the toilet flushes itself." (fast pour only, per HOOKS.md)
- The proof of the claim is in the viewer's own hands.

**6. A secret hidden in something you use every day**
- barcode: "Every barcode hides a math check in its last digit."
- lock: "Your house key is a tiny code."
- Familiar object plus hidden mechanism is Loewenstein's gap in its purest form.

**7. "You've been told X, but…" (myth correction)** [opinion]
- skyblue: "The sky isn't blue because it reflects the ocean." (true; the reel's Rayleigh sources)
- harvestmoon: "The giant horizon moon is a trick of your brain."
- Use only for real, common myths. Don't invent a myth to knock down.

**8. Stakes or consequence** (what goes wrong without the mechanism)
- submarine: "Get the air wrong, and a submarine can't come back up." (needs a source on the ballast-blow emergency)
- lock: "If one pin sits too high, the key won't turn."
- Keep it about a mechanism, not danger to people (owner rule: nothing gory).

**9. A number that sounds wrong** (best as line 2 or the 25 s re-hook, not the opener [HOOKS.md])
- spidersilk: "Weight for weight, spider silk is stronger than steel." (needs the reel's source; say "weight for weight")
- search: "Google's index is over 100 million gigabytes." (Google, How Search Works)

**10. The open loop with a promised payoff** (state the payoff, deliver it at 45-70 s)
- catseyes: "Your eyes turn red in photos, and a cat's turn green. Here's why."
- recommend: "Out of millions of videos, a few dozen reach you. This is how they're picked."
- The payoff has to arrive. Galloway's data is about holding viewers past about 50 s, and an unpaid loop loses the comments. [report + opinion]

Patterns 1-3 and 6 fit our topics best. They need the strongest first frame, and our styles are strongest at showing a mechanism.

## (2) First-frame rules (what must be on screen at 0.0 s)

1. **The subject, large, already moving.** It should fill at least about a third of the frame and be legible at phone size, at the strange moment: the magnet already cut, the cap already glowing, the water already swirling. No title card, no logo, no fade from black, no slow zoom out of empty space. (Our own QA gate found lock opening on black, so it happens.)
2. **The template's most striking image, not its quietest.** The style is part of the hook (STYLE_FIT: frame 0 should be the template's signature image).
3. **A 3-6 word burned-in caption from frame 0**, in the top third or centre, clear of the right button column (x>900, y 900-1650) and the bottom 220 px. The key word goes in the accent colour. Caption, image and first spoken noun must name the same thing. [data: the TikTok text-overlay figure; survey: muted viewing]
4. **One focal point.** At thumbnail size, a viewer should be able to say "toilet", "cat" or "moon" within 1 s with the sound off (HOOKS.md checklist item 3).
5. **Motion in the first 0.5 s that points at the mechanism.** The flash pops, the pin lifts, the moon rises. Not decorative drift.
6. **No episode badge or brand mark before 5 s** (brand.py already waits until 5 s).
7. **Frame 0 must also work as the loop point.** Shorts loop, so the last frame flows into the first. A clean, bold frame 0 reads well on the replay too. [opinion: third-party guides]

## (3) Hook checklist v2 (run on every script and on the first rendered second)

1. **Line 1 is 12 words or fewer** and the topic noun lands in the first 5 words.
2. **Frame 0 shows the subject**, large and in motion (no title, logo, black or empty space). `reels/qa.py` checks it automatically.
3. **The sound-off test passes.** Frame 0 plus the 3-6 word caption tells the topic within 1 s.
4. **Line 1 is one of the ten patterns above**, not a topic announcement and not a greeting.
5. **One specific open question is written down** in NOTES.md, and the script answers it, explicitly, between 45 and 70 s.
6. **A "but" turn or stake by 5 s.** Line 2 deepens or flips line 1 instead of defining terms.
7. **The mechanism is on screen by about 3 s.** The tool, the cross-section or the diagram arrives early.
8. **A re-hook at about 25 s and about 50 s**: a mini-question, a try-it test or a surprising number.
9. **The hook is true and sourced.** Check numbers, "never", "always" and myth claims. Title, caption, thumbnail and line 1 make the same promise (legit bait, not a click trap).
10. **The first second is checked by eye on the 8 s preview** and with the sound off, before the full render is queued.

## (4) Stop doing

- Openers with "Have you ever wondered", "Did you know", "Today we'll learn" or "Let's talk about". No top-performing title in our study opens like that. [data, HOOKS.md]
- Fading in from black, or opening on an empty set that fills up later (lock did this).
- Building the image up slowly. If the template's signature move is a reveal, start mid-reveal.
- First lines over 12 words, or lines that put the topic noun at the end.
- Captions that say something different from the voice and the image.
- Opening with a bare statistic. Numbers work as line 2 or as re-hooks.
- Unverified numbers in hooks, such as "cats see with 1/6 of the light" or "42 vs 12 insects". Drop them or soften them, as we did.
- Holding one static card for 4 s or more, at any point. The QA gate flags it, and search does it at 59-64 s.
- Answering the hook's question in the first 10 s, or never answering it at all.
- Brand badges, logos or "EP" marks in the first 5 s.
