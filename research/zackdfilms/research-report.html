01

Title Patterns

  • Short: the median top-200 title is 7 words, in Title Case.
  • Every title ends with one reaction emoji (100%). The most common are 🤔 (46 of top 200), 😮 (30), 😨 (30), 😱 and 😳.
  • Opening words in the top 200: "How…" 29%, "Why…" 6%, "What…" 6%. Questions ("Could…?", "What Is…?") appear in 20% of the top 200, versus 11% overall.
  • An optional parenthetical tag ("(explained)", "(ouch)", "(How?)") appears in 12% of the top 200.
  • No series numbers and no channel name in titles.

02

Thumbnail Analysis

These are Shorts, so the "thumbnail" is a frame from the video: a 3D render with the burned-in caption word in the centre. No text is added, no faces are graded, and nothing is designed separately. The subject is always a big, centred, close-up object or body part on a clean, bright background (often sky blue). The strangeness of the image itself is the hook.

03

Hook Structures

Every top-12 video opens mid-situation in the second person. There's no greeting and no title card.

"If you get a splinter most people just go ahead and pull them out but…"
"If you accidentally touched a table saw you'd probably think your fingers would be cut off but this isn't always the case…"
"Have you ever noticed how odd the McFlurry spoon is?"
  • "If you / When you" + a relatable body or everyday situation (6 of 12).
  • Misconception pivot: "you might think / most people / some people think" → "but" (6 of 12).
  • "You see" opens the explanation (4 of 12).

05

Pacing & Story Arc

The narration runs at about 165 words per minute in a calm, even voice. Each clause gets one continuous camera move, mostly slow push-ins or cutaways inside the body or object. Captions change every 1–3 words. The tension comes from the topic (mild "ouch" or "gross" curiosity), not from editing. The last line ends abruptly, and the loop restarts on the premise.
