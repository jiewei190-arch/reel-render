## Script A: Why Your Fingers Wrinkle In Water 🤔  (94 words, ~36 s)
If you stay in the bath too long, your fingertips wrinkle up. Most people think your skin is just soaking up water. But your nerves are actually behind it. Your nervous system squeezes the blood vessels under your fingertips, and the skin on top pulls down into ridges. That is why a finger with a damaged nerve can stay smooth. In a Newcastle University test, people with wrinkled fingers moved wet marbles about 12 percent faster. The wrinkles might work like the treads on a tire. Follow for more of how it really works.

Sources:
- Newcastle University press release (2013), Kareklas, Nettle & Smulders, *Biology Letters*: wrinkling caused by blood vessels constricting below the skin, controlled by the autonomic nervous system; "12% faster with the wet marbles"; "could be working like treads on your car tyres". https://www.ncl.ac.uk/press/articles/archive/2013/01/getagrip-whywegetwrinklyfingersandtoes.html
- Damaged nerve → no wrinkling (Lewis & Pickering 1930s; O'Riain wrinkle test 1973): https://www.the-scientist.com/the-science-behind-pruney-fingers-71410 and https://www.popsci.com/science/why-do-fingers-wrinkle-in-water/
- Softened: "might work like treads". The grip benefit was shown, but the mechanism wasn't proven.

## Script B: Why You Hate Hearing Your Own Voice 😳  (92 words, ~36 s)
When you hear your voice on a recording, it sounds strange, like someone else. Most people blame the phone. But the recording is actually closer to what everyone else hears. When you talk, the sound reaches your inner ear two ways: through the air, and straight through the bones of your head. Your skull carries the deep, low notes especially well, so inside your head your voice sounds richer. A microphone only catches the air, so the bass you are used to is gone. Follow for more of how it really works.

Sources:
- Scientific American, "Why does my voice sound so different when it is recorded and played back?": bone-conducted sound reaches the cochlea through head tissue; head enhances lower-frequency vibrations; recordings eliminate the bone-conducted path. https://scientificamerican.com/article/why-does-my-voice-sound-different
- Softened: "closer to what everyone else hears". The microphone and speaker also colour the sound, so it is not identical.

## Script C: Bubble Wrap Was Supposed To Be Wallpaper? 😮  (89 words, ~35 s)
In 1957, two inventors sealed two sheets of plastic together and trapped little bubbles of air inside. Alfred Fielding and Marc Chavannes were not trying to protect anything. They wanted to sell it as textured wallpaper. It flopped. So they tried it as greenhouse insulation, and that did not work either. Then in 1961, IBM needed a way to ship its 1401 computer without breaking it. The bubbles cushioned every bump on the road, and bubble wrap finally found its job. Follow for more of how it really works.

Sources:
- Smithsonian Magazine, "The Accidental Invention of Bubble Wrap": 1957, Fielding and Chavannes, textured wallpaper; greenhouse insulation "proved ineffective"; Sealed Air founded 1960; IBM 1401 packaging the next year (1961). https://www.smithsonianmag.com/innovation/accidental-invention-bubble-wrap-180971325/
- Softened: "two sheets of plastic". Some outlets say "two shower curtains", but Smithsonian doesn't, so it is left out.

## Packaging changes the research suggests (for ALL future Shorts)
1. Open on the spoken premise from frame 0. No title card, no "Ep. N".
2. Drop "| Ep. N" from titles: it uses title space, and none of their top 200 use a series tag.
3. Captions: 1–3 words at a time, centred, large and plain.
4. Shorts "thumbnail" = a strong mid-video frame. Their thumbnails carry no added text.
