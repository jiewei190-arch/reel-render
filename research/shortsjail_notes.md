# "How I FINALLY got out of Shorts Jail (and went viral)" — Creator Rant
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnMYPL927i8 (9:06). Notes are paraphrased; the timestamps point into the video.
The full transcript is not committed, because it is the creator's copyrighted text. To get it locally, run
`python research/get_transcript.py DnMYPL927i8` (uses youtube-transcript-api, MIT).

## The model: three "jails", each with a view band and one fix
| Jail | Stuck at | What it means | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Swipe jail | < 1,000 views | Fails the ~3-second test: viewers swipe away | A real hook in the first seconds (ideally a "triple hook") |
| 2. Reaction jail | 1K–10K views | People watch but feel nothing, so they don't share | Pick the target emotion before writing; cut everything that doesn't build to it |
| 3. Format jail (the "30K jail") | ~10K–30K, then a jump to 100K+ | Content is good, but the presentation isn't a proven format | Copy the *format* of outliers and put your own spin on it |

## Tactics, in order
1. **[0:19] The 3-second test.** Under ~1K views means you failed it: nothing in the opening makes anyone want to stay.
2. **[0:38] Triple hook.** Hook on three channels at once:
   - **Visual:** something odd or curious on screen from frame 1. Their example: a plane flying strangely.
   - **Verbal:** the first spoken line.
   - **Textual:** an on-screen caption that *adds to the story*, e.g. "devs didn't plan for this" or "craziest landing ever".
3. **[1:10] Cut hard.** He trimmed a 77 s clip because "77 seconds is way too long", and changed the ending so it has a payoff.
4. **[1:31] One very strong hook can be enough.** A 61M-view example used only visual + verbal hooks. Subtitles don't count as a text hook because they add nothing; a caption that adds story does.
5. **[2:29] A caption can carry an ordinary clip.** Without it the clip would look like "random camera roll".
6. **[2:52] Perspective.** Viewers don't care about you, your job or your game; you have to make them care.
7. **[3:24] Choose the reaction first** (borrowed from Shaan Puri, My First Million). People share feelings, not information: LOL, WOW, WTF, OMG, AW, or "so true / I agree". Pick one, write the script toward it, then check each line: if it doesn't build to that reaction, cut it.
8. **[4:34] Every second counts.** In a LOL example, cuts skip the dull bits; most creators keep 30 s of setup before the payoff.
9. **[4:59] How distribution works (his claim).** A Short gets a first batch of up to ~100 impressions; enough engagement in that batch unlocks more.
10. **[5:30] Never pad for retention.** Never make a Short longer to "help retention"; that's the golden rule. A 15 s tutorial beats a 40 s one on the same subject.
11. **[6:17] Format jail.** If you've passed hooks and reactions but stall around 30K, the presentation is the problem.
12. **[6:41] "Success leaves clues"** (Tony Robbins). Find creators already getting the result and copy how they do it.
13. **[6:50] Outliers.** An outlier is a video doing far above that channel's normal, e.g. 1M views on a channel that usually gets 5K. For Shorts, study the *format* (how it's presented), not the topic or the packaging.
14. **[7:22] Re-skinnable formats.** His examples: "filming yourself as two characters, and someone else filming that"; a strong opening visual (the camera inside a microwave); and "two thinking perspectives" (both nervous customer and nervous barista), which gets a "so true" reaction.
15. **[8:24] Use outlier-finder tools,** then put your own spin on what works.
16. The rest is promotion (viral.school, a "lock pick" guide, a 3D-animation course); nothing to act on.

## Numbers mentioned
- 3-second test; < 1K / 1K–10K / ~30K / 100K view bands; ~100 first impressions.
- 77 s was "way too long"; 15 s beats 40 s for the same tutorial; his outlier example is 5K normal vs 1M.
- No posting cadence is given in this video.

## What this means for our cozy bear series
- Frame 1 needs a **visual hook**: the bear already mid-action in a curious moment, e.g. a glowing pot at midnight.
- Use a **caption that adds story**, not subtitles: one POV / relatable line at the top. Didi's top reels do exactly this (see `didi_format_notes.md`).
- Target emotion **AW + "so true"** for every episode. Cut any beat that doesn't build to it.
- **No padding.** Stop as soon as the payoff lands. The Instagram rule (60–70 s for 2D) is the upper limit; the top Didi reels run 24–51 s.
- Study the **format** of Didi's outliers, never their art or characters. Our bear is the owner's own character.
