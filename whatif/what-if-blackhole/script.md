Cast: HERO ("you", the See in the Dark look: MUSTARD HOODIE #E1AD01, dark-teal trousers, black round head; in space a clear round bubble helmet over the hood and a slim white backpack, nothing else), DEV (best friend, ORANGE BEANIE, films everything, jokes, loses his toy robot BOLT), MINA (RED SCARF + bun, careful, checks everything twice, owns the ship's telescope), BOLT (Dev's knee-high toy robot: square head, one antenna, round blue eye, grey body with an orange stripe). Ship: a small round cartoon ship ("the Pebble") with one big window. Lip-sync ONLY the quoted words (HERO line 7, DEV lines 12, 13, 19, MINA line 44). Built to IAWAKEN_PLAYBOOK.md: package first (package.md), cold open = flash-forward, 0:20 open loop answered in the last minute, escalation small black hole -> giant black hole -> inside, a pattern interrupt every 20-30 s, the biggest reveal in the last 60 s.

Thought experiment, said on screen and in the ending: nobody can fly 1,560 light-years, and nobody has fallen into a black hole. The physics is real (sources.md).

## COLD OPEN
1. You just fell into a black hole. And you feel fine.
2. A smaller one would have stretched you like spaghetti. This one is gentle.
3. Your friends are watching from the ship. To them, you've stopped moving, and you're turning red.
4. But you're still falling. The centre is twelve point eight seconds away.
5. So who's right? Your friends, or you? The answer is in the last minute, and it's the part nobody expects.

## TITLE: What if you fell into a black hole?

## THE TRIP
6. Rewind. You, Dev and Mina are on a tiny ship, a very long way from home.
7. Dev films everything. Mina checks everything twice. And you're the one who says, "Let's get closer."
8. First stop: the closest black hole we know of, Gaia BH1, about fifteen hundred and sixty light-years away.
9. A black hole is what you get when a huge amount of stuff is squeezed into a tiny space. Its gravity is so strong that nothing can climb back out. Not even light.
10. Gaia BH1 has about ten times the mass of our Sun, squeezed into a ball about sixty kilometres wide.
11. The edge of that ball is called the event horizon. It's not a surface. It's the point of no return.
12. Dev leans on the window. "That's it? It's tiny. Can we go closer?"
13. Mina says no. So Dev sends Bolt instead, his little robot. "Go on, buddy. Wave for the camera."

## RULE 1 CARD: GRAVITY PULLS YOUR FEET HARDER.
14. Gravity gets stronger the closer you get. Even on Earth, your feet are pulled a tiny bit harder than your head. Far too little to notice.
15. Near a small black hole, that tiny difference becomes enormous.
16. A thousand kilometres from the edge, Bolt's feet are pulled over three hundred times Earth's gravity harder than his head.
17. So Bolt gets stretched long, and squeezed thin. Scientists really call this spaghettification.
18. And it's real. In 2020, European telescopes watched a black hole pull apart a star about as heavy as our Sun.
19. Dev stares at the empty screen. "He was waterproof," he whispers.
20. So a small black hole would stretch you before you even reach its edge.
21. But here's the strange part. The bigger the black hole, the gentler its edge.

## THE GIANT
22. Next stop: the centre of our galaxy, twenty-seven thousand light-years from Earth.
23. Sagittarius A star. About four million times the mass of our Sun. In 2022, the Event Horizon Telescope got its first picture: a glowing ring around a dark shadow.
24. Its event horizon is about twenty-five million kilometres across. It would fit inside the orbit of Mercury.
25. It's so big that at its edge, the stretch is less than a ten-thousandth of Earth's gravity. You wouldn't feel a thing.
26. Mina checks your suit twice. Dev holds the camera steady. And you let go of the ship.

## THE FALL
27. In 2024, NASA scientists simulated this exact fall on a supercomputer.
28. Starting far out, you'd fall for about three hours, looping around the black hole twice on the way down.
29. The sky gets strange. Gravity bends the starlight, so the stars smear into arcs and rings.
30. The dark shadow ahead grows bigger and bigger, until it fills almost everything.
31. And then you cross the event horizon.
32. There's no wall. No bump. No alarm. Nothing.

## RULE 2 CARD: THE EDGE ISN'T A WALL.
33. The event horizon isn't made of anything. It's just a distance.
34. Outside it, a rocket fast enough could still pull you away. Inside it, you'd need to go faster than light. And nothing can.
35. So you drift right past the point of no return, and feel exactly the same.
36. But back on the ship, Dev and Mina see something very different.

## RULE 3 CARD: GRAVITY SLOWS TIME.
37. Einstein worked out that gravity slows down time. The stronger the gravity, the slower clocks tick.
38. That's not just theory. In 2010, scientists in Colorado lifted a super-accurate clock just thirty-three centimetres, and it ticked faster than one below it.
39. Near a black hole, that effect becomes enormous.
40. Through the telescope, Mina sees you slowing down. Your wave takes longer, and longer.
41. The light leaving you has to climb out of all that gravity. It loses energy, and stretches into redder colours.
42. So you turn red. Then dim. Then, just above the edge, you seem to freeze.
43. They never see you cross. Your light just fades, until there's nothing left to see.
44. Dev lowers the camera. Mina keeps watching. "You're still there," she says. "You just stopped."

## INSIDE
45. Meanwhile, you're inside. And for you, time feels completely normal.
46. NASA's simulation says that from the moment you cross, you have about twelve point eight seconds.
47. Turn around, fire your rockets as hard as you like. It won't help.
48. Because inside a black hole, something flips.

## RULE 4 CARD: THE CENTRE IS NOT A PLACE.
49. Outside, the centre is a place. Something you could point at, and steer away from.
50. Past the horizon, space and time swap roles. Every path you can take leads inward.
51. The centre stops being a place in front of you. It becomes a moment in your future.
52. You can't dodge it, any more than you can dodge tomorrow.
53. What's at the very centre? Nobody knows. Einstein's equations break down there.

## THE ANSWER
54. So who was right? Your friends, who saw you freeze? Or you, with twelve point eight seconds left?
55. Both. They saw the last light you sent before you crossed. You lived every second after it.
56. You didn't stop. You and your friends just stopped sharing the same time.
57. And here's the part nobody expects. Light can still fall in. So even inside, you can look back up and see the stars.
58. And somewhere in that shrinking circle of light is a tiny ship, with Dev and Mina still waving.

## ENDING
59. In real life, nobody has fallen into a black hole, and the closest one is fifteen hundred light-years away. We're safe.
60. But black holes are real. We've photographed two of them, and watched one tear a star apart.
61. So, would you take the plunge? And what would your last message be? Tell us in the comments.
62. And to see what happens when time stops completely, watch this one next.
