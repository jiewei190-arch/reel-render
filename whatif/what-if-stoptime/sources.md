# Sources: What If You Could Freeze Time for 10 Seconds? (checked Nov 5, 2026)

Legal note (same "safe version" approach as eps 3-5): an original story. No film, show, anime, game or comic is named or copied. The power is only a finger snap (no remote, stopwatch, pocket watch or gadget), the hero's look is original (see the script header), and the frozen kitchen is shown by the hero walking and moving things by hand, never by a sped-up runner circling a room to music, with no goggles, headphones or silver hair. No franchise names appear anywhere, including here.

Thought-experiment framing: stopping time is impossible, and the script says so (lines 4, 30, 61). The physics is used to ask "what would happen if only these things stopped?". The snap "learning" between snaps (lines 13, 24) and frozen objects keeping their motion (line 30, said as "in our story") are the story's rules, not fact claims. Story beats with no fact claim (the juice, the puddle, Biscuit and the butter, the bin, the stuck kitchen clock, the 40-second watch gap, the milk jug and the bowl, "zero point zero seconds", Biscuit moving in frozen time, the snap disappearing) are the hero's own story.

| 8 | Light travels about 300,000 km every second; nothing is faster | Speed of light in vacuum is exactly 299,792,458 m/s (SI definition; NIST, physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?c). | 19 | Air pressure comes from molecules bumping; breathing in, the diaphragm makes room in the chest and outside air flows in | OpenStax 13.4 (above): pressure is the result of molecular collisions with surfaces. | 36-39 | The stopped clock illusion (chronostasis): eyes move in jumps (saccades) about three times a second; vision is blurred/suppressed during them; the brain fills the gap; the first tick seen seems too long; 2001 London study: the brain extends the new view back in time to just before the eye movement | Yarrow, Haggard, Heal, Brown & Rothwell 2001, "Illusory perceptions of space and time preserve cross-saccadic perceptual continuity", Nature 414:302-305, doi:10.1038/35104551 (University College London): "the brain extends the percept of the saccadic target backwards in time to just before the onset of the saccade"; illusory duration grows with saccade length. Full text: City Research Online, openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/333. Saccade rate: "In everyday life we make about three saccades per second" (2021 review, Cognitive Neurodynamics, PMC8286912); free viewing ~3/s (PMC3625326). Saccadic suppression (vision dimmed during saccades) is standard; the blur is "hidden" in the same sense. A 2020 Scientific Reports paper (doi:10.1038/s41598-020-73830-6) argues the backward shift is not the whole story, so line 39 reports what the 2001 study "showed" and doesn't claim it's settled. |

## Dropped or softened
- **Human reaction time "~0.25 s":** the 250 ms figure appears only in secondary/blog sources; peer-reviewed young-adult measurements cluster around 210-225 ms (e.g. a PMC study of simple visual reaction time by stimulus colour, PMC6916673). It was not needed for the story and was cut rather than stated as "about a fifth of a second".
- **Hau 1999 "17 m/s, about bicycle/car speed":** verified (Nature 397:594; Harvard Magazine gave ~38 mph), cut for length. The ending uses only the 2001 stopped-light result.
- **Harvard-Smithsonian CfA room-temperature stopped light (Phillips, Walsworth et al., 2001, rubidium vapour):** real but a second experiment would crowd the ending; not used. The script says "Lene Hau's team" so it doesn't claim to be the only one.
- **"Stopped light" wording:** softened in the notes above (information stored in atoms); "trapped a pulse of light ... then let it go again" matches Harvard's own press wording.
- **Blinking and tear film:** you'd blink with no tear film to spread if moisture froze; mechanism plausible but no clean source tying it to the thought experiment, and the episode was full. Cut.
- **Silence (sound needs moving air, ~343 m/s):** true and simple, but cut from the spoken script for length; the dark/air rules carry the "frozen world is a trap" idea. Could return as an on-screen gag in Snap 2 (no narration).
- **Frozen objects keep momentum when time restarts (line 30):** nobody can test a time freeze, so this is presented as "in our story", consistent with Newton's first law, not as a fact about real time.
- **Molecule speed:** "roughly five hundred metres a second" (rms/typical speed); not a single exact average.
- **Kitchen air weight:** a worked estimate for one room size, said as "about as much as you do".
- **GPS "10 km a day":** softened to "soon be off by kilometres" (see line 48).
- **Chronostasis mechanism:** reported as the 2001 study's finding, not as settled science, because later work (Sci Rep 2020) debates it.
- **"Light is the fastest thing":** kept in its everyday sense (nothing travels faster than light in vacuum); no mention of light being slower in glass or water.
