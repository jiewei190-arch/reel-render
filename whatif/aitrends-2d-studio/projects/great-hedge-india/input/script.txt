Britain built a 2,500-mile barrier across India. Then forgot it.
British-ruled India, the 1800s. The goal? Taxing salt.
To stop salt smugglers, they built a customs line about 2,500 miles long.
Hundreds of miles of it was a living hedge of thorny bushes, up to 12 feet high.
People compared it to the Great Wall of China.
At its peak, in 1872, more than 14,000 men guarded it.
But here's the twist...
In 1879, it was abandoned. And it was forgotten so completely, standard histories skipped it.
In the 1990s, a London library conservator made three trips to India just to find it.
What he found: one small raised embankment. It may be all that's left.
