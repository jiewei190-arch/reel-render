One night in 1888, tens of thousands of sheep lost their minds.
November third. Oxfordshire, England. Around eight at night.
Across about two hundred square miles, flocks panicked at the same moment.
They broke out of their fields and scattered for miles in the dark.
But here's the strange part...
The next morning, sheep were found crowded into corners of fields, some panting with terror under hedges.
The Times wrote that a thousand men could not have frightened and released all these sheep.
And a year later... it happened again. In Berkshire.
The best explanation ever published? An intensely dark night, with occasional flashes of lightning. And sheep are notoriously timid.
To this day, nobody knows what really set them off.
