Britain planned to fight U-boats with an aircraft carrier made of ice.
World War Two. U-boats were hunting convoys where planes couldn't reach.
Inventor Geoffrey Pyke's fix: a floating airfield, made of ice.
Not plain ice. Pykrete: wood pulp mixed with water, then frozen.
Code name: Habakkuk. 600 metres long. It would have been the biggest ship ever.
But here's the part... at a 1943 conference, Mountbatten reportedly fired his pistol at a block of it.
The bullet bounced off... and ricocheted around the room.
A prototype was built on Patricia Lake, Canada, chilled by a one-horsepower motor.
But costs ballooned, and longer-range planes closed the gap.
By the end of 1943, the ice ship was shelved.
The real ship was never built. The prototype took three summers to melt.
