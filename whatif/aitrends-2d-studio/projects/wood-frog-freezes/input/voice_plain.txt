This frog freezes solid every winter, and its heart stops.
It's the wood frog, the only North American frog found north of the Arctic Circle.
But in winter, up to two thirds of the water in its body turns to ice.
No heartbeat. No breathing. No blood flow.
But how does it survive? Its liver floods its body with sugar.
That sugar works like antifreeze, so ice can't form inside its cells.
But in Alaska, it goes further: frozen for up to 218 days.
And before winter sets in, it can freeze and thaw again and again.
Then spring comes. It thaws out and comes back to life.
And it hops off to a pond to breed... like winter never happened.
