In 1814, a wave of beer flooded London. Over a million pints. In one wave. October 17th. The Horse Shoe Brewery, central London. Its giant vat of porter stood 22 feet tall, held together by iron hoops. That afternoon, one hoop slipped off. Nobody was too worried. About an hour later, the whole vat burst. The wave knocked open other vats, and smashed down the brewery wall. Beer poured into St Giles, one of the poorest slums in London, where families lived in basements. Eight people died.
