Fall into a small black hole, and you'd turn into spaghetti.
It's a thought experiment, but here's what scientists say would happen.
Near a small one, gravity pulls your feet harder than your head.
You'd stretch long and thin. Scientists really call it spaghettification.
But a supermassive black hole, about 4 million times the Sun's mass, is different.
The stretch at its edge is so gentle, you'd cross the point of no return without noticing.
But to a friend watching from far away, you'd never quite get there.
You'd slow down, fade to red, and seem to freeze at the edge.
But inside, every path leads to the center. NASA's simulation says the stretching hits 12.8 seconds later.
So either way... it's spaghetti. Just later.
