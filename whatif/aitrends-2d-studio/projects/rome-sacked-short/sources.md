# Sources: "The Day Rome Got Sacked" (Toon 3D test Short)

Every spoken claim, with its source. Softened claims are marked.

| Line | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Visigoths entered Rome on **24 August 410**. | Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Sack of Rome (410)" / "Alaric" (date 24 Aug 410, the sack lasted three days). P. Heather, *The Fall of the Roman Empire* (2005), ch. 5. |
| 2 | Nobody had broken into Rome in **almost 800 years**. | The previous capture was the Gallic sack of c. 390 BC (Livy 5.38-49; Polybius gives 387/386 BC). 410 AD is ~795-800 years later. Britannica, "Sack of Rome (410)": first time in nearly 800 years the city fell to a foreign enemy. Said as "almost". |
| 4 | It was the **Salarian Gate**. | Procopius, *History of the Wars* III.2.14-24 (the Goths entered by the Salarian Gate); Britannica. |
| 5 | **"Some say, from the inside."** (softened) | Procopius gives two stories: slaves given to Roman nobles opened the gate, or the servants of the noblewoman Proba opened it out of pity for the starving city. Other sources (Orosius, Sozomen) speak of betrayal/surprise without detail. Historians treat both stories as uncertain, hence "some say". |
| 6 | The Visigoths (Alaric's army) were in. | Britannica, "Alaric"; Heather (2005). |
| 7 | The looting lasted **three days**. | Orosius, *Seven Books of History Against the Pagans* 7.39-40 (left on the third day); Britannica. Orosius also says Alaric ordered churches (St Peter's, St Paul's) respected as sanctuaries; the sack was relatively restrained, which is why the Short shows fire/smoke/silhouettes and no violence. |
| 8 | **Two years earlier** Rome paid them off with **3,000 pounds of pepper**. | Zosimus, *New History* 5.41: the ransom of 408 AD (first siege) was 5,000 lb of gold, 30,000 lb of silver, 4,000 silk tunics, 3,000 scarlet-dyed skins and **3,000 lb of pepper**. 410 - 408 = 2 years. |
| 9 | "This time, pepper won't fix it." | Narrative line: after 408 (ransom) and 409 (second siege, puppet emperor Attalus), negotiations with Honorius failed and the 410 siege ended in the sack. Heather (2005); Britannica, "Alaric". |
| 10 | The emperor (**Honorius**) was not in Rome but in **Ravenna**. | The Western court moved to Ravenna in 402 AD. Britannica, "Honorius" and "Ravenna". |
| 11-12 | Told "Rome has perished", he panicked about his **chicken named Roma**. (softened: "Or so the story goes.") | Procopius, *History of the Wars* III.2.25-26: a eunuch, keeper of the poultry, told Honorius that Roma had perished; Honorius cried out that it had just eaten from his hand, thinking of his favourite bird "Roma", until told it was the city. Procopius wrote ~140 years later; most historians regard it as a legend/satire, so the Short says "Or so the story goes." |
| 13 | "How did the greatest empire on Earth end up here?" | Open question, the hook into the long History Hook episode. "Greatest empire" is rhetorical, not a measured claim. |

## Visuals that are artistic licence (not claims)
- Gaius the guard, Senator Pomponius, the messenger and all faces are invented characters. The emperor is a cartoon, not a portrait of Honorius.
- The chicken's look, its perch and the "ROMA" plaque are invented to stage Procopius' story.
- The "PIPER" sacks (Latin *piper* = pepper) and the house where the ransom is handed over are staging. The 408 ransom was paid to Alaric's army outside the city.
- Visigoth soldiers are drawn as dark silhouettes with simple conical helmets (no horned helmets, which are a myth). No violence is shown: fire, smoke, silhouettes and sound only.
