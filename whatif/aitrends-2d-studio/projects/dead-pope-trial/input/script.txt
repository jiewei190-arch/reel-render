A pope once put a dead pope on trial.
The dead pope lost.
Rome, eight ninety-seven.
Pope Stephen the Sixth has his predecessor, Formosus, dug up.
He'd been dead for nine months.
They dress him in his papal robes, and sit him on a throne.
Wait. How does a dead man defend himself?
You see, a deacon stands next to him and answers for him.
Badly.
Verdict: guilty. Everything he did as pope? Cancelled.
Months later, a new pope cancelled the verdict...
and banned putting dead people on trial.
