In 1980, one tiny drilling mistake swallowed an entire lake.
A Texaco rig was drilling for oil on Lake Peigneur, Louisiana, right above a salt mine.
At about 1,230 feet down, the drill punched into the mine.
The lake started draining like a bathtub.
The whirlpool swallowed the oil rig, eleven barges, a tugboat, and 65 acres of land.
Then the canal reversed. The Gulf of Mexico flowed in backwards, creating Louisiana's tallest waterfall.
Days later, nine of the sunken barges popped back up like bath toys.
Every person got out alive.
The lake went from 10 feet deep to 200 feet of salt water.
