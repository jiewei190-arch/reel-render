In 1997, the ocean made a noise nobody could explain.
Underwater microphones were listening for volcanoes in the Pacific. Instead, they caught... this.
Ultra-low, extremely loud, and about a minute long.
Sped up 16 times so humans could hear it, it went... bloop.
Sensors about 3,000 miles apart picked it up, from the remote South Pacific.
Reportedly, it was far louder than a blue whale, the loudest known animal.
So people guessed: a giant sea creature nobody had ever seen.
But here's the part... in 2005, NOAA found the source.
Not a sea monster. An icequake.
An iceberg, cracking and breaking away from an Antarctic glacier. The monster... was ice.
