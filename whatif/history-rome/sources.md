# Sources: How Did the Roman Empire Get Destroyed? (checked Nov 8, 2026)

Approach: mainstream reference works (Encyclopaedia Britannica), the ancient writers the modern books rely on (Ammianus Marcellinus, Jerome, Orosius, Procopius, Zosimus, Prosper), and the standard modern histories: Peter Heather, *The Fall of the Roman Empire: A New History* (2005); Bryan Ward-Perkins, *The Fall of Rome and the End of Civilization* (2005); Peter Brown, *The World of Late Antiquity* (1971); Kyle Harper, *The Fate of Rome* (2017); Alexander Demandt, *Der Fall Roms* (1984; 2nd ed. 2014). Where scholars disagree the script says so ("historians argue", "historians still argue", "historians still fight about it").

Story framing: LIVIA, GAIUS, FELIX and Felix's son, and the family coin are fictional people used to tell the story; nothing about them is a fact claim. The years and events they witness are real. Emperors, Alaric, Stilicho, Odoacer, Jerome and Ammianus are real people, drawn as stylised generic figures with name chips (no portrait likeness). No gore: violence is shown only as silhouettes, fire, smoke and sound.

## Dropped or softened
- **Child-selling detail at the Danube (Ammianus 31.4.11):** sourced, but cut for the audience; line 36 says "cheated and mistreated".
- **Absolute numbers:** no figures for the Goths at the Danube, the dead at Adrianople, Alaric's army or the 30,000 of Zosimus; only "tens of thousands", "two-thirds (Ammianus)", "thousands".
- **"First sack in 800 years":** said as "about eight hundred years" because the Gallic sack is dated 390 or 387/386 BC.
- **Number of 3rd-century emperors:** "more than twenty" (counts range from ~20 to 50+ depending on usurpers).
- **Silver percentages:** only "often less than five percent" by the 270s (figures vary by mint and assay).
- **Inflation size:** no multiplier is given; Rathbone argues for stable prices until the 270s. The script says "historians argue about how bad it got".
- **Pope Leo and Gaiseric (455):** the tradition that Leo persuaded Gaiseric not to kill or burn comes from Prosper; cut for length.
- **Who opened the gate in 410:** unknown (Britannica lists slaves, a bribed official or someone else); the script just says the gate "swings open".
- **476 as the end:** the script presents it as the "history book" date and then questions it (Julius Nepos to 480, the East to 1453).
- **Plague and climate (Harper 2017):** real and important, but a sixth cause would crowd the story; it can go in the pinned comment.
- **"Mistakes":** narrative wording; historians argue over how far these were choices and how far bad luck.
