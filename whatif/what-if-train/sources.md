# Sources: What If You Trained Every Day for 3 Years? (checked Oct 30, 2026)

Routine: 100 push-ups, 100 sit-ups, 100 squats, 10 km run, every day. 3 years = 1,095 days (ignoring a leap day).
Totals: 100 x 1,095 = 109,500 of each exercise; 10 km x 1,095 = 10,950 km. Earth's equatorial circumference is ~40,075 km (NASA), so 10,950 km ≈ 27%, "more than a quarter of the way around the Earth". 10 km = 6.2 miles.

Story beats with no fact claim (the 23 push-ups on day 1, the 1 h 20 min first run, the sub-55-minute run in year 1, the textbook backpack, the hair in the drain) are the hero's own story and are not presented as typical numbers.

## Dropped or softened
- **Push-up and heart-disease fact (Yang et al. 2019, JAMA Netw Open, firefighters >40 vs <10 push-ups, incidence rate ratio 0.04):** verified, but cut for length; it is also only an association in male firefighters.
- **Muscle gain in kg per month or year for a natural beginner:** no peer-reviewed meta-analysis gives a clean figure (most numbers online are coach models), so the script makes no kg claim. It only says light work stops building once it's easy.
- **Bone density gains from running:** the 2023 meta-analysis on impact exercise and bone (J Bone Miner Res) is mixed and site-specific, so it was left out.
- **"Over 32 km a week doubles or triples stress fracture risk":** from a Cureus review only, so the script just says overuse can become a stress fracture.
- **Exact resting heart rate drop in bpm from Reimers 2018:** the abstract gives no single figure, so the script gives the Mayo Clinic range instead and doesn't say how far the hero's dropped.
- **DOMS "peaks at 48 h":** reviews disagree (24-48 vs 24-72 h), so it's "one to three days".
- **Telogen effluvium in the hero:** framed as underfueling or weight loss, which are AAD-listed triggers, not as exercise itself causing hair loss.
