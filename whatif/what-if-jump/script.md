# What If Every Day, Your Jump Doubled? (script v1, Oct 30)

Narrator: the locked What If voice (whatif/VOICE.md), second person, calm and warm. Cast: HERO ("you", never lip-synced except quoted lines), MINA (little sister), DEV (best friend, basketball), BISCUIT (the cat). About 1,000 words, so ~6 minutes at the ep 1 pace.

## COLD OPEN
1. You wake up, stretch, and jump off your bed. You hit the ceiling. Hard.
2. Nobody knows why, but the rule is simple: every day, your jump doubles.
3. Today, you jump like a normal person. About half a metre. Tomorrow, one metre. Then two. Then four.
4. That doesn't sound like much. Until you keep doubling.
5. Let's see how far you get. And whether you make it back down.

## TITLE: What if every day, your jump doubled?

## DAY 1-3: THE FUN PART
6. Day one. Dev bets you can't touch the basketball rim. You jump, you miss, and Biscuit doesn't even look up.
7. Day two. One metre. You take one step, rise, and dunk on a regular ten-foot hoop. The whole court goes silent.
8. Dev drops his phone. "Do that again."
9. Day three. Two metres. Dev raises the hoop to twelve feet, as high as it goes. You dunk it like it's nothing.
10. Your jump is now higher than the best vertical leap any athlete has ever recorded.
10b. Dev films everything. By dinner, the clip has a million views. The comments all say the same thing: fake.
10c. Mina just wants to know one thing. "Can you get my kite out of the tree?"
10d. You can. Easily. For now.

## RULE 1 CARD: JUMPING IS EASY. LANDING ISN'T.
11. Here's the thing nobody tells you. Every metre you go up, you have to come down.
12. Day four. Four metres. You float over the world record high-jump bar, two point four five metres, from a standing start, and land with a crunch.
13. Your knees hurt. That's new.
14. Day five. Eight metres. You clear the pole-vault world record, six point three one, without a pole, and land on your neighbour's roof.
15. Mina has to bring a ladder.
15b. Biscuit watches from the window, deeply unimpressed. She's been jumping on roofs her whole life.

## RULE 2 CARD: GRAVITY DOESN'T CARE HOW YOU GOT UP THERE.
16. Day six. Sixteen metres. That's a five-storey building.
17. Jumping up is fine. But falling sixteen metres is the same fall, no matter how you got there.
18. Doctors say that from about four storeys up, roughly half of falls are fatal.
19. So you stop jumping, and start planning your landings. A trampoline. A pool. A very large haystack.
20. Day seven. Thirty-two metres. To go that high, you leave the ground at ninety kilometres an hour. The wind rips your hoodie off.
21. Day eight. Sixty-four metres. You could jump over Niagara Falls. You don't. Mina has hidden your shoes.
21b. Day nine. A hundred and twenty-eight metres. You land in the town pond, and the ducks file a complaint.
21c. Day ten. Two hundred and fifty-six metres. The local news calls you "the boy who won't stay down".
21d. Day eleven. Five hundred metres. Every jump takes ten seconds up and ten seconds down. You start timing your jumps with the traffic lights.

## RULE 3 CARD: AIR FIGHTS BACK.
22. Here's a surprise. The higher you go, the more the air starts to matter.
23. Falling through air, your body can't go faster than about two hundred kilometres an hour. Skydivers call it terminal velocity.
24. So from day ten, every landing hits about the same: like a car crash at highway speed. Every single time.
25. Day twelve. One kilometre. You sail over the tallest building on Earth, the Burj Khalifa, eight hundred and twenty-eight metres.
26. The view is incredible. The landing needs a lake.
26b. Days thirteen and fourteen are a blur. Two kilometres. Four. You jump above the clouds, and they're colder and wetter than they look. You come down soaked.
27. Day fifteen. Eight kilometres. You almost reach the top of Mount Everest. Almost. Eight thousand eight hundred and forty-nine metres. So close.

## RULE 4 CARD: THE SKY RUNS OUT OF AIR.
28. Day sixteen. Sixteen kilometres. You're higher than Everest, higher than passenger jets.
29. And there's barely anything left to breathe. Up here, you'd pass out in seconds.
30. Mina makes you wear a diving tank. Dev makes you wear a helmet with his name on it.
30b. On days seventeen and eighteen, you go higher than any balloon. Higher than the highest skydive ever, forty-one kilometres up. The sky above you turns from blue to black.
31. Day nineteen. A hundred and thirty-one kilometres. You've passed the edge of space, a hundred kilometres up.
32. For a few minutes, you're technically an astronaut. The Earth curves below you. It's silent.
33. Then you fall. All the way. To leave the ground that fast, you'd be moving faster than a rifle bullet, and air at that speed glows hot.
33b. That night, Mina can't sleep. She sits on the end of your bed and asks the question nobody else will.
33c. "What happens when it doesn't stop?"
33d. You don't have an answer. You do the maths instead. And the maths says there's a day when you go up, and gravity can't bring you back.

## RULE 5 CARD: UP ISN'T ORBIT.
34. Day twenty-one. Over five hundred kilometres. You pass the International Space Station.
35. The astronauts wave. They're not floating because they're high. They're floating because they're falling sideways, around the Earth, at almost eight kilometres a second.
36. You're only going up. So you always come back down.
36b. Days twenty-two, twenty-three, twenty-four. A thousand kilometres. Two thousand. Four thousand. Every fall takes longer than the last. You've started bringing snacks.
37. Until day twenty-five.


## THE LAST JUMP
38. Day twenty-five. To jump this high, you'd have to leave the ground faster than eleven point two kilometres a second.
39. That's escape velocity. The speed where gravity never pulls you back.
40. Mina holds your hand. "Don't," she says. "Please."
41. Dev holds up a sign: "Bring me a moon rock."
42. Biscuit finally looks up.
43. You bend your knees.

## ENDING
44. In real life, nobody's jump doubles. The best human leap is about a metre and a half, and that's plenty.
45. But if it did, the scariest part wouldn't be going up. It would be every single time you came down.
46. So, what day would you stop? Tell us in the comments.
47. And subscribe for more What Ifs.
