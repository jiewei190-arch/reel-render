# What If You Could Level Up Your Strength Every Day? (script v1, Oct 31)

Narrator: the locked What If voice (whatif/VOICE.md, Kokoro am_fenrir), second person, calm and warm. Cast: HERO ("you", never lip-synced except quoted lines), MINA (little sister, red scarf, times everything), DEV (best friend, orange beanie, films everything and reads your stats out loud), BISCUIT (the cat). About 990 spoken words, so ~6.5 minutes at the ep 4 pace.

Hero look for this episode (original design, keep it this way; full art brief in STYLE.md): lean and a bit gangly, short dark chestnut-brown hair with one stubborn swoop at the front, a hip-length navy bomber jacket with a cyan zip pull and cyan cuff stripes, a plain white tee, charcoal joggers, white trainers. Warm brown eyes that never glow. Never a long coat, black-on-black, hair over the eyes, a hood up, gloves, daggers or armour. The leveling-anime nod stays a feeling, not a copy: our own rounded "Stat Card" (gold level badge, STR in kilos, chevron XP bar, "LEVEL UP!"), our own small zipper-shaped rift ("the Zip") that it pops out of, cyan-and-gold glow, a steam aura at night. No series names, no character or place names, no "System", "Player", "Quest", "Arise", gates, hunters, ranks, statues, shadows or its music.

Strength rule used all episode: you start able to lift 40 kg. Each level makes you about 26% stronger than the day before, so every three levels your strength doubles (STR = 40 x 2^(level/3)). One level every morning.

## COLD OPEN
1. Monday. Breakfast. You lift your spoon, and the air above your cereal unzips.
2. A tiny glowing tear, edged with gold sparks. Out of it floats a screen, like a video game.
3. A big gold number one. A little dumbbell. And the words "LEVEL UP!"
4. Dev reads it out loud. "Strength, fifty kilos." He's already filming.
5. Every morning, one more level. Each one makes you about a quarter stronger. Stack three, and your strength doubles.
6. Nobody knows where it came from. Biscuit tries to eat it. Let's see what thirty days does to you.

## TITLE: What if you could level up your strength every day?

## LEVEL 1: THE BAGS
7. Level one. You carry all the shopping home in one hand.
8. Dev squints at your arms. "They look exactly the same."
9. He's right. And that's real life, too.

## RULE 1 CARD: STRONGER STARTS IN YOUR BRAIN.
10. When people start lifting weights, they get stronger fast. But at first, their muscles barely grow.
11. What changes is the wiring. Your nervous system learns to switch on more muscle fibres at once.
12. A classic study from 1979 found most early strength gain came from the nerves. Muscle growth took over later, after weeks.

## LEVEL 6: THE SOFA
13. Level six. A hundred and sixty kilos. The TV remote is under the sofa.
14. You lift the sofa with one hand. Biscuit stays asleep on it.

## LEVEL 11: THE RECORD
15. Level eleven. "Five hundred and eight kilos," Dev reads. Then he goes quiet.
16. The heaviest deadlift ever recorded is five hundred and one kilos. Hafþór Björnsson, in Iceland, in 2020.
17. You beat it at breakfast. Then a red line appears. "Bones: not included."

## RULE 2 CARD: MUSCLES PULL. BONES HAVE TO HOLD.
18. Muscles pull on your bones through tough cords called tendons, and the bones take the load.
19. Bone is amazing. A piece of thigh bone the size of your fingernail can take around two tonnes of squeezing along its length.
20. But pull, bend or twist it, and it gives up much sooner.
21. Training makes bones and tendons stronger too, but more slowly than muscle. In teen athletes, muscle can outpace tendon, and that gap may raise the risk of injury.
22. That record took years. Level-eleven muscles on level-zero bones would snap something.
23. Next morning, the line reads "Bones: upgraded." Real life has no update.

## LEVEL 16: THE CAR
24. Level sixteen. Over sixteen hundred kilos. Mina's football is under the car, parked on the grass.
25. A small family car weighs about one and a half tonnes. You lift.
26. The car goes up. You go down. Ankle deep in the lawn.
27. You try the patio instead. Crack. Mum's tiles.

## RULE 3 CARD: PUSH DOWN, AND THE GROUND PUSHES BACK.
28. Newton's third law: when you push on something, it pushes back on you just as hard.
29. Push up on a car, and it pushes down on you. That weight, plus yours, goes through your feet into the ground.
30. Pressure is force divided by area. Two shoe soles are tiny.
31. Holding that car, your feet press more than five times harder than soft, wet ground is rated to hold.
32. Spread the force over a bigger area, and the pressure drops. That's how snowshoes work. Mina fetches two planks.

## LEVEL 19: NIGHT TRAINING
33. Level nineteen. Daylight lifting is getting awkward, so you train at night, in the park.
34. Push-ups under the streetlights. Squats with the park bench. Mina times. Dev films.
35. As the night gets colder, you start to glow. A shimmering aura rises off your shoulders.
36. Dev leans closer. "Wait. Is that steam?"

## RULE 4 CARD: MUSCLES RUN ON FOOD, AND THEY GET HOT.
37. Muscles turn food energy into movement, but your body only manages about a quarter. Most of the rest becomes heat.
38. The heat warms your skin, and moisture rising off it turns to cloud in the cold air. Your aura is steam.
39. Here's a surprise: lifting a car half a metre, once, uses about the food energy of one bite of an apple.
40. Doing it all night adds up. Hafþór has said he eats around ten thousand calories a day in training. Most adults need about two thousand.
41. At home, you empty the fridge. Biscuit guards her bowl.

## LEVEL 23: THE TRUCK
42. Level twenty-three. Over eight tonnes. And it's nearly Halloween.
43. By the park, a truck of festival pumpkins has slid off the path, one back wheel sunk in mud.
44. The sticker says seven and a half tonnes. Easy. You push.
45. Your trainers spin. Squeak, squeak. The truck doesn't move. You slide into a puddle.

## RULE 5 CARD: NO GRIP, NO PUSH.
46. To push something forward, your feet push back on the ground. Only friction stops them sliding.
47. The most grip you can get is roughly your weight times a number called the coefficient of friction. Rubber on dry concrete is close to one. Wet mud is much lower.
48. You weigh about fifty kilos. On dry concrete, that's about as hard as you can push. On mud, far less, however strong you are.
49. Your muscles are level twenty-three. Your grip is level one.

## THE RESCUE
50. So you stop pushing, and lift instead. Lifting pushes you down, not sideways.
51. You stand on Mina's planks, rule three, and slowly raise the truck's back corner.
52. Dev wedges gravel under the wheel. Mina pours playground sand in front of it, for grip.
53. The truck crawls out of the mud. Not one pumpkin falls.
54. The driver gives you the biggest one. You carry it home on one finger.

## LEVEL 30: THE LAST MORNING
55. Day thirty. Breakfast. The air unzips, and the screen floats out.
56. Level thirty. Over forty tonnes. "LEVEL UP!" Then it flips over. "Trial over. Levels returned."
57. You're back to forty kilos. Dev stops filming.
58. Then the screen blinks once more. "Strength, forty-four."
59. Four extra kilos. Not from the screen. From those nights in the park. Rule one.
60. The screen zips away. Then a tiny new one opens above Biscuit's head. "Level one."

## ENDING
61. In real life, no screen pops out of the air. But training really works.
62. Strength comes fast at first, from your nervous system, then slowly, over years, as muscles, bones and tendons catch up.
63. Nobody doubles every three days. Getting bigger isn't a shortcut either. Remember the square-cube law? Twice as tall, you'd be about four times stronger, but eight times heavier.
64. Doctors say kids and teens can strength train safely, with good coaching and technique.
65. If you levelled up every morning, what would you lift first? Tell us in the comments.
66. And subscribe for more What Ifs.
