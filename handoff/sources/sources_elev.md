# Sources: How an elevator really works (urban-sketch style)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Car and counterweight hang from ropes (or belts) that run over the machine sheave; they move in opposite directions, "similar to a see-saw" | Otis UK, The basic workings of a lift: https://www.otis.com/en/uk/w/the-basic-workings-of-a-lift |
| As the car rises the counterweight descends, reducing the energy needed | Otis UK (above) |
| The electric motor turns the sheave (through a gearbox in geared lifts) | Otis UK (above) |
| Friction between the sheave's grooves and the ropes drives the ropes up and down (traction) | Elevator World, Ropes and Traction: https://elevatorworld.com/?p=18182 |
| Counterweight is usually the weight of the empty car plus half the full load capacity, so the motor doesn't lift the full weight | Elevator World (above) |
| Several ropes hold one car; a rope safety factor of about 10 (US) / at least 12 (Europe) for speeds up to 2 m/s | Elevator World (above) |
| An overspeed governor is connected to the car by a rope; if speed exceeds a set limit it locks and this sets the safety gear against the guide rails; a governor is mandatory on a traction lift | Otis UK (above) |
| 1854 New York fair: Otis's safety brake used ratchet (saw-tooth) teeth on the guide uprights; pads were held back by rope tension, and if the rope broke a spring pushed them into the teeth | Elevator World, Elisha Otis' "Improved Elevator": https://elevatorworld.com/?p=44063 |
| In the 1854 demonstration Otis rode the platform and the rope was cut; the safety device held | Elevator World (above); Encyclopedia.com: https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/technology-biographies/elisha-graves-otis |
| First passenger elevator, 1857, Haughwout store, New York | Encyclopedia.com (above); not used in script |
| NOT CLAIMED: who cut the rope (Elevator World's son account says Otis; Encyclopedia.com says an assistant), the "All safe, gentlemen" quote (Elevator World says it is unconfirmed), the height, P.T. Barnum link |
| NOT CLAIMED: that traction is the most common type, that ropes are steel, how the motor brake works, free-fall myths |
