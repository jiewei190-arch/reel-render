# Sources: How a rainbow really forms (impasto style)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Sunlight enters a round raindrop and bends (refracts); reflects off the inner curved surface; refracts again leaving toward the observer | NWS Flagstaff: https://www.weather.gov/fgz/Rainbow |
| White light separates into colors because each color has a different wavelength | NWS (above) |
| The sun must be behind the observer with rain in front | NWS (above) |
| The sun must be lower than 42 degrees above the horizon (above that, no rainbow is visible from the ground) | NWS (above) |
| Rainbows are circular; the ground blocks part of the circle; the lower the sun, the more you see | NWS (above) |
| Light concentrates at 42 degrees from the antisolar point (the point directly opposite the sun); that direction forms a circle, so rainbows are circular arcs | Scientific American: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-are-rainbows-curved-i/ |
| Different colors are refracted by different amounts; primary bow is red on the outside, blue on the inside | Scientific American (above); NWS (blue inner, red outer) |
| Water droplets that make a rainbow lie on a circle; the full ring exists even if we don't see it; from an aeroplane you can see the whole ring; the ground hides the lower part | Science Focus: https://www.sciencefocus.com/science/why-are-rainbows-circular-when-viewed-from-an-aeroplane |
| Double rainbow: secondary bow comes from two internal reflections, exits at about 50 degrees, colors reversed | NWS (above) |
| NOT CLAIMED (not stated by the sources): "one color per drop", "each person sees their own rainbow", "it is fainter" |
