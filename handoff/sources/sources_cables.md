# Sources: How the internet crosses oceans (undersea fiber cables)

Research date: 2026-10-26. Confidence tags: **verified by 2 sources** = two independent publishers agree; **single source** = one publisher only, not cross-checked.

## Numbered claims

1. TeleGeography counted 570 in-service submarine cable systems in February 2025, and 607 in 2026, with 102 more planned.
   - Source: https://resources.telegeography.com/how-many-submarine-cables-are-there-anyway (607, 102 planned)
   - Source: https://www.suboptic.org/news/how-many-submarine-cables-are-there-anyway (570, February 2025)
   - Confidence: single source (both figures come from TeleGeography; no independent count checked)

2. Deep-ocean cables without armour are typically 17 to 20 mm across, roughly garden-hose size.
   - Source: https://www.cablejoints.co.uk/upload/Submarine_Cables___ISCPC_Guide.pdf (ISCPC Guide)
   - Confidence: single source

3. Armoured fibre-optic cables may reach 50 mm across.
   - Source: https://www.cablejoints.co.uk/upload/Submarine_Cables___ISCPC_Guide.pdf (ISCPC Guide)
   - Confidence: single source

4. Data travels as light through thin glass fibres that guide the light by total internal reflection.
   - Source: https://www.britannica.com/technology/optical-fiber (total internal reflection, core and cladding)
   - Source: https://subtelforum.com/how-submarine-cables-work/ (fibres are "thin strands of glass that carry information using light")
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

5. A fibre's glass core can be as small as 10 micrometres across.
   - Source: https://www.britannica.com/technology/optical-fiber (core diameter "as small as 10 micrometers")
   - Confidence: single source (core only; overall fibre thickness is not given in the same source)

6. Each submarine cable holds fibre pairs, with one fibre carrying data in each direction.
   - Source: https://subtelforum.com/how-submarine-cables-work/ (one fibre per direction within a pair)
   - Source: https://www.cablejoints.co.uk/upload/Submarine_Cables___ISCPC_Guide.pdf (each fibre pair carries digitised information)
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

7. Repeaters are spaced along the cable to boost the light signal.
   - Source: https://www.ciena.com/insights/articles/to-repeat-or-not-repeat-that-is-the-question.html (optical amplifiers "spaced at regular intervals along the cable")
   - Source: https://www.cablejoints.co.uk/upload/Submarine_Cables___ISCPC_Guide.pdf (repeaters "installed along the cable to boost the signal")
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

8. Repeaters are spaced about 25 miles (about 40 km) apart, per one industry article.
   - Source: https://www.marineinsight.com/how-undersea-cables-are-laid-by-cable-ships/ ("spaced about 25 miles apart")
   - Confidence: single source (no range given; spacing varies by system design)

9. Power is fed from shore as a constant current sent through the cable to run the repeaters.
   - Source: https://www.spellmanhv.com/-/media/en/Technical-Resources/Articles/PFE-6-Page-Article.pdf (power feed equipment supplies constant current to repeaters)
   - Source: https://www.cablejoints.co.uk/upload/Submarine_Cables___ISCPC_Guide.pdf (cable "forms a power conductor for repeaters")
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

10. One land-based power-feed model is rated at 15 kV and 1.5 A.
    - Source: https://www.spellmanhv.com/-/media/en/Technical-Resources/Articles/PFE-6-Page-Article.pdf (Gen4 "nominal rating of 15kV, 1.5A")
    - Confidence: single source (equipment rating, not a universal cable figure; the same source lists an older Gen3 rating of 5 kV and 1 A)

11. In shallow water, cables are typically buried 1 to 3.5 m under the seabed, sometimes up to 10 m.
    - Source: https://www.cablejoints.co.uk/upload/Submarine_Cables___ISCPC_Guide.pdf (ISCPC Guide)
    - Confidence: single source

12. Deep-ocean cables often lie unburied on the seabed because human activity there is limited.
    - Source: https://subtelforum.com/how-submarine-cables-work/ ("external human activity is limited")
    - Source: https://www.marineinsight.com/how-undersea-cables-are-laid-by-cable-ships/ (deep-ocean cables placed directly on the seabed)
    - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

13. Fishing and ship anchors cause about 65 to 75 percent of cable faults, according to the ICPC.
    - Source: https://www.fibre-systems.com/news/sharks-dont-dine-subsea-fibre-optic-cables-says-icpc (ICPC figure, quoted by the article)
    - Source: https://www.cablejoints.co.uk/upload/Submarine_Cables___ISCPC_Guide.pdf ("~70% of all cable faults are from fishing & anchoring")
    - Confidence: verified by 2 sources (the ICPC figure is reached through a secondary article; the primary ICPC page was not fetched)

14. Fish bites, including sharks, caused about 0.5 percent of cable faults from 1959 to 2006.
    - Source: https://www.fibre-systems.com/news/sharks-dont-dine-subsea-fibre-optic-cables-says-icpc (ICPC analysis)
    - Confidence: single source (the same article gives "under 1%" up to 2006; the ISCPC Guide lists at least 40 fish and shark bite faults but gives no percentage)

15. The ICPC recorded no cable faults attributed to sharks in its 2007 to 2014 analysis.
    - Source: https://www.fibre-systems.com/news/sharks-dont-dine-subsea-fibre-optic-cables-says-icpc ("recorded no cable faults attributable to sharks")
    - Confidence: single source

## Extra single-source facts (not in the 15; use if space allows)

- The first recorded shark bites on a deep-ocean fibre cable occurred off the Canary Islands around 1985 to 1987, damaging cables four times. Source: https://www.fibre-systems.com/news/sharks-dont-dine-subsea-fibre-optic-cables-says-icpc (single source)
- Transatlantic cables run roughly 6,000 km and transpacific roughly 10,000 km. Source: https://www.ciena.com/insights/articles/to-repeat-or-not-repeat-that-is-the-question.html (single source; "roughly")
- Cable ships store thousands of miles of coiled cable in their holds. Source: https://www.marineinsight.com/how-undersea-cables-are-laid-by-cable-ships/ (single source)
- Burial trenches are under 1 m wide, cut by water jet or plough. Source: https://www.cablejoints.co.uk/upload/Submarine_Cables___ISCPC_Guide.pdf (single source)
