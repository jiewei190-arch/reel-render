# Sources: How rain really forms (ink-wash style)
| Claim used in script | Source |
|---|---|
| Evaporation turns liquid water into water vapor, and it takes heat energy | USGS Water Science School, Evaporation: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/evaporation-and-water-cycle |
| About 90% of atmospheric moisture came from water evaporating from oceans, seas, lakes and rivers | USGS, Evaporation (above) |
| Clouds form because air containing water vapor rises and cools; the vapor condenses into droplets that form clouds | USGS, Condensation: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/condensation-and-water-cycle |
| A rising block (parcel) of air expands because of lower pressure higher up | NOAA JetStream, How clouds form: https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/clouds/how-clouds-form |
| Vapor condenses onto "cloud condensation nuclei" | NOAA JetStream (above) |
| Tiny dust, salt or smoke particles act as the nucleus droplets form on | USGS, Precipitation and the water cycle: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/precipitation-and-water-cycle |
| Most cloud water stays aloft because fall speed is not large enough to overcome the updrafts that support clouds | USGS, Precipitation (above) |
| Droplets grow by collisions and by more condensation | USGS, Precipitation (above) |
| When droplets collide, the larger one absorbs the smaller and keeps growing | USGS, Raindrops are different sizes: https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/raindrops-are-different-sizes |
| It takes millions of cloud droplets to make one raindrop | USGS, Precipitation (above) |
| A growing droplet counts as a raindrop at 0.5 mm or bigger | USGS, Raindrops (above) |
| A drop falls once it is heavy enough that the cloud can no longer hold it up | USGS, Raindrops (above) |
| Precipitation is the main way atmospheric water returns to the surface | USGS, Precipitation (above) |
| NOT CLAIMED (not found in sources): the sun as the evaporation energy source (USGS says only "heat energy"), raindrop shape, droplet counts per cloud, the ice-crystal route (left out for simplicity) |
