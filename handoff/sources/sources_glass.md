# Sources: How stained glass really gets its colour (stained-glass style)
| Claim used in script | Source |
|---|---|
| Colour comes from metals added (as compounds) to the glass while it is melted; sand melted at about 3,000°F | WSU "Dr. Universe": https://cas.wsu.edu/?p=15922 |
| "Glass was coloured by adding metallic oxides while it was in a molten state" | History Answers: https://www.historyanswers.co.uk/inventions/how-was-stained-glass-made/ |
| Cobalt oxide makes blue | WSU (above); Wikipedia Glass coloring and color marking: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_coloring_and_color_marking ; geology.com: https://geology.com/articles/color-in-glass.shtml |
| Gold (chloride) makes red; tiny gold spheres absorb and reflect sunlight into a deep red | WSU (above); COMSOL: https://www.comsol.com/blogs/the-science-behind-stained-glass |
| Manganese dioxide makes purple | WSU (above); geology.com (above); Wikipedia (above: amethyst colour at higher amounts) |
| Iron oxide gives a green tint | Wikipedia (above); geology.com ("greens and browns") |
| Impurities/metals "selectively absorb certain wavelengths of light, resulting in coloured glass" | Wikipedia (above) |
| Light hitting glass is partly absorbed, partly transmitted (T + A + R = 1): the light left over is what passes through | Lehigh optical properties of glass lecture: https://www.lehigh.edu/imi/teched/OPG/lecture15.pdf (supports transmitted/absorbed split; does not spell out "colour = what's left", so the script says "the rest passes through" only) |
| Details (faces, patterns) are painted with a glass paint (iron oxide, ground copper, powdered glass in wine/vinegar), brown to black | Lumen/SUNY NCC: https://courses.lumenlearning.com/suny-ncc-zeliart/chapter/how-stained-glass-is-made/ ; History Answers (above) |
| Painted pieces are fired in a kiln; the heat melts the glass in the paint so it fuses to the glass permanently | Lumen/SUNY NCC (above); Wikipedia (above: colours "fired in a kiln or furnace to fix them") |
| Pieces held together by narrow flexible lead strips (cames) | WSU (above); Lumen/SUNY NCC (above) |
| Glass has no light of its own, so it glows only with the sun behind it | Basic physics (glass transmits, does not emit); not a quoted claim |
| NOT CLAIMED (unverified): copper as a green colourant, cadmium yellow in medieval glass, why particle size sets colour, any dates or cathedral names |
