# Sources: bats / echolocation (Halloween reel)

| Claim in reel | Source |
|---|---|
| Bats are not blind; small eyes with very sensitive vision | USGS FAQ "Are bats blind?" https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/are-bats-blind |
| Calls are above human hearing (ultrasound); bat ears are finely tuned to recognise their own calls; "feeding buzz" = rapid calls that pinpoint prey before the bat swoops in | NPS, Bat echolocation https://www.nps.gov/subjects/bats/echolocation.htm |
| Most bats echolocate (not all); call frequencies span roughly 11,000 to 212,000 Hz; pulse rate rises from about one per second to several hundred per second near the target | Britannica, Echolocation https://www.britannica.com/science/echolocation |
| Call made in the larynx and usually emitted through the open mouth; delay between call and echo gives distance; echo timing/loudness differences at the two ears give horizontal direction; echoes carry size/shape/density information; search rate about 10-20 calls/s rising to as high as ~200 calls/s in the terminal buzz | Wikipedia, Animal echolocation https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_echolocation (secondary source; claims attributed with "about" / "up to") |
| "It works in total darkness because the bat makes its own signal" | Logical consequence of active sonar (the bat supplies the sound, no light needed); not a quoted claim |

## Dropped / not used
- Exact human hearing limit and per-species call frequencies (kept only the qualitative "too high for human ears").
- Call loudness (60-140 dB) and species-specific details (CF vs FM bats, Doppler); too detailed for 80 s.
- Insects-eaten-per-night figures (could not verify from a fetched page).

## Unverified / softened
- The 200 calls/s figure is from Wikipedia only; Britannica says "several hundred per second". The reel says "up to about 200".
- "Most do" comes from Britannica (all of Microchiroptera plus the genus Rousettus); the reel does not name groups.
- Visuals are schematic (not to scale): the bat/moth diagrams, ear shape and the echo "fingerprints" are illustrations.
