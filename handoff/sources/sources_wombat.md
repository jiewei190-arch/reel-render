# Sources: Why is wombat poop cube-shaped? (paper pop-up style)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Wombats can produce up to about 100 cubes in a day/night (a bout passes 4-8 pieces at a time) | Smithsonian (2021): https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/scientists-have-solved-mystery-how-wombats-poop-cubes-180976898/ |
| "Between 80 and 100" cubes per night | Smithsonian (2018): https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/why-wombats-make-cube-shaped-poos-180970847/ |
| Each cube is about 2 cm across (almost an inch) | Smithsonian 2021 (above); Smithsonian 2018 (above) |
| The intestine's walls shape the poop; the study is in the journal Soft Matter | Smithsonian 2021 (above) |
| "The rigid and elastic parts contract at different speeds, which creates the cube shape and corners" (Patricia Yang) | Smithsonian 2021 (above) |
| Corners get sharper over about 40,000 contractions (David Hu) | Smithsonian 2021 (above) |
| The wombat intestine has two grooves where elasticity differs; pig intestines were uniform; cubes form in the final ~8% of the intestine | Smithsonian 2018 (above) |
| Wombat intestine is about 33 ft (~10 m), roughly ten times body length; digestion is ~4x slower than in humans, so the poop is drier | Smithsonian 2021 (above) |
| Wombats leave cubes around their territory, possibly to communicate or attract mates; the cube shape is believed to stop them rolling off rocks and logs | Smithsonian 2021 and 2018 (above) |
