# Sources: Why the moon has phases (paper-lantern style)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| The Moon does not make its own light; moonlight is reflected sunlight | NASA Moon Phases: https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/ |
| The Sun always illuminates half of the Moon while the other half is dark | NASA Moon Phases (above); NASA StarChild: https://starchild.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/StarChild/questions/question3.html |
| Phases happen because our view of the half-lit Moon changes as the Moon orbits Earth | NASA (https://science.nasa.gov/?p=142063) |
| The Moon takes about 27.3 days to orbit Earth | NASA Moon Phases (above) |
| New moon to new moon takes about 29.5 days (29.53), because Earth and Moon also move around the Sun; this is the lunar month | NASA (https://science.nasa.gov/?p=142063); NASA StarChild (above) |
| Full moon: the side nearest us is fully lit. New moon: the far side is fully lit and the near side is dark | NASA (https://science.nasa.gov/?p=142063) |
| Phase names: new, crescent, first quarter, gibbous, full, third (last) quarter | NASA Moon Phases (above) |
| Earth's shadow only affects our view of the Moon during a lunar eclipse; it does not cause the phases | NASA (https://science.nasa.gov/?p=142063) |
| Everyone on Earth sees the same phases (hemispheres see them at different angles) | NASA (https://science.nasa.gov/?p=142063) |
| People on Earth only ever see one side of the Moon (tidally locked) | NASA Moon Phases (above) |
| NOT CLAIMED: meaning of "waxing" and "waning" (the NASA pages don't define them); the Moon's shape (not claimed) |
| 27.3 days (one orbit) vs 29.5 days (new moon to new moon): the difference is because Earth and Moon also move around the Sun | NASA (https://science.nasa.gov/?p=142063) |
