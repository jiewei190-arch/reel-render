# Sources: Why are flamingos pink? (crayon style)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Chicks hatch with grey down (bill and legs pink at hatching, black within a week) | San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance: https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/flamingo |
| "Flamingos are born with gray plumage" | Smithsonian: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/for-some-species-you-really-are-what-you-eat-40747423/ |
| Pink comes from carotenoid pigments in the algae and small crustaceans they eat | San Diego Zoo (above); Smithsonian (above) |
| Brine shrimp eat microscopic algae that make carotenoids; flamingos eat the shrimp | Smithsonian (above) |
| Their bodies break the carotenoids into pink and orange pigments that go into feathers, legs and beaks | Smithsonian (above) |
| The pigment is laid down in new feathers as they grow | ScienceABC: https://www.scienceabc.com/nature/animals/why-are-flamingos-pink.html |
| Without that food the pink would eventually fade | Smithsonian (above); ScienceABC (zoo flamingos fed unnatural diets lost colour) |
| Zoos add pigment to the diet to keep them "in the pink" | San Diego Zoo (above); Smithsonian (beta-carotene, canthaxanthin) |
| Both parents feed the chick crop milk from the upper digestive tract | San Diego Zoo (above) |
| Feeding crop milk drains the parents' colour (pale pink or white); they regain it as chicks become independent | San Diego Zoo (above) |
| Carotenoids are the pigment family that colours carrots (beta-carotene) | Smithsonian (above) names beta-carotene as a carotenoid |
| NOT CLAIMED: the exact age chicks turn pink (no source found), so the script says "as they grow" |
