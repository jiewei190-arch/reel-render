# Sources: Why does an octopus have 3 hearts and blue blood? (iso style)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Octopuses have three hearts | Smithsonian: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/ten-wild-facts-about-octopuses-they-have-three-hearts-big-brains-and-blue-blood-7625828/ |
| Two hearts move blood past the gills (where it drops CO2 and picks up oxygen) | Smithsonian (above) |
| The third heart sends oxygen-rich blood to the organs and muscles | Smithsonian (above) |
| The main heart stops beating when the octopus swims; swimming tires them out, so they prefer to crawl | Smithsonian (above) |
| Blood uses hemocyanin, a copper-containing protein; the copper makes it look blue | Smithsonian (above) |
| Human blood is red because of iron-containing hemoglobin | ScienceABC: https://www.scienceabc.com/nature/animals/why-do-octopuses-have-three-hearts.html |
| Hemocyanin transports oxygen better in cold, low-oxygen water | Smithsonian (above) |
| Overall hemocyanin is less efficient than hemoglobin and makes the blood thicker, so it must be pumped at higher pressure; three hearts are the solution | ScienceABC (above) |
