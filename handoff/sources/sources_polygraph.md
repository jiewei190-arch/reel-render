## Numbered claims

1. A polygraph records breathing, heart rate, blood pressure, and skin electrical conductance, which tracks sweat.
   - Source: [NRC-ch3] https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/3 ; [Psych-Today] https://www.psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/the-nature-of-deception/202206/how-do-polygraphs-work
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

2. The machine does not measure lying directly; it measures body responses that an examiner must interpret.
   - Source: [NRC-ch3] https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/3 ; [NIJ-1983] https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/scientific-validity-polygraph-testing-review-and-evaluation
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

3. In a control question test, examiners compare reactions to specific questions with reactions to general questions meant to provoke concern.
   - Source: [NRC-ch3] https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/3 ; [Psych-Today] https://www.psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/the-nature-of-deception/202206/how-do-polygraphs-work
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

4. The control question test assumes innocent people react more to control questions, and guilty people react more to the specific questions.
   - Source: [NRC-ch3] https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/3 ; [Psych-Today] https://www.psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/the-nature-of-deception/202206/how-do-polygraphs-work
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

5. The 2003 National Academies report called the scientific evidence on polygraph accuracy "scanty and scientifically weak."
   - Source: [NRC-ch10] https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/10 ; [NRC-press] https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2002/10/the-polygraph-and-lie-detection (press release says "severely limited")
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources (two National Academies pages; same publisher, different documents)

6. The 2003 report said the evidence does not allow any precise numerical estimate of polygraph accuracy.
   - Source: [NRC-ch10] https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/10 ; [NIJ-1983] https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/scientific-validity-polygraph-testing-review-and-evaluation (1983 review: no single validity statistic can be established)
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

7. The report found polygraphs can tell lies from truth "well above chance, though well below perfection."
   - Source: [NRC-ch10] https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/10 ; [Faigman-2003] https://issues.org/faigman-polygraph-lie-detector/ (quotes the report)
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

8. The report said countermeasures may degrade polygraph accuracy, so trained people might beat the test.
   - Source: [NRC-ch10] https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/10 ; [NRC-press] https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2002/10/the-polygraph-and-lie-detection
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

9. The report called polygraph testing an "unacceptable choice" for Department of Energy employee security screening.
   - Source: [NRC-ch10] https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10420/chapter/10 ; [NRC-press] https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2002/10/the-polygraph-and-lie-detection (says accuracy does not justify federal reliance)
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

10. The report warned that errors cut both ways: loyal employees could be wrongly flagged, or real security threats could go undetected.
    - Source: [NRC-press] https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/2002/10/the-polygraph-and-lie-detection
    - Confidence: single source

11. Most US states have rules that exclude polygraph evidence from court outright.
    - Source: [Scheffer-1998] https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/523/303 ("Most States maintain per se rules excluding polygraph evidence") ; [Faigman-2003] https://issues.org/faigman-polygraph-lie-detector/ ("Many courts, especially state courts, maintain a per se rule excluding polygraph evidence")
    - Confidence: verified by 2 sources (note: both are older; check for newer state-by-state counts before publishing)

12. In 1998 the US Supreme Court said "there is simply no consensus that polygraph evidence is reliable."
    - Source: [Scheffer-1998] https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/523/303 ; [Faigman-2003] https://issues.org/faigman-polygraph-lie-detector/ (quotes the Court: scientific community "remains extremely polarized")
    - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

13. In 1998 the Supreme Court upheld a military rule that bars polygraph evidence at court-martial.
    - Source: [Scheffer-1998] https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/523/303 ; [Faigman-2003] https://issues.org/faigman-polygraph-lie-detector/
    - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

14. In a hypothetical example, a test that catches 8 of 10 spies could still wrongly flag about 1,598 innocent examinees.
    - Source: [Faigman-2003] https://issues.org/faigman-polygraph-lie-detector/ (the authors label this a hypothetical "best case" estimate)
    - Confidence: single source (hypothetical illustration, not a measured result; label it as such on screen)

## Best hook (3 lines)

The lie detector doesn't detect lies.
It measures sweat, heartbeat and breathing, then guesses what those changes mean.
Even the National Academies called the evidence "scanty and scientifically weak."

Hook source: claims 2 and 5 above.

- Britannica (https://www.britannica.com/technology/polygraph) could not be retrieved (client error). No Britannica claims are used.

- The American Polygraph Association's accuracy claims (80 to 98 percent) appear in the NRC preface only as a contested claim. Not used as a fact.

- No real individuals are named as liars. The CQT example question in Psychology Today is generic and not used.
