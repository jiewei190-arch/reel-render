# Sources: How a Computer Chip Is Made (70-second reel)

Accessed 2026-10-26. Each claim is one plain sentence under 25 words.
Tags: **verified by 2 sources** = two independent sources agree. **single source** = one source only; use with care or cut.

---

1. Silicon is the second most abundant element in Earth's crust, after oxygen.
   - Sources: https://www.britannica.com/science/silicon (silicon is 27.7 percent of the crust); https://blogs.intel.com/jobs/2012/02/from-sand-to-silicon-the-making-of-a-chip
   - Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**

2. Chipmakers purify silicon until there is less than one stray atom per billion.
   - Source: https://blogs.intel.com/jobs/2012/02/from-sand-to-silicon-the-making-of-a-chip (Intel wording: "less than one alien atom per billion")
   - Confidence: **single source**

3. Silicon is pulled into a single crystal about 300 millimeters wide and about 100 kilograms.
   - Source: https://blogs.intel.com/jobs/2012/02/from-sand-to-silicon-the-making-of-a-chip (ingot "about 300 mm in diameter and roughly 100 kg")
   - Confidence: **single source**

4. The crystal is sliced into round wafers about 300 millimeters, or 12 inches, across.
   - Sources: https://blogs.intel.com/jobs/2012/02/from-sand-to-silicon-the-making-of-a-chip; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wafer_(electronics) (300 mm = 11.8 in; 300 mm wafers introduced 1999)
   - Note: the two sources give different wafer thicknesses (Intel "about 1 mm", Wikipedia 775 µm). Do not use thickness in narration.
   - Confidence: **verified by 2 sources** (Wikipedia is secondary)

5. Making one chip takes hundreds of steps.
   - Sources: https://www.asml.com/technology/all-about-microchips/how-microchips-are-made ("hundreds of steps"); https://blogs.intel.com/jobs/2012/02/from-sand-to-silicon-the-making-of-a-chip ("hundreds of precisely controlled steps")
   - Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**

6. From design to mass production can take about three to four months.
   - Sources: https://www.asml.com/technology/all-about-microchips/how-microchips-are-made ("up to four months"); https://www.semi.org/en/blogs/semi-news/chip-industry-fun-facts-super-cleanroom-microchip-sprint-computing-power-moonshot-microscopic-scale ("about 3 months," listed as a fun fact)
   - Note: use as a range. SEMI's figure is a fun-fact blurb, so it is the weaker source.
   - Confidence: **verified by 2 sources** (range)

7. Photolithography uses light to expose a light-sensitive coating on the wafer through a mask.
   - Sources: https://www.britannica.com/technology/photolithography (light, often ultraviolet, exposes photoresist through a chrome-on-glass mask); https://blogs.intel.com/jobs/2012/02/from-sand-to-silicon-the-making-of-a-chip (photoresist spun onto wafer, UV light through masks)
   - Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**

8. The light-and-mask sequence repeats with a different mask for each layer.
   - Source: https://www.britannica.com/technology/photolithography
   - Confidence: **single source**

9. Modern chips can have up to 100 layers, aligned to nanometer precision.
   - Source: https://www.asml.com/technology/all-about-microchips/how-microchips-are-made
   - Note: Intel's 2012 blog says 22 nm chips have "over 30 layers" of interconnect. These are different counts, so narrate as "dozens of layers" or cite ASML's "up to 100" with attribution.
   - Confidence: **single source**

10. EUV light has a wavelength of 13.5 nanometers.
    - Sources: https://www.asml.com/discover/hot-machines ("13.5 nanometers"); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_ultraviolet_lithography ("near 13.5 nanometers"). Supporting: https://www.nist.gov/publications/first-results-updated-nistdarpa-euv-reflectometry-facility (mirrors built for "13 nm")
    - Confidence: **verified by 2 sources** (Wikipedia is secondary)

11. An EUV machine makes this light by hitting falling tin droplets with lasers to create plasma.
    - Sources: https://www.asml.com/discover/hot-machines (molten tin droplets, two-pulse CO2 laser, plasma at about 500,000 K); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_ultraviolet_lithography ("laser-pulsed tin plasma")
    - Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**

12. EUV light is absorbed by air, so its entire light path must run in a vacuum.
    - Source: https://www.asml.com/discover/hot-machines
    - Confidence: **single source**

13. A chip fabrication cleanroom is about 10,000 times cleaner than the outside air.
    - Sources: https://www.asml.com/technology/all-about-microchips/how-microchips-are-made ("about 10,000 times cleaner than the outside air"); https://www.imec-int.com/en/semiconductor-education-and-workforce-development/microchips/how-are-microchips-made/cleanroom ("10,000 times cleaner than the air in a typical urban environment")
    - Note: SEMI's blurb compares the same figure to a hospital operating room. Do not use that comparison, because the two comparators conflict.
    - Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**

14. Billions of transistors fit on a chip, and some chips now hold tens of billions.
    - Sources: https://asml.com/technology/all-about-microchips/moores-law ("tens of billions today"); https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/article/sand-silicon-making-microchip.html ("billions of these tiny switches," in an area no larger than a fingernail)
    - Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**

15. Apple's M1 chip, built on a "5-nanometer" process, held 16 billion transistors.
    - Source: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2020/11/apple-unleashes-m1/ (Apple's own claim)
    - Note: this is also the best example for claim 16.
    - Confidence: **single source** (manufacturer claim)

16. Node names like "5 nanometer" no longer measure any physical feature on the chip.
    - Sources: https://eejournal.com/article/no-more-nanometers (names "have not been a representation of any actual dimension on the chip" since about 1997; "marketing names only"); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5_nm_process (the number "does not indicate" any physical feature; IBM's 2017 "5 nm" chip had about 41 nm transistor spacing)
    - Confidence: **verified by 2 sources** (Wikipedia is secondary; EE Journal is an opinion-leaning analysis)

---

## Best hook (3 lines)

Your phone's chip starts as sand.
Hundreds of steps later, it holds billions of switches, built in a room 10,000 times cleaner than the air outside.
Each layer is printed with light at a 13.5-nanometer wavelength.

---

## Excluded (unverified, conflicting, or not sourced)

- Human-hair size comparisons: no source found that states a figure.
- EUV machine cost ($150M vs $250M in news reports): conflicting, no official figure.
- SEMI's "hospital operating room" cleanroom comparison: conflicts with ASML and imec.
- Wafer thickness: Intel and Wikipedia disagree.
- Intel 2012 layer count ("over 30") vs ASML ("up to 100"): different scope, not used as a single figure.
- Intel newsroom "factory" page: blocked by robots rules, not fetched.
- imec "How are microchips made" page: fetch failed, not used.
- Intel 10 nm vs TSMC/Samsung 7 nm equivalence claim: single analysis source, excluded.
