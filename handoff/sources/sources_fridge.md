# Sources: How a refrigerator really works

1. A refrigerator absorbs heat Qc from a cold space, rejects heat Qh to a hot space, and needs work W from a motor; W = Qh − Qc; "a heat engine run in reverse."
   Source: OpenStax University Physics Vol. 2 (UCF Pressbooks) https://pressbooks.online.ucf.edu/osuniversityphysics2/?p=1193
   Confidence: verified by 2 sources (also Wikipedia, Vapor-compression refrigeration)
2. Compressor raises refrigerant gas pressure and temperature; hot gas goes through condenser coils (back/underneath), releases heat to room air, condenses to liquid.
   Sources: iFixit https://www.ifixit.com/Wiki/How_a_Refrigerator_Works ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vapor-compression_refrigeration
   Confidence: verified by 2 sources
3. Liquid passes through expansion valve/capillary tube; pressure drops, some flashes to vapor, mixture becomes colder than the compartment.
   Sources: same two. Confidence: verified by 2 sources
4. Cold mixture boils in evaporator inside the fridge, absorbing the latent heat of vaporization from interior air without a temperature rise; cool low-pressure gas returns to compressor.
   Sources: same two. Confidence: verified by 2 sources
5. Thermostat/sensors start the compressor when the compartment warms above the set point and stop it when the target is reached.
   Source: iFixit. Confidence: verified by 1 source (consistent with general appliance descriptions)
6. Food safety: refrigerator 40 °F or below, freezer 0 °F.
   Source: FDA consumer page https://www.fda.gov/food/resourcesforyou/consumers/ucm253954.htm
   Confidence: verified by 1 official source
7. Inference (energy conservation, W = Qh − Qc): a fridge with its door open puts more heat into the room than it removes, so the room warms. Labeled as inference from source 1.

## Excluded or unverified
- Specific refrigerant names, pressures, or coil temperatures (vary by model, not cited).
- "The hum is the compressor" (not sourced).
## Best hook
"Your fridge doesn't make cold. It moves heat."
