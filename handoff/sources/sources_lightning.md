## Claims

1. Storm clouds build electric charge when ice particles and water droplets collide and transfer electrons. Source: [NWS ZHU: How Lightning is Created](https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/lightning_stuff/lightning2/lightning.html); [NWS St. Louis: Science of Lightning](https://www.weather.gov/media/lsx/wcm/Heat/The%20Science%20of%20Lightning.pdf). Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**.

2. Charged particles sort so the top of a storm cloud is positive and the base is negative. Source: [NWS ZHU: How Lightning is Created](https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/lightning_stuff/lightning2/lightning.html); [NWS St. Louis: Science of Lightning](https://www.weather.gov/media/lsx/wcm/Heat/The%20Science%20of%20Lightning.pdf). Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**.

3. Lightning starts only after charge builds up enough to break down the air's insulating ability. Source: [NSSL Severe Weather 101: Lightning](https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/lightning/). Confidence: **single source**.

4. A lightning strike begins with a negatively charged stepped leader that moves downward in short steps. Source: [NWS Negative Charged Flash](https://weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-negative-charged-flash); [NWS ZHU: How Lightning is Created](https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/lightning_stuff/lightning2/lightning.html); [NWS St. Louis: Science of Lightning](https://www.weather.gov/media/lsx/wcm/Heat/The%20Science%20of%20Lightning.pdf). Confidence: **verified by 3 sources**.

5. Each step of a stepped leader is about 50 meters (150 feet) long. Source: [NWS Negative Charged Flash](https://weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-negative-charged-flash). Confidence: **single source**.

6. A stepped leader travels at about 200,000 mph, but estimates range up to about 335,000 mph. Source: [NWS Negative Charged Flash](https://weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-negative-charged-flash) (about 200,000 mph); [Britannica: How Fast is Lightning](https://www.britannica.com/science/How-Fast-is-Lightning) (about 1.5 x 10^5 m/s, roughly 335,000 mph). Confidence: **verified by 2 sources, values differ**. Use "roughly 200,000 to 335,000 mph" on screen, or avoid a single number.

7. As the stepped leader nears the ground, tall objects send up a positive upward streamer to meet it. Source: [NWS Connection to Ground](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-connection-ground); [NWS Negative Charged Flash](https://weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-negative-charged-flash); [NWS St. Louis: Science of Lightning](https://www.weather.gov/media/lsx/wcm/Heat/The%20Science%20of%20Lightning.pdf). Confidence: **verified by 3 sources**.

8. When the leader meets an upward streamer, the lightning channel is complete and charge flows quickly to the ground. Source: [NWS Connection to Ground](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-connection-ground); [NWS Return Stroke](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-return-stroke). Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**.

9. The bright flash people see is the return stroke, a rapid discharge that travels up the established channel. Source: [NWS Return Stroke](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-return-stroke); [Britannica: How Fast is Lightning](https://www.britannica.com/science/How-Fast-is-Lightning). Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**.

10. The return stroke climbs the channel at roughly 200 million mph, about 1 x 10^8 m/s. Source: [NWS Negative Charged Flash](https://weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-negative-charged-flash) (200 million mph); [Britannica: How Fast is Lightning](https://www.britannica.com/science/How-Fast-is-Lightning) (about 1 x 10^8 m/s). Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**.

11. The lightning channel reaches about 30,000 degrees C (about 50,000 to 54,000 degrees F), roughly five times hotter than the Sun's surface. Source: [NWS Thunder](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-thunder) (50,000 F, 5 times the Sun); [NWS ZHU: Sound of Thunder](https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/lightning_stuff/lightning2/thunder.html) (54,000 F); [Britannica: How Fast is Lightning](https://www.britannica.com/science/How-Fast-is-Lightning) (30,000 C, 50,000 F). Confidence: **verified by 3 sources, values differ**. One outlier: [NWS ZHU: How Lightning is Created](https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/lightning_stuff/lightning2/lightning.html) gives about 18,000 F; leave it out of the reel. Use "about 50,000 F, five times the Sun's surface" for the script.

12. Thunder is a shock wave made when the air around the channel heats and expands explosively. Source: [NWS Thunder](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-thunder); [NWS ZHU: Sound of Thunder](https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/lightning_stuff/lightning2/thunder.html); [NWS St. Louis: Science of Lightning](https://www.weather.gov/media/lsx/wcm/Heat/The%20Science%20of%20Lightning.pdf). Confidence: **verified by 3 sources**.

13. Under normal conditions, thunder can be heard up to about 10 miles away. Source: [NWS Thunder](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-thunder); [NWS ZHU: Sound of Thunder](https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/lightning_stuff/lightning2/thunder.html). Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**.

14. To estimate distance, count seconds from flash to thunder and divide by 5; sound covers one mile in about 5 seconds. Source: [NWS Thunder](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-thunder); [NOAA Education: node 10809](https://www.noaa.gov/node/10809). Confidence: **verified by 2 sources**.

## Notes for the script

- Use 14 claims as the spine. Claims 3 and 5 are the weakest, since each has a single source. Drop them or keep them as narration only.
- Speed and temperature numbers differ across sources. Say "roughly" and give the range in the script notes.
- Unverified claims left out: the "three times hotter than the Sun" figure from the Live Science article (a 2006 article, not checked against a primary source); the 75 to 80 percent in-cloud share (single source, ZHU page; can be added if a second source is found); and the "visible flash travels at light speed" statement (Britannica only).
