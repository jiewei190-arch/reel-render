# Sources: How a vaccine really works
- CDC, "Explaining How Vaccines Work" (cdc.gov/vaccines/basics/explaining-how-vaccines-work.html): white blood cells make antibodies that identify and neutralize foreign substances; active ingredient of every vaccine is an antigen (weakened/killed germ, pieces, genetic material, or inactivated toxin); immune response may cause tiredness/discomfort for a day or two; immunity can take weeks to develop; flu vaccine reformulated yearly.
- WHO, "How do vaccines work?" (who.int feature story): each antibody recognizes one specific antigen; first exposure is slow; memory cells remain after pathogen is defeated; re-exposure gives a much faster, more effective response; vaccines don't cause the disease but prompt a response as to the real pathogen; some need multiple doses to build long-lived antibodies and memory; when many are vaccinated the pathogen has a hard time circulating, protecting some who can't be vaccinated; no vaccine is 100% protective.
- NIAID/NIH, "Vaccine types" (niaid.nih.gov/research/vaccine-types): live-attenuated, inactivated, subunit, toxoid, mRNA (body's cells make the antigen), viral vector.
