## Added for the full reel (all from the same USDA page, https://www.usda.gov/node/19309, re-read Oct 26)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Pigments are the chemicals in leaves that produce the colors we see | USDA |
| Chlorophyll is green and is instrumental in photosynthesis | USDA |
| Chlorophyll is continually replaced but begins to break down over time | USDA |
| Carotenoids are yellow and a natural component of healthy leaves during the growing season | USDA |
| Autumn's shorter days and colder temperatures are less conducive for growth; not as much chlorophyll is replaced | USDA |
| The first frost triggers a dramatic increase in the breakdown of chlorophyll | USDA |
| Once chlorophyll breaks down, yellow and orange carotenoids are "unmasked" and become the dominant color in many tree species | USDA |
| Red and purple pigments called anthocyanins develop in late summer and fall, largely a response to external stress such as the first frost | USDA |
| The more anthocyanins, the more brilliant the red | USDA |
| Forest color is a mosaic of green, yellow and red depending on species and the timing of cues like frosts | USDA |
| Photosynthesis makes food (sugars) from sunlight: general science, USDA mentions sugars as photosynthesis products only | USDA (partial) |
| NOT CLAIMED: tannins/brown, cool nights as a trigger, sugar as a color trigger, leaf drop, or that "orange" specifically is in every species (script says "yellow and orange ... in many trees") |
