# Sources: How a skyscraper really stays up (skyscr)
All claims in the narration are checked against these pages (fetched 2026-10-27).

- 508 m height; 380 piles 1.5 m wide, driven ~80 m, up to 30 m into bedrock; outrigger trusses every 8 floors tie the core to the outer mega-columns; designed for 60 m/s (216 km/h) gale winds; designed to be flexible; damper cuts movement up to 40%; ball 5.5 m; Typhoon Soudelor moved it 1 m on 8 Aug 2015: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_101
- Damper 660 tonnes, pendulum, hangs between floors 87/88 and 92; opposes the building's motion; springs/hydraulic pistons convert energy to heat; farthest swing 1 m in Aug 2015 (Typhoon Soudelor): https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/774616-largest-tuned-mass-damper
- Golden sphere on 92 cables, can cut sway by 40% (cross-check): https://thecivilengineer.org/news/taipei-101s-impressive-tuned-mass-damper
- Burj Khalifa sways about 1.5 m total at its tallest point; buttressed core; 192 piles: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burj_Khalifa

Dropped as unverified: the "88th floor viewing" is stated as visible to visitors (Wikipedia) so kept as "visitors can see it"; no CTBUH general wind-sway numbers were fetched, so no generic sway claims are made. Pile count (380) and "80 m" are from Wikipedia only; one source (Civil Engineer) says "100 feet" so the narration says only "380 piles" and "into bedrock".
