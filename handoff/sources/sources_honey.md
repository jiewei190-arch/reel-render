# Sources: How bees really make honey (engraving style)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Foragers store nectar in the crop ("honey stomach"), used solely for storage; the bee does not digest it | NC State News: https://news.ncsu.edu/2013/06/how-do-bees-make-honey |
| Back at the hive the forager regurgitates the nectar into the crop of a "processor" bee near the entrance (the NCSU article does not say "house bee") | NC State News (above) |
| Returning foragers pass partly processed nectar to house bees (trophallaxis) | Carolina Honeybees: https://carolinahoneybees.com/how-honey-bees-make-honey/ |
| Processor bees add the enzyme invertase each time nectar is passed; it breaks sucrose into glucose and fructose; many loads fill a cell | NC State News (above) |
| Nectar is usually about 70% water; honey has less than 18.6% water; bees dry it | NC State News (above) |
| Moisture drops from about 80% to 20% as honey is finished (looser figure, NOT used as a number in the script) | Carolina Honeybees (above) |
| Bees fan their wings to move air over the comb so water evaporates | NC State News; Carolina Honeybees; Smithsonian |
| Ripe honey is sealed in the cell with an airtight wax cap; without it honey takes up moisture and can spoil | NC State News (above) |
| Honey does not spoil: little water, acidic (pH roughly 3 to 4.5), and bee enzymes make hydrogen peroxide; stays good while sealed and dry | Smithsonian: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/the-science-behind-honeys-eternal-shelf-life-1218690/ |
| Honey thousands of years old has been found in ancient Egyptian tombs, still preserved (anecdotal) | Smithsonian (above) |
| A worker bee makes about 1/12 teaspoon of honey in her lifetime | UC ANR: https://ucanr.edu/node/155127 |
| A worker visits 50 to 100 flowers per trip | UC ANR (above) |
| Workers in a hive visit more than two million flowers to make one pound of honey | UC ANR (above) |
| NOT CLAIMED: tongue-flicking/enzyme names beyond invertase; glucose oxidase is mentioned by Smithsonian but not used; "house bee" jobs beyond passing; trip counts per day |
