# Sources: Why you really get hiccups
All claims in the reel come from these. Nothing beyond them is advised.
- Cleveland Clinic, "Hiccups" (my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/17672-hiccups): diaphragm spasm + glottis closes = "hic"; vagus and phrenic nerves form a reflex arc; triggers (eating fast, spicy, carbonated, alcohol); often no clear cause; "verdict is still out"; remedies described as anecdotal, may work via vagus stimulation or CO2; see a provider if >2 days.
- Mayo Clinic Health Information Library, "Hiccups": repeated involuntary diaphragm contractions, vocal cords snap shut; usually a few minutes; excitement/emotional stress listed as short-term cause; home remedies "not proven effective"; see a provider after 48 h.
- MedlinePlus (NIH), "Hiccups" (medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003068.htm): spasms of the diaphragm followed by sudden vocal cord closing; often no specific cause, usually stop within minutes; "No method reliably stops hiccups."
- Stacey SK, Bassett MS. "Hiccup Relief Using Active Prolonged Inspiration." Cureus 2024;16(1):e53045 (PMC10895902): reflex arc (afferent phrenic/vagus/sympathetic; efferent phrenic to diaphragm, recurrent laryngeal branch of vagus to glottis); home remedies' efficacy "not rigorously established"; evolutionary hypotheses (remnant of primitive breathing / fetal respiratory muscle training; gastric clearance in neonates); no theory identifies an adult benefit.
## Unverified / hedged in the reel
- Evolutionary purpose: stated only as a theory.
- Why remedies might work (vagus / CO2): stated as a guess, per Cleveland Clinic.
- Specific numbers avoided; "two days" per Cleveland Clinic and Mayo.
- Simplification: the reel says the signal loops via "body and brain" without naming the brainstem.
