- Paper: Nagaya et al., "Melt re-injection into large magma reservoir after giant caldera eruption at Kikai Caldera Volcano", Communications Earth & Environment (2026), DOI 10.1038/s43247-026-03347-9
Verified facts used: Kikai caldera, Japan, mostly underwater; last giant eruption ~7,300 years ago (largest of the Holocene); airgun seismic pulses + ocean-bottom seismometers; a region with a large proportion of magma beneath it; interpreted as NEW magma (lava dome growing ~3,900 years with different chemistry); "magma re-injection" model, consistent with Yellowstone/Toba shallow reservoirs; researchers "ill-equipped to make predictions"; goal: refine monitoring of indicators.
Not claimed: any eruption timing, sizes, depths.
