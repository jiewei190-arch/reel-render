# Sources: How a Crime Scene Is Processed and How Forensic Evidence Works

Research date: 2026-10-26. Each claim is one plain sentence (under 25 words). Confidence: "verified by 2 sources" means two independent pages support the claim as written. "single source" means one page supports it; check before final publication.

## Crime scene processing

1. Officers arriving first should treat the incident as a crime scene and assume the crime is ongoing until shown otherwise.
   - Source: https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf (BJA / National Forensic Science Technology Center, *Crime Scene Investigation: A Guide for Law Enforcement*, 2013, p. 1)
   - Confidence: single source

2. Securing a scene means controlling who enters and leaves, and logging every entry and exit.
   - Source: https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf (p. 4-5); the step is listed in the NIJ abstract https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/crime-scene-investigation-guide-law-enforcement ("from securing the scene to submitting the evidence")
   - Confidence: single source (the NIJ abstract confirms the step but not the detail)

3. Investigators photograph the scene and make a rough sketch before anything is moved or destroyed.
   - Source: https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/forensics/Crime-Scene-Investigation.pdf (p. 18: "Draw the rough sketch before anything is moved or destroyed, and after photographs are taken.")
   - Confidence: single source

## Locard's exchange principle

4. Locard's principle holds that when two things touch, material is exchanged between them.
   - Source 1: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locard%27s_exchange_principle ("with contact between two items, there will be an exchange"; "Every contact leaves a trace")
   - Source 2: https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/locards-exchange-principle (*World of Forensic Science*: "with contact between two items, there will be an exchange")
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

5. Locard's own writings never used the term "exchange principle," which first appeared in a 1940 book.
   - Source: https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/locards-exchange-principle ("Locard's publications make no mention of an 'exchange principle'"; term first appears in *Police and Crime-Detection*, 1940)
   - Confidence: single source
   - Note: Use "every contact leaves a trace" only as a popular phrasing. The Wikipedia page attributes it to Locard, but neither source quotes it from his original writing.

## Fingerprints: formation and development

6. Latent prints are not visible until a physical or chemical process brings them out.
   - Source: NIJ, *Fingerprint Sourcebook, Chapter 7: Latent Print Development* (2010), p. 7-3: https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf ("Latent prints are undetectable until brought out with a physical or chemical process")
   - Confidence: single source (NIJ chapter abstract https://www.nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/fingerprint-sourcebook-chapter-7-latent-print-development says development uses "optical, physical, and chemical processes")

7. Some fingerprints form when sweat from the finger is deposited on a surface during contact.
   - Source: NIJ Fingerprint Sourcebook Ch. 7, p. 7-13: https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf ("Some fingerprints are created by the deposition of sweat from the fingers when they come into contact with a surface.")
   - Confidence: single source

8. Development powders stick to the moisture and oily parts of a print, and their pigment makes the ridges visible.
   - Source: NIJ Fingerprint Sourcebook Ch. 7, p. 7-10: https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/nij/225327.pdf
   - Confidence: single source

9. Fingerprint comparison follows four steps: Analysis, Comparison, Evaluation, and Verification (ACE-V).
   - Source 1: NIST, latent print process flowchart: https://www.nist.gov/forensics/upload/latent-print-process-poster2.pdf (steps for analysis, comparison, evaluation, verification)
   - Source 2: SWGFAST, *Standard for the Documentation of Analysis, Comparison, Evaluation, and Verification (ACE-V)*, Ver. 2.0: https://www.nist.gov/document/swgfaststandard-documentation-ace-v20121124pdf (document title)
   - Confidence: verified by 2 sources

## Fingerprints: uniqueness and reliability

10. Researchers say it is impossible to prove empirically that no two fingerprints are identical.
    - Source 1: Duke University, *Judicature*, "After Uniqueness": https://judicature.duke.edu/articles/after-uniqueness-the-evolution-of-forensic-science-opinions/ ("it is impossible to demonstrate empirically that this claim is true")
    - Source 2: National Academies slide deck: https://sites.nationalacademies.org/cs/groups/pgasite/documents/webpage/pga_049917.pdf ("it is impossible to offer decisive proof that no two fingerprints bear identical patterns"; the slides do not name the author of this quote)
    - Confidence: verified by 2 sources
    - Note: Use "uniqueness is untested" only as "cannot be proven." Do not say fingerprints are "not unique." Neither source says that.

11. A President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology review found a substantial false-positive rate in fingerprint analysis.
    - Source: Duke, *Judicature*, "After Uniqueness" (cites the PCAST review): https://judicature.duke.edu/articles/after-uniqueness-the-evolution-of-forensic-science-opinions/
    - Confidence: single source (secondary report of PCAST; check the primary PCAST report before publication)

## Blood spatter basics

12. Bloodstain patterns are described by cause, including airborne spatter and transfer from contact.
    - Source 1: Forensic Science Simplified, "Principles of Bloodstain Pattern Analysis": https://www.forensicsciencesimplified.org/blood/principles.html ("Impact stains result from blood projecting through the air"; "Transfer stains result from objects coming into contact with existing bloodstains")
    - Source 2: NIST, *Terms and Definitions in Bloodstain Pattern Analysis*: https://nist.gov/system/files/documents/2017/11/15/bpa_terms_and_definitions_in_bloodstain_pattern_analysis.pdf ("Spatter Stain": an airborne blood drop created when external force is applied to liquid blood; "Transfer Stain": a bloodstain resulting from contact between a blood-bearing surface and another surface)
    - Confidence: verified by 2 sources
    - Note: Forensic Science Simplified also lists gravity-driven "passive" stains (drops, flows, pools). The NIST glossary does not define that category, so do not claim it as verified by 2 sources.

13. Angle calculations for blood on most fabrics are unreliable, and no validated method yet dates a bloodstain.
    - Source: NIST, *Annotated Bibliography on Bloodstain Pattern Analysis*: https://www.nist.gov/document/annotated-bibliography-bloodstainpdf (quotes White 1986 on fabric angle calculations and SWGSTAIN research needs on bloodstain age: "Currently there are no validated methods to determine the age of a bloodstain")
    - Confidence: single source (NIST document quoting others)

## Chain of custody

14. Chain of custody is a signed record of everyone who handles evidence, with dates and times.
    - Source 1: NIJ, *Law 101: Legal Guide for the Forensic Expert*, "Maintaining Chain of Custody": https://nij.ojp.gov/nij-hosted-online-training-courses/law-101-legal-guide-forensic-expert/sources-scientific-evidence/testing-or-evaluating-evidence-and-writing-reports/maintaining-chain-custody ("Each person who takes physical custody of an evidence item must sign for it as custody is taken.")
    - Source 2: StatPearls, "Chain of Custody": https://www.statpearls.com/articlelibrary/viewarticle/19272/ ("Signatures of everyone involved in the chain of possession with date and time")
    - Confidence: verified by 2 sources
    - Note: The NIJ Law 101 page appears as an "Archival Notice" in search results. The fetch returned the body text, but confirm that the page still loads before linking it in a published reel.

15. A documented chain of custody shows evidence integrity, and missing records may get evidence excluded in court.
    - Source: StatPearls, "Chain of Custody": https://www.statpearls.com/articlelibrary/viewarticle/19272/ ("The chain of custody proves the integrity of a piece of evidence"; without a documented trail "the evidence may be inadmissible in the court")
    - Confidence: single source
