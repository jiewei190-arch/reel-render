# Sources: How a battery really works (silkscreen style)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| A battery stores electrical energy as chemical energy and converts it back to electricity | MIT Ask an Engineer: https://engineering.mit.edu/ask-an-engineer/how-does-a-battery-work ; DOE: https://www.energy.gov/node/4447224 |
| Two terminals, the anode and the cathode, made of different chemicals (typically metals) | MIT (above); DOE (above) |
| A chemical medium, the electrolyte, sits between the electrodes and lets charge move | MIT (above) |
| Ions (charged atoms or molecules) move through the electrolyte; electrons travel through the external circuit | MIT: "The ions transport current through the electrolyte while the electrons flow in the external circuit."; DOE (above) |
| Ions balance / keep each half of the cell electrically neutral | DOE (above); LibreTexts: https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/Lakehead_University/CHEM_1110_1130/18%3A_Electrochemistry/18.2%3A_Galvanic_Cells |
| Oxidation (electron loss) happens at the anode; electrons flow from the anode to the cathode through the outside circuit; reduction at the cathode | LibreTexts (above) |
| Chemical reactions at the electrodes drive the current | MIT (above) |
| A disposable battery makes electricity until its reactants are used up; both electrodes then reach the same chemical potential and the flow stops | MIT (above) |
| Rechargeable: the reactions can be reversed by applying energy from outside, e.g. a charger | MIT (above); DOE (above) |
| The reversal is not complete each cycle, so materials change and performance drops over time | DOE (above) |
| NOT CLAIMED: equilibrium wording (LibreTexts page didn't support it), exact voltages, specific battery chemistries (Li-ion etc.), how many cycles a battery lasts |
