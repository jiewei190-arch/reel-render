# Sources: How your heart really pumps blood (one-line style)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| An adult heart is about the size of a fist | Live Science https://www.livescience.com/amp/34655-human-heart.html ; NHLBI https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart |
| It beats more than/about 100,000 times a day | Live Science (above); Science News Explores https://www.snexplores.org/article/explainer-how-heart-beats |
| Blood carries oxygen and nutrients to organs, and carbon dioxide back to the lungs | NHLBI (above) |
| Four chambers: two upper atria, two lower ventricles | Cleveland Clinic https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/21704-heart ; Live Science |
| Oxygen-poor blood enters the right atrium, moves to the right ventricle, which pumps it to the lungs | Cleveland Clinic (above) |
| After the lungs add oxygen, blood returns to the left atrium, then the left ventricle, which pumps oxygen-rich blood to the rest of the body | Cleveland Clinic (above) |
| Valves work like doors between chambers: open to let blood through, and keep it from flowing backward | Cleveland Clinic; NHLBI ("valves keep blood flowing in the right direction") |
| "Lub-dub": the first sound is the valves between upper and lower chambers closing as the ventricles squeeze; the second is the exit valves closing | Britannica https://www.britannica.com/science/heart-sound ; Science News Explores |
| The SA node is the heart's natural pacemaker and sends an electrical signal that starts each beat in the upper chambers | Science News Explores; Cleveland Clinic ("signals start at the top of your heart and move down") |
| The heart pumps about 1.5 gallons (5.7 liters) of blood per minute | Live Science (above) |
| NOT CLAIMED: why the left side is stronger, exact valve counts in narration, any lifetime totals beyond sources |
