### Ep. 7 · catseyes: Thu Oct 29, 3:00 PM
**Title:** Why cat eyes glow in the dark 🐈‍⬛ | Ep. 7

**Description:**
```
In flash photos, a cat's eyes glow, but yours turn red. Why? Behind a cat's retina is a mirror-like layer called the tapetum lucidum. Light that slips past the light-sensing cells bounces back for a second chance, which helps cats make the most of dim light (with a little blur as the price). A phone flash sits right beside the lens, so that bounced light comes straight back into the camera. People have no mirror, so the flash lights up the blood-rich layer at the back of the eye instead: red-eye. 🐈‍⬛👀
```

### Ep. 9 · spidersilk: Thu Oct 29, 8:00 PM
**Title:** Is spider silk stronger than steel? 🕸️ | Ep. 9

**Description:**
```
One thread of spider silk can stop a flying insect, but it isn't stronger than steel. Thread for thread, good silk is about as strong as a mid-range steel; it's about six times lighter, so for its weight, silk wins. It starts as a liquid protein in the spider's glands and hardens into a fibre as it's pulled out through the spinnerets, and an orb weaver can make up to seven kinds. The real trick is the catch: the stiff spokes absorb most of the impact, much of the energy turns into heat so the bug doesn't bounce out, and the stretchy sticky spiral holds it. Walk into a web this Halloween and you'll know why it clings. Did you think silk was stronger than steel?

Sources: University of Cambridge / The Conversation, "Spider silk is a wonder of nature, but it's not stronger than steel"; Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Is spider silk stronger than steel?"; Sensenig et al., J. R. Soc. Interface (2012); Babb et al., Nature Genetics (2017).
```

```
Carve a pumpkin and in a few days its face starts to sag. An uncut pumpkin has a hard, waxy rind that keeps water in and germs out, and a pumpkin is about 90% water. ```
```
```
Why is snow white if ice is clear? There's no white in snow at all. A snowbank is mostly air, packed with tiny clear ice crystals. Light bounces off and bends through crystal after crystal, and since ice barely absorbs visible light, every colour comes back out together: white. A smashed windowpane does the same thing. Why are yawns contagious? Scientists still argue. Just thinking about yawning can set one off. Dogs can catch human yawns (21 of 29 in one study, though later results were mixed), and some chimps yawn at videos of yawning chimps. Idea 1: brain cooling. ### magnet: Tue Nov 3, 11:00 AM
```
```
Cut a magnet in half and you never get a "north piece". You get two magnets, every time. Inside iron, the electrons act like teeny magnets, and they line up in zones called domains.
```
