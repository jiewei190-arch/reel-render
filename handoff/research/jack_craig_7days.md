# Notes: "I BLEW UP a YouTube Channel in 7 Days with AI" (Jack Craig), shared by the owner Oct 28

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1r0eyM7suUg (read from its auto-captions). One creator's experience, mostly YouTube Shorts, with a coaching pitch. Treat it as tips to test, not proven rules.

## What he did
- **Niche:** found a trending niche (a Roblox game) via a channel with 200M views in under 20 uploads. Didn't copy it; took what works and improved the storytelling. "Original ideas don't win on YouTube. Better execution does."
- **Account:** used an *aged* account (7+ days old, with watch history). A brand-new account "looks like spam" and gets no views.
- **Research:** wrote out scene by scene what the top shorts do (hook, scene lengths, title, views). Looked for *outliers*, e.g. a short with an unusually high comment count, and put those proven ideas into his own videos.
- **Hook:** sudden movement in the very first frame (a character running at the camera).
- **Story:** a "conflict arc" (hook → rising action → conflict → comeback → rising action → payoff) instead of a straight line; it makes the video less predictable and raises retention.
- **Edit:** 1-3 s clips for fast pacing, sound effects on everything, a music bed. The short was 43 s.
- **Upload:** a simple title + 2 emojis, the trending keyword, description = title + #shorts, a couple of tags, "not made for kids".
- **Pacing a new channel (his key rule):** in the first 5 uploads, wait at least 48 h between uploads. Post the next one only when the last video has been under ~100 views/hour for 12 h straight. Rushing his 4th upload made it flop under 1,000 views; re-posted later, the same video got 13M.
- **Numbers:** 1st short got 27k views in 20 h with a 61% "stayed to watch" rate (he aims for 80%+). The 2nd got 80%+ stayed and 38 s average watch on a 43 s video, sat flat for days, then got pushed to 150k. YouTube tests a short in waves; a flat line isn't the end if the metrics are strong.
- **AI:** some viewers complained it was AI when the story was exaggerated. Keep AI content realistic, not gimmicky.

## What it means for us (main session, Oct 28)
1. **We may be posting too fast for brand-new accounts.** We planned 5 a day per platform from day 1, plus 40 TikTok deletes/reschedules today. His rule for a new channel is about 1 upload every 2+ days until videos take off. That's a likely factor in the TikTok 0 views. This is the owner's call: it changes their "5 a day" rule.
2. **Lead with our strongest reels**, not EP order, while the account is new: the ones with the best first-second hook and motion.
3. **First frame needs motion.** Check every reel's frame 0 for movement, not a still title card.
4. **Conflict arc:** our reels are mostly straight explanations. Myth-bust structure (what you think → "but" → the twist → payoff) is close to his arc; lean on it in new scripts.
5. **Length test:** his 43 s short held 38 s on average. Ours run 66-86 s (owner rule: over 60 s). Worth testing a few shorter cuts against the long ones once we have data. The owner decides.
6. **Outlier research:** find science-explainer shorts with unusually high comments or views for their channel size, and borrow the proven topic or angle.
7. **Metrics to watch** (already in STUDY.md): "stayed to watch" ≥ 80%, average view duration vs length.
