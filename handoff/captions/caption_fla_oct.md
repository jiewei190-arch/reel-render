# Captions: flamingo (crayon) and octopus (iso)

## fla: Why are flamingos pink? (crayon picture book, female voice)
Flamingos are born GREY 🩶 The pink is 100% from their food.
Algae make natural pigments called carotenoids → brine shrimp eat the algae → flamingos eat the shrimp and turn it into pink feathers, legs and beaks.
Stop eating it and the pink fades. That's why zoos add pigment to their food 🦩
Sweetest part: parents feed their chick "crop milk" and go pale doing it.

Sources: San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance; Smithsonian Magazine.

## oct: Why does an octopus have 3 hearts? (isometric cutaway, male voice)
An octopus has THREE hearts 💙 and blue blood.
2 hearts push blood through the gills, 1 sends it to the body, and that main one stops beating when it swims (so they'd rather crawl 😮‍💨).
Its blood uses copper-based hemocyanin instead of iron, so it's blue. It works better in cold, low-oxygen water but it's thick, so it needs the extra pumps.

## wombat (paper pop-up, male voice)
Wombats poop in CUBES 🟫 Real ones. Your gut makes them, not a machine.
Their intestine is about 10x their body length. Near the very end, parts of the wall are stiffer and parts stretch, and the squeezing at different speeds shapes the corners.
Why bother? Scientists think the cubes stay put on rocks and logs, helping mark territory. Follow for more of how it really works 🐨
Sources: Smithsonian Magazine.

## leaf (watercolor, female voice)
Leaves don't turn orange in fall. The orange was in there all summer 🍂
Green chlorophyll covers it up. When days get shorter and cooler, the leaf stops making chlorophyll, the green fades and the hidden yellow and orange show. Reds are made new in fall.
Follow for more of how it really works.
Source: USDA.

Clocks go BACK this Sunday, Nov 1 at 2 a.m. 🕑 People call it "fall back."
Sunrise and sunset come an hour earlier by the clock. Hawaii and most of Arizona don't change. Turn back any clock that won't do it itself before bed Saturday.
