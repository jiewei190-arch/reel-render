# Hook playbook (howitreallyworkstudio)

Purpose: the owner says our intros aren't catchy enough. This file covers what top explainer Shorts do in the first 1-3 s, the rules our reels should follow, a checklist for writers, and 3 hook options for each of our 6 current topics.

## Read this first: how much data is behind this

- **View counts and titles: complete.** I listed every Short on 8 channels with yt-dlp (about 5,200 Shorts) and took the top performers by view count. Section 5 has the 30 that were studied.
- **Verbatim first lines and first-frame visuals: only 2 of 30.** YouTube started returning "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot" after the first few requests. I did not work around it (no cookies, no login, no alternate clients). The two Zack D. Films #1 and #2 Shorts (255M and 216M views) were fully captured: word-timed transcript plus storyboard frames from 0 to about 3 s.
- **For the other 28, the table records title and views only.** The first-line column says "not captured". Where a pattern below rests on titles and not on transcripts, I say so.
- **To fill the gaps later,** re-run from a machine that YouTube is not rate-limiting. The commands are listed at the end.

## 1. Hook patterns that work, ranked

The ranking weighs three things: what the two fully captured Shorts do, what the 30 top titles promise, and the published advice in section 2.

**1. Mid-action, relatable body or everyday moment.** The first frame is the event already happening, and the first spoken line is "When/If you [everyday thing]," in under 2 s. This is Zack D. Films' core format and the most-viewed format in the whole set.
- Zack D. Films, *How Stitches Work*, 255M views. Spoken: **"When you get stitches,"** (4 words, finished by 1.8 s). It continues straight into the mechanism: "a doctor threads a needle through your wound and pulls it tight to close the skin." Frame 0 shows a leg with a fresh open gash, and the camera pushes in. The needle and thread are working by about 2 s.
- Zack D. Films, *How Splinters Get Unstuck*, 216M views. Spoken: **"If you get a splinter,"** (5 words, finished by 1.8 s). Then comes the "but" turn at about 5 s: "most people just go ahead and pull them out, but sometimes they get lodged so deep into your skin..." Frame 0 shows a finger sliding along rough wood, the moment the splinter goes in. Tweezers are on screen by about 3 s.
- Title evidence: other titles in the same family include *Blown Veins* (138M), *Why Bees Die After They Sting You* (115M), *How To Get Water Out Of Your Ear* (42M), *How Electrical Outlets Work* (61M) and Professor Of How's *How do Fish Breathe Underwater?* (69M).

**2. The weird object or result, shown first and explained second.** The payoff image comes first and the "why" follows. Galloway's "treat your video like a thumbnail" describes this, as does MrBeast's "stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them".
- Zack D. Films, *The Reason The McFlurry Spoon Looks Weird*, 166M (title only).
- Steve Mould, *How does this table stand up?*, 24M (title only).
- Steve Mould, *Can water solve a maze*, 29M (title only).

**3. A short question the viewer can't answer.** It must be specific and about something they can picture. A vague "Have you ever wondered..." does not count.
- Zack D. Films, *How Long Are Your Blood Vessels?*, 157M (title only).
- Zack D. Films, *Could Ancient Armor Stop Bullets?*, 156M (title only).
- Mark Rober, *Are Bananas Actually Slippery?*, 201M (title only).

**4. A contradiction of common belief or a "hidden reason".**
- Zack D. Films, *Why One Shooter Gets A Blank Round*, 139M (title only).
- Kurzgesagt, *Something in Your Kitchen Emits Antimatter*, 14M (title only).
- Veritasium, *I call this the 'No, You Don't' Law*, 43M (title only).

**5. Danger or stakes plus a concrete thing.**
- Kurzgesagt, *The Deadly Power of a Coin-Sized Black Hole*, 20M, the channel's #1 Short (title only).
- Professor Of How, *Can WELDING really make you BLIND?*, 55M (title only).
- Veritasium, *What happens if you cut the green rope?*, 51M (title only).

**6. "What happens if..." with an absurd premise.**
- Veritasium, *Can you swim in shade balls?*, 64M, the channel's #1 Short (title only).
- Mark Rober, *Turning An Egg Into A Bouncy Ball*, 329M (title only).
- Kurzgesagt, *Earth as a Cube*, 13M (title only).

**7. A number or scale that sounds wrong.** It works best as a support line in second 2-4, not as the opener.
- Steve Mould, *This bug produces a g-force of 500 g!*, 10M (title only).
- Kurzgesagt, *How Tiny Can a Black Hole Be?*, 13M (title only).

**8. A plain "How X works".** It only works when the visual is strong.
- Steve Mould, *How Do Washing Machines Actually Work?*, 18M (title only).
- Kurzgesagt, *How do Lasers Work?*, 12M (title only).
- In the top 30, a plain "How X works" title almost always came with a striking first image (a wound, a bulletproof vest being hit). The phrase alone does not hook.

**Patterns that did not show up:** "Hey guys", channel intros, logo stings, "In this video...", and "Have you ever wondered...". None of the top titles, and neither captured opener, starts slowly.

### What the first 1-3 s do (from the 2 captured Shorts)

- **Frame 0 is already moving and shows the subject.** There is no title card and no establishing shot.
- **The first spoken unit is 4-5 words** ("When you get stitches,"), finished by about 1.8 s.
- **The topic is clear by about 1 s,** from the image and the noun together.
- **Mechanism is on screen by about 2-3 s.** The tool (needle, tweezers) arrives immediately.
- **The curiosity turn is a "but" at about 5 s** ("but sometimes they get lodged so deep...").
- **The payoff is promised by the title** ("Get Unstuck") and lands in the last third.
- **Captions are burned in at center-low, 2-4 words at a time.**
- **These are short.** Both are 30-31 s long. We run 66-86 s, so we need re-hooks (see the rules in section 3).

## 2. Published hook advice, filtered to 60-90 s explainer Shorts

- **Paddy Galloway (Shorts study of 33 channels / ~5,400 Shorts):**
  - Best performers had 70-90% "viewed vs swiped away"; Shorts under ~60% rarely did well.
  - If that metric is low, the opening seconds are the problem. His advice: "Treat your video like a thumbnail."
  - Longer Shorts did better *only when held*: average view duration over 50 s averaged 4.1M views, against 1.8M at 40-50 s.
  - For our 66-86 s reels, this means the hook has to buy enough curiosity to carry past second 50.
  - Source: secondary summary at nowbam.com/decoding-the-youtube-shorts-algorithm; I could not find the original thread.
- **MrBeast production handbook (leaked 2024; former producers confirmed it to Passionfruit):**
  - "The first minute of each video is the most important minute"; most viewers are lost there. For a Short, that is the first 3 s.
  - "Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them."
  - The title and thumbnail set the expectation, and the opening must meet it right away.
  - Source: full text reproduced at alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production.
- **Kallaway (hook formula, via a video summary):**
  - Three steps: (1) *context lean-in*: make the topic obvious in line 1 and give a reason to care; (2) *scroll-stop interjection*: a "but" line; (3) *contrarian snapback*: flip the expectation, then explain.
  - Put 3-5 words of big bold text on screen in frame 1. Visual, text and voice must say the same thing.
  - Keep enough motion, but not chaos. Use staccato short sentences at the start and longer ones later.
  - Source: opentools.ai summary of "How to create irresistible hooks..."
- **vidIQ (Shorts hook guide):**
  - Optimize the first *frame*, not the "first 3 seconds".
  - Show the payoff and not the setup, or action already in progress.
  - Spoken hook 5-10 words, on-screen text 3-7 words in the top third, clear of the UI.
  - No filler greetings.
  - Source: vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts.
- **YouTube's own metric:** Studio shows "Viewed vs. Swiped away" ("Stayed to watch") per Short. That is the number to watch when testing hooks. YouTube publishes no official target; the 70-90% band is Galloway's.

## 3. Rules for our reels

1. **First spoken line: at most about 12 words and about 2.5 s at our TTS speed (0.97).** Our current openers run 13-17 words, for example "When you search, Google doesn't go out and search the web. It already did that, before you asked." Split them. Put the hook in the first sentence and the elaboration in the second.
2. **Frame 0 shows the subject, already moving.**
   - Show the magnet, the toilet bowl or the submarine at the strange or interesting moment: cutting, swirling, flooding.
   - No title card, no logo, no slow zoom from empty space, no fade-in from black.
   - Our styles (crayon, watercolor and so on) are fine, but the first frame needs one clear subject at large scale.
3. **Burned-in caption from frame 1.** Use 3-6 words with the key word in the accent color, in the top third or center, clear of the IG/YT UI.
4. **Open a curiosity gap and say how it closes.** The hook raises one specific question (why does it suck and not push?). The script answers it, explicitly, between about 45 s and 70 s, not in the first 10 s.
5. **"But" turn by second 5.** The second line flips or deepens the first ("...but that's not how it works", "...and that's the trick").
6. **Re-hook at about 25 s and about 50 s** with a fresh mini-question or reveal. Galloway's data says long Shorts only win if held past 50 s.
7. **Use "you/your" in the opener when it fits.** Both captured Zack D. Films #1 and #2 openers start "When you / If you". It makes the viewer the subject.
8. **No "Have you ever wondered...", "Did you know...", "Today we'll learn..." or "Let's talk about...".** None of the 30 top performers open this way (titles plus the 2 captured openers).
9. **Accuracy beats shock.** A contrarian hook must be true, or the comments will punish it. Every claim with a number or a "never" needs a source in the spec.
10. **Match title, caption and hook.** The post title, the on-screen caption and the first line make the same promise.

## 4. 10-point hook checklist (run on every script)

1. Is the first spoken line 12 words or fewer, and does it take about 2.5 s or less when read aloud? Count the words.
2. Does frame 0 show the actual subject, large and already in motion, and not a title, a logo or an empty background?
3. Could a viewer with the sound off tell the topic from frame 0 plus the caption within 1 s?
4. Does line 1 make a claim, ask a specific question, or show a contradiction, and not just announce a topic?
5. Does it avoid the slow openers ("Have you ever wondered", "Did you know", "Today", "In this video", any greeting)?
6. Is there a single, specific open question in the viewer's head after line 1? Write it down.
7. Is that exact question answered later, and is the answer the satisfying payoff (about 45-70 s)?
8. Does line 2 (by about 5 s) add a "but" turn or a stake, not a definition?
9. Do the visual, the caption (3-6 words) and the spoken line all say the same thing?
10. Is every claim in the hook true and sourced? Check numbers, "never" and "always". Do the title and the post caption make the same promise?

## 6. Hook options for our 6 topics

Format for each option:
- **Line** is the first spoken line, with its word count.
- **Frame 0** is the first-frame visual.
- The **Caption** is the burned-in text.
- **[source]** marks a claim the spec must cite.

Best pick per topic is marked ★.

### Magnet

1. ★
   - **Line:** "Cut a magnet in half, and you get two magnets." (10 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A bar magnet already being snipped by bolt cutters. Both halves spring apart, and each one lights up with its own N and S.
   - **Caption:** "CUT IT = 2 MAGNETS"
   - **Gap:** Why can't you ever get a lone north pole?
   - **Payoff:** It is billions of tiny electron magnets all the way down.
2. 
   - **Line:** "Flip a fridge magnet over, and it barely sticks." (9 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A hand flips a flat fridge magnet, and it slides straight down the fridge door.
   - **Caption:** "WRONG SIDE?"
   - **Payoff:** Flexible fridge magnets are magnetized in alternating stripes, which concentrates the field on one face. [source: Halbach-style / "one-sided flux" magnetization; verify for the typical flexible magnet]
3. 
   - **Line:** "Every magnet is secretly billions of tiny magnets." (8 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A fast zoom into a magnet. Thousands of tiny arrows snap into line.
   - **Caption:** "BILLIONS OF TINY MAGNETS"

### Search engine

1. ★
   - **Line:** "When you hit search, Google doesn't search the web." (9 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A finger mid-tap on Enter. A glowing globe of websites gets a big red X, and a giant book index slides in.
   - **Caption:** "IT DOESN'T SEARCH THE WEB"
   - **Gap:** Then what does it search?
   - **Payoff:** Its index, built in advance by crawlers. [source: Google, *How Search Works*, "organizing information"]
2. 
   - **Line:** "Google answers you from a giant index, not the live internet." (11 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A split screen: live internet on the left, crossed out; a towering index book on the right, pages flipping.
   - **Caption:** "NOT THE LIVE INTERNET"
3. 
   - **Line:** "Google's index is over 100 million gigabytes. Here's how it's used." (11 words)
   - **Frame 0:** An endless book index scrolling past, with a counter racing up to 100,000,000 GB.
   - **Caption:** "100,000,000 GB"
   - **Source:** Google *How Search Works*: "hundreds of billions of webpages... well over 100,000,000 gigabytes". [source]
   - **Note:** A number line works better as the second line than as the first.

### Recommendation algorithm

1. ★
   - **Line:** "Out of millions of videos, only a few dozen reach your screen." (12 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A torrent of thumbnails pouring into a funnel, with a phone at the bottom catching just a handful.
   - **Caption:** "MILLIONS → A FEW DOZEN"
   - **Gap:** Who decides, and how?
   - **Payoff:** Candidate generation, then ranking.
   - **Source:** [Covington, Adams & Sargin, "Deep Neural Networks for YouTube Recommendations", RecSys 2016: millions → hundreds → dozens]
   - **Note:** Describe it as "how YouTube described it in 2016", since the current system isn't public.
2. 
   - **Line:** "Most of what you watch on YouTube, you never searched for." (11 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A phone feed auto-scrolling with no search bar in sight.
   - **Caption:** "70% PICKED FOR YOU"
   - **Source:** [Neal Mohan, CES 2018, reported by Tubefilter: more than 70% of watch time comes from recommendations]
   - **Note:** The figure is from 2018, so say "YouTube said in 2018".
3. 
   - **Line:** "A click doesn't tell YouTube you liked a video. Watching does." (11 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A thumb taps a thumbnail and swipes away in under a second, and a red "doesn't count" stamp lands on it.
   - **Caption:** "CLICKS LIE"
   - **Source:** [YouTube's 2012 switch to watch time; cite the YouTube Creator Blog. The current spec says "2011" and needs checking. The Oct 2012 announcement is the usual citation.]

### Toilet flush (siphon)

1. ★
   - **Line:** "Your toilet doesn't push the water down. It sucks it." (10 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A cutaway bowl with the water already swirling down. The hidden S-shaped channel glows as the water gets pulled through.
   - **Caption:** "IT SUCKS, NOT PUSHES"
   - **Gap:** Sucks how, with no pump?
   - **Payoff:** The siphon in the bowl.
   - **Accuracy:** This applies to *siphonic* toilets, which are standard in North America. Many European "washdown" toilets flush by force, not by siphon. Say "most North American toilets" in the body or the caption. [source: This Old House, "How does a siphon work"; HowStuffWorks, toilet]
2. 
   - **Line:** "Pour a bucket of water in fast, and the toilet flushes itself." (12 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A bucket mid-pour into the bowl, and the bowl gulping it all down.
   - **Caption:** "NO HANDLE NEEDED"
   - **Source:** [This Old House: about 2 gallons poured quickly triggers the siphon]
   - **Note:** It must be poured *fast*. A slow pour just spills over the weir.
3. 
   - **Line:** "That gurgle at the end of a flush? That's air breaking the siphon." (13 words; trim to "That final gurgle? It's air breaking the siphon." at 8)
   - **Frame 0:** An extreme close-up of the bowl's last gulp, with an air bubble racing into the bend.
   - **Caption:** "THE GURGLE = AIR"
   - **Source:** [same as option 2]

### Air fryer

1. ★
   - **Line:** "An air fryer doesn't fry anything." (6 words)
   - **Frame 0:** Fries tumbling in a basket. A sizzling oil pot crashes into frame and is stamped "NOT THIS".
   - **Caption:** "IT DOESN'T FRY"
   - **Gap:** Then why are the fries crispy?
   - **Payoff:** Fast hot air plus a little oil gives Maillard browning on a dry surface.
   - **Source:** [Fan-forced (convection) heating; Maillard browning, roughly 140 °C and above. Cite a food-science source, for example the Serious Eats or Consumer Reports explainer.]
2. 
   - **Line:** "Your air fryer is really a tiny convection oven." (9 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A full-size oven shrinking down into an air fryer shape, with a fan spinning on top.
   - **Caption:** "A TINY OVEN"
   - **Source:** [Consumer Reports and similar describe air fryers as countertop convection ovens]
3. 
   - **Line:** "No oil bath. Just a storm of hot air." (9 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A fry dropping in slow motion through swirling orange air arrows that wrap around it.
   - **Caption:** "HOT AIR STORM"
   - **Note:** Fine as a metaphor. Do not state a wind speed unless it is sourced.

### Submarine (ballast tanks)

1. ★
   - **Line:** "To dive, a submarine lets the ocean in on purpose." (10 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A cutaway sub at the surface. Vents on top pop open, air geysers out, and seawater rushes into the tanks.
   - **Caption:** "LET THE OCEAN IN"
   - **Gap:** Then how does it ever come back up?
   - **Payoff:** Compressed air blows the water out.
   - **Source:** [Wikipedia, "Ballast tank"; HowStuffWorks, "How Submarines Work"]
2. 
   - **Line:** "A submarine comes back up by blowing air into its tanks." (11 words)
   - **Frame 0:** A sub deep in the dark with a huge burst of bubbles blasting out underneath, already rising.
   - **Caption:** "BLOW THE TANKS"
   - **Source:** [same as option 1]
3. 
   - **Line:** "Underwater, a submarine weighs about the same as the water it pushes aside." (13 words; trim to "A diving submarine weighs the same as the water it moves." at 11)
   - **Frame 0:** A sub sitting on one pan of a balance scale, and an identical sub-shaped block of seawater on the other. The scale is level.
   - **Caption:** "PERFECTLY BALANCED"
   - **Payoff:** Neutral buoyancy, held by small trim tanks and dive planes.
   - **Note:** Say "about", since trim is adjusted continually. [source: as above]

## 7. Re-running the data collection (to fill the table)

From a network where YouTube is not bot-checking, run this for each ID in the table. The ID list is in the scratchpad file `ids.txt` from this session.

```
yt-dlp --skip-download --write-auto-subs --sub-langs en --sub-format vtt -o "subs/%(id)s" https://www.youtube.com/shorts/<ID>
yt-dlp -f "worst[ext=mp4]" --download-sections "*0-5" -o "clips/<ID>.mp4" https://www.youtube.com/shorts/<ID>
ffmpeg -i clips/<ID>.mp4 -vf "select='eq(n,0)+eq(n,45)+eq(n,90)'" -vsync 0 frames/<ID>_%d.jpg
```

Space requests about a minute apart. Fast parallel requests triggered the bot check in this session.

# v2 (Oct 28, from research/HOOKS_V2.md; this REPLACES the older checklist above where they differ; 10 ranked hook patterns are in that file)

## Hook checklist v2 (run on every script and on the first rendered second)

1. **Line 1 is 12 words or fewer** and the topic noun lands in the first 5 words.
2. **Frame 0 shows the subject**, large and in motion (no title, logo, black or empty space). `reels/qa.py` checks it automatically.
3. **The sound-off test passes.** Frame 0 plus the 3-6 word caption tells the topic within 1 s.
4. **Line 1 is one of the ten patterns above**, not a topic announcement and not a greeting.
5. **One specific open question is written down** in NOTES.md, and the script answers it, explicitly, between 45 and 70 s.
6. **A "but" turn or stake by 5 s.** Line 2 deepens or flips line 1 instead of defining terms.
7. **The mechanism is on screen by about 3 s.** The tool, the cross-section or the diagram arrives early.
8. **A re-hook at about 25 s and about 50 s**: a mini-question, a try-it test or a surprising number.
9. **The hook is true and sourced.** Check numbers, "never", "always" and myth claims. Title, caption, thumbnail and line 1 make the same promise (legit bait, not a click trap).
10. **The first second is checked by eye on the 8 s preview** and with the sound off, before the full render is queued.

## Stop doing

- Openers with "Have you ever wondered", "Did you know", "Today we'll learn" or "Let's talk about". No top-performing title in our study opens like that. [data, HOOKS.md]
- Fading in from black, or opening on an empty set that fills up later (lock did this).
- Building the image up slowly. If the template's signature move is a reveal, start mid-reveal.
- First lines over 12 words, or lines that put the topic noun at the end.
- Captions that say something different from the voice and the image.
- Opening with a bare statistic. Numbers work as line 2 or as re-hooks.
- Unverified numbers in hooks, such as "cats see with 1/6 of the light" or "42 vs 12 insects". Drop them or soften them, as we did.
- Holding one static card for 4 s or more, at any point. The QA gate flags it, and search does it at 59-64 s.
- Answering the hook's question in the first 10 s, or never answering it at all.
- Brand badges, logos or "EP" marks in the first 5 s.
