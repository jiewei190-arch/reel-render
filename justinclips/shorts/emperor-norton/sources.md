# Sources: Emperor Norton

| Claim in the Short | Source |
|---|---|
| Lost his fortune trying to corner the rice market (1852-53) | American Heritage, "Emperor Norton I" ("in 1853 he overextended himself in one grand effort to corner every grain of rice"): https://www.americanheritage.com/emperor-norton-i?page=2 ; TIME archive "Heroes: Emperor Reburied": https://time.com/archive/6767361/heroes-emperor-reburied |
| September 1859: his proclamation, printed by the San Francisco (Evening) Bulletin: "declare and proclaim myself Emperor of these United States" | American Heritage (as above); Wikipedia, "Emperor Norton" (Sept 17, 1859, delivered to the Bulletin): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Norton |
| He issued his own money (scrip / imperial bonds, usually 50 cents) accepted by some restaurants/establishments he frequented | Wikipedia ("accepted from him by some restaurants in San Francisco"); American Heritage ("His imperial bonds - usually issued in the amount of fifty cents - were honored"); TIME ("people took his imperial banknotes") |
| Decree abolishing Congress (Oct 12, 1859); ignored | Wikipedia ("Norton's orders were ignored by the Army and Congress"); American Heritage ("proclamations abolished Congress") |
| Decrees ordering a bridge from Oakland/across the bay; built decades later (begun ~63 years later; Bay Bridge opened 1936) | TIME ("directed the erection of a bridge from Oakland to Yerba Buena Island which was begun 63 years later"); Wikipedia (decrees calling for a suspension bridge or tunnel) |
| Police saluted him in the street | Wikipedia ("Police officers of San Francisco thereafter saluted Norton as he passed in the street", after the 1867 arrest and the chief's apology) |
| Died January 1880; thousands came to see him off | TIME ("Ten thousand San Franciscans attended his funeral"); American Heritage ("A reported thirty thousand"). Figures disagree, so the Short says "thousands". |

Softened / avoided: crowd number (say "thousands"); Bummer and Lazarus are NOT shown as his dogs (myth); the uniform's origin is not stated;
"Protector of Mexico" not used (sources disagree whether he dropped it). The army-style coat follows the sources (a uniform given to him by officers at the Presidio: Wikipedia; American Heritage); the
plumed hat and all faces are our own stylised Toon 3D designs, not portraits.
The SF Museum page (https://sfmuseum.org/hist6/lazarus.html) was checked: it covers the dogs only (used for the "not his dogs" guard).
