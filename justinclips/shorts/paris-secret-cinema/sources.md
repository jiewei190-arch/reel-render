# Sources — The Secret Cinema Under Paris

Fetched Nov 20 2026 (raw wikitext via `en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=...&action=raw`).

## 1. Wikipedia, "Catacombs of Paris" (the card's source)
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs_of_Paris

Lead:
> The Catacombs of Paris (French: Catacombes de Paris) are underground ossuaries in Paris, France, which hold the
> remains of more than six million people.

Section on later use (cited to Urban-Resources, "La Mexicaine De Perforation", 2004):
> In 2004, police discovered a fully equipped movie theater in an area of the catacombs underneath the Trocadéro. It was
> equipped with a giant cinema screen, seats for the audience, projection equipment, film reels of recent thrillers and
> film noir classics, a fully stocked bar, and a complete restaurant with tables and chairs. The group les UX took
> responsibility for the installation.

Used for lines 1-5, 7 and 10: 2004, police, "movie theater", catacombs, beneath the Trocadéro, more than six million
people, giant cinema screen, seats for an audience, projection equipment, film reels of recent thrillers and film noir
classics, fully stocked bar, complete restaurant with tables and chairs, les UX "took responsibility".

## 2. Wikipedia, "Les UX" (linked from the sentence above; used for the escalations in lines 8, 9 and 11)
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_UX

> In September 2004, French police discovered an underground movie theatre run by La Mexicaine De Perforation ...
> The makeshift theatre contained a movie screen, a well stocked bar, and a kitchen, and was protected by a security
> system that played a recorded sound of barking dogs. ... Electricity and a phone connection had been brought in from
> an unknown location. When the police returned for a formal investigation, the cinema had been dismantled and the power
> and phone lines cut — they found a note reading "Ne cherchez pas" ("Do not search [for us]").

(Cited there to The Guardian, Jon Henley, 11 Sep 2004, "Paris's new slant on underground movies", and The Telegraph,
Henry Samuel, 30 Aug 2017.) The same article: "The UX (short for Urban eXperiment) is an underground organization of
urban explorers ... building a cinema — complete with a bar and a restaurant — in a section of the Paris Catacombs
underneath the Trocadéro."

Used for: line 8 (electricity and a phone line brought in from an unknown location), line 9 (security system = a
recording of barking dogs), line 11 (police came back to investigate, it had been dismantled, a note "Ne cherchez pas" =
"Do not search").

## Wording notes
- "stumbled onto" (line 1) follows the card's "stumbled onto"; Wikipedia says "discovered".
- "Do not search" is the literal translation of "Ne cherchez pas" (Wikipedia: "Do not search [for us]"). The note is
  shown in French on screen with the English under it.
- The theater was in the quarry tunnels Wikipedia calls "an area of the catacombs"; line 3 states the six-million figure
  as a fact about "the catacombs", as the article's lead does. Nothing claims the cinema sat among the bones.
