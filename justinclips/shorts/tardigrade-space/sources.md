# Sources: Tardigrade, the animal that survived space naked

## Claims and where they come from
| Claim in the video | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| In 2007 scientists sent tardigrades up on the OUTSIDE of a satellite | ESA; Jönsson et al. 2008 | ESA's FOTON-M3 (launched Sept 14, 2007), TARDIS experiment. **Dramatized:** they were not taped to the hull; dried-out (tun-state) animals sat in an open experiment container on the outside of the capsule. |
| No suits, no oxygen, open space for 10 days | Jönsson et al. 2008; Space.com; ESA | Exposed to space vacuum (+ solar and cosmic radiation) in low Earth orbit (~270 km) for 10 days of a 12-day flight. |
| Radiation out there is 1000x stronger than on Earth | Jönsson et al. 2008; Wikipedia "Tardigrades in space" | Solar UV in orbit was over 1,000 times the dose at Earth's surface. |
| They came home alive | Jönsson et al. 2008; ESA; National Geographic | Vacuum alone barely affected survival. **Only a few** animals (Milnesium tardigradum) survived the full combined vacuum + unfiltered solar UV dose; most of that group died (Scientific American: ~10 % recovered). |
| Some even laid eggs | Jönsson et al. 2008; Phys.org | Survivors reproduced normally after the flight (eggs laid and hatched). |
| Half a millimeter long, lives in moss, 8 legs, "water bear" | Wikipedia "Tardigrade" | Typically ~0.3-0.5 mm (up to ~1.2 mm); common in moss and lichen; 4 pairs of legs. "Gummy bear" is our comparison. |
| First animal in history to survive outer space naked | Jönsson et al. 2008; National Geographic; Space.com ("Creature Survives Naked in Space") | First animal known to survive the combined space vacuum + radiation of open space (before: lichens and bacteria). |

**Dramatized/cartoon only:** taping to the satellite, sunglasses/sunbathing, newspaper/pushups, the radiation meter, the fainting scientist, the trophy and helmet, the astronaut "versus" card. Nobody is hurt; no realistic photos.
