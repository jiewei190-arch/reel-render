# Sources: the Kentucky Meat Shower (1876)

Checked Nov 16 2026 (web search + page fetches).

## Every claim, sourced
- **March 3, 1876, Bath County, Kentucky: chunks of meat fell from a clear sky onto a farm (an area of about 100 x 50 yards near Olympia Springs)**: Wikipedia "Kentucky meat shower": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_meat_shower ; Mental Floss ("a patch of farmyard about 100 yards long and 50 yards wide"): https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/time-it-rained-flesh-kentucky ; the original report, The New York Times, March 10, 1876: "Flesh Descending in a Shower; An Astounding Phenomenon in Kentucky - Fresh Meat Like Mutton or Venison Falling From a Clear Sky."
- **"Clear blue sky"**: Scientific American blog (Bec Crew, 2014): "The sky was perfectly clear at the time" https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/running-ponies/the-great-kentucky-meat-shower-mystery-unwound-by-projectile-vulture-vomit/ ; NYT subhead above.
- **Chunks about 2 inches wide (top caption)**: Wikipedia (most about 2 x 2 inches, at least one 4 x 4); Scientific American (most about 5 x 5 cm).
- **A woman making soap outside watched it land**: Wikipedia (Mrs. Crouch "was making soap on her porch"); Scientific American ("outside making soap"). Her first name differs between sources, so the VO says "a woman".
- **Two men reportedly tasted it and guessed mutton or venison**: the 1876 report ("two gentlemen, who tasted the meat, express the opinion that it was either mutton or venison"); Scientific American. Hedged with "reportedly"; the men are unnamed in the sources, and unnamed in the video.
- **Lab tests later found lung, muscle and cartilage**: Wikipedia ("Two samples were identified as lung, three as muscle, and two as cartilage"). The lab card also says "(species: unclear)" (Wikipedia: a later test was inconclusive).
- **"The most popular explanation? Vultures, throwing up mid-flight."**: Dr. L. D. Kastenbine, Louisville Medical News, 1876, quoted in Scientific American: "the disgorgement of some vultures that were sailing over the spot". Wikipedia: "often treated as the leading scientific explanation"; its infobox: "Inconclusive, possibly regurgitating vultures". The VO says "most popular explanation", not "solved".

## Kept hedges
- "reportedly" on the tasting; "most popular explanation" (not proven) for the vultures; nobody saw birds at the time (Wikipedia), so the video never shows a witness looking at vultures.
- No time of day, duration or first name (sources disagree or don't say). "Near Rankin", often repeated online, is not in the sources and is not used.

## Dramatised (comedy, not claimed as fact)
Chunk sizes on screen are exaggerated for readability (frame 1), the PLOP!, the sun beams, the woman's deadpan stare, the men's MUTTON?/VENISON? signs, the scientist, the BLEH! Meat is drawn as cartoon steak chunks (pink with a cream fat stripe): no blood, no gore, no realistic photos. All characters are original toons.
