# Sources: the rats who got a lawyer (Autun, France, early 1500s)

Checked Nov 16 2026 (WebSearch + WebFetch). The accounts are old and retold, so the VO keeps "reportedly", as the brief asks.

## Every claim, sourced
- **Rats in and around Autun (Burgundy, France) were put on trial for destroying the barley crop, early 16th century**: Wikipedia, "Barthélemy de Chasseneuz": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barth%C3%A9lemy_de_Chasseneuz ("...made his reputation as a criminal lawyer by his eloquent defense of a group of rats ... put on trial for destroying the barley crop of the province", citing E. P. Evans, *The Criminal Prosecution and Capital Punishment of Animals*, 1906, p. 18). Sky HISTORY, "The law is an ass: 8 famous animal trials from history": https://www.history.co.uk/articles/the-law-is-an-ass-8-famous-animal-trials-from-history ("In the ancient French town of Autun in the early 16th century..."). Sources give different years (about 1508-1522), so the VO says "the early 1500s".
- **The rats were summoned to court**: Sky HISTORY ("...been summoned to court"). Pests such as rats and insects were usually tried in church (ecclesiastical) courts (Sky HISTORY). The VO just says "a court".
- **The rats didn't show up; their lawyer, Barthélemy de Chasseneuz, argued one summons wasn't enough because they lived scattered across many villages**: Evans (1906), via Grunge, "Inside Barthélemy de Chasseneuz's trial of the rats": https://www.grunge.com/597357/inside-barthelemy-de-chasseneuzs-trial-of-the-rats ; The Vintage News: https://www.thevintagenews.com/2019/02/18/rats/ ; First Things, "Crime Rats": https://firstthings.com/?p=67990 .
- **He then argued they couldn't come safely because of the town's cats, and asked for the cats to be kept indoors**: Sky HISTORY ("implored the townsfolk to keep their cats indoors, as the presence of the felines in the streets was also preventing the attendance of his 'clients' the rats"); Grunge; Discover Magazine: https://www.discovermagazine.com/yes-animals-and-insects-have-stood-trial-in-court-44543 .
- **The owners said no, and the case was reportedly thrown out**: Sky HISTORY ("The owners of the moggies being unwilling to impose a kitty curfew, the case was dismissed."). HEDGED: other retellings say the record simply ends with no verdict, so the VO says "reportedly thrown out" and the end card repeats "(reportedly)". This is also why the line isn't the brief's suggested "and their lawyer won".

## Kept hedges
- "reportedly" on the rats eating the barley and on the outcome.
- "the early 1500s" (the year differs between accounts).

## Dramatised (comedy, not claimed as fact)
Rats in tiny hats sitting in the dock, the town crier, the empty bench with "...", the map of villages with a rat in each, the cats lounging in the street, the townsfolk hugging their cats, the gavel and the stamps. Chasseneuz, the presiding official and the townsfolk are original toon beans, not likenesses. No animal is hurt or chased.
