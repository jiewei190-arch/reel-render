# Sources: The (second) Defenestration of Prague, 1618

Checked Nov 16 2026 (WebSearch + WebFetch of Wikipedia; Britannica's page refused automated access, HTTP 403). Quotes are verbatim.

## Every claim, sourced
- **Three men got thrown out of a castle window (Prague, 1618), by angry nobles**: Wikipedia, "Defenestrations of Prague": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenestrations_of_Prague . On 23 May 1618 the Protestant estates "threw two royal governors and their secretary out of a window of the Hradčany Castle". The regents were Jaroslav Bořita of Martinice and Vilém Slavata of Chlum, and the secretary was Philip Fabricius. History Revealed explainer: https://historyrevealed.com/period/stuart/1618-defenestration-prague-facts-history-explained-what-happened-why-castle-protestant-catholic . The nobles were Protestant Bohemian estates angry at the Catholic Habsburg regents. The VO says "angry nobles" and doesn't take sides.
- **About 70 feet down**: Wikipedia ("they survived the 70-foot (21-metre) fall from the third floor"). The same article also says "around 16–20 meters", and another summary gives "sixty-nine feet". So the VO says "About 70 feet" and the screen shows "~70 FT (about 21 m)", hedged with "about" and "~".
- **All three survived**: Wikipedia ("they survived the 70-foot (21-metre) fall").
- **Catholics said angels caught them**: Wikipedia ("Catholics maintained the men were saved by angels or by the intercession of the Virgin Mary, who caught them"). Labelled "CATHOLIC VERSION" on screen.
- **Later pamphlets said they landed in a pile of manure**: Wikipedia ("later Protestant pamphleteers asserted that they survived due to falling onto a dung heap"). Labelled "PAMPHLET VERSION" on screen, and the pamphlet card says "(printed later)".
- **Historians think that part was probably made up**: Wikipedia ("a story unknown to contemporaries and probably coined in response to divine intervention claims"). On screen: "PROBABLY MADE UP".
- **The secretary got a noble title, Baron von Hohenfall ("Baron of High Fall")**: Wikipedia ("Philip Fabricius was later ennobled by the Emperor and granted the title Baron von Hohenfall (literally 'Baron of Highfall')").

## Kept hedges (brief: "Hedge the dung-heap account")
- The manure story is attributed ("Later pamphlets said...") and then flagged ("Historians think that part was probably made up").
- The angels are attributed ("Catholics said...").
- The height is "about 70 feet" (sources give 16–21 m).
- Left out on purpose: the event "led to the Thirty Years' War" (Wikipedia). True, but it is a war and would break the one emotion (LOL) and the "nobody hurt" tone guard.

## Tone guards
Nobody is shown hurt. The fall is cartoon flailing in mid-air, and every landing is cut away from or shown soft (angels catching them, a squashy cartoon manure heap). The survivors sit up dazed (cartoon stars) but fine. No gore, no realistic photos, no likenesses. All characters are original toons, and the ruler in the title scene is a generic crowned toon, not a portrait of Emperor Ferdinand II.
