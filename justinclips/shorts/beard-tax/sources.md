# Sources — Peter the Great's Beard Tax

Re-verified Nov 15 2026 (WebSearch) against the topic-bank sources.

## Every claim, sourced
- **1698: Peter the Great, back from his Western European tour, wanted Russia to look modern/European and taxed beards** — Smithsonian Magazine, "Why Tsar Peter the Great Established a Beard Tax": https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/why-tsar-peter-great-established-beard-tax-180964693/ · Atlas Obscura: https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/a-beard-tax-is-being-proposed-in-england-and-its-not-the-first
- **Paying got you a token; patrols checked and fined or arrested men without one** — Wikipedia, "Beard tax" (citing numismatic literature): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beard_tax · Smithsonian (above)
- **"One token even says: the beard is a useless burden"** — Wikipedia: the 1699/1705 tokens read "money taken" (ДЕНГИ ВЗѦТЫ); the later rhomboid token of 1724/25 carries a phrase meaning "the beard is a superfluous burden" (here translated "useless burden"). The video shows that diamond-shaped later token for this line and says "one token", not "every token". (A numismatic newsletter, E-Sylum, puts both phrases on the 1705 token: https://www.coinbooks.org/esylum_v15n46a19.html; we follow Wikipedia + the British Museum.)
- **"The British Museum still has the tokens"** — British Museum collection, beard tokens C.3701, C.3702, C.3703, 1906,1103.5152: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_C-3702 · https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_C-3703 · https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_C-3701. The museum case label in the video says "Russia, 1705", matching the round 1705 type.

## Kept hedges (from TOPICS.md)
- Rates varied by social class and differ between sources → **no amounts said or shown**.
- Tokens appear from 1705 (earliest copper type 1698/99 is very rare) → the video doesn't date the token in the VO; the museum label says 1705.
- The English (Henry VII/VIII) beard tax is dubious → **not used**.

The barber chase with giant scissors, the official with a coin chest, the patrol's halt, the offended old man, the museum visitor with heart eyes. The street, church and museum building are generic toon sets, not real locations.
