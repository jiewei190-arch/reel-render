# Sources — Miss Unsinkable

Violet Jessop (1887–1971), an Argentine-born Irish stewardess and nurse, was aboard all three White Star "Olympic-class" sister
ships when each met disaster: RMS Olympic (collision with the cruiser HMS Hawke, 1911), RMS Titanic (sank 1912) and HMHS
Britannic (struck a mine and sank, 1916). Facts checked 15 Nov 2026 against the owner's PDF sources (Wikipedia, Sky HISTORY;
the National Geographic link could not be confirmed, so it is not cited).

## Deliberately left out / softened (tone guard)
- Real deaths: Titanic (~1,500) and Britannic (30) are not dramatised; no bodies, nobody shown hurt. No comedy on either sinking.
- The owner's "shredding lifeboats" line is cut (MAIN tone guard); the propeller shot shows only the propeller and water.
- Her head injury in the Britannic jump is not shown or mentioned.
- The baby: Wikipedia notes her memoir says a woman, presumably the mother, took the baby back on the Carpathia; the video says
  only that an officer "reportedly" handed it to her, and does not claim a reunion.
- The exact mine date differs between sources (Wikipedia 21 Nov 1916; Sky HISTORY 16 Nov), so the video gives the year only.

## Dramatised (stylised, not claimed as fact)
Toon characters and faces, the dining-saloon teacup, the "TRANSFER ORDER" card and its stamp (a sad-trombone sting on the
paperwork only), the three-checkmark scoreboard, the ship models (stylised White Star liners and a grey cruiser), Violet sipping
tea on dry land at the end.
