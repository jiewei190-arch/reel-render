# Sources — What if Earth stopped spinning for one second?

Every claim was checked against Wikipedia plus at least one more reliable source (pages fetched 2026-11-27). This is a
**thought experiment**: the video says so in line 2 and follows NASA's own Q&A explanations.

| Line | Claim (as spoken) | Wikipedia | Second source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 5, 6 | Stop the spin and everything not bolted down keeps moving, sliding east | — (physics of momentum; see 3) | NASA Cosmicopia (Goddard), "What Happens if Earth Stops Rotating?" |
| 2 | It's a thought experiment / it can't really happen | — | Live Science (Zimbelman, Smithsonian): "This is just a thought experiment" |
| 3, 7(s07 chip) | Ground at the equator moves east at over 1,600 km/h | Earth's rotation: ~1,674.7 km/h at the equator | NASA Cosmicopia: "~1000 mph"; Live Science: "about 1,100 mph (1,770 km/h)" |
| 7 | The oceans would slosh over the land | — | NASA Goddard "Stargazers" FAQ #108 |
| 8 | At the North Pole you'd barely move | Earth's rotation: speed "becoming zero at the poles" | NASA Cosmicopia: "Only the people at the poles would be safe" |
| 9 | NASA: stopping Earth would take a miracle | — | NASA Goddard "Stargazers" FAQ #165 |

"About 2 milliseconds" is the round middle of the published 1.7-2.3 ms range.

## Quotes used

**Wikipedia — Earth's rotation** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_rotation
> "Earth's rotational speed is about 1,674.7 km/h at the equator, but it decreases with latitude, becoming zero at the poles."
> "Atomic clocks show that the modern day is longer by about 1.7 milliseconds per century compared to several centuries ago"
> "the length of a day has increased by about 2.3 milliseconds per century since the 8th century BCE."

**NASA Cosmicopia (Goddard), Ask Us — Earth and Moon** — https://science.gsfc.nasa.gov/attic/cosmicopia.gsfc.nasa.gov/qa_earth.html
(old URL https://cosmicopia.gsfc.nasa.gov/qa_earth.html redirects here)
> "What happens to all the earthlings, if Earth stops spinning? ... Of course, this can't happen, but if it did, everything not
> attached would go flying off to the east, parallel to the surface of the Earth. The speed would depend upon your latitude.
> Only the people at the poles would be safe. You wouldn't go flying off into space because the 1000 mph maximum (at the
> equator) isn't enough to overcome gravity" — Dr. Eric Christian

**NASA Goddard, "Stargazers" FAQ #108 (Stopping the rotation of Earth?)** — https://pwg.gsfc.nasa.gov/stargaze/IstarFAQ6.htm
> "everything on the surface of Earth would still continue its motion ... Of course the oceans too would slosh over and flood
> everything, unless restrained by the same miracle."

**NASA Goddard, "Stargazers" FAQ #165 (If the Earth's Rotation would Stop)** — https://pwg.gsfc.nasa.gov/stargaze/StarFAQ10.htm
> "It would take a miracle, of course ... because the spinning Earth has so much angular momentum, and so much energy, that if
> something suddenly caused the spin to stop, then following Newton's laws, that "something" would itself have to absorb a huge
> amount of energy and angular momentum."

**Live Science — What would happen if Earth suddenly stopped spinning?** — https://www.livescience.com/what-if-earth-stopped-spinning.html
> "'This is just a thought experiment,' said James Zimbelman, senior geologist emeritus at the Smithsonian's National Air and
> Space Museum ... 'There is no natural force that would stop the Earth from spinning.'"
> "land at the equator moving at about 1,100 mph (1,770 km/h), with rotational velocity decreasing to zero at the poles"

**Live Science — Slowing of Earth's Spin Revealed in Ancient Astronomers' Tablets** — https://www.livescience.com/57118-earth-spin-slowdown-shown-in-ancient-tablets.html
> "Each century, the length of the solar day ... grows by 1.8 milliseconds ... measurements of this tidal effect suggest that the
> planet should be slowing in its rotation by 2.3 milliseconds per century"

## What the video does NOT claim
- No death toll, injuries or "everyone dies" lines: the NASA pages are blunt about survival; the video keeps to what moves
  and where, and shows nobody hurt (cartoon objects slide, the farmer flails and is fine, the cow floats on a rubber ring).
- No supersonic-wind claim: NASA's FAQ #108 says the air would share the same motion, so the script leaves winds out.
- No six-month day: that applies to a permanent stop, not a one-second one.
