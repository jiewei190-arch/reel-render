# Sources: Immortal Jellyfish (Turritopsis dohrnii)

## Claims and where they come from
| Claim in the video | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| A jellyfish that "refuses to die of old age", "in theory it can do this forever" | NHM London | NHM: "This life cycle reversal can be repeated." and "In perfect conditions, it may be that these jellyfish would never die of old age." Kept "in theory". In the lab it has been seen up to ~10 cycles (NHM; Hasegawa et al. 2022). "Immortal" is a nickname. |
| Injured or starving -> it doesn't die, it resets | NHM London; Hasegawa et al. 2022 | NHM: "When the medusa of this species is physically damaged or experiences stresses such as starvation..." The lab also triggers it with heat shock / chemicals (DNA Research). |
| Melts into a blob, sinks to the seafloor, becomes a baby (polyp) | NHM London; The Conversation | NHM: it "shrinks in on itself, reabsorbing its tentacles... settles on the seafloor as a blob-like cyst... Over the next 24 to 36 hours, this blob develops into a new polyp". "Baby" = the polyp, the juvenile stage. The pacifier is a joke. |
| Grows up again, and again (new jellyfish bud off) | NHM London; The Conversation | NHM: "medusae bud off"; The Conversation: the new polyp "in turn gives rise to new jellyfish". |
| It's called transdifferentiation | NHM London | NHM: "The process behind this small jellyfish's remarkable transformation is called transdifferentiation." (The Conversation also uses "dedifferentiation".) |
| Smaller than your pinky nail (card: about 4.5 mm) | NHM London; The Conversation | NHM: "about 4.5 millimetres wide and tall"; The Conversation: "no more than four millimetres". The pinky-nail comparison is ours. |
| The catch: fish and turtles eat it | NHM London | NHM: prey "to other animals, such as fish and turtles". **Changed from the brief's "Everything eats it"** (no source says that; NHM names fish and turtles). No eating is shown. |
| It doesn't even have a brain | BBC Wildlife / Discover Wildlife | Jellyfish live "without a centralised brain", with "networks of nerves" (a nerve net). True of all jellyfish. |
| (Not claimed) "the only animal that does this" | Hasegawa et al. 2022; NHM | Some other cnidarians show life-cycle reversal too, so the video does not say "only". |
