# Sources: dagen-h ("A Whole Country Switched Road Sides in a Minute")
Checked Nov 11 2026. Britannica's Dagen H page returned HTTP 403 to the cloud fetcher, and MoneyWeek returned only a stub,
so the claims below rest on Wikipedia (with its cited Swedish sources) and Mental Floss. Add Britannica or a Swedish
museum page before posting if MAIN wants a top-tier source (topic bank "source gap" note).

| Claim in the video | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Sweden switched from left- to right-hand traffic on Sunday 3 September 1967 ("At 5 AM") | [Wikipedia: Dagen H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagen_H) ("Sunday, 3 September 1967"; infobox time 05:00) · [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/day-everyone-sweden-had-start-driving-other-side-road) | |
| Every driver, same minute | Wikipedia: vehicles "had to come to a complete stop at 04:50, then carefully change to the right-hand side of the road and stop again... before being allowed to proceed at 05:00" | Non-essential traffic was banned 01:00-06:00 (longer in Stockholm/Malmö), so "every driver" = everyone on the road. |
| Every neighbour drives on the right | Wikipedia: "All neighbouring countries drove on the right, including Norway and Finland"; Mental Floss: "Sweden's neighboring countries all had right-side traffic patterns" | The border gag (two cars meeting nose to nose) is a cartoon of this. |
| 83% voted to keep the left | Wikipedia: "in a 1955 referendum, 83 percent voted to keep driving on the left"; Mental Floss: "82.9 percent" | Card says "(1955 referendum)". |
| The government switched anyway | Wikipedia: the Riksdag approved the change in 1963 despite the referendum | |
| At 4:50, every car stops | Wikipedia (quote above); Mental Floss: "At 4:50 a.m., 'cycles and taxis were guided across the road by the police.'" | |
| About 350,000 road signs had to be swapped | Wikipedia: "Approximately 350,000 signs had to be removed or replaced, including some 20,000 in Stockholm alone" | Said "about". The topic bank's "hundreds of thousands" is consistent. |
| Accidents went down, "for a few weeks, anyway" | Wikipedia: "a temporary reduction in the number of accidents... motor insurance claims returned to 'normal' over the next six weeks and, by 1969, the accident rates were back to the levels seen before"; Mental Floss: "Accident levels returned to normal after six weeks." | Topic bank: "an initial drop, not forever" — that is exactly the last line. No percentage is shown. |

Visual liberties: the street, cars, clock, the yellow "H" campaign sign and the keep-left/right signs are stylised; the
blue round "keep" sign flip stands for the sign swap. Nobody is hurt; no crash is shown.
