# Sources: "He Survived Both Atomic Bombs" (yamaguchi)
Checked Nov 9 2026. Tone guard: told with respect; no injuries or victims shown, the bombs appear only as distant white light.

| Claim (as said) | Source | Quote / note |
|---|---|---|
| "He survived Hiroshima. Then he went home to Nagasaki." / "In August 1945, he was in Hiroshima on a business trip." | NBC News (AP), Mar 24 2009 https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna29865222 | "Yamaguchi was in Hiroshima on a business trip on Aug. 6, 1945 ... He then returned to his hometown of Nagasaki just in time for the second attack" |
| "a ship designer from Nagasaki" | Wikipedia, "Tsutomu Yamaguchi" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsutomu_Yamaguchi (cites Telegraph, Guardian, Times, Independent) | "a draftsman designing oil tankers" for Mitsubishi Heavy Industries; Nagasaki was his hometown. |
| "the first atomic bomb exploded about 3 km away" | Wikipedia (above) | "only 3 kilometres (1.9 mi) away" |
| "He was badly burned. But he survived." | Wikipedia (above) | serious burns to the upper body (not shown on screen). |
| "The next day, he went home to Nagasaki." | Wikipedia (above) | "He returned to Nagasaki the following day". The train is only a visual; the script does not claim how he travelled. |
| "On August 9, he was at work, telling his boss what he had seen. Then the second bomb fell on Nagasaki." | Wikipedia (above) | "while he was being told by his supervisor that he was 'crazy' after describing how one bomb had destroyed the city, the Nagasaki bomb detonated." |
| "For decades, his papers only listed Nagasaki." | Wikipedia (above) | "In 1957, he was recognized as a hibakusha of the Nagasaki bombing" ... his identification "stated only that he had been present at Nagasaki." |
| "In his later years, he spoke out against nuclear weapons." | Wikipedia (above) | "Yamaguchi became a vocal proponent of nuclear disarmament"; pleaded for abolition at a UN screening of a 2006 documentary. |
| "In 2009, at 93, Japan officially recognized him as a survivor of both." | NBC News (AP) (above) | "A 93-year-old Japanese man has become the first person certified as a survivor of both U.S. atomic bombings" (certified 24 March 2009). |
| "The first, and so far only, person to get that recognition." | NBC News (AP); Wikipedia (above) | Official: "As far as we know, he is the first one to be officially recognized"; Wikipedia: "the only person officially recognized as a survivor of both bombings". About 160-165 people are thought to have experienced both bombings; he is the only one officially certified, so the script says "officially recognized". |

Not read (paywall / server down): Japan Times (402), Hiroshima Peace Media Center (503). He died on 4 January 2010, aged 93 (Wikipedia); not in the script.
