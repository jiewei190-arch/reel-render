# Sources: moon-hoax ("A Newspaper Said Bat-People Lived on the Moon")
Pages read Nov 11 2026 (the LOC Folklife page loaded only partly, so it is not relied on for any line below).

- **LH** = Linda Hall Library, "Scientist of the Day: Richard Adams Locke": https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/richard-adams-locke
- **MoH** = Museum of Hoaxes, "The Great Moon Hoax": https://hoaxes.org/archive/permalink/the_great_moon_hoax
- **LOC** = Library of Congress Folklife Blog, "The Great Moon Hoax" (2014): https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2014/08/the-great-moon-hoax/ (background)

| Line in the Short | Source and what it says |
|---|---|
| "A newspaper said bat-people lived on the Moon." | LH and MoH: the series described the "Vespertilio-homo, or man-bat", with "wings composed of a thin membrane". |
| "And people bought it." | LH: "Nevertheless, many did believe." Sales: see the 60,000 line. (Not "everyone": MoH says belief was likely "not quite as widespread as frequently reported".) |
| "New York, August 1835. The Sun runs a front-page scoop." | LH: "On Aug. 25, 1835, the New York Sun ... ran a front-page story." MoH: six parts, Aug 25-31, 1835. The front page in the video is a simplified drawing; its headline paraphrases the real one ("Great Astronomical Discoveries lately made by Sir John Herschel ... at the Cape of Good Hope", MoH). |
| "It says the famous astronomer John Herschel has seen life on the Moon. Through a giant new telescope." | MoH headline: "BY SIR JOHN HERSCHEL ... At the Cape of Good Hope"; the telescope "measured 24 feet in diameter" (the lens, as described by the hoax). |
| "First, animals like bison. Then, unicorns." | MoH: "herds of brown quadrupeds similar to bison". LH: "such as unicorns". (MoH describes it as "a goat of a bluish lead color", hence the blue goat-unicorn.) |
| "Then beavers, walking on two legs, carrying their babies." | MoH: "They walked on two feet and bore their young in their arms. They lived in huts." LH: "bipedal beavers that cradled their young". |
| "And finally... people. With bat wings." | LH, MoH: the man-bats (the last and most famous creatures of the series). |
| "Herschel was in South Africa, and had no idea." | MoH: the Cape of Good Hope (in today's South Africa); "Herschel wasn't even aware until much later." LH: he "would not hear about the series, and could issue no denials, until long after the series was over." |
| "A writer at the Sun made it up. To poke fun at people, and to sell papers." | LH: composed "by a writer at the Sun, Richard Adams Locke, for the purpose of satirizing contemporary fascination with life on other worlds, and to boost circulation." |
| "According to one account, the story sold 60,000 copies as a pamphlet." | MoH, citing William Gowans (1859): "it sold 60,000 pamphlets in less than a month." Said as "one account" because it is a single later claim. |
| "And the Sun? It never admitted it was fake." | LH: "The New York Sun never publicly confessed that the series was a work of fiction"; Locke admitted it in a private letter five years later. |

Not used on purpose: "it sold out" / "sales went from 8,000 to 19,360" (MoH says that rise is disputed: the Sun already sold ~19,000-20,000 a day before the hoax).
Visual notes: original bean characters (the astronomer is not a portrait); the HERO (cast) cameo asks "Did Herschel really see that?".
