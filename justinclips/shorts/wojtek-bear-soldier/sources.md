# Sources: Wojtek the Soldier Bear

Checked Nov 16 2026 (web search + page fetches).

## Every claim, sourced
- **A bear served with the army**: Imperial War Museums, War Memorials Register (Wojtek memorial): "Wojtek, a Syrian brown bear... Found in Persian mountains in 1942" https://memorials.iwm.org.uk/memorial/78065 ; Wikipedia "Wojtek (bear)": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojtek_(bear) ; British Library, "Wojtek the soldier bear from the Polish army" (M. Szkuta, 2015): https://www.bl.uk/stories/blogs/posts/wojtek-the-soldier-bear-from-the-polish-army
- **1942: Polish soldiers in Iran adopted a bear cub, named Wojtek**: Wikipedia (bought from an Iranian shepherd near Hamadan, "accounts differ"); British Library ("sold to Polish soldiers for a few cans of corned beef"; adopted by the 22nd Artillery Supply Company, Polish II Corps). The VO only says "adopted".
- **He loved beer, wrestling and long showers**: Wikipedia (rewarded with beer, "which became his favourite drink"); British Library ("loved beer and cigarettes", "His favourite game was wrestling with his fellow soldiers", his "much-loved showers"). The top caption "his favourite drink was beer" is from Wikipedia.
- **To ship him to Italy, the army listed him as a soldier**: Wikipedia (the British transport's rules "forbade mascot and pet animals", so he was "officially drafted into the Polish Army as a private"). The British Library calls the formal enlistment partly legend (he travelled on a special permit). So the VO says "**listed** him as a soldier", the roster card shows "soldier", and no rank, serial number or rations are claimed.
- **At Monte Cassino (1944) he reportedly carried crates of artillery shells**: IWM ("He is reported to have helped move artillery ammunition at Monte Cassino"); Wikipedia ("at least one account exists of a British soldier recalling seeing a bear carrying crates of ammunition"). Hedged with "reportedly" in the VO and on screen.
- **His unit's badge became a bear carrying a shell**: Wikipedia (a bear carrying an artillery shell "was adopted as the official emblem of the 22nd Company"); IWM plaque description ("A Brown Bear standing on its hind legs holding a large artillery shell").

## Kept hedges / left out
- "reportedly" on the crates; "listed as a soldier" instead of "enlisted as a private"; no "never dropped a shell", no "under heavy fire", no serial number, no double rations, no spy-in-the-shower story (all doubtful per the British Library / single-source).
- No combat is shown or claimed: he carried supplies.
- Not used: Winfield (Scotland) and Edinburgh Zoo 1947-1963, the statues (Edinburgh 2015, Krakow 2014 and others).

## Dramatised (not claimed as fact)
The exact way he walked, the cheering, the patting, the soldier with the towel, the gangplank, the typed roster's other names (invented placeholder surnames), the patch-sewing beat. The badge in the video is our own toon drawing of the description, not the real emblem artwork. All characters are original toons.
