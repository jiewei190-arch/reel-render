## Description
On 3 November 1888, at about 8 pm, tens of thousands of sheep across some 200 square miles of Oxfordshire bolted from their fields at the same moment. The next morning they were found crowded into corners of fields, some panting with terror under hedges. The Times wrote that "a thousand men could not have frightened and released all these sheep." In 1889 it happened again, in Berkshire. In 1921 the journal Nature offered one explanation: an intensely dark night with occasional flashes of lightning, and sheep that are "notoriously timid and nervous animals". The cause was never confirmed.
