# Sources: A Pope Put a Dead Pope on Trial (dead-pope-trial)
Checked Nov 9 2026. Atlas Obscura (listed in the topic bank) returned HTTP 403, so it was not used.

| Claim in the script | Source (quote) |
|---|---|
| A pope put a dead pope on trial (the "Cadaver Synod") | Wikipedia, "Cadaver Synod": "The Cadaver Synod (also called the Cadaver Trial; Latin: Synodus Horrenda)"; National Geographic: "Known as the 'Cadaver Synod,'" |
| Rome, 897 | Wikipedia: "Probably around January 897, Stephen VI ordered that the corpse of his predecessor Formosus be removed from its tomb"; National Geographic: "early 897" |
| Stephen VI had his predecessor Formosus dug up | National Geographic: "Pope Stephen VI and Lambert ordered that Formosus's corpse be disinterred"; JSTOR Daily: "had the body disinterred" |
| Dead for nine months | Wikipedia: "who had been dead for about nine months"; National Geographic: "nine months after his death" |
| Dressed in papal robes, sat on a throne | JSTOR Daily: "dressed in its ecclesiastical robes, and propped up on the papal throne"; Wikipedia: "With the corpse propped up on a throne" |
| A deacon answered for him | Wikipedia: "a deacon was appointed to answer for the deceased pontiff"; National Geographic: "A deacon was appointed to speak for the deceased." |
| "Badly." | Narrator's joke on the outcome (he was found guilty, next line). |
| Verdict: guilty; everything he did as pope cancelled | JSTOR Daily: "the dead pope was found guilty of usurping the papacy. Stephen VI declared all his acts as pope null and void"; Wikipedia: "formally invalidating all of Formosus's acts and ordinations" |
| Months later a new pope cancelled the verdict | Wikipedia: "In December 897 Pope Theodore II ... convened a synod that annulled the Cadaver Synod" |
| ...and banned putting dead people on trial | Wikipedia: "In 898, John IX ... also nullified the Cadaver Synod" and "prohibited any trial of a corpse in the future"; National Geographic: "Pope John IX reinstated Pope Formosus and banned further trials for dead popes" (NatGeo dates this "two years later"; the script keeps it undated as part of "months later... and banned"). |

death in prison. No transcript survives; the script makes no claim about what was said beyond the deacon answering.
