# Sources: Oregon's Exploding Whale (1970)

Re-verified Nov 15 2026 (WebSearch + WebFetch of Wikipedia) against the owner's kit (`justinclips/briefs/funny-kits-nov15_owner.pdf`).

## Every claim, sourced
- **1970, Florence, Oregon; a dead ~45 ft, ~8-ton sperm whale on the beach; the Oregon Highway Division blew it up with dynamite (detonated Nov 12, 1970)**: Wikipedia, "Exploding whale": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploding_whale ("On November 9, 1970, a 45-foot-long (14 m) sperm whale washed ashore at Florence..."; "...detonated on November 12"). Live Science: https://www.livescience.com/exploding-whale-park-oregon.html
- **Half a ton of dynamite / 20 cases; a man with explosives training said 20 sticks would do it**: Wikipedia ("A charge of one-half short ton (450 kg) of dynamite was selected"; "A military veteran with explosives training ... warned that the planned twenty cases of dynamite was far too much, and that 20 sticks ... would have sufficed"). Mental Floss: https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/florence-oregon-exploding-whale-1970 . The VO says "an explosives guy" (his exact words are paraphrased).
- **"The blast blasted blubber beyond all believable bounds"**: the line from reporter Paul Linnman's KATU voice-over, quoted in Wikipedia ("...for the blast blasted blubber beyond all believable bounds"). KATU 50th-anniversary coverage: https://katu.com/news/local/exploding-whale-turns-50-should-a-whale-ever-was-ashore-in-lane-county-again
- **A chunk crushed a brand-new car**: Wikipedia ("a local explosive expert had purchased a new automobile ... It was damaged by a chunk of falling blubber"); Jalopnik on the crushed 1969 Oldsmobile: https://www.jalopnik.com/heres-the-story-behind-the-oldsmobile-crushed-by-300-lb-1848658789/
- **"Reportedly flew a quarter mile"**: HEDGED. Several accounts say the car was parked about a quarter mile away (Salem Reporter: https://www.salemreporter.com/?p=158935 ; Mental Floss, above; owner's kit). Wikipedia only says flesh was thrown "up to 800 feet". So the VO says "reportedly", and the counter's "≈ ¼ MILE" follows that VO line.
- **Most of the whale was still there**: Wikipedia ("Only some of the whale was disintegrated; most of it remained on the beach").
- **Oregon now buries whales**: Wikipedia ("Currently, Oregon State Parks Department policy is to bury whale carcasses where they land"); ABC/KATV "Amazing America": https://katv.com/amazing-america/florence-oregon-1970-exploding-whale-incident-bizarre-america-kevin-kotwas
- **Exploding Whale Memorial Park**: Florence residents voted for the name in 2020. Wikipedia; Eugene Weekly: https://eugeneweekly.com/2020/06/16/florence-gives-park-an-explosive-new-name/ ; Willamette Week: https://www.wweek.com/outdoors/2020/07/01/florence-has-named-a-park-after-the-legendary-1970-whale-explosion/ . (The park is on the Siuslaw River, not at the blast site. The video shows a riverside park and doesn't claim it is the spot.)

## Kept hedges
- "reportedly" on the quarter mile (see above).
- The expert's exact words are paraphrased; "20 cases" was the planned/selected charge (≈ half a ton).
- Whale species: Wikipedia and the owner's kit say sperm whale. The VO doesn't name the species.
