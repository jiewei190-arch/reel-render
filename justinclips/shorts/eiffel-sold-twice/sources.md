## Every claim
- **1925** — all three ("In 1925, Lustig traveled back to France" / "In May 1925, an article appeared in a Paris newspaper..." / "By 1925...").
- **Tower repairs too costly (newspaper on screen)** — Mental Floss (May 1925 newspaper article on upkeep); Wikipedia (upkeep "becoming too much for Paris").
- **Forged government letterhead** — all three: fake government stationery; Smithsonian: letters on fake stationery as "Deputy Director" of the Ministère des Postes et Télégraphes (shown on the s04 letter).
- **"6 scrap dealers" + "being torn down"** — demolition/scrap pitch: all three (Smithsonian: bids "to demolish the tower and take possession of 7,000 tons of metal"). **Count: sources differ.** Mental Floss says *five*; Wikipedia and Smithsonian give no number; several popular retellings surfaced by WebSearch say *six* (not individually re-read). Kept "6" because the brief lists "six scrap dealers"; **MAIN: if you want zero risk, change to "five" or drop the number (one line, re-voice ~5 min).**
- **Andre Poisson paid, plus a bribe** — all three (Smithsonian: $20,000 for the deal plus $50,000 more; Wikipedia: a large bribe, ~70,000 francs). No amount is said or shown.
- **Lustig fled the country** — all three (fled to Austria). The train is dramatised.
- **Poisson too embarrassed to call the cops** — Wikipedia ("too ashamed and embarrassed to inform the French police"), Mental Floss, Smithsonian ("chose not to report").
- **"came back, and reportedly sold it... AGAIN"** — HEDGED on purpose: Mental Floss says he "sold the Eiffel Tower again" and the mark then went to the police; Wikipedia and Smithsonian say he returned and tried again, but the police were tipped off (or he suspected it) before the deal closed, and he fled to the US. Hence "reportedly"; no second payment is shown.
- **Alcatraz, not for the tower, for counterfeiting** — Wikipedia (arrested 1935 for counterfeiting; 15 years on Alcatraz + 5 for his jail escape); Smithsonian ("sentenced to 20 years in Alcatraz", counterfeit "Lustig money" US bills). The "worried the US Treasury" line from the PDF was cut (no source names the Treasury; Smithsonian attributes the worry to the Secret Service).
