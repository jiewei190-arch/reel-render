## Description
In the 1800s, British-ruled India ran a customs line about 2,500 miles long to stop salt smuggling and collect the salt tax. Hundreds of miles of it were a living hedge of thorny bushes up to 12 feet high, and it was compared to the Great Wall of China. At its 1872 peak, more than 14,000 men worked on the line. It was abandoned in 1879 and so thoroughly forgotten that standard histories left it out. In the 1990s, Roy Moxham, a conservator at a London library, made three trips to India to find it. All he found was a small raised embankment, which may be all that remains.
