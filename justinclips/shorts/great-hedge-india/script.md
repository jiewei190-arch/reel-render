# The Great Hedge of India — script

**EMOTION:** WTF
**LINE 1:** "Britain built a 2,500-mile barrier across India. Then forgot it."  *(source fix, see below)*
**CAPTION (top, 0-3.5 s):** "a 2,500-mile barrier / then FORGOTTEN"  *(source fix)*
**Frame 1:** an endless thorn hedge to the horizon, a British officer on horseback tiny beside it, camera already flying along it.
**Target:** ~42 s

## Narration (exact VO)
1. Britain built a 2,500-mile barrier across India. Then forgot it.
2. British-ruled India, the 1800s. The goal? Taxing salt.
3. To stop salt smugglers, they built a customs line about 2,500 miles long.
4. Hundreds of miles of it was a living hedge of thorny bushes, up to twelve feet high.
5. People compared it to the Great Wall of China.
6. At its peak, in 1872, more than fourteen thousand men guarded it.
7. But here's the twist...
8. In 1879, it was abandoned. And it was forgotten so completely, standard histories skipped it.
9. In the 1990s, a London library conservator made three trips to India just to find it.
10. What he found: one small raised embankment. It may be all that's left.

## Source fixes (builder, checked against Wikipedia "Inland Customs Line", Nov 20)
- Line 1 + top caption: the ~2,500 mi was the whole customs line; the thorn hedge was only part of it (at least ~800 mi of
  live hedge, the rest dry hedge, stone wall, ditches and rivers). "2,500-mile wall of thorns" -> "2,500-mile barrier across India";
  caption "2,500 miles of thorns" -> "a 2,500-mile barrier / then FORGOTTEN". Line 4 keeps the thorns ("hundreds of miles of it").
- Line 9: Roy Moxham was a conservator at the University of London library, not a librarian -> "a London library conservator".
- All other lines match the source (more than 2,500 mi; up to 12 ft; Great Wall comparison; 14,188 staff in 1872; abandoned
  1 April 1879; standard histories neglected it; three trips to India; a small raised embankment in Etawah that "may be all
  that remains").

## Tone / accuracy guard
- The salt tax hurt ordinary people: no jokes about the guards or villagers; the WTF is the scale and the forgetting.
- Respectful depiction of Indian villagers/workers; no caricature.
