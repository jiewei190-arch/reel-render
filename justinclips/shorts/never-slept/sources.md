# Sources: Randy Gardner's 11 days awake

| Claim in the Short | Source |
|---|---|
| Randy Gardner, 17, stayed awake 11 days (264 hours), Dec 1963 - Jan 1964, for a science fair project | NPR (2024): https://www.npr.org/2024/01/28/1227217274/sleep-deprivation-record ; Wikipedia, "Randy Gardner sleep deprivation experiment" ("the then 17-year-old Gardner stayed awake for 11 days and 25 minutes (264.4 hours)"): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randy_Gardner_sleep_deprivation_experiment |
| He went for the world record | Wikipedia (the record attempt; "Gardner's record was then broken repeatedly until 1997") |
| Moodiness, trouble concentrating, memory problems, hallucinations | Wikipedia (Navy researcher John Ross "reported serious cognitive and behavioral changes. These included moodiness, problems with concentration and short-term memory, paranoia, and hallucinations"); NPR (2017): https://www.npr.org/2017/12/27/573739653/the-haunting-effects-of-going-days-without-sleep |
| Friends kept him awake (in shifts) | NPR (2024): "His two friends would take turns monitoring his mental and physical reaction times as well as making sure Gardner didn't fall asleep." |
| Day 10: beat Stanford sleep researcher William Dement at pinball | NPR (2024): Dement played "games of pinball with Gardner"; "Gardner actually won"; Wikipedia (Dement "stated that on the tenth day of the experiment, Gardner had been ... able to beat Dement at pinball") |
| Then slept almost 15 hours | Wikipedia ("Gardner slept for 14 hours and 46 minutes"); NPR (2024) rounds it to "14 hours" |
| Guinness no longer accepts the record: too dangerous | Wikipedia ("1997, when Guinness World Records ceased accepting new attempts for safety reasons") |

Softened / avoided: no day-by-day symptom claims beyond the order shown; no claim about lasting harm or its absence (Gardner later
reported insomnia he linked to the stunt; Dement reported no lasting damage: sources differ, so the Short says neither); no medical advice,
only "Don't try this." The coat-rack "eyes" are a cartoon stand-in for "seeing things that aren't there", not a specific reported hallucination.
NPR (2024) was re-read on Nov 11 2026 (it also confirms the science fair: the trio "won first place at the San Diego science fair", and the San Diego setting).
