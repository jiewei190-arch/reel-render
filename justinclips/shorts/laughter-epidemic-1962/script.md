## Narration (exact VO, as voiced; source-checked Nov 20 against Wikipedia oldid 1381363546)
1. In 1962, laughing shut down 14 schools.
2. January thirtieth. A girls' boarding school in Kashasha, in what is now Tanzania.
3. Three students started laughing... and couldn't stop.
4. It spread to 95 of the school's 159 students. Some attacks lasted hours. Some lasted days.
5. In March, the school had to close.
6. But it didn't stop there...
7. It spread to a village where several of the girls lived. 217 people were affected.
8. Then more villages. More schools. About 1,000 people in total. 14 schools shut down.
9. It wasn't funny. The laughing came with crying, fainting and pain. Doctors called it mass hysteria.
10. It took about 18 months to finally fade away.

### Source fixes (builder, before voicing)

- Line 7: **"a village fifty-five miles away"** → "a village where several of the girls lived". The source says Nshamba is
  "55 miles west of Bukoba", not 55 miles from the school; "where several of the girls lived" is the source's wording.
  "Two hundred and seventeen" kept (217, "in April and May 1962").
- Line 9: **"No drugs. No gas. No poison."** removed: no source mentions drugs, gas, poison or ruling them out. Replaced by

  with crying, fainting and pain." "Doctors called it mass hysteria" kept: Wikipedia calls it an outbreak of mass hysteria,
  citing Kagwa (1964, East African Medical Journal) and Rankin & Philip (1963, Central African Journal of Medicine);
  on screen it is labelled "(mass psychogenic illness)" too.
- Unchanged and verified: 30 Jan 1962, girls' mission boarding school in Kashasha; three girls; 95 of 159; a few hours to
  16 days ("UP TO 16 DAYS" on screen); closed March 1962; 14 schools, ~1,000 people; died off 18 months after it started.
- TTS only: "Kashasha" is spelled "Kah-shasha" in tts_script.md so the first vowel is clear; captions say "Kashasha".
