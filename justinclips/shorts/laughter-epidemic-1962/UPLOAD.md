## Description
On January 30, 1962, three girls at a boarding school in Kashasha, Tanganyika (now Tanzania), started laughing and couldn't stop. It spread to 95 of the school's 159 pupils, the school had to close in March, and then it reached a nearby village where 217 people were affected. In the end about 1,000 people were affected and 14 schools were shut down. It wasn't funny: the laughing came with crying, fainting and pain. It is described as mass hysteria (mass psychogenic illness), and it faded out by around March 1963, about 14 months after it began (sources range from 6 to 18 months).
