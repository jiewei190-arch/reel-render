# Sources: "Sharks Are Older Than Trees. Seriously."

Checked on 2026-11-14 against the owner's listed sources (Natural History Museum, Live Science, IFLScience) plus Discover Wildlife / Britannica.

| Claim in the Short | What the source says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Sharks have been around for about 450 million years | NHM: "The earliest fossil evidence for sharks or their ancestors are a few scales dating to 450 million years ago" (scientists still debate whether these were true sharks). Discover Wildlife: "As a group, sharks emerged roughly 450 million years ago in the Late Ordovician". | https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/shark-evolution-a-450-million-year-timeline.html · https://www.discoverwildlife.com/prehistoric-life/are-sharks-older-than-trees |
| Sharks are older than trees | NHM: sharks appeared "in the fossil record before trees even existed". Live Science: yes for the shark lineage (depends on how "shark" is defined; modern shark groups are younger). | NHM (above) · https://www.livescience.com/animals/sharks/are-sharks-really-older-than-trees |
| Trees: only 350 to 390 million years | Live Science: the first trees evolved in the Middle Devonian, ~380-390 million years ago (Archaeopteris, 385-million-year-old fossil forest near Cairo, New York). Discover Wildlife: trees "first appeared 385 million years ago". The owner's 350-390 range brackets these figures (definitions of "tree" vary). | Live Science (above) · Discover Wildlife (above) · IFLScience, "Sharks Are 450 Million Years Old, Meaning They Were Here Before Trees" (cited by the owner; page not reachable from the cloud) |
| 60 million years before the first tree | 450 - ~390 = ~60 million years (both figures above). | derived |
| Older than dinosaurs by about 200 million years | NHM: the first dinosaurs date to about 245 million years ago (Triassic); 450 - 245 ≈ 205. | https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/when-did-dinosaurs-live.html |
| Older than the Atlantic Ocean | Discover Wildlife: sharks are "older than ... the Atlantic Ocean". The Atlantic began to open as Pangaea split, ~180 million years ago (central North Atlantic, Early Jurassic). On-screen tag: "PANGAEA SPLITS: ~180M YEARS AGO". | Discover Wildlife (above) · https://www.britannica.com/place/Pangea · https://theconversation.com/the-atlantic-the-driving-force-behind-ocean-circulation-and-our-taste-for-cod-146534 |
| Survived all five mass extinctions, including the one that killed the dinosaurs | NHM: "they have survived five mass extinction events, including" the asteroid strike; "There's no single reason sharks survived all five major extinction events"; the end-Cretaceous asteroid "killed off many of the largest species of shark" but sharks persisted. | NHM timeline (above) |

Not used (owner rule): "older than Saturn's rings".
Visual liberties (cartoon, not claims): the shark at a birthday party, a video-game HUD, a sapling with a face. No real photos, nobody hurt; the dinosaur is never shown near the asteroid.
