# Sources — What if the Moon vanished tonight?

Every claim was checked against Wikipedia plus at least one more reliable source (pages fetched 2026-11-27). This is a
**thought experiment** (said in line 2); it follows NASA's tides explainer and a Popular Science explainer built on interviews
with a NASA JPL Moon scientist (Matt Siegler) and a University of Washington ecologist.

| Line | Claim (as spoken) | Wikipedia | Second source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 6 | Without the Moon, tides would shrink to about a third | Tide: the Sun's tide is "46% of that of the Moon" -> Sun alone = 0.46 / 1.46 ≈ 0.32 of today's combined tide | Popular Science (NASA JPL's Siegler): "about one third of their current fluctuation" |
| 3 | Nights get darker, like a new moon every night | Moon: "the brightest celestial object in Earth's night sky" | Popular Science: "nighttime would be significantly darker" |
| 4 | The Moon does most of the pulling | Tide (Sun 46% of the Moon) | NASA Science, Tides: the Sun has "a little less than half of the Moon's tide-generating force" |
| 5 | The Sun makes tides too, but pulls less than half as hard | Tide: 46% | NASA Science, Tides (same line) |
| 7 | Animals would get confused | — (general) | Popular Science: "The moon's sudden absence would also confuse animals" |
| 7 | Some dung beetles steer by moonlight | Dung beetle: Scarabaeus zambesianus "navigates by polarization patterns in moonlight, the first animal known to do so" | Nature (Dacke et al. 2003), "Insect orientation to polarized moonlight" |
| 8 | They'd switch to steering by the Milky Way | Dung beetle: "can also navigate when only the Milky Way or clusters of bright stars are visible" | (Wikipedia cites Dacke et al. 2013, Current Biology) — the line is framed as "would have to switch to", i.e. what they already do on moonless nights |
| 9 | Over a few hundred thousand years, Earth's tilt could wobble | Axial tilt: "Earth was stabilized by the Moon" | Laskar & Robutel 1993, Nature: "Without the Moon ... the obliquity of the Earth would suffer very large chaotic variations"; Popular Science (Siegler): tilt "would probably shift drastically over time" ... "in just a few hundred thousand years" |
| 10 | Mars has no big moon; its tilt swings by up to 60 degrees | Axial tilt: Mars "varies as much as 0° to 60° over some millions of years" | Laskar & Robutel 1993: Mars "is still in a large chaotic zone, ranging from 0° to 60°" |

Caveat noted (not contradicted by the video): later work (reported by phys.org, 2015: https://phys.org/news/2015-01-earth-moon-critical-life.html) argues Earth's
tilt would vary less than Laskar's original estimate. The script therefore says the tilt "could wobble" (Siegler's timescale), not how far, and leaves out seasons and ice ages.

## Quotes used

**Wikipedia — Tide** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tide
> "the tidal force of the Sun is less than that of the Moon ... it is only 46% as large as the lunar"
> "The Sun similarly causes tides, of which the theoretical amplitude is about 25 cm (46% of that of the Moon)"

**NASA Science — Tides** — https://science.nasa.gov/moon/tides/
> "The Sun ― with about 27 million times the mass of the Moon ― ... about 390 times farther away than the Moon, which gives it a
> little less than half of the Moon's tide-generating force."

**Popular Science — What would happen if the moon suddenly disappeared?** — https://www.popsci.com/what-would-happen-if-moon-suddenly-disappeared
> "Right away, we would notice that 'nighttime' would be significantly darker. The moon's surface reflects the sun's light,
> brightening our night sky."
> "The moon's sudden absence would also confuse animals."
> "Without the moon, tides would rise and fall at a much slower rate, about one third of their current fluctuation, says Matt
> Siegler, a research scientist at the NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory"
> "Without the moon's gravity holding the Earth in place, the tilt of our home planet's axis would probably shift drastically over
> time. Earth could go from no tilt with virtually no seasons, to a drastic tilt with extreme seasonal weather changes and ice
> ages in just a few hundred thousand years, Siegler adds. He points to Mars as an example, with its extreme climate variations as the tilt of its axis changes dramatically. It
> has no large, stabilizing moon to stop it."

**Wikipedia — Moon** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon
> "The Moon is the brightest celestial object in Earth's night sky"

**Wikipedia — Dung beetle** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dung_beetle
> "The African Scarabaeus zambesianus navigates by polarization patterns in moonlight, the first animal known to do so."
> "Dung beetles can also navigate when only the Milky Way or clusters of bright stars are visible"

**Nature — Dacke et al. (2003), Insect orientation to polarized moonlight** — https://www.nature.com/articles/424033a
> "Here we show that an African dung beetle, Scarabaeus zambesianus, uses the polarization of a moonlit sky to orientate itself
> so that it can move along a straight line."

**Wikipedia — Axial tilt** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt
> "Earth was stabilized by the Moon ... Mars's obliquity is quite variable over millions of years and may be in a chaotic state;
> it varies as much as 0° to 60° over some millions of years"

**Laskar & Robutel (1993), "The chaotic obliquity of the planets", Nature 361** — https://perso.imcce.fr/jacques-laskar/pdf/Laskar_Robutel_1993.pdf
> "Without the Moon, for a spin period ranging from about 12 h to 48 h, the obliquity of the Earth would suffer very large chaotic variations."
> "the Earth may have been stabilized by the capture of the Moon, and Mars is still in a large chaotic zone, ranging from 0° to 60°."

## What the video does NOT claim
- No "life would end" / "we'd all die" lines (Popular Science's headline mood); no extreme-weather or ice-age claims.
- No "days get longer/shorter" claim (not needed, and timescale-dependent).
- Owls, lions and rodents from the UW study are left out (no second source checked); the dung beetle carries the animal beat.
