# Sources — Wrong Way Corrigan

Checked 28 Nov 2026. Each claim: Wikipedia + one more source, quoted. [W] = Wikipedia, [H] = HistoryNet.

- [W] Wikipedia, "Douglas Corrigan" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Corrigan
- [H] HistoryNet, "The True Story of 'Wrong-Way' Corrigan's Atlantic Adventure" (C. Fasolino; first published in Aviation History, 2001) — https://www.historynet.com/the-adventures-of-wrong-way-corrigan.htm

| Line in the video | Quoted support |
|---|---|
| "A pilot flew to Ireland... and said he was heading for California." | [W] "he then flew from Floyd Bennett Field in Brooklyn to Ireland, although his flight plan was filed to return to Long Beach." [H] his words on landing: "I left New York yesterday morning headed for California." |
| "1938. Douglas Corrigan wanted to fly across the Atlantic. Officials said no." | [W] "He had been denied permission to make a nonstop flight from New York to Ireland." / "The application was rejected; his plane was deemed unsound for a nonstop transatlantic trip." [H] "When officials denied him permission for a transoceanic attempt, he was determined not to let red tape get in his way." |
| "His plane was a nine-year-old Curtiss Robin. No radio." | [W] a "1929 Curtiss Robin OX-5 monoplane"; contemporary quote calls it "a nine-year-old Curtiss Robin"; "He had no radio and his compass was 20 years old." [H] a "modified Curtiss Robin"; "even though his plane had no radio." |
| "Extra fuel tanks reportedly blocked his view straight ahead." | [W] "Corrigan's plane had fuel tanks mounted ahead of his position, allowing him to see only out of the sides." [H] the Robin was fitted with extra gas tanks. The view detail is Wikipedia-only → **"reportedly"**. |
| "So on July 17th, he filed a flight plan to California." | [H] took off July 17, 1938, from Floyd Bennett Field; "His flight plan was filed–New York to California, just as his license said." [W] "His logged flight plan had him returning to California on July 17." |
| "He took off from New York. California is west. He went east." | [W] flew "from Floyd Bennett Field in Brooklyn to Ireland". [H] took off from Floyd Bennett Field and landed in Dublin. (Ireland is east of New York.) |
| "28 hours later, he landed in Dublin, Ireland." | [W] "He landed at Baldonnel Aerodrome, County Dublin, on July 18, after flying 28:13 hrs." [H] "Twenty-eight hours later, he landed in Dublin and instantly became a national hero." |
| "He said he got lost in the clouds and misread his compass." | [W] the "navigational error" he claimed was "caused by heavy cloud cover that obscured landmarks and by misreading his compass in low-light conditions." [H] "I got mixed up in the clouds, and I must have flown the wrong way."; "I just made a mistake in reading the compass." Framed as **"he said"**. |
| "But here's the part... his license was suspended only until his ship got home." | [W] "his pilot's certificate was suspended for 14 days"; he returned "on the steamship Manhattan and arrived on August 4, the last day of his suspension." [H] "His pilot's license was suspended until August 4–the day the ship arrived in New York." |
| "New York threw him a ticker-tape parade." | [W] "More people attended his Broadway ticker-tape parade than had honored Lindbergh after his triumph." [H] "The next day he was given a ticker-tape parade up Broadway". |
| "And publicly, he never admitted a thing." | [W] "he never publicly admitted to having flown to Ireland intentionally." [H] there were "several reports in the late 1980s that he finally admitted" it — unconfirmed reports, so the video says only **"publicly, he never admitted"** (Wikipedia's wording), not that he never admitted it at all. |

## Dramatised (cartoon, not claimed as fact)
"Hi, California!" (a gag on his reported landing words), the sheep, the cobweb in the empty radio slot, the shrugging and head-shaking
officials, the x-ray fuel tank, the spinning compass badge, the smirk. The maps are schematic. The plane is a generic 1930s
high-wing monoplane with no markings; the ship is a generic liner (no real line's colours or name). The pilot, officials, Irish
officer and crowd are generic toons, not likenesses of real people.
