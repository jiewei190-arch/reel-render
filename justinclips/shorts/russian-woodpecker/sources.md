## Wording changes vs the card (also in STATUS.md)
- Card line 1 "…and nobody outside the USSR knew what it was" is **not used**. The article says experts and hobbyists
  suspected an over-the-horizon radar "even from the earliest reports" and NATO had a reporting name (STEEL WORK / STEEL YARD).
  What the article supports is that the signal was **unclaimed**, so the video says "the Soviet Union never claimed it".
- "over 10 megawatts" keeps the card's and the lead's wording (the body's figure is an estimate, "as high as 10 MW").
- "Some radios even came with Woodpecker Blankers" follows the article's "some communications receivers".
- The article's Chernobyl site notes conflict on which site held transmitters vs receivers; the video only says
  "near Chernobyl", which both readings support.

## Dramatised (cartoon, not claimed as fact)
The radio falling off the stump, the armchair listener, the cartoon woodpecker bird pecking a radio (the nickname made
visible), the shrugging official, the mail sacks, the cork on the bird. The tapping sound is synthesized (10 clicks a
second), not a recording of the real signal. The map is schematic.
