The Signal That Hijacked the World's Radios for 13
 Years
 TARGET EMOTION: WTF
 3-SECOND HOOK (frame 1)
 Frame 1: a giant wall of antennas in a snowy forest pulsing out sound waves that knock radios off
 tables worldwide. Caption: "the Woodpecker". First line: "For 13 years, a mysterious tapping sound
 hijacked radios all over the world, and nobody outside the USSR knew what it was."

 THE VERIFIED PAYOFF
 From 1976 to 1989, a Soviet over-the-horizon radar blasted a sharp, repetitive tapping signal
 across shortwave radio at 10 taps per second and over 10 megawatts of power. Shortwave
 listeners called it the Russian Woodpecker. It disrupted broadcasts and aviation and amateur radio
 worldwide, generating thousands of complaints, and receiver makers literally added "Woodpecker
 Blankers" to block it. One of the radar arrays still stands, abandoned, next to Chernobyl.
