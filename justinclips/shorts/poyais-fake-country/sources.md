# Sources: Poyais, the country that didn't exist (1820s)

Checked Nov 16 2026 (WebSearch + WebFetch).

## Every claim, sourced
- **Gregor MacGregor, a Scottish soldier, sold land and bonds in "Poyais", an invented Central American country, from 1821-22**: Wikipedia, "Gregor MacGregor": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_MacGregor ("...tried from 1821 to 1837 to draw British and French investors and settlers to 'Poyais', a fictional Central American territory over which he claimed to rule as 'Cazique'"). Global Financial Data, "The Fraud of the Prince of Poyais on the London Stock Exchange": https://globalfinancialdata.com/the-fraud-of-the-prince-of-poyais-on-london-stock-exchange ; Amusing Planet, "The Poyais Scam: A Nation That Never Was" (2025): https://www.amusingplanet.com/2025/05/the-poyais-scam-nation-that-never-was.html . The VO "a country that didn't exist" = the country he described (towns, a capital, a government) did not exist; the coast it was placed on is real.
- **In 1822 he "told London he ruled" Poyais**: Wikipedia (he arrived in London in 1821-22 as "Cazique of Poyais"; the 1822 Poyais loan). On screen: '"ruler" of Poyais' (in quotes).
- **A guidebook**: "Sketch of the Mosquito Shore, Including the Territory of Poyais" (Edinburgh: William Blackwood, 1822), credited to "Captain Thomas Strangeways", a promotional book for the scheme (Wikipedia, references section). The on-screen card shows its title.
- **Its own money**: Wikipedia and Amusing Planet describe Bank of Poyais banknotes printed for the settlers. The on-screen note is an original toon design.
- **Government bonds; investors bought about £200,000**: Wikipedia (a £200,000 Poyais loan underwritten in October 1822); Global Financial Data (2,000 bonds of £100 offered on 23 Oct 1822 → £200,000). HEDGED: the VO says "about 200,000 pounds" and the counter shows "~£200,000" (the bonds were sold below face value, so cash raised was less).
- **About 250 settlers sailed there (Honduras Packet and Kennersley Castle, 1822-23)**: Wikipedia ("About 250 emigrated to MacGregor's invented country in 1822–1823"). The VO says "about 250".
- **They found jungle, no towns, no capital**: Wikipedia (the settlers found untouched jungle, not the developed country he advertised; no capital "St Joseph"). Amusing Planet.
- **"It went very badly."**: TONE GUARD. Wikipedia: "more than half of them died"; fewer than 50 returned to Britain. The VO deliberately keeps this to "It went very badly" over an empty, rainy shore with no people, as the brief asks. The description states it plainly once.
- **Tried for fraud in France and acquitted (1826)**: Wikipedia ("A French court tried MacGregor and three others for fraud in 1826 ... but convicted only one of his associates. Acquitted, MacGregor..."). The VO says "later tried for fraud in France... and acquitted"; the badge says "FRANCE, 1826".

## Kept hedges
- "about" on the £200,000 and on the 250 settlers.
- The deaths are stated only in the description, and only as the sources give them ("more than half").

## Dramatised (not claimed as fact)
The podium sale with a map on an easel and banknotes raining, the strut down a London street, the crowd grabbing bonds, the coin pile and counter, the ship's waving settlers, the crossed-off checklist, the courtroom staging (judge, dock, gavel). MacGregor is an original gaudy-uniform toon, not a likeness of his portrait.
