## Description
In 1875, one Rocky Mountain locust swarm was estimated at about 198,000 square miles, bigger than California, with around 12.5 trillion insects. Guinness calls it the greatest concentration of animals ever recorded. Less than 30 years later, the species was gone. The last specimen was collected in 1904. One theory (entomologist Jeffrey Lockwood): settlers plowed, irrigated and grazed cattle on the river-valley breeding grounds in the Rockies and probably destroyed the eggs. Remains are still found frozen in Grasshopper Glacier, Montana.
