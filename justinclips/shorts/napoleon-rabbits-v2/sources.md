# Sources: napoleon-rabbits-v2 (Napoleon Was Attacked by Rabbits)
Owner's kit (funny-kits-nov15_owner.pdf) + re-checked Nov 15 2026 (WebSearch). The whole story rests on one memoir (Gen. Paul Thiébault, published posthumously in the 1890s), so it is told as an account, with every hedge kept.

|---|---|---|
| "Napoleon once lost a battle. To rabbits." | [Mental Floss: The time Napoleon was attacked by rabbits](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/51364/time-napoleon-was-attacked-rabbits) · [History Answers: Napoleon's battle against rabbits](https://www.historyanswers.co.uk/people-politics/napoleons-battle-against-rabbits/) · [Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/u-s-world/2023/10/4/23901896/the-battle-napoleon-bonaparte-lost-to-rabbits) | Comic framing of a hunt anecdote. |

| "A general wrote that the conqueror of conquerors... retreated." | Thiébault's memoir, quoted by Mental Floss / The Star ("forced the conqueror of conquerors... to retreat") | On-screen card paraphrases the quote and keeps "the conqueror of conquerors" verbatim. Historians are cautious: written decades later by a general who disliked Berthier. |
| "He fled to his carriage. Some reportedly hopped in after him." | Mental Floss · History Answers · [The Vintage News](https://www.thevintagenews.com/2018/05/18/napoleon-and-the-battle-of-the-rabbits/) | The carriage detail is in later retellings, not confirmed in a primary source: "reportedly" kept. |

Nobody hurt: no guns are shown, and the rabbits only want food. Napoleon and his officers are toon caricatures.
