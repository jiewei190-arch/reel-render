# Sources — Struck by Lightning 7 Times (Roy Sullivan)

Re-verified Nov 15 2026 (WebSearch + Wikipedia article source) against the owner's PDF sources (Guinness World Records, Wikipedia, Washington Post).

## Every claim, sourced
- **Roy Sullivan, a Shenandoah National Park ranger, was struck by lightning seven times between 1942 and 1977 and survived them all** — Wikipedia, "Roy Sullivan": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Sullivan · Guinness World Records, "Most lightning strikes survived": https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/most-lightning-strikes-survived · Guinness World Records news (2023), "Incredible story of man who survived being struck by lightning SEVEN times": https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2023/1/incredible-story-of-man-who-survived-being-struck-by-lightning-seven-times-733932 · The Weather Network, "Roy Sullivan was struck by lightning seven times": https://www.theweathernetwork.com/en/news/weather/severe/this-day-in-weather-history-june-25-1977-human-lightning
- **Ranger in Shenandoah National Park (from 1936)** — Wikipedia (above).
- **July 1969: struck while driving, knocked unconscious; "the uncontrolled truck kept moving until it stopped near a cliff edge"** — Wikipedia (above, strike 2). The Short shows the truck stopping at the edge.
- **July 1970: struck in his front yard (via a nearby power transformer)** — Wikipedia (above, strike 3).
- **Lightning set his hair on fire** — Wikipedia: 1969 ("set his hair on fire"), spring 1972 (ranger station), 1976, and June 1977. No single year is named on screen.
- **June 25, 1977: struck while fishing in a freshwater pool** — Wikipedia (above, strike 7) · The Weather Network (above).
- **"He said a storm cloud chased him."** — his own claim. Wikipedia, strike 5 (Aug 7, 1973): "the cloud, he said later, seemed to be following him"; strike 6 (1976): he "thought that it was following him". The owner's brief says "a bolt chased him"; the sources say a *cloud* followed him, so the VO says "storm cloud" and keeps "He said" (+ a "HE SAID" tag on screen).
- **"He still holds the Guinness World Record."** — Guinness World Records record page "Most lightning strikes survived" (above) still lists Roy C. Sullivan; Wikipedia: "recognized by Guinness World Records as the person struck by lightning more recorded times than any other human".
- **"The odds? By one estimate, about 1 in 10 septillion."** — an extrapolation, not a statistic. Published back-of-envelope figures multiply single-strike odds as if the strikes were independent and land between about 1 in 2.2 septillion (lifetime odds 1 in 3,000, per National Geographic, ^7) and about 1 in 10^28 (1 in 10,000 ^7): see Mental Floss, "Meet the man struck by lightning 7 times": https://www.mentalfloss.com/science/weather/meet-man-struck-lightning-7-times · National Parks Traveler (on why the multiplication is misleading): https://www.nationalparkstraveler.org/2008/07/shenandoah-national-park-ranger-roy-sullivan-set-world-record-being-hit-lightning · The Weather Network (above: "the mathematical odds don't really apply" to him). The owner's figure "about 1 in 10 septillion" sits inside that range; the VO keeps "about" and adds "By one estimate", and the card shows "about" + "BY ONE ESTIMATE".

## Kept hedges
- "By one estimate, about 1 in 10 septillion" (extrapolation).
- "He said a storm cloud chased him" (his claim).
- Note (not in the VO): Wikipedia says the strikes were "claimed" and documented by superintendent R. Taylor Hoskins, who was not present at any of them. Guinness accepts the record; the VO states only the record.
