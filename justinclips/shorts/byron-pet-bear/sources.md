# Sources: Lord Byron's college bear (Cambridge, around 1807)

Checked Nov 16 2026 (WebSearch + WebFetch). The brief's hook angle was verified, and the hedges are kept in the VO.

## Every claim, sourced
- **Lord Byron (the poet) was a student at Trinity College, Cambridge, from 1805**: Wikipedia, "Lord Byron": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Byron . The VO says "around 1807", the year of the letter below.
- **The college reportedly didn't allow dogs, so he kept a tame bear (the rules didn't mention bears)**: Far Out Magazine, "The strange tale of Lord Byron's pet bear" (Jan 2025): https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/lord-byron-and-his-pet-bear/ ("to rebel against the rule that said students couldn't keep dogs in the college"); Mental Floss, "Lord Byron facts": https://www.mentalfloss.com/literature/authors/lord-byron-facts ; Popular Science, "Weird pets in history": https://www.popsci.com/environment/weird-pets-history/ ; The Wordsworth Trust, "Bears, badgers and Boatswain: Lord Byron and his animals": https://wordsworth.org.uk/blog/2015/04/21/bears-badgers-and-boatswain-lord-byron-and-his-animals/ . HEDGED: the dog rule and the "no rule against bears" loophole come from how the story is usually told, not from a statute we could read. So the VO says "reportedly had a rule", and "the rules said nothing about bears" is the popular account.
- **"I have got a new friend, the finest in the world, a tame bear"**: Byron's letter to Elizabeth Pigot, Trinity College, 26 October 1807, quoted by Far Out (above) and in Byron's collected letters. The VO paraphrases it ("He wrote to a friend that it was the finest in the world"), and the on-screen card quotes it word for word.
- **Asked what he meant to do with the bear, he said "he should sit for a fellowship"**: the same letter (Far Out quotes "he should sit for a fellowship"). A fellowship = a post as a Fellow (a college teacher/member of the governing body). The on-screen gloss says "(a fellow = a college teacher)".

## Kept hedges
- "reportedly" on the no-dogs rule.
- "around 1807" (he was at Trinity 1805-1808; the letter is from 1807).

## Dramatised (comedy, not claimed as fact)
The bear in a gown and mortarboard, walking it on a ribbon lead through the quad, the NO DOGS sign, the porter, the two dons and the bear at the high table with a FELLOW badge. Popular Science notes the bear was most likely kept in a stable rather than in his rooms, so the video never claims where it lived. Byron, the porter and the dons are original toon beans, not likenesses. Not mentioned (unclear in the sources): the dog's name, where the bear came from, what happened to it later.
