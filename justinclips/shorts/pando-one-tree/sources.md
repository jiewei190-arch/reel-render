# Sources: Pando, the forest that is ONE tree
Checked 2026-11-27 against Wikipedia plus Utah State University (news release + the PLOS ONE paper it covers) and Utah's state history site (all fetched). Lines quoted as used.

## Wikipedia
- **Pando (tree)** — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pando_(tree)
  - "Pando ... is the name of a quaking aspen (Populus tremuloides) clone located in Sevier County, Utah, United States, in the Fishlake National Forest." (line 2)
  - "A male clonal organism, Pando has an estimated 47,000 stems (ramets) that appear to be individual trees but are genetically identical parts of a single tree connected by a root system" (lines 1, 2, 3)
  - "it has been estimated that Pando ... weighs an estimated 6,000 metric tons (6,600 short tons) or 13.2 million pounds, and features an estimated 47,000 stems" (lines 3, 4)
  - "As a multi-stem tree, Pando is the world's largest tree by measures of weight, landmass and species." (line 5)
  - "Many news sources list Pando's age as 80,000 years, but this claim derives from a now-removed National Park Service web page, which redacted that claim in 2023 and was inconsistent with the Forest Service's post ice-age estimate." (line 6)
  - "...conservatively between 9,000 and 16,000 years old by a 2024 estimate." / "Ages greater than approximately 16,000 years therefore require Pando to have survived climate conditions during the Pinedale glaciation, something that appears unlikely" (line 7)
  - "Today, approximately 80% of the acres covered by Pando is protected by 8-foot wildlife control fences, which work to control populations of mule deer ... and elk ... who can destroy new growth before it can mature" (lines 9, 10)

## Utah State University
- **"First Comprehensive Assessment of Pando Aspen Clone Reveals Critical Threats"**, USU Today, 22 Oct 2018 — https://www.usu.edu/today/story/first-comprehensive-assessment-of-pando-aspen-clone-reveals-critical-threats
  - "Pando is widely considered the world's largest single organism weighing in at an estimated 13 million lbs. (5.9 million kg). ... the clonal colony consists of more than 47,000 genetically identical above-ground stems or 'ramets' originating from a single underground parent clone." (lines 2, 3, 4, 5)
  - "Mule deer chronically browse off young aspen stems leading to total loss of 'next generation' forests. This forest, made up almost entirely of old trees, has a bleak future" (lines 8, 9)
  - "the majority of Pando ... is diminishing by attrition" (line 8, "It's shrinking")
  - "mule deer appear to be finding ways to enter through weak points in the fence or by jumping over the eight-foot barrier. While Pando has likely existed for thousands of years—we have no method of firmly fixing its' age" (lines 7, 10)
- **Rogers & McAvoy, "Mule deer impede Pando's recovery"**, PLOS ONE, 17 Oct 2018 — https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0203619
  - "The 'Pando' clone is 43 ha and estimated to contain 47,000 genetically identical aspen ramets." (line 3)
  - "a gross estimate of a post-glaciation origin seems plausible, meaning the clone is likely thousands of years old though not older than 14,000 years." (line 7)
  - "The Pando aspen clone is putatively the largest known organism on earth in terms of dry-weight mass" (line 5)
- **Utah Division of State History, "The Pando Clone"** — https://ilovehistory.utah.gov/the-pando-clone
  - "Pando—Latin meaning I spread—is actually a single tree. It is a 'forest of one' that sprouts over 40,000 stems through an expansive root system." (lines 1, 2)
  - "Some scientists and forest managers believe that the poor health of the Pando is due in large part to the browsing of deer, elk, and cattle. These animals eat the young shoots trying to grow." (line 9)

## Wording notes
- Weight: "about 6,000 tonnes" = Wikipedia's 6,000 metric tons; USU gives 13 million lbs (5.9 million kg). The insert card shows "≈ 13 million pounds".
- "That MAY make it the heaviest living thing": sources say "widely considered" (USU) / "putatively" (PLOS), so the line is hedged and the on-screen text ends in "?".
- Age: the old 80,000-year figure is called out as too old (Wikipedia). The spoken range "maybe 9,000 to 16,000" is Wikipedia's 2024 estimate; PLOS caps it at 14,000 and USU says there is no method of firmly fixing its age, so the card says "nobody knows exactly".
- Fences: USU (2018) found about half of Pando unfenced; Wikipedia (current) says ~80% is now fenced after the 2024 Pando Protection Plan work, hence "Fences NOW protect most of it".
- Cattle also browse (Utah history page); the line names deer and elk, as the brief and USU/Wikipedia do. No cattle claim is made.
