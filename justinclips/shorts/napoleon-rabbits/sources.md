# Sources: Napoleon Was Defeated by Rabbits (napoleon-rabbits)
Checked Nov 9 2026. **Legend flag:** the whole story comes from one source, the memoirs of General Paul Thiébault
(French edition 1895, after his death), so Line 1 says "According to one general" and the carriage detail says "Reportedly".

| Claim in the script | Source (quote) |
|---|---|
| According to one general, rabbits attacked Napoleon | Mental Floss: "General Paul Charles ... Thiébault described the scene in his memoirs"; Deseret News: "Baron Thiébault, a general in Napoleon's army, described the event in his memoir." |
| Hundreds of them / some say 3,000 | Mental Floss: "Some say Berthier took in hundreds of bunnies, while others claim he collected as many as 3000." (Deseret News says 1,000; Ripley's says over 3,000.) |
| 1807, just after a peace treaty | Mental Floss: "most agree it happened in July 1807. Napoleon had recently signed the Treaties of Tilsit"; Deseret News: "After the peace treaty of Tilsit in July 1807 was signed" |
| To celebrate, his chief of staff set up a rabbit hunt | Mental Floss: "in celebration, a rabbit hunt was organized by Napoleon's chief of staff, Alexandre Berthier"; Deseret News: Berthier "organized the hunt" |
| The rabbits charged straight at Napoleon | Mental Floss: "they bounded toward Napoleon and his men"; Deseret News: "instead of dashing away as expected, they approached the hunters looking for food" |
| They were tame farm rabbits; they thought he was bringing dinner | Mental Floss: "his emissary had bought tame rabbits from local farmers" and "they saw him as a waiter bringing out the day's food"; Ripley's: "believed it was feeding time" |
| He swats them with his riding crop; the coachmen crack their whips | Mental Floss: "Napoleon tried shooing them with his riding crop, as his men grabbed sticks" and "The coachmen cracked their bullwhips to scare the siege." |
| Napoleon runs for his carriage | Mental Floss: "Napoleon fled to his carriage"; Ripley's: "he retreated and ran to his imperial coach for safety." |
| Reportedly, some jump in after him | Mental Floss: "some reportedly leapt into the carriage"; Ripley's: "Some even made their way into his horse-drawn carriage." (Deseret News says some tried and did not succeed, hence "Reportedly".) |
| "Defeated by bunnies" | Deseret News quotes Thiébault: the rabbits "forced the conqueror of conquerors, fairly exhausted, to retreat". |
