# Sources: whisky-war ("Canada and Denmark Fought a War With Whisky")
Pages read Nov 11 2026. CBC (listed in the topic bank) returned HTTP 403 to the fetcher, so every claim below rests on the two pages that loaded.

- **LOC** = Library of Congress Law Blog, "The Hans Island Peace Agreement Between Canada, Denmark, and Greenland" (June 2022): https://blogs.loc.gov/law/2022/06/the-hans-island-peace-agreement-between-canada-denmark-and-greenland/
- **CG** = Canadian Geographic, "Canada, Denmark end 50-year 'whiskey war' over Hans Island": https://canadiangeographic.ca/articles/canada-denmark-end-50-year-whiskey-war-over-hans-island/

| Line in the Short | Source and what it says |
|---|---|
| "Canada and Denmark fought a war with whisky." | CG and LOC both call it the "Whisky/Whiskey War". Flags + bottles of liquor, no fighting (CG: "took turns staking their claim ... by planting flags and bottles of liquor"). |
| "For almost 50 years." | 1973 treaty to the June 14 2022 agreement = 49 years (computed). CG headline: "50-year whiskey war". |
| "Hans Island ... between Canada and Greenland." | CG: "a small uninhabited island in the Nares Strait between Ellesmere Island and northern Greenland." |
| "Just over one square kilometer" | CG: 1.2 km²; LOC (quoting Prof. Michael Byers): 1.3 km². Both = "just over one". |
| "In 1973, the two countries drew a border... and left the rock out." | LOC: "The 1973 treaty deliberately left the border undefined through tiny, uninhabited Hans Island." |
| "In 1984, Denmark's minister for Greenland left a bottle on it. 'Welcome to the Danish island.'" | LOC: the tradition "appears to have evolved" after "Denmark's minister for Greenland, Tom Høyem, first left a bottle of cognac in 1984, welcoming visitors to the Danish Island." (The script does not name the drink; the on-screen note paraphrases LOC's wording.) |
| "Then Canadian soldiers showed up. With a flag. And Canadian whisky." | LOC: both militaries "occasionally sent troops to the island, raised the Maple Leaf or the Dannebrog flag, and left a bottle of Canadian Club whiskey or aquavit." |
| "Nobody fired a shot. They just took turns. Plant a flag, leave a bottle. For decades." | CG: "beginning in the 1980s, Canada and Denmark took turns staking their claim to the island by planting flags and bottles of liquor." CG quotes Canada's foreign minister: "the friendliest of all wars." |
| "In 2022, they finally made peace. They split the island in two..." | CG: the agreement "divides the 1.2-square-kilometre island roughly in half, creating a land border between Canada and Denmark." LOC: divided "along a natural ridge", roughly 60 % to Denmark. The Short says "in two" (not "in half") because the sources differ on the exact split. |
| "...and swapped bottles." | LOC: the ministers "exchanged bottles of Canadian maple whiskey Sortilège and Danish bitter Gammel Dansk." CG: they "exchanged bottles of alcohol for the last time." |

No legend parts. Visual notes: original bean characters (not portraits of the real ministers); bottles are shown, nobody drinks; the HERO (cast) cameo asks "How is this a war?".
