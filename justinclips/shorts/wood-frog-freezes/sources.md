# Sources: The wood frog freezes solid every winter, then thaws back to life

Every claim is checked against Wikipedia plus at least one more reliable source (university, conservation org, museum/encyclopedia).
Quotes are copied from the pages (fetched Nov 27, 2026).

## Notes
- "Freezes solid" (line 1) is the popular phrasing (Audubon, NWF); the measured amount is in line 3: up to about two thirds (65–70 %) of its body water.
  Other sources give lower figures for whole-body ice (Audubon "up to 60 percent of their bodies", ADW "35 to 45%"); "up to" keeps line 3 at Wikipedia's limit.
- 218 days is the UAF maximum (field study near Fairbanks); Wikipedia gives 193 ± 11 days for frogs in natural hibernation. The insert card says "up to 218 days" and "about 7 months" (218 days ≈ 7.2 months; Audubon: "as long as seven months").
- "No brain activity" (Wikipedia only) was cut from line 4; "No blood flow" is in both Wikipedia ("blood circulation") and ADW.
