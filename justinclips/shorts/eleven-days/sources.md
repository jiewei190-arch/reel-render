# Sources — Britain Deleted 11 Days (1752)

Re-verified Nov 15 2026 (WebSearch) against the topic-bank sources.

## Every claim, sourced
- **Britain's (Julian) calendar had drifted out of step with the seasons / the solar year; by 1752 it was 11 days off the Gregorian calendar** — timeanddate.com, "Julian-Gregorian switch": https://www.timeanddate.com/calendar/julian-gregorian-switch.html · History Today, "The Gregorian Calendar Adopted in England": https://www.historytoday.com/archive/gregorian-calendar-adopted-england
- **Parliament's Calendar (New Style) Act 1750 made Wednesday 2 September 1752 followed directly by Thursday 14 September** — Historic UK, "Give Us Our Eleven Days": https://www.historic-uk.com/HistoryUK/HistoryofBritain/Give-us-our-eleven-days · History Today (above) · Johns Hopkins Libraries blog, "The days that never were": https://blogs.library.jhu.edu/2013/09/the-days-that-never-were
- **The change also applied to the British colonies in America** — timeanddate.com (above) · Historic UK (above)
- **"Legend says people rioted for their 11 days back. Historians aren't sure."** — The "Give us our eleven days" riots are disputed. The slogan is traced to William Hogarth's 1755 election painting, and Robert Poole's study ("'Give us our eleven days!': calendar reform in eighteenth-century England", *Past & Present* 149, 1995) finds little evidence of riots: https://insight.cumbria.ac.uk/id/eprint/684/ · Britannica, "Did a Calendar Change Cause Riots in England?": https://www.britannica.com/story/did-a-calendar-change-cause-riots-in-england · IFLScience: https://www.iflscience.com/the-calendar-riots-the-myth-and-truth-of-britains-missing-11-days-75336

## Kept hedges (from TOPICS.md)
- The calendar riots are disputed: the VO says "Legend says", the riot shots carry a "LEGEND SAYS" tag in sepia, and the next line is "Historians aren't sure."

## Dramatised (comedy, not claimed as fact)
The sleeper "going to bed Sept 2 and waking up Sept 14" (the calendar dates are real; the sleeper is a gag), flying pages, the wigged speaker and gavel, the farmer, the protest signs, the historian. The weekdays printed on the skipped pages (3–13) continue the old count from Wednesday 2; those days never happened.
