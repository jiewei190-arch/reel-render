# Sources: Lake Peigneur (Nov 20, 1980)
Checked Nov 14, 2026 (WebSearch/WebFetch). Claim -> source.

- Date Nov 20, 1980; Texaco-contracted rig drilling on the lake; drill stuck at 1,228 ft; 11 barges, a tugboat, the platform and 65 acres (much of Jefferson Island) swallowed; Delcambre Canal reversed; "for a few days the tallest waterfall in Louisiana, at 164 ft"; 400-ft geysers through the mineshafts; 9 of 11 barges popped back up; all 55 miners and the 7-man rig crew escaped, no human deaths (3 dogs reported killed); 10-ft freshwater lake -> max depth 200 ft, salt water; MSHA gave no official reason.
  Wikipedia, "Lake Peigneur": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Peigneur
- Rig operated for Texaco by Wilson Brothers; the breach was at "the mine's 1,300-foot level"; out-of-court settlement ($32M to Diamond Crystal).
  UPI Archives, "Settlement reached in Jeff Island accident" (Jul 7, 1983): https://www.upi.com/Archives/1983/07/07/Settlement-reached-in-Jeff-Island-accident/4485426398400/
- First-day report: rig crew of seven abandoned the rig, miners fled, "No one was injured".
  UPI Archives, "Salt company sues over drained lake, ruined salt mine" (Nov 21, 1980): https://www.upi.com/Archives/1980/11/21/Salt-company-sues-over-drained-lake-ruined-saltmine/2980343630800/
- Wilson Brothers drilling from a floating platform; "the puncture created a powerful whirlpool that sucked down anything in its path".
  64 Parishes (Louisiana Endowment for the Humanities), "Lake Peigneur Drilling Accident": https://64parishes.org/entry/lake-peigneur-drilling-accident
- Drill "seized up at about 1,230 feet"; nine sunken barges "popped back to the surface like corks"; no official blame decided.
  Damn Interesting, "Lake Peigneur: The Swirling Vortex of Doom": https://www.damninteresting.com/lake-peigneur-the-swirling-vortex-of-doom/

Notes: some sources give the waterfall as ~150 ft (64 Parishes, Damn Interesting); the video uses Wikipedia's 164 ft and "for a few days". Miner count is 55 (Wikipedia) or ~50 (UPI); the video only says "every person got out alive". The owner's kit "Engineering Oops: The Maelstrom of Lake Peigneur" article was not found online; the sources above cover every claim.
