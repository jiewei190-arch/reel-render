# Sources: The Great Stink (London, 1858)

Checked Nov 16 2026 (WebSearch + WebFetch of Wikipedia). Quotes are verbatim.

## Every claim, sourced
- **London stank so bad, Parliament had to act / the name "The Great Stink"**: Wikipedia, "Great Stink": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Stink . It was an event "during July and August 1858 in which the hot weather exacerbated the smell of untreated human waste". London Museum, "A very smelly summer in the city": https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/great-stink-of-1858/
- **Summer 1858, a heatwave hit the Thames**: Wikipedia (section "June to August 1858"; "hot weather").
- **The Thames was full of London's sewage**: Wikipedia ("untreated human waste"; "raw effluent from the sewers remained on the banks of the river"). At the time, London's sewers emptied straight into the Thames.
- **Parliament sits right on the river**: the Palace of Westminster is on the north bank of the Thames. Wikipedia mentions "the curtains on the river side of the building".
- **They soaked the curtains with chemicals**: Wikipedia ("the curtains on the river side of the building were soaked in lime chloride to overcome the smell"). The bucket in the shot is labelled "CHLORIDE OF LIME". The on-screen "(it didn't help much)" follows popular accounts (e.g. Historic UK: https://historic-uk.com/HistoryUK/HistoryofBritain/Londons-Great-Stink/), which say the curtains did not mask the smell. The parliament kept sitting, and the smell is what pushed the bill.
- **They even talked about moving Parliament out of London**: Wikipedia ("discussions were held about possibly moving the business of government to Oxford or St Albans"). The map shows "Oxford?" and "St Albans?".
- **They passed a new sewer law in about seven weeks**: Wikipedia ("On 15 June Disraeli tabled the Metropolis Local Management Amendment Bill" ... "was passed into law on 2 August"). 15 June to 2 August is 48 days, about seven weeks. FACT FIX: some popular accounts say "18 days". The Wikipedia dates give ~7 weeks, so the VO says "about seven weeks" and the card shows "June 15 → Aug 2, 1858".
- **Engineer Joseph Bazalgette built 82 miles of giant sewers**: Wikipedia ("82 miles (132 km) of main interconnecting sewers", plus "1,100 miles (1,800 km) of additional street sewers"; works "started at the beginning of 1859 and lasted until 1875"). The VO and counter say 82 miles of *main* sewers.
- **London still uses them today**: Wikipedia ("His sewer system operates into the 21st century"); London Museum ("still form the basis of London's sewers today").

## Kept hedges
- "about seven weeks" (the exact figure is 48 days).
- "(it didn't help much)" on screen, not stated as a measured fact.
- 82 miles is the main sewers only, labelled "MILES of main sewers".

## Dramatised (comedy, not claimed as fact)
Clothespins on noses, green stink fog inside the chamber, the giant thermometer, the boatman pinching his nose, MPs dunking a curtain into a bucket, the APPROVED stamp, the hi-vis sewer worker's thumbs-up. Sewage is a cartoon brown outflow, never graphic. All characters are original toons (the engineer is a generic toon, not a portrait of Bazalgette, and the parliament building is stylised, not a replica).
