# Sources — The naked mole-rat barely ages

Checked 27 Nov 2026. Every claim has Wikipedia plus at least one more reliable source (Smithsonian's National Zoo, or the
peer-reviewed paper itself via its PubMed abstract). Quotes are copied from the pages.

- **W** = Wikipedia, "Naked mole-rat": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naked_mole-rat
- **NZ** = Smithsonian's National Zoo & Conservation Biology Institute, "Naked mole-rat": https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/naked-mole-rat
- **RUBY** = Ruby, Smith & Buffenstein (2018), *eLife* 7:e31157, "Naked Mole-Rat mortality rates defy gompertzian laws by not increasing with age" — abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29364116/
- **TIAN** = Tian et al. (2013), *Nature* 499:346, "High-molecular-mass hyaluronan mediates the cancer resistance of the naked mole rat" — abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23783513/
- **PARK** = Park et al. (2017), *Science*, "Fructose-driven glycolysis supports anoxia resistance in the naked mole-rat" — abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28428423/

## Fixes and hedges (also in STATUS.md)
- Brief said "lives ~30+ years": kept as "Over 30" (TIAN/RUBY ">30", NZ "as long as 30 years"); Wikipedia's 37-year figure is not used, so the video does not depend on it.
- Mouse: TIAN says a similar-sized house mouse has a **maximum** lifespan of 4 years (W gives c. 3 years as typical) → "four years, tops".
- "Never gets cancer" would be wrong (2016: two cases in zoo animals) → "almost never"; the 0-cases card is tied to "years of watching" (TIAN).
- "Like plants" (a newspaper headline about PARK) was dropped; the line says only what PARK and W say: it burns fructose.
- "Can survive minutes without oxygen" (brief) → the exact figure, 18 minutes (PARK + W).
