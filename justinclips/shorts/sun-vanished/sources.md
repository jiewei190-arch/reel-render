# Sources: "What If the Sun Disappeared?" (Justin clips Short #13, sun-vanished)

Checked Nov 9 2026. The topic bank's Live Science page returned no article body to the fetcher, so its claims are not used.
The cooling and straight-line claims come from IFLScience instead.

- **NRAO:** National Radio Astronomy Observatory, Ask an Astronomer — https://public.nrao.edu/?p=32404
- **UT:** Universe Today, "How Fast Is Gravity?" — https://www.universetoday.com/121284/how-fast-is-gravity/
- **IFL:** IFLScience (Dr. Alfredo Carpineti, May 14 2026), "If The Sun Suddenly Vanished, It Would Take Earth 8 Minutes And 20 Seconds Before Feeling Any Change" — https://www.iflscience.com/if-the-sun-suddenly-vanished-it-would-take-earth-8-minutes-and-20-seconds-before-feeling-any-change-83514

| Line | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Imagine the Sun vanished: you wouldn't know for 8 minutes | NRAO: "we would not notice the Sun's disappearance until about 8 minutes and 20 seconds". **Softened:** said as "Imagine" (the Sun can't really vanish), and "8 minutes" in speech while the card says 8 min 20 s (average). |
| 2 | Even gravity takes 8 minutes to get the news | IFL: "8 minutes and 20 seconds is the time it takes for both its light and gravity to reach us." NRAO: "due to the light (and gravity wave) travel time". |
| 3 | Sunlight needs 8 minutes to reach us; the sky looks normal | UT: "it takes light about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to make the trip". INSERT card: "8 MIN 20 S, Sun to Earth (average)". |
| 4 | Earth keeps circling nothing | IFL / NRAO: Earth feels no change for 8 min 20 s, so it keeps orbiting. |
| 6 | Gravity is not instant either; it travels at the speed of light | UT: "The speed of gravity equals the speed of light" (binary pulsar PSR 1913+16 "within 1%"). IFL: "Light and gravity both move with the same speed". |
| 7-8 | At minute 8: lights out | NRAO: "leaving us in darkness except for light from stars". |
| 9 | The Moon goes dark too; only the stars are left | NRAO: "the Moon would be dark as it is illuminated by the Sun", "darkness except for light from stars". (Planets would also fade later. Not mentioned.) |
| 10 | Earth flies off in a straight line, into space | IFL: Earth "would go off on a literal tangent, a mostly straight line from the last place it felt the gravity of the Sun." NRAO: "We would also drift out of our orbit". |
| 11 | In a few weeks, the whole planet freezes | IFL: "a matter of days before the surface temperature plummets below zero" and "about 20 days before the average surface temperature is at the freezing point of water". Said as "a few weeks". |
| 12-13 | The last sunshine you saw was already 8 minutes old | Same light-travel time as line 3: the sunlight you see left the Sun about 8 min 20 s earlier. |
