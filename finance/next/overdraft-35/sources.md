# Sources and maths: overdraft-35 (draft, for MAIN QA)

**Hypothetical example, not advice.** Fees differ by bank and account; some banks charge less or nothing.

- "$35" and "at many big banks": CFPB, overdraft final rule (Dec 2024): *most very large financial institutions continue to charge $35 per overdraft transaction*; a CFPB page: the vast majority of banks and credit unions over $10 billion in assets charge $30–$37, and more than half charge $35.
  - https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_overdraft-final-rule_2024-12.pdf
  - https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-proposes-rule-to-close-bank-overdraft-loophole-that-costs-americans-billions-each-year-in-junk-fees/
  - Note: that rule's $5 cap was overturned by Congress in 2025 before it took effect (CRS, IN12513): https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2025-09-10_IN12513_7f82cb854408a4c2ce7e2e777f36a2e089fdc9c0.pdf
- Context (not in the VO): most debit-card overdrafts are for less than $26 and repaid within three days (CFPB Data Spotlight, 2023): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/data-spotlight-consumer-experiences-with-overdraft-programs/full-report/
- Optional on-screen tip (UPLOAD description, not the VO): for one-time debit card and ATM overdrafts, banks need your opt-in before charging a fee (Regulation E, 12 CFR 1005.17): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/17/

Maths: 1 coffee: $5 + $35 = $40. Three coffees: 3 × $35 = $105 in fees (VO), $120 total paid vs Saver's $15.
