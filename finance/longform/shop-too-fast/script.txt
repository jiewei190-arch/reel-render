Your favourite corner shop made forty thousand dollars last year. Two years later, it was gone.
This is how a shop that was winning went broke.
Sam opened a tiny store on a busy street with ten thousand dollars of savings.
Lines out the door. Sales doubled in the first year.
Profit after rent and wages: forty thousand dollars. Life was good.
So why did it close? It starts with a phone call from the bank.
The bank offered Sam two hundred thousand dollars to open three more shops.
Sam said yes. Three new leases, three new teams, one big monthly payment.
The loan cost four thousand dollars every month, sales or no sales.
On paper, four shops meant four times the profit. But there was a catch nobody checked.
The new shops were on quiet streets. Each one sold half as much as the first.
Rent kept coming. Wages kept coming. The loan payment kept coming.
The first shop's forty thousand dollars now paid for the other three.
By month ten, the cash ran out. Sam put the payroll on a credit card at twenty four percent.
Then a supplier asked to be paid upfront. And the real reason it died is not what you think.
Sam's shop did not die from losing customers. It died from fixed costs.
Every new lease and loan is a bill you pay even on a slow day.
Before you grow, ask one question. If sales drop by half, can I still pay every bill?
If the answer is no, you are not growing. You are gambling.
