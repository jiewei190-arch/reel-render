How do planes land when pilots can't see the runway?
In thick fog, the runway itself does the guiding.
An antenna past the far end sends a radio beam down the centre line. That's the localizer.
Another antenna beside the runway sends a beam sloping up at three degrees. The glideslope.
Each beam is two signals. When both are equally strong, you're right on the path.
Antennas in the nose catch both beams, and the autopilot follows them down.
In the thickest fog, it can even land the plane while pilots watch.
