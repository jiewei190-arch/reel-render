# Viral 3D / animated Shorts: web research for Block Money

Researched 2026-11-19. Problem being solved: 55–60% "stayed to watch" (target 80%), 500–700 views and flat.

**Labels used throughout**
- **[E] Evidence**: a measured number, a named video with its view count, or a figure the creator states about their own channel.
- **[O] Opinion**: practitioner or blog advice with no data behind it.
- **[PITCH]**: the source sells a course, tool or coaching. Its numbers are self-reported and its framing is promotional. I used the facts and ignored the funnel.

No primary interview with Zack D. Films or with the Art of War animators turned up. Most of the craft detail below is secondhand: freelancers, frame-by-frame teardowns, and Tim Danilov's own (sales) material. Weigh it accordingly.

---

## 0. The core diagnosis from the evidence

- **The 55–60% swipe-through is the main problem, and it is a frame-0 problem.**
  - [E] Paddy Galloway studied 5,400 Shorts and 3.3B views. Shorts under 60% "viewed vs swiped away" (VVSA) "rarely performed well". The best ones sat at 70–90%. His advice: "make the first second punchy… treat the intro like a thumbnail." (https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
  - Our 55–60% sits exactly in his "probably in trouble" band.
- **Art of War, the model channel, needed about 80%+.**
  - [E/PITCH] Its breakout (video #7, "Reversed Bullets in WW1", 39.1M views) is reported with 81.2% retention.
  - Danilov calls 82% swipe-through the "minimum for the algorithm to push" and targets 110% average view duration (AVD). (https://keynote.theartofyt.com/)
  - Tim Chesney (another 3D documentary-Shorts channel, on the 1of10 podcast) gives the same targets: swipe rate around 80% and AVD ≥110%. (https://podwise.ai/episodes/5743964)
- **Art of War also flatlined at first.**
  - [E/PITCH] Its first 5 videos were stuck at 12K–15K views ("15K views jail"). Then one format hit 39M. (https://keynote.theartofyt.com/)
  - Lesson: the fix is format and hook, not volume of the same thing.

## 1. 20 ranked, actionable rules

Ranked by expected impact on our two numbers: VVSA first, then AVD.

1. **Frame 0 shows something strange already happening, never a title card or an establishing shot.**
   - [E] ReelPantry measured 4 channels frame by frame. The opening frames "show something strange happening, not a title card". Zack D. Films' top hook type is the cold-open story.
   - [E] In Zack D. Films' and The Infographics Show's top videos, the first cut-away comes earlier (1.5s and 1.3s) than in the rest of their videos (2.0s and 2.3s).
   - Source: https://www.reelpantry.com/guides/how-to-make-animated-explainer-videos-like-zack-d-films ([PITCH]: the page sells an AI tool, but the measurements and method are stated.)
   - For us: frame 0 is Spender mid-disaster, e.g. a credit card bill wrapped around his head, not two avatars standing in a room.

2. **First cut by about 1.5s, then a new shot every 2.5s.**
   - [E] Zack D. Films: a shot every 2.5s, about 21 cuts per minute, opening shot 1.7–2.45s. Low Poly Shorts: every 2.45s.
   - Source: same ReelPantry page.
   - [E] A 2D "What If" channel analysis found about 27 cuts per minute in the original, against 14 in a weaker remake. (https://www.freshstack.ai/resources/claude-2d-animation, [PITCH])
   - For us: a 30s Short means 12–14 shots, not 5–6 long camera holds.

3. **Narrate at 165–210 words per minute, so 80–100 words for a 30s Short, with no dead air over about 1s.**
   - [E] Measured narration speeds: Zack D. Films 168 wpm, Low Poly Shorts 213, The Infographics Show 205 (ReelPantry).
   - [E] Prepublish's corpus: a median of 216 wpm across 121 Shorts under a minute. (https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-shorts-retention, [PITCH])
   - [O] Fill any pause over 1s with a cut or overlay (Prepublish).

4. **Steal a proven format and change one thing (the topic or market). Don't invent formats yet.**
   - [E/PITCH] Danilov: Art of War is the Zack D. Films format moved into war history. Wholesome Wendy and Zeck Felms are the same format moved into family and meme markets.
   - [E/PITCH] "Three viral videos ~70M → turning them into formats → 700M+". One Art of War hit became about 40 uploads with the same hook, scenario template and closing beat.
   - [O] Replicate a proven format for the first 10–20 uploads. Experiment only after 3 videos beat your median by 3x.
   - Source: https://new.theartofyt.com/3-channels-1m-followers ([PITCH])
   - [E] Tim Chesney: "We never steal the entire video; we change one little thing… but we keep the rest." (https://podwise.ai/episodes/5743964)
   - For us: pick the closest proven formats and port them to money: "How did X survive…" (Art of War), "What happens if you…" (Zack D. Films), and "X vs Y" (see rule 7).

5. **Write the hook as a specific, impossible-sounding consequence with a number, and make it readable as text.**
   - [E] Art of War's top video (85.9M views): "How Did King Louis Survive A 25 Barrel Attack?" (https://www.themeasure.net/the-art-of-wars-shorts-charge-toward-history-channels-audience-youtube/)
   - [E] Zack D. Films title patterns: "[Company]'s $[Huge Number] Mistake", "The [Thing] That [Shocking Consequence]". (https://outlierkit.com/channel/zackdfilms; this page's long-form/Shorts split is internally inconsistent, so use it for patterns only.)
   - [E] A hook formula reported from viral cartoon Shorts: "It's impossible to get fired in Japan, but companies found something way worse." (https://sozai.app/transcript/tried-making-viral-cartoon-shorts-using-only-claude/index.md, [PITCH])
   - [O] Jenny Hoyos (10M average views per Short): hook ≤3s; specific "power words" such as free, banned, secret, $1; she will rewrite the whole idea to get a stronger hook. (https://www.marketingexamined.com/blog/jenny-hoyos-short-form-video-playbook)

6. **Pair the spoken hook with a visual hook, and put on-screen text on frame 0.**
   - [O] Hooks should be "visual, readable without sound".
   - [O] One unverified blog claims 60–80% of Shorts start muted.
   - [E] Zack D. Films changes on-screen text about 55 times a minute (word-by-word captions). Low Poly Shorts: about 40. The 2D channels: 11–14 (ReelPantry).

7. **Use the two-character comparison our cast is built for.**
   - [E] A "lawyer vs electrician: how savings rate affects when you can retire" Reel got 7.8M views. A "last one is the hardest" personal-finance listicle got 3.6M. Comparison and countdown framings dominate the trending finance list. (https://getreelyze.com/trending/personal-finance)
   - [E] vidIQ hook list: "X vs Y" (28M reach) and "Did you know…" (31M) are among the top hook types by views. (https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)
   - For us: Spender vs Saver, same paycheck, split screen, a running counter on each.

8. **Put a running visual counter or meter on screen from frame 0, and pay it off at the end.**
   - [O] "A visible counter or progress bar in frame one creates an instant I-need-to-know-how-this-ends state." (gofaceless / aibrify, cited in search: https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/mastering-first-impressions-two-second-rule, https://aibrify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-playbook)
   - Not tested, but cheap to do in Remotion. A ticking debt counter is our natural version.

9. **Every sentence opens a loop. Use the "but / then / you see…" rhythm.**
   - [O] Hoyos: a "but/then" structure, e.g. "But is that enough to beat Chick-fil-A?"
   - [O] Viral cartoon Shorts: impossible claim → escalating scenes → laugh beat → a "You see…" explanation line → a closing line that flips the opening. The creator says "almost every hit uses the 'You see' line". (sozai transcript, [PITCH])
   - [O] Zack D. Films beats are hook → setup → value → twist (ReelPantry).

10. **End on the punchline or twist, then cut immediately: no CTA, no outro, no summary.**
    - [O] Zack D. Films style: "No long intros or calls to action. Videos start immediately and end abruptly." (https://ivideonow.com/blog/why-zack-d-films-dominates-youtube-shorts-with-unsettling-3d-animation-content-en)
    - [O] Danilov's script prompt requires "a non-summary close" ([PITCH]).
    - [O] Hoyos: end on the biggest twist or punchline, "satisfied, but surprised".
    - [E] Jake Fellman's videos "end before viewers see the ending coming". (https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/11-tips-from-youtubes-fastest-growing-creator/)

11. **Design the loop: make the last frame and last line flow back into frame 0.**
    - [E] Jake Fellman matched first and last frames on "Minecraft RTX 24% Arrowhead Loop". It got 15M views in 48h, against his normal 3–4M. His percentage viewed reached 187.1%. YouTube's own 2021 Shorts report recommended endings that flow into beginnings. (https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts)
    - [E/PITCH] Danilov: the 110% AVD target is reached because "viewers who finish get pulled back in by the loop."
    - Caveat [E]: since 31 Mar 2025, YouTube counts loops as views, but "engaged views" exclude them (vidIQ).
    - For us: the final line is the first half of the opening sentence, e.g. "…and that's why Spender's card…" cuts back to "Spender's card has a secret."

12. **Plant one Easter egg per Short that rewards a rewatch.**
    - [E] Tim Chesney names Easter eggs as a rewatch driver, alongside his 110% AVD target (https://podwise.ai/episodes/5743964).
    - For us: a tiny background price tag that changes, a Saver piggy bank that grows each episode, or a recurring prop. Cheap in three.js.

13. **Use one recurring character gag across episodes (a format beat, not a one-off).**
    - [E/PITCH] Art of War's 40 uploads kept "the same hook, scenario template and closing beat".
    - [E] Skibidi Toilet: each episode ends on a recurring beat (a jumpscare or a thumbs-up) and escalates. The series "blew up somewhere during episodes 8–9". (https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/dafuqboom-skibidi-toilet-interview-2194741)
    - For us: Spender always ends broke in a cardboard box. Saver always adjusts his glasses before the reveal.

14. **Use high-contrast, simple lighting and readable silhouettes rather than set detail.**
    - [O] Zack D. Films: "High-contrast, cinematic lighting makes models stand out against dark or neutral backgrounds" (ivideonow).
    - [E] Zack D. Films' look is "rounded cartoon 3D with miniature-scale mechanisms". Low Poly Shorts is deliberately PS1 low-poly (ReelPantry).
    - [E] Skibidi Toilet is built from reused Half-Life 2 and CS:Source assets in Source Filmmaker (Dexerto). Set fidelity is clearly not the bottleneck.

15. **Show money as a physical object or mechanism: x-ray, cutaway, miniature machinery.**
    - [E] Zack D. Films' signature x-ray and inside-the-object views ("How Stitches Work", 250M+ views, per ivideonow). Low Poly Shorts uses x-ray cutaways (ReelPantry).
    - For us: show interest as a little machine eating coins inside the card, and compound growth as a coin-tree growing branches.

16. **Exaggerate expressions on stylised characters; don't chase realism or lip-sync.**
    - [E] A 2025 *Virtual Reality* lab study: stylised characters with *augmented* expression intensity were rated more attractive, more familiar, less eerie and more trustworthy than realistic ones. (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10055-025-01238-6)
    - [E] Another study: shape drives perceived expression intensity; material and texture smoothness drive appeal. (search summary, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221282985_The_evaluation_of_stylized_facial_expressions)
    - Caveat: lab stills, not Shorts data.
    - [O] Zack D. Films narration is voice-over, not character dialogue, so lip-sync is mostly moot in the top format.

17. **Use a calm, steady narrator over intense visuals, with one consistent voice.**
    - [O] Zack D. Films: "a calm, steady voice contrasts with the intense visuals" (ivideonow).
    - [E] 88–100% of videos at the measured channels are voiced (ReelPantry).
    - [E] Tim Chesney started on ElevenLabs and moved to a hired voice actor (podwise).
    - [O] Keep one voice per channel ("voice = brand").
    - [O] ElevenLabs stability around 55–65%; a flat voice at ≥80% is a common mistake. (https://fluxnote.io/guides/elevenlabs-youtube-voiceover-2026, vendor)

18. **Sound: duck the music under the voice, use SFX only on physical events, and resolve the music on the last line.**
    - [O] Practitioner and tool guidance converges on these points:
      - Duck the music under the narration.
      - A whoosh on cuts is common, at around -15 dB.
      - But "a sound on every single cut begins to feel noisy". Use effects "only where something physical happens", and don't repeat the same whoosh more than twice.
      - Lock picture before building sound.
      - Music should resolve on the last sentence.
    - Sources: https://github.com/mromidev/claude-animated-shorts/blob/main/SKILL.md, https://www.creatoros.ca/blog/ai-sound-effects-for-youtube-shorts/, https://github.com/Anil-matcha/zack-d-films-ai-video-generator/blob/main/SKILL.md
    - No measured data on sound exists. A/B test it.

19. **Volume: aim for 1–2 a day once the format is validated. Before that, ship small and fast.**
    - [E] Art of War posts 2 Shorts a day (themeasure.net, Tubular data).
    - [E/PITCH] Art of War went from 15 to 60 videos a month while revenue went from $6K to $54K a month.
    - [E] Zack D. Films averages about 2 a day across about 4.1K videos (outlierkit); ivideonow claims 4–5 a day.
    - [E] Tim Chesney targets a 48h turnaround per video at about $120 each.
    - [E] Jake Fellman: about 8h per Short (8h render overnight). He made 90 Among Us Shorts in about 3 months after the first one hit 22M. (https://www.tubefilter.com/2021/07/21/creators-on-the-rise-jake-fellman/)
    - [O] Fellman: keep your first 50 videos small (15–30 minutes each) and favour quantity early (YouTube Blog).
    - [O/PITCH] Danilov: double or triple output when real-time views reach 3x baseline. He says he regrets not doing it at Art of War's 82M real-time peak.

20. **Iterate on two metrics only (VVSA and AVD), one variable at a time, and judge slowly.**
    - [E/PITCH] Danilov: "Two metrics. Everything else is noise."
    - [E] Galloway: engagement (likes, comments, shares) had no strong relationship with performance. AVD over 50s averaged 4.1M views. Most Shorts were 20–40s.
    - [O] Test hook variants by changing only the opening seconds (Prepublish, aibrify).
    - [E] Fellman: Shorts "often take nearly a month to reach their first million views" and keep gaining traction for a year.
    - [E] Tim Chesney validates concepts on TikTok first, because his first YouTube upload got 13 views while doing well on TikTok.

## 2. Answers to the seven questions

### Q1. How the first second is built

- **Camera**
  - [O] Start in motion, already mid-action.
  - [E] The first cut comes earlier in top videos (1.3–1.5s, ReelPantry).
  - [O] Zack D. Films style edits use a zoom-in on the key impact beat and screen shake on impacts (Anil-matcha skill doc, a tool interpretation).
- **Composition**
  - [E] One strange thing, centred, no title card.
  - [O] "Lead with the payoff": show the result first, e.g. the finished cake being sliced (Prepublish).
- **Emotion**
  - [O] Shock or disgust for Zack D. Films (medical and morbid).
  - [O] For Broken Finger Paradise: "a striking first image… find the horror that could relate to someone in everyday life." (https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/06/01/youtube-millionaires-broken-finger-paradise/)
  - For money, the equivalent is dread or shock at a bill, a number or a loss.
- **Text**
  - [E] Word-by-word captions change about 55 times a minute.
  - [O] Text readable on mute.
- **Sound**
  - [O] The first word carries the hook. No "hey guys" or "in this video" (Prepublish).
  - [O] Zack D. Films uses a calm voice against an intense image.
- **Galloway**: "Treat the intro like a thumbnail."

### Q2. Retention devices mid-video

- **Open loops**: every sentence opens one (Anil-matcha). Use "but / then" (Hoyos) and the "You see…" turn (sozai).
- **Pattern interrupts**: a cut every 2.5s and about 55 text changes a minute (E). A laugh beat midway (O).
- **Escalation**:
  - Danilov's keynote names "listicle + escalation" as a top format (PITCH).
  - The What-If template: escalating wins → a cost backed by one real number → a powerful figure notices → a stakes choice → a cliffhanger (freshstack, PITCH).
- **Easter eggs**: Tim Chesney, for rewatches (E, but details are thin).
- **Recurring gags and closing beats**: Art of War reused the same closing beat across about 40 videos (E/PITCH). Skibidi Toilet has a recurring end beat (E).
- **Double-payoff endings**:
  - [O] Hoyos: "satisfied, but surprised".
  - [O] sozai: the closing line flips the opening.
  - That gives two payoffs: (1) the answer to the hook's question, then (2) a gag that flips it.
- **Loop design**:
  - [E] Fellman's matched frames gave 15M in 48h, about 4–5x his norm.
  - [E] YouTube's 2021 report advice on endings that flow into beginnings (vidIQ).
  - [O] The narrative loop: the last line sets up the first line's question.

### Q3. Animation quality: what matters

- **No source has engagement data on render quality for Shorts.** The evidence points one way:
  - [E] Skibidi Toilet (reused game assets in Source Filmmaker) reached billions of views.
  - [E] Low Poly Shorts is deliberately PS1-grade.
  - [E] Tim Chesney: "I don't care about creating the most perfect video. I want to create as many good quality videos as possible."
  - [O/PITCH] Danilov claims "four 90% videos beat one 100% video".
  - [E] Fellman, YouTube Blog: success is "99% content and 1% everything else"; "there is no clickbaiting in short form".
- **What does matter** (E where marked):
  - Pacing and cut rate (E).
  - Readable action: the "technical scenario depicted quickly and accurately" (Zack D. Films freelancer, https://supersets.chrisreillyportfolio.com/zackdfilms.php).
  - Exaggerated expressions on stylised characters (E, lab).
  - High-contrast lighting (O).
  - Physics-assisted body motion: Zack D. Films' freelancer uses Cascadeur for this (E). It suggests weighty, believable body motion matters more than faces.
  - Timing polish: Fellman trims beats by "5 milliseconds" (O, https://rosetta.to/u/thefutur/building-an-audience-of-20-million-in-1-year-masterclass-with-jake-fellman).

### Q4. Scripting

- **Length and pace**
  - [E] 160–215 wpm, so a 20s Short is about 55–70 words and a 35s Short about 95–120 words.
  - [E] Galloway: most Shorts are 20–40s, and longer ones that still hold attention do better. Don't shorten just to raise percentage viewed; raise hold instead.
- **Hook length**
  - [O] ≤3s (Hoyos).
  - [O] One tool-made analysis of 57 Zack D. Films Shorts claims hook sentences of about 18 words. I could not verify it; the GitHub skill page fetched shows no such data.
  - Our recommendation: a hook of 8–14 words, landing by 2.5s.
- **Sentence length and reading level**
  - [O] Hoyos: fifth-grade reading level or lower; swap any word that slows comprehension.
  - [O] Prepublish: halve the first sentence.
- **Order of writing**
  - [O] Hoyos writes the hook and the final line first, then the foreshadowing (two lines, ≤3s), then the body.
- **Tone**
  - [O] Calm and matter-of-fact over shocking content (Zack D. Films).
  - [O/PITCH] Danilov's prompt rules: a specific opener, a mid-body rhythm break, a non-summary close, no em dashes.
- **AI scripts**
  - [O/PITCH] Danilov claims generic ChatGPT scripts all sound alike. He feeds Claude a "format library" of top transcripts. Using reference transcripts is sensible; the "demonetisation clustering" claim is unsupported.

### Q5. Sound design

- **Music**
  - [E] Fellman added popular music tracks in the Shorts tool (YouTube Blog).
  - [E] Tim Chesney earned about $20K from TikTok music deals, so trending audio has value off-platform.
  - [O] Duck the music under the voice; resolve it on the last line; loop it cleanly for replays (vidIQ: if the music doesn't loop, "it ruins the effect").
- **SFX density**
  - [O] Conflicting advice. Recommendation: whoosh only on camera moves and cuts that carry meaning, at about -15 dB; one impact SFX per physical event; at least 2–3 silent cuts per Short.
  - [E] In film, sound reduces the animation needed to sell tension ("the heartbeat and the wipers"). (https://share.transistor.fm/s/d19a3b74/transcript.txt, a Rally short-film discussion)
- **Voice processing**
  - No evidence found on EQ or compression. ElevenLabs stability guidance is in rule 17 (O).

### Q6. Workflow and volume

- **Zack D. Films**
  - [E] About 2 a day on average over 5+ years (outlierkit), or 4–5 a day (ivideonow, O).
  - Production: templates (base 3D file, compositing and edit templates), Flamenco render farm, Cascadeur, prefab assets, new topics weekly (freelancer page, E).
- **Art of War**
  - [E] 2 a day (Tubular).
  - [E/PITCH] 60 a month at about $150 each, made by hired animators from CIS markets. Paid test task: remake 20s of a reference video.
- **Tim Chesney**
  - [E] A pipeline in Asana: script → storyboard → voiceover → animation → distribution.
  - [E] 48h turnaround, about $120 a video, TikTok used for pre-validation.
- **Fellman**
  - [E] About 8h per Short, reused rigs and models, renders overnight.
  - [E] After a hit, 90 follow-ups in 3 months; switched games when the trend faded.
  - [O] Change formats every 6–8 months.
- **Analytics loop**
  - [O/PITCH] Danilov:
    - Read comments for 30 minutes a week and turn them into 5 ideas.
    - Return to the proven format after two flat videos.
    - Double output at 3x baseline real-time views.
  - [E] Galloway: likes and comments don't predict performance; VVSA and AVD do.

### Q7. Faceless finance and money animation

- **No verified case study of an animated finance *Shorts* channel exists.** Everything here is inferred from adjacent evidence:
  - [E] Two-person savings comparisons go viral: lawyer vs electrician, 7.8M (getreelyze).
  - [E] Emotional-permission topics work: "keep investing even if you're scared to check your balance" got 2.5M; "student debt: too worried to check it?" (BBC) got 808K. Fear of looking at your money is a proven emotional hook.
  - [E] Numbered countdowns work: "the last one is the hardest", 3.6M.
  - [E] Zack D. Films' money hits: "Toyota's $2,300,000,000 Mistake" (10M) and "The Pepsi Contest That Killed 5 People" (12M). These are corporate money disasters told as stories.
  - [O] "Talking object" finance (e.g. a credit card with anxiety) is pitched as under-saturated (https://james-palm.medium.com/5-talking-objects-niches-quietly-printing-money-on-youtube-shorts-in-2026-8d5642f41bf0, [PITCH] for AI prompts).
- **Money economics**
  - [E] Galloway: Shorts RPM was about $0.06 in 2023.
  - [E/PITCH] Danilov assumes $0.15.
  - [O] Finance Shorts likely earn more, but blogs quote long-form RPMs ($10–25). Treat those as long-form only. (https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-youtube-channels/)

## 3. Ten hook rewrites for Block Money topics

All figures were computed for this file. They are illustrative: state the assumptions on screen. Format for each: **frame-0 visual / first line / final visual gag (and loop).**

1. **Credit card minimums**
   - Frame 0: Spender, smiling, pays a $5,000 bill with a single coin labelled "$50". The bill doesn't shrink; it grows a second page.
   - First line: "Spender paid his credit card every single month… and owed money for 19 years."
   - Final gag: Spender, now grey-bearded with a walker, makes the last payment. The card prints a receipt: "Interest: $8,887". Saver, still young, hands him a party hat. Loop: the receipt flutters down onto frame 0's bill.
   - Assumes $5,000 at 24% APR; minimum = 1% + interest, $25 floor; 234 months; $8,887 interest.

2. **Credit card minimums (x-ray variant)**
   - Frame 0: x-ray view inside Spender's credit card. A tiny machine with teeth eats coins as fast as he feeds them in.
   - First line: "This is what's actually inside your credit card when you pay the minimum."
   - Final gag: Saver pays in full. The machine starves, burps one coin back and dies. Spender feeds it a sandwich.

3. **$5 coffee invested**
   - Frame 0: Spender's coffee cup is visibly full of gold coins, steaming. He takes a sip.
   - First line: "This coffee cost Spender one hundred and eighty-five thousand dollars."
   - Final gag: 30 years later, Saver sips the same coffee on a yacht named "LATTE". Spender is the barista. Loop: the barista hands over the frame-0 cup.
   - Assumes $5 a day, about $152 a month, at 7% for 30 years, which gives about $185.5K.

4. **$5 coffee (counter variant)**
   - Frame 0: two cups, two counters. Spender's reads $0 → -$5 → -$10. Saver's says "invested" and ticks upward.
   - First line: "Same coffee. Same price. One of them turns into a house."
   - Final gag: Saver's counter becomes a house key. Spender's cup is just a cup, with his name spelled wrong on it.

5. **Employer match**
   - Frame 0: Spender's boss holds out a stack of cash. Spender walks past it, waving "no thanks", toward the vending machine.
   - First line: "Spender's boss tried to give him free money. He said no. For 30 years."
   - Final gag: the cash stack, now a tower labelled "$183,000", falls on Spender. Saver pulls him out and asks "you okay?" while pocketing one bill. Loop: back to the boss holding out the stack.
   - Assumes a $150-a-month match ($60K salary, 50% match up to 6%, so $1,800 a year) at 7% for 30 years, which gives about $183K.

6. **Inflation**
   - Frame 0: Spender's $100 bill has a bite taken out of it and is visibly shrinking in his hand.
   - First line: "Spender hid $100 under his mattress. Something is eating it."
   - Final gag: 24 years later he lifts the mattress. The bill is half-size ($49), and a tiny creature in a "3%" shirt is chewing on it. Saver's money is out in the world, swole.
   - Assumes 3% a year for 24 years, so $100 is worth about $49.

7. **Car loan length**
   - Frame 0: Spender drives a shiny car off the lot. A chain behind it is attached to a tiny Spender pushing a wheelbarrow of coins.
   - First line: "The dealer said 'only $563 a month.' He didn't say for how long."
   - Final gag: 7 years later the car falls apart at the final payment and the wheelbarrow is empty. Saver's 4-year-loan car is fine, and Saver is the one driving away. Text: "+$5,495 interest".
   - Assumes $35K at 9%: 84 months is $563/mo and $12,302 interest; 48 months is $871/mo and $6,807 interest.

8. **Car loan (vs format)**
   - Frame 0: split screen, same car. Spender's price tag reads "$563/mo". Saver's reads "$871/mo". Spender laughs at Saver.
   - First line: "Spender's payment is $300 cheaper. Spender is losing."
   - Final gag: the reveal stamps "84 months" vs "48 months". Spender's laugh freezes and his glasses-less face gets the glasses-adjust gag Saver normally does.

9. **Compound interest (start age)**
   - Frame 0: two blocky saplings. Saver plants his at 25; Spender is asleep on a couch next to an empty pot.
   - First line: "They saved the exact same $200 a month. One ended up with twice as much."
   - Final gag: at 65, Saver's tree rains $525K in coins. Spender's tree, planted at 35, gives $244K, and one coin bonks his head. He finally wakes up. Loop: he falls back onto the frame-0 couch.
   - Assumes $200 a month at 7%: 40 years gives about $525K; 30 years gives about $244K.

10. **Compound interest (the "you see" turn)**
    - Frame 0: Saver drops one coin into a jar. The jar immediately spits out a second coin. Spender's jaw drops off his head.
    - First line: "Saver's money makes babies. And the babies make babies."
    - Final gag: the coin family overflows the jar into a city. Spender tries the trick with his jar; it spits out a parking ticket.

**Pattern across all ten**
- Frame 0 is mid-event with an impossible-looking object.
- The first line puts a specific number on a consequence, in 8–14 words.
- The middle turns with "You see…" plus one real number.
- The ending pays off twice: the number, then a Spender gag.
- The last frame cuts back to frame 0.

## 4. Animation priorities, in order of impact

1. **Frame-0 image and motion** (rules 1, 6, 8). Something strange already moving, text on screen, first cut by about 1.5s. Galloway: VVSA under 60% kills a Short.
2. **Cut rhythm and camera energy.** A new shot about every 2.5s (12–14 per 30s Short), a push-in on key beats, shake on impacts. Measured on top channels.
3. **Readable visual metaphor for the money mechanic.** Counters, x-ray cutaways, physical coins and machines (Zack D. Films' miniature mechanisms and x-ray views). Clarity of the "technical scenario" is what the freelancer brief prioritises.
4. **Word-by-word captions in sync with the voice.** About 40–55 text changes a minute on top 3D channels.
5. **Exaggerated poses and expressions** (squash, big eyes, the glasses-adjust and jaw-drop gags). The lab evidence favours augmented expression on stylised characters. Pose reads at phone size; subtle facial nuance doesn't.
6. **Weighty body motion and timing polish.** Physics-feel (Cascadeur in the Zack D. Films pipeline), anticipation and overshoot, trimmed beats (Fellman).
7. **Loop-matched final frame and recurring Easter eggs or props.** Helps AVD and rewatches.
8. **Lighting.** High-contrast key light, a simple dark or neutral background, colour-coded characters (Spender red, Saver green) so the silhouette reads instantly.
9. **Sound layer.** Ducked music, SFX only on physical events, a clean audio loop.
10. **Set detail and props.** Low priority: Skibidi Toilet and Low Poly Shorts show reused or low-poly assets are fine.
11. **Lip-sync.** Lowest priority. Top formats are voice-over narration; a simple mouth flap or none is acceptable.
