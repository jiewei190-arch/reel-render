# Hook rules from long-form breakdowns (paraphrased)

All timestamps are [m:ss] in the source video. Everything below is paraphrase; none of it is quoted transcript.
Note: none of these three creators publish a controlled dataset in the video. Where they cite a number it is flagged "claim" (creator's own experience), not a study.

## 1. Kallaway - "Give me 15 mins, and I'll make your hooks impossible to skip"
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2byPP_9F0-Q (1.19M views, Jul 2025, 489K subs; says he has about 1B views of short-form experience)

- [0:37] A hook has one job: get the viewer to opt in and keep watching. It must stop the scroll and make the viewer stay.
- [0:52] A hook needs two things only: topic clarity (viewer instantly knows what the video is about) and on-target curiosity (viewer believes it is for them and wants to know what comes next).
- [1:42] Mistake 1, delay: if the topic is first stated in line 3 of the script, cut lines 1-2. State the topic in the first 1-2 seconds ("speed to value").
- [2:16] Vague teaser hooks ("you won't believe what I saw") fail because they give no context; the viewer cannot decide to opt in.
- [2:49] Claim: short-form retention is an exponential decay curve, steepest in the first ~2 seconds; every second without context loses a big share of viewers.
- [3:11] Claim: vague hooks only work when the face, emotion, or on-screen text is carrying the hook; the spoken words add nothing.
- [4:15] Mistake 2, confusion: awkward phrasing hurts comprehension. Use fewer, simpler words (about 6th-grade level), active voice, and check that the hook cannot be read two ways.
- [8:48] Mistake 3, irrelevance: say "you/your" not "I/me" so the viewer feels it is for them; frame around a pain point or a need, not a "nice to have" trend.
- [10:57] Mistake 4, disinterest: open a curiosity loop using contrast, i.e. the gap between what the viewer believes (A) and a contrarian or better alternative (B).
- [13:18] Contrast can be stated (A vs B said out loud) or implied (only B said; the audience already knows A).
- [14:20] Structure: sentence 1 = topic clarity, sentences 2-3 = contrast/curiosity. A hook is 2-3 short lines, not always one line.

## 2. heyDominik - "I Studied 1,000 Hooks, Here's How to ACTUALLY Go Viral"
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xqy9L-88wcw (866K views, Jul 2025, 231K subs; critiques creator videos)

- [0:34] Viewers swipe on autopilot; the hook must trigger a "hmm, that is interesting" moment before the muscle-memory swipe.
- [0:57] Claim: users now often swipe, then swipe BACK a beat later; he treats this "scroll-back" as a strong signal for the algorithm, so the first frame must register fast.
- [1:22] Open loops and keep them hanging; when one closes, open another.
- [2:12] A frame that starts mid-sentence with no context (someone saying "I definitely do not cut carbs") confuses people; add the context line on screen at the top.
- [2:56] Keep text inside the safe zone (not under the comment/like buttons), and keep it small enough not to cover the subject. Caption was too big in his example.
- [3:58] One of the strongest hook forms: challenge a common belief.
- [5:05] A cluttered first frame (face + kitchen + big text) kills the hook; use a clean background or a backing box behind the text.
- [6:31] The first frame should itself be a scroll-stopping visual with contrast (e.g. unhealthy food in the lower half) not a bland shot.
- [7:39] "First impression mismatch": the profile feed and the reels must both match what the viewer wants, otherwise credibility drops.
- [9:43] Plant a specific question in the viewer's head; "who can relate?" fails because no question is planted.
- [11:33] A storytelling hook (a visible journey/before-after, emotion on the face) beats a pile of pretty images.
- [17:07] Do not state the answer in the hook text (naming the flavor gave away the curiosity); blur or hide the key object so the viewer has to keep watching.
- [18:01] Colour of text: pure red looked cheap; yellow or white-on-dark read better.

## 3. Daniel Bitton Extra - "How To Write A Killer YouTube Shorts Script That Is Impossible To Skip"
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vgq14_IqdYM (139K views, 39K subs; faceless Shorts/clipping creator)

- [0:00] Claim: two of his Shorts, one about 34M views and one about 30K, differed mainly in the first seconds.
- [0:32] Get straight to the point; everything in the intro must lead to the payoff; cut fluff.
- [2:03] Best hook = striking visual or a psychological trigger. Controversial or taboo-adjacent curiosity performed hugely; a bland "have you ever felt deja vu" concept flopped.
- [3:07] Name a recognisable subject plus a big number in sentence 1 ("why did X turn down $1 billion?"); show fast clips of the person at the same time.
- [3:48] "Supporting hook" in sentence 2: guess the obvious answers and rule them out so the viewer is wrong and must keep watching.
- [5:34] Not every Short needs a supporting hook; a strong premise can carry itself.
- [6:16] Sentences 3-4: set the scene (when/where), then add context fast; do not over-explain things the audience already knows.
- [7:21] Sentence 5-6: raise the stakes so curiosity becomes investment ("why would he give up becoming the youngest self-made billionaire?").
- [7:52] Tease hints toward the payoff but do not resolve; keep the viewer unsure.
- [8:44] Deliver the payoff fast and then end; viewers leave once they get what they came for.
- [9:23] CTA should relate to the story ("subscribe if you knew it was X").
- [11:25] A guest creator (said to earn about 30K a month on faceless Shorts; claim) recommended putting the money amount in the first 5 seconds and adding a "scam"-type word, and praised re-hooks and loop endings. Payoff around the third quarter of the video.


## 4. Jenny Hoyos on Creator Science (Jay Clouse) - "Shorts Genius Shares Everything She Knows"
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=As7abwNhG7Y (5.0M views, Oct 2023; guest says she had about 600M Shorts views in a year, about 10M per video; she is a money/frugal-challenge creator, so very close to Block Money)
Numbers are the guest's own channel data (claims, not an independent study).

- [2:28] Test of a good Short hook: could it work as a long-form title/thumbnail? It must be understandable visually without sound and simple enough to explain to a 5-year-old.
- [3:35] Design the first visual FIRST (sketched like a thumbnail), then fit the words to it.
- [3:44] Keep hook text concise and run it through a readability checker; target about 5th-grade level or lower. Claim: she scraped transcripts of thousands of Shorts and the biggest ones sat at roughly 1st-5th grade level.
- [5:14] Finance-specific: a single word like "business", "finance" or "profit" can push readability from grade 5 to grade 8, so she avoids jargon and explains the definition each time instead. (Directly relevant to Block Money scripts.)
- [7:11] Claim: a video at 50K views in 5 days (her usual is ~1M) had retention fall from 70% to 45% in the last second; trimming that single second moved retention 83% to 88% and the video took off. "Every second counts."
- [8:30] Claim: average swipe-through (viewed vs swiped away) is about 70% for typical Shorts; hers is about 85%. Her average retention is about 95% because viewers rewatch. She treats about 90%+ retention (looping/rewatch) as the bare minimum for a blow-up.
- [9:44] The first frame is a deliberate branded visual: location/logo in the middle and the "$1 item" text centred, always the same layout so viewers recognise the series.
- [11:17] She started by copying other creators' hooks, wrote about 10 hook variants per idea, and picked the one with the best percentage.
- [16:27] Use a "mechanism" that pulls viewers to the end (a countdown, "three things we need to do" list so viewers know how far along they are).
- [17:25] Follow the expectation the hook set, but add a twist at the end.
- [18:37] Claim: her sweet spot is exactly ~34 s; for her channel Shorts under 30 s needed over 100% retention (rewatches) to take off.
- [20:45] Structure: hook (1 line) + foreshadow (1 line) in about 3 s or less; foreshadow = say what will happen at the end.
- [22:35] Use "pacing breaks without breaking pace": a connective line ("so I cooked illegally") instead of "let's get started".
- [23:07] "But / therefore" storytelling: each beat changes the situation.
- [24:47] Write the hook and the LAST line before filming; she says the end should be abrupt, trimmed right after the payoff.
- [30:34] Claim: on YouTube, slower and more story-driven performed better than the 10-20 s dense style that worked on TikTok.

## 5. Logan Smith - "How to get retention SO HIGH your shorts always go viral"
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IPW-MF8Kp0I (378K views, Jul 2024, 51.5K subs; faceless Shorts creator)

- [0:35] Claim: retention is the single biggest factor in the Shorts algorithm.
- [1:56] The hook should be concise, explain the idea of the video, AND mention the payoff, all in under about 5 s.
- [2:30] Text alone can be a full hook: a caption that makes viewers want to see what it refers to, plus visuals that show the game/situation, so no voice is needed.
- [2:57] Example hook shape: "[person] thinks X is expensive, so I'm going to prove them wrong by doing Y for $5" (problem + stated payoff + a small money amount).
- [3:43] For voiceover/faceless content, put conflict in the script: something goes wrong, then it is resolved; use "but" and "however" for suspense.
- [4:50] Tension (anticipation of a payoff) matters more than the concept itself; keep building toward the payoff.
- [5:32] Deliver the payoff you promised; a lame payoff costs return viewers.
- [6:39] Keep pacing fast; cut dead moments (his old gaming channel: an hour of footage condensed into one minute).
- [8:03] Change the music with each section to shift mood, and use music with a build-up to create tension; sound effects only if they fit the style.

## Cross-video agreement (what 5 sources agree on)
1. State the topic in the first 1-3 s (Kallaway, Bitton, Hoyos, Smith).
2. Put a concrete number/amount early (Bitton, Smith, Hoyos' "$1"/"$5" series).
3. Plain words, about 5th-6th grade (Kallaway, Hoyos).
4. Hook first frame must work without sound (Hoyos, Smith, heyDominik).
5. Open a question or promise and do not answer it early (Kallaway, heyDominik, Bitton).
6. Pay off quickly and end; loop/rewatch lifts retention (Hoyos, Bitton).
