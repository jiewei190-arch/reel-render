# Sources and maths: prices-double

**Rule of 72** applied to inflation: years for prices to double ≈ 72 ÷ inflation rate (%).
Investor.gov (SEC), "Rule of 72": https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/rule-72

| VO | rule of 72 | exact: ln 2 / ln(1+r) |
|---|---|---|
| "At two percent, prices double in about 36 years." | 72/2 = 36 | 35.0 |
| "At three percent, about 24." | 72/3 = 24 | 23.4 |
| "At four percent, just 18." | 72/4 = 18 | 17.7 |

**Since 1970** (BLS CPI-U, U.S. city average, not seasonally adjusted): 1970 annual average 38.8; October 2026 337.511
(same data as dollar-1970/sources.md; BLS series CUUR0000SA0, https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CUUR0000SA0, mirrored on
FRED CPIAUCNS, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCNS).
- Ratio 337.511 / 38.8 = 8.70 → average rate 8.70^(1/56.3) − 1 = **3.9 % a year** → "about four percent a year on average".
- Doublings: log2(8.70) = **3.12** → "three times over"; years per doubling 56.3 / 3.12 = **18.0** → "roughly every
  eighteen years".

Hedges: "about", "roughly", "on average"; the CPI is an average basket, and the Rule of 72 is an approximation.
