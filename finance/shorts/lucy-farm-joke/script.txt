He sold his farm on the back of a restaurant check, then said it was a joke. But a court made him sell.
Virginia, nineteen fifty-two. Two men share whiskey at A. H. Zehmer's restaurant.
W. O. Lucy offers fifty thousand dollars for the Ferguson farm.
Zehmer writes it on a guest check. He and his wife sign.
By their account, he whispers to her: it's a joke.
Lucy isn't joking. He hires a lawyer to check the title.
Zehmer testifies he was high as a Georgia pine. The court doesn't buy it.
What counts is what you say and do, not a secret intention. It orders the sale.
And it began the night
