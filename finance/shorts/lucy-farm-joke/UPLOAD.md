**Description:**
Lucy v. Zehmer (Virginia, 1954): a man wrote out the sale of his 471.6-acre farm for $50,000 on the back of a restaurant guest check, he and his wife signed it, and then he said the whole thing was a joke. Virginia's highest court ordered the sale to go through.
What counts, the court said, is the outward expression of a person's intention, not a secret one.

What the 36 seconds leave out: both men had been drinking, but the court found Zehmer was "not intoxicated to the extent of being unable to comprehend" what he signed, and his own lawyer conceded as much. The deal was discussed for forty minutes or more, the first draft was rewritten from "I" to "We" so his wife could sign, and Lucy offered $5 on the spot to bind it. The trial court had sided with Zehmer; this ruling reversed it. The court also found no fraud or sharp practice by Lucy.
