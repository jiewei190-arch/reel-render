A company promised a hundred pounds to anyone who caught the flu while using its product. Then a customer did, but it refused to pay.
Victorian England. The Carbolic Smoke Ball Company's newspaper ad says:
use our smoke ball three times daily for two weeks. Catch influenza, and collect one hundred pounds.
To show it's sincere, it says a thousand pounds sits in the bank.
Mrs. Carlill uses it as directed. She gets the flu anyway.
The company argues the ad was a mere puff, a promise to nobody.
Three judges disagree: the bank deposit showed it meant it. Pay her.
