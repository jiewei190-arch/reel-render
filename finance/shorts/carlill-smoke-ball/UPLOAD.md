**Description:**
Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Company (Court of Appeal, England, 1892): a company advertised a £100 reward for anyone who caught influenza after using its smoke ball three times daily for two weeks. Mrs. Carlill did exactly that, caught the flu, and asked for the money. The company refused.
Three judges held the advertisement was a real offer, and the company had to pay.

What the 36 seconds leave out: the company's arguments were serious ones for the time. It said the ad was a "mere puff" that no one would take literally, that it was not made to anyone in particular, that she never told them she accepted, and that the terms were too vague. The judges answered each, and leaned on the ad's own line that £1000 was deposited with a bank "shewing our sincerity in the matter". The video does not say the product did or did not work for anyone else.
