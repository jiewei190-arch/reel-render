# Sources and maths: employer-match

Stated example (on screen as EXAMPLE): salary **$50,000**; the employer matches **50 % of what you contribute, up to
6 % of pay**. You contribute 6 %: 0.06 × 50,000 = **$3,000**; the match: 0.5 × 3,000 = **$1,500** → "fifty cents on
every dollar, before any growth" (1,500 / 3,000 = 0.50).

Sources:
- **IRS 2026 limit** ("the IRS caps your part at 24,500 for 2026"): the 401(k) elective-deferral limit for 2026 is
  $24,500 (IRS Notice 2025-67, Nov 13, 2025; up from $23,500). Reported e.g. by EY Tax News:
  https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-2287-irs-releases-qualified-retirement-plan-limitations-for-2026-401k-pretax-limit-increases-to-24500
  IRS page for the current limits: https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-401k-and-profit-sharing-plan-contribution-limits
- **Matching and vesting** ("some make you wait to keep it"): U.S. Department of Labor, *What You Should Know About Your
  Retirement Plan* (vesting of employer contributions).
  https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/what-you-should-know-about-your-retirement-plan
- Investor.gov (SEC), 401(k) plans glossary: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/401k-plans

Hedges: "Plans differ" (match formulas, caps and vesting vary; some employers offer no match); the $24,500 limit is the
employee limit for 2026 (catch-up rules for 50+ not covered); no fund, provider or investment is named; nothing about
what to invest in.
