# Sources and maths: credit-score-weights

The "widely used credit score" is the FICO Score; its maker publishes the category weights for the general population.
The brand is not named on screen or in the VO (no brands); it is named here as the source.

| claim (VO) | myFICO, "What's in my FICO Scores?" |
|---|---|
| "paying on time counts for 35 percent" | "payment history (35%)"; "Payment history is the most influential factor ... accounting for 35% of the calculation" |
| "How much you owe: 30." | "amounts owed (30%)" |
| "How long you've had credit: 15." | "length of credit history (15%)" |
| "New credit: 10. The mix of credit: 10." | "new credit (10%), and credit mix (10%)"; 35+30+15+10+10 = 100 |
| "It's how much of your limit you use." | "Credit utilization falls under 'amounts owed,' which accounts for about 30%" |
| "Even paid on time, a nearly maxed card can weigh on that 30." | utilization uses the balance reported by the card issuer (usually the statement balance), so a high reported balance counts even if it is later paid in full. Hedge: "can". |
| "The weights vary by person." | myFICO: the importance of the categories can vary from person to person; the percentages are for the general population |

Sources:
- myFICO, What's in my FICO Scores?: https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/whats-in-your-credit-score
- CFPB, What is a credit score?: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-credit-score-en-315/

Hedges: one scoring model's published weights; other models (and versions) weigh things differently.
