# segway-replace-cars: fact check

Checked 2026-11-23 with live web reads. The company name is spoken and shown as plain text only. No logo and no drawing
of the real machine (the kit has no scooter prop): the Short opens on the stake board and tells the story with generic
executives, a street, a warehouse and the chart. Executives are faceless; nobody is named in the voice.

| Claim in script.txt | Check | Hedge / note |
|---|---|---|
| "The Segway cost a hundred million dollars to develop." | "Developed at a cost of more than $100 million" ([TIME, "Reinventing the Wheel", 2 Dec 2001](https://time.com/archive/6905012/reinventing-the-wheel-3)). | TIME says "more than"; the board reads "$100M". Single primary source (Wired reported about $90 million raised in 2000). |
| "It was supposed to replace cars." | Dean Kamen: the Segway "will be to the car what the car was to the horse and buggy" (TIME, above; [HeraldNet / AP, Dec 2001](https://www.heraldnet.com/business/much-hyped-inventions-a-scooter/)). | The inventor's own stated ambition (for city travel). Stamp: "REPLACE CARS?". |
| "It arrived in two thousand one." | Unveiled on Monday 3 Dec 2001 on Good Morning America (HeraldNet / AP, above). | Unveiled in 2001; sales to the public began in 2002. Board: "UNVEILED · 2001". |
| "Its inventor said it would do to the car what the car did to the horse and buggy." | As row 2. | Paraphrase of the quote. |
| "One investor predicted the fastest climb to a billion dollars in sales, ever." | "Doerr predicts, the Segway Co. will be the fastest outfit in history to reach $1 billion in sales." (TIME, above) | Counter titled "PREDICTED SALES → $1B": a forecast, not a result. The investor (John Doerr) is not named in the voice. |
| "The factory was built for forty thousand a month." | A factory "capable of churning out 40,000 Segways a month by the end of next year" (TIME, above). | Planned capacity, not output. Board: "BUILT FOR, MONTHLY · 40,000". |
| "In nearly twenty years, about a hundred forty thousand were sold." | "only 140,000 Segways were ever sold." ([Fortune, 23 Jun 2020](https://fortune.com/2020/06/23/the-segway-rolls-into-the-sunset/)); also Engadget and IEEE Spectrum. 2001 to 2020. | "about"; board "SOLD, ~19 YEARS · ~140,000". |
| "Production ended in twenty twenty." | "Production... will end July 15" (Fortune, above; [The Register, 24 Jun 2020](https://www.theregister.com/2020/06/24/segway_to_heaven/)). | Production of the original two-wheeled Segway PT ended; the brand continued under Ninebot with other products, so nothing in the Short says the company closed. The street going dark is stylised. |
| "The lesson: hype is not demand." | Opinion / the lesson. | |

**Not used:** the 2010 death of the company's then owner (a real tragedy, not a business point); recall figures; the
share of revenue the PT represented (snippet only); the launch price (sources give $3,000, $4,950 and $5,000).

**The chart** is illustrative (a forecast-style climb, then a fall, tagged 2001 and 2020), not plotted from sales data.
