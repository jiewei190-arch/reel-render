**Description:**
Hamer v. Sidway (New York, 1891): an uncle promised his nephew $5,000 to give up drinking, tobacco, swearing and gambling until he turned 21. The nephew did it. The uncle died before the money changed hands, and the estate refused to pay.
New York's Court of Appeals ruled the promise was enforceable.

What the 36 seconds leave out: the uncle never refused to pay. When the nephew turned 21 in 1875 the uncle wrote that he had the money in the bank and would hold it, with interest, until the nephew could take care of it, and the nephew agreed. The uncle died in 1887. By the time of the lawsuit the nephew had assigned the claim, so the person who actually sued, and won, was Louisa Hamer; Franklin Sidway was the executor. "No gambling" is short for "playing cards or billiards for money".
