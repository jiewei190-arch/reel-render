An uncle promised his nephew five thousand dollars to stay sober, but died without paying. Then it went to court.
New York, eighteen sixty-nine. William Story makes his nephew an offer:
no liquor, no tobacco, no swearing, no gambling until twenty-one.
The nephew keeps his word for nearly six years. The uncle writes: you shall have it, as I promised.
Then the uncle dies, the money still in his hands. His executor refuses to pay, arguing the uncle got nothing in return.
New York's top court disagrees: the nephew gave up a legal freedom, and that counts.
