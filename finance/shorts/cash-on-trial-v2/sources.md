## Hedges that hold

- "No drug charges" only. The Short never says he was "never charged" (the sources support drug charges only).
- The appeals court "reverses". The Short never says a court "took" or "kept" the money: the 8th Circuit reversed and
  remanded, it did not itself order forfeiture, and what happened on remand could not be confirmed. Nothing in the Short
  says what finally happened to the money.
- Both sides are in the story. For the government: the bundles wrapped in foil in a cooler (lines 1-2, shown), the denial
  to the trooper (line 4), the dog alert (line 5). For the driver: no drugs found, no drug charges, his explanation and
  the trial judge's ruling (lines 5-8). The fourth point the court relied on, a rental car in another person's name, is
  not in the 33 s script: it goes in the description (UPLOAD.md).
- No real person's likeness: the driver, the trooper and the judges are generic figures. No name is spoken or shown.
  The car is a generic sedan with no brand and blank plates; the patrol car and the uniform carry no insignia.
- Staging, not record: the cooler stands on the car's trunk and the bundles are counted on its hood so they can be seen
  (the opinion puts the cooler on the back seat). The dog alert is shown at the rear door, as in the opinion. The mile
  marker number on the set is generic.
