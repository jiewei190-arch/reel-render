## Hedges, limits and fairness
- **Allegations, not findings.** The ruling came before any trial. Nothing in the script says the doctors did what the complaint says; lines 1 and 4 attribute it. The doctors and the university are not named in the spoken script.
- **"Then he sued."** The opinion does not give the filing date. That the suit followed the patent rests on the complaint itself describing the patent and on Wikipedia ("After learning of the patent, Moore filed a lawsuit"). No year is given for the filing.
- **"Does not own"** is plain English for: no conversion claim, because he kept no ownership interest in cells once removed. The court did not rule on every possible property question, and three justices wrote separately.
- **The three billion dollar forecast** is hedged twice in the line ("one forecast", "quoted in the complaint") and with a question mark in the hook. It was a market prediction, not the value of the patent or of his cells.
- **Fair to both sides:** the court's stated reason for refusing the property claim was to protect medical research; the description says so.
- **Not claimed:** how he found out (a 1983 consent form, per Wikipedia only), any settlement or payment, what he asked for in money, his later life, and what the law is today.
