**Description:**
Moore v. Regents of the University of California (California Supreme Court, 1990): John Moore was treated for leukemia at UCLA starting in 1976. His complaint alleged that a cell line was developed from his cells and patented in 1984 without his knowledge. He sued.
The court ruled he could not sue for conversion, the claim that his cells were his property. But it ruled a doctor must tell a patient about a research or financial interest, and let that claim go forward.

What the 36 seconds leave out: this ruling came before any trial, so the court treated the complaint's allegations as true without deciding whether they were. The three billion dollar figure was a market forecast quoted in the complaint, not money anyone was shown to have made. The court's main reason for refusing the property claim was that it could chill medical research; three justices wrote separately.
