## Description
Before Facebook, a social network turned down thirty million dollars from Google. It was Friendster. In 2003, three million people joined in months. Google offered to buy it. Investors urged the founder to take their thirteen million instead. He did. Then the site buckled: pages took up to forty seconds to load. Months later, the board replaced him as chief executive. Paid in Google stock, one insider estimated (in 2006), he would be worth a billion dollars. Friendster's site went dark in 2015.
