# law-demo-54-million-pants: fact check (DEMO for the law3d kit, synthetic timings, no voice yet)

Case: *Pearson v. Chung*, D.C. Superior Court (bench trial, Judge Judith Bartnoff), affirmed by the D.C. Court of
Appeals, No. 07-CV-872 (Dec. 18, 2008). Story only, not legal advice.

| Line in script.txt | Check |
|---|---|
| 1. "A judge sued his dry cleaner for $54 million over one pair of pants." | Roy L. Pearson Jr. was a D.C. administrative law judge (AP; ABA Journal). His claim was first put at about $65-67 million and he lowered it to $54 million on May 30, 2007, before trial (AP; TIME; the appeals opinion cites the $67 million figure). |
| 2. "In 2005, Roy Pearson dropped off his suit pants for a $10.50 alteration." | He left the pants of a suit at Custom Cleaners for alterations on May 3, 2005; the bill was $10.50 (ABC News/AP, June 2007; appeals opinion). |
| 3. "They went missing. The family who ran the shop found a pair. He said, 'These are not my pants.'" | The pants were not ready on the promised day; the owners, Soo Chung, Jin Nam Chung and their son Ki Chung, later produced a pair of charcoal-gray cuffed pants they said were his. He testified "These are not my pants." (ABC/AP; appeals opinion). The court found he had not proved they were not his. |
| 4. "They offered $3,000. Then $4,600. Then $12,000. He said no." | AP reported three settlement offers of $3,000, $4,600 and $12,000, all rejected; the ABA Journal confirms the $12,000 offer. |
| 5. "His case? Two signs on the wall. Satisfaction Guaranteed. Same Day Service." | He sued under the D.C. Consumer Protection Procedures Act (plus common-law fraud) over the shop's "Satisfaction Guaranteed" and "Same Day Service" signs (AP; ABA Journal; appeals opinion). |
| 6. "He charged $1,500 per violation, per day, for each of three owners." | His figure was $1,500 per violation per day, x 12 violations x about 1,200 days x 3 defendants (AP, April 2007). He also asked $15,000 to rent a car every weekend for 10 years to reach another cleaner (not used in the script). |
| 7. "On the stand, he cried." | He broke down in tears while testifying at the June 12, 2007 trial, and testimony paused (ABC News/AP, June 12-13, 2007). |
| 8. "The verdict: no reasonable customer reads that sign as a promise to meet unreasonable demands." | Judge Bartnoff ruled for the Chungs on June 25, 2007: "A reasonable consumer would not interpret 'Satisfaction Guaranteed' to mean that a merchant is required to satisfy a customer's unreasonable demands" (AP via ABC News and NBC News). The script paraphrases this. The stamp says DENIED as shorthand for "his claims were rejected, judgment for the defendants." |
| 9. "Pearson got zero dollars, about $1,000 in court costs, and soon lost his job as a judge." | He was awarded $0 and ordered to pay the Chungs' court costs of "just over $1,000" (AP via Fox News; ABC News). A commission voted not to reappoint him as an administrative law judge (reported Oct. 2007; the city confirmed it Nov. 14, 2007, ABA Journal). His term had expired in May 2007, and the commission also weighed his judicial temperament, so "lost his job" is the plain-language version that AP and the ABA Journal used. The Chungs' separate ~$83,000 attorney-fee motion was withdrawn on Aug. 13, 2007 (ABA Journal), so the script does not mention it. |
| On-screen: case card "2005-2007 · WASHINGTON, D.C."; verdict sub "JUNE 25, 2007"; tag "NOV. 2007 / NOT REAPPOINTED" | Dates as above. |

Later, for a follow-up: the D.C. Court of Appeals affirmed on Dec. 18, 2008 (his reading of "Same Day Service" "frankly
defies logic"); the Chungs closed that shop in 2007; he later received a 90-day law-license suspension (upheld June 4, 2020).

Characters are generic stylized figures: no one's likeness is used; names appear only in the narration and on the
case-file card. The newspaper masthead ("The Daily Chronicle") is fictional, and the headlines are generic paraphrases.
