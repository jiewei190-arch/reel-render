A judge sued his dry cleaner for $54 million over one pair of pants.
In 2005, Roy Pearson dropped off his suit pants for a $10.50 alteration.
They went missing. The family who ran the shop found a pair. He said, "These are not my pants."
They offered $3,000. Then $4,600. Then $12,000. He said no.
His case? Two signs on the wall. Satisfaction Guaranteed. Same Day Service.
He charged $1,500 per violation, per day, for each of three owners.
On the stand, he cried.
The verdict: no reasonable customer reads that sign as a promise to meet unreasonable demands.
Pearson got zero dollars, about $1,000 in court costs, and soon lost his job as a judge.
