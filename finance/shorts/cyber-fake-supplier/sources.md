# Sources: "The fake supplier" (cyber3d demo)

Case: the business-email-compromise (BEC) scheme run by Evaldas Rimasauskas of Vilnius, Lithuania, 2013-2015
(US District Court, Southern District of New York). All quotes were read on the pages below on 2026-11-21.

| line | claim in the script / on screen | source and wording |
|---|---|---|
| 1, hook | "got Google and Facebook to wire him over $100 million" | DOJ SDNY plea release, 20 Mar 2019: the scheme "induced two U.S.-based Internet companies (the 'Victim Companies') to wire a total of over $100 million to bank accounts he controlled." DOJ does not name the companies; they were identified by Fortune (27 Apr 2017: "Facebook confirmed it was one of the victims") and by NPR / WGBH (25 Mar 2019: "the two tech giants confirmed to NPR they are Victim-1 and Victim-2"). |
| 2 | "He didn't hack them. He sent emails." | DOJ: "fraudulent phishing emails were sent to employees and agents of the Victim Companies ... These emails purported to be from employees and agents of Company-1". No intrusion is alleged; the charge is wire fraud. |
| 3 | "a real hardware maker in Taiwan" | DOJ: "an Asian-based computer hardware manufacturer ('Company-1')" with which the victims "regularly conducted multimillion-dollar transactions". Fortune: "Quanta Computer, which was founded in Taiwan in 1988 ... In late March, Quanta publicly acknowledged it was the innocent supplier named in the indictment." (The Short never shows Quanta's name or any logo.) |
| 4 | "a company in Latvia with the exact same name" | DOJ: "registered and incorporated a company in Latvia ('Company-2') that bore the same name as an Asian-based computer hardware manufacturer". |
| 5 | "fake emails, fake invoices, even fake corporate stamps" | DOJ: "forged invoices, contracts, and letters that falsely appeared to have been executed and signed by executives and agents of the Victim Companies, and which bore false corporate stamps embossed with the Victim Companies' names, to be submitted to banks". |
| 6 | "From 2013 to 2015 ... Over $120 million in total" | DOJ SDNY sentencing release, 19 Dec 2019: "From at least in or around 2013 through in or about 2015 ... caused the Victim Companies to transfer a total of over $120,000,000". (The earlier releases say "over $100 million"; the hook uses the lower, earlier figure and line 6 the final one.) Bloomberg via WGBH: "about $23 million from Google in 2013 and about $98 million from Facebook in 2015" (not used on screen). |
| 7 | "Cyprus, Slovakia, Hungary, Hong Kong" | DOJ: "caused the stolen funds to be quickly wired into different bank accounts in various locations throughout the world, including Latvia, Cyprus, Slovakia, Lithuania, Hungary, and Hong Kong." |
| 8 | "In 2017, Lithuanian authorities arrested him" | DOJ: "arrested by Lithuanian authorities in March 2017, pursuant to a provisional arrest warrant". |
| 9 | "Extradited to New York. Five years in prison." | DOJ: "extradited to the Southern District of New York in August 2017"; "sentenced today to 60 months in prison" (19 Dec 2019). Also: "to forfeit $49,738,559.41, and to pay restitution in the amount of $26,479,079.24" (not used on screen). |
| 10 | "a supplier suddenly sends new bank details. Call them on a number you already trust." | FBI IC3 public service announcement I-060923-PSA (9 Jun 2023), BEC tips: "Use secondary channels or two-factor authentication to verify requests for changes in account information." |
| case card | NAME E. RIMASAUSKAS / YEARS 2013-2015 / STOLEN $120M+ / CAUGHT | DOJ sentencing release (above). |
| checklist | NEW BANK DETAILS / CHECK THE SENDER / CALL A KNOWN NUMBER | IC3 PSA (above): "Use secondary channels ... to verify requests for changes in account information." and "Verify the email address used to send emails ... ensuring the sender's address appears to match who it is coming from." |

Recovery note (for the description, not the script): both companies said they recovered all or most of the money (NPR via WGBH; Facebook to Fortune: "Facebook recovered the bulk of the funds shortly after the incident").
