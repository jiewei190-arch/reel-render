# mortgage-year-ten: script (draft for MAIN)

- **Style:** S2 RICH-VS-POOR (split: same house, 30-year vs 15-year loan)
- **Voice:** Tyler
- **Hook text (frame 0, ≤ 5 words):** $400K HOUSE. REAL PRICE?
- **Frame 0:** CLOSE on a plain blocky homebuyer face (Spender, unnamed until line 2) grinning, holding a house key; a red "$400,000 LOAN" block slams down beside the head (impact at 0.00 s), then the grin turns to worry at about 1 s.
- **Two-person race:** split screen from line 2. Two counters, PAID and STILL OWE, for each of them. Spender's PAID ticks up fast while STILL OWE barely moves; Saver's STILL OWE falls visibly.
- **Mechanism visual (line 3):** an x-ray of one $2,690 payment: a big red INTEREST stack ($2,370) slides to the bank vault; a thin green sliver ($320) lands on the house.
- **Open loop (≈ 48-56 %):** "But wait till you see year ten..." with a wall calendar flipping YEAR 1 → 2 → 3 ..., the YEAR 10 page still covered.
- **Twist (≈ 57-77 %):** YEAR 10 reveals. Spender: PAID $323K slams, STILL OWE $344K slams on top of it (PAID < OWE). Spender collapses. Saver's split: STILL OWE $178K.
- **Payoff / loop:** "Spender's house costs almost a million dollars." A $968K price tag stamps on the little house → loops to frame 0's "$400,000 LOAN" block.
- **Eggs:** the blocky cat in the bank stealing a coin from the vault pile; sticky note on the calendar: "YEAR 30: FREE HOUSE?". Final gag: catCoin.
- **Rules checked:** line 1 has "you", a dollar figure and no names (9 words); names after 2 s; 79 words; hedged ("about", "in this example"); no brands, no picks, no CTA, no advice (both choices shown with their trade-off: Saver pays more each month).

## VO (one line per row; `script.txt` is the same text)
1. You borrow four hundred thousand dollars for a house.
2. Same house. Spender takes thirty years at about seven percent. Saver takes fifteen.
3. Spender's first payment: about twenty-seven hundred dollars. Only three hundred twenty pays off the house.
4. But wait till you see year ten...
5. Ten years in, Spender has paid three hundred twenty-three thousand... and still owes three hundred forty-four thousand.
6. Saver paid more monthly, and owes under half.
7. In this example, Spender's house costs almost a million dollars.
