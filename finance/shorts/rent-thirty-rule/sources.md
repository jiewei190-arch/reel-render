# Sources and maths: rent-thirty-rule

| claim (VO) | source | 
|---|---|
| "It started with a 1969 law for public housing. It capped rent at 25 percent of a tenant's income." | NLIHC, *A Brief Historical Overview of Affordable Rental Housing* (Advocates' Guide 2025): "In 1969, Congress passed the Brooke Amendment, codifying a limitation on the percentage of income a public housing resident could be expected to pay in rent. The original figure was 25% of a person's total income and was later raised to the 30% standard that exists today." |
| "In 1981, Congress raised that cap to 30 percent." | Harvard JCHS, *Rent Eats First* (Airgood-Obrycki, Hermann, Wedeen, 2021): "...the 1981 Brooke Amendment raising the cap that households in public housing programs should spend from 25 percent of income to 30 percent of income (US Department of Housing and Urban Development, n.d.; Pelletiere, 2008)." (The 1981 change was made by the Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1981, which amended the Brooke rule.) |
| "a public housing cap became everyone's rule of thumb" | JCHS (same paper): "the percent or ratio standard has remained the most widely used method" for measuring housing affordability; HUD's cost-burden definition (paying more than 30 % of income for housing) |
| "On 4,000 dollars a month, that cap means 1,200 for rent." | stated example: 4,000 × 0.30 = 1,200 |
| "A limit to protect tenants, not a spending target." | NLIHC/JCHS describe it as a cap/limit on what tenants could be charged |

Sources:
- NLIHC: https://nlihc.org/sites/default/files/AG-2025/1-8_A-Brief-Historical-Overview-of-Affordable.pdf
- Harvard JCHS, *Rent Eats First*: https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/research/files/harvard_jchs_rent_eats_first_airgood-obrycki_hermann_wedeen_2021.pdf
- HUD PD&R Edge, "Rental Burdens: Rethinking Affordability Measures" (Sep 22, 2014): https://www.huduser.gov/portal/pdredge/pdr_edge_featd_article_092214.html (could not be fetched from the cloud; cited by the sources above)

Hedges: the VO names no person (the amendment is named after a senator); "rule of thumb" is a description, not a law for private renters.
