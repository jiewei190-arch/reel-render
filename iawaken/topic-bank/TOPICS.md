# I.Awaken Shorts topic bank: 30 ideas, ranked best-first

Researched Nov 8 2026 for the **SHORTS-ONLY FOCUS** formula in `IAWAKEN_PLAYBOOK.md` (branch
`claude/beautiful-hypatia-0w2o96`): frame 1 = craziest image, Line 1 <= 10 words, twist by 3-5 s, 2-3 escalations,
payoff in the last 3-5 s, loop, 25-40 s, Toon 3D bean cast, deadpan narrator, no gore.

- **20 CRAZY TRUE STORIES** (tag `TRUE`) + **10 WHAT IF** science ideas (tag `WHAT IF`), mixed into one ranking.
- **Already on the channel, so excluded:** Rome sacked, the prison story (reference channel), See in the Dark, Jump
  Doubled, Train 3 Years, Freeze Time, Spider Powers, Level Up, Zombie Apocalypse, Moon Disappeared, Ant Size, Earth Stops
  Spinning, Hole Through the Earth, Black Hole.
- **Closer:** "Subscribe for more." unless the Short ends on a cliffhanger or Part 1 ("Subscribe to find out.").
- **Virality score:** my judgment from (a) the "wait, what?" factor of Line 1, (b) how widely the story is already
  told (big outlets, memes, existing viral retellings = proven demand), (c) how visual and shareable it is in Toon 3D,
  and (d) risk (tone, disputed facts). I did **not** measure search volumes or view counts; "evidence of demand" below
  means coverage by major outlets and meme status, not numbers.
- **Accuracy:** every claim below comes from the listed sources. Where sources disagree or a detail is a legend, it is
  flagged **SAY "according to..."**. Numbers marked *(computed)* are my arithmetic from sourced figures.
- **Tone guard:** two topics (#15 Yamaguchi, #23 Beer Flood) involve real deaths. They are included because they were
  suggested and have proven demand, but they must be told with awe/empathy, never as jokes about the deaths.

## 1. The Great Emu War (Australia, 1932) [TRUE]
1. **Line 1:** "Australia declared war on birds. And lost."
2. **Twist:** "The army brought machine guns. The emus brought... running."
3. **Escalation:**
   - About 20,000 emus are eating the wheat; soldiers arrive with two Lewis machine guns.
   - A gunner mounts a gun on a truck; the emus are faster than the truck on rough ground and it can't aim.
   - Thousands of rounds later the official tally is under a thousand birds, and the army goes home.
4. **Payoff:** "The army left. The emus stayed. Officially: Emus 1, Army 0." (Loop: cut back to the emu "general".)
5. **Pinned question:** "Emus or army: who really won?"
6. **Title:** "Australia Lost a War to Birds 🐦" (31 chars)
7. **Virality: 10/10.** The best-known "wait, what?" war in history: a long-running internet meme, covered by Britannica,
   National Geographic and BBC Wildlife. Huge comedy potential (bean soldiers vs smug emus), no human deaths.
8. **Sources:** [Britannica: Emu War](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Emu-War) ·
   [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/australian-emu-war-history) ·
   [BBC Wildlife / Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/animal-facts/birds/the-emu-war-of-1932)
   **SAY "according to...":** kill counts and rounds fired vary between accounts; say "the commander reported 986
   birds" (Major Meredith's report). Show emus running and fleeing, never hit.

## 2. The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice. [TRUE]
1. **Line 1:** "This man sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice."
2. **Twist:** "He didn't own it."
3. **Escalation:**
   - 1925: posing as a French official, he tells scrap dealers Paris will tear the tower down for its 7,000 tons of metal.
   - A dealer, André Poisson, pays up, and Lustig vanishes.
   - Months later he comes back and runs the exact same scam on new dealers.
4. **Payoff:** "This time a dealer went to the police. Lustig just... left town again." (Loop: "This man sold the Eiffel
   Tower..." over the tower.)
5. **Pinned question:** "Would you have bought it: yes or no?"
6. **Title:** "He Sold the Eiffel Tower… Twice 🗼" (33 chars)
7. **Virality: 9/10.** Famous one-liner, Smithsonian has run two long features on him. The con is easy to follow in 30 s,
   and the "he did it AGAIN" beat is a built-in second laugh. No violence.
8. **Sources:** [Smithsonian: The Man Who Sold the Eiffel Tower. Twice.](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/man-who-sold-eiffel-tower-twice-180958370/) ·
   [Smithsonian: The Smoothest Con Man That Ever Lived](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-smoothest-con-man-that-ever-lived-29861908/) ·
   [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2020/01/14/financial-scams-lessons-eiffel-tower-sale/)
   **SAY "according to...":** the number of dealers and why the first victim stayed quiet vary by account; say
   "according to the story".

## 3. The 1904 Olympic Marathon [TRUE]
1. **Line 1:** "The Olympic marathon winner rode in a car."
2. **Twist:** "He got the gold medal. Then someone noticed."
3. **Escalation:**
   - Fred Lorz drops out at mile 9, rides in a car until it breaks down near mile 19, then jogs in "first".
   - The real winner, Thomas Hicks, is kept going by his team with strychnine mixed with egg white and brandy (legal then).
   - A Cuban runner stops at an orchard, eats rotten apples, takes a nap... and still finishes fourth.
4. **Payoff:** "Slowest Olympic winning time in history, by half an hour." (Loop: back to the car.)
5. **Pinned question:** "Car guy or poison guy: who's worse?"
6. **Title:** "The Olympic Winner Rode in a Car 🚗" (34 chars)
7. **Virality: 9/10.** Three absurd characters in one race = three escalation beats for free. Covered by Smithsonian,
   History.com and the IOC itself. Very shareable among runners.
8. **Sources:** [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/how-the-1904-marathon-became-one-of-the-weirdest-olympic-events-of-all-time-14910747/) ·
   [Olympics.com (IOC)](https://www.olympics.com/ioc/news/the-strange-case-of-the-st-louis-1904-marathon) ·
   [History.com](https://www.history.com/news/the-olympic-marathons-outlandish-early-history)
   **SAY "according to...":** the rotten-apples-and-nap story (Andarín Carvajal) is widely told but a historian says
   there's no record of it; say "according to legend". Don't show poisoning as a joke on Hicks; frame it as "1904
   sports science".

6. **Title:** "Banned From Having a Dog, He Got a Bear 🐻" (41 chars)
7. **Virality: 9/10.** Huge "loophole" energy, a well-known viral fact, and the University of Cambridge itself tells it.
   The cast's HERO as a bean poet + the bear = great comedy.
8. **Sources:** [University of Cambridge](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/lord-byron-and-the-bears-beneath-cambridge) ·

## 6. What If You Went to Space Without a Suit? [WHAT IF]

   10-second timer.)
5. **Pinned question:** "Would you risk 10 seconds: yes or no?"
6. **Title:** "What If You Went to Space Without a Suit? 🚀" (43 chars)
7. **Virality: 9/10.** One of the most searched space questions; "myth-busting the movies" beats are very shareable.
   Mina's stopwatch makes the countdown a natural visual.
8. **Sources:** [Guinness World Records: 1966 NASA vacuum chamber accident](https://guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/447311-highest-equivalent-altitude-exposure-survived) ·
   [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/human-body-no-spacesuit) ·
   [BBC Sky at Night](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-missions/astronaut-take-off-helmet-in-space)
   **Softened claims:** say "about 10 seconds" (NASA data puts conscious time at roughly 9-11 s; the 1966 volunteer
   lasted about 14 s). Don't name the volunteer (keep him "a NASA test volunteer").

## 13. What If the Sun Disappeared? [WHAT IF]
1. **Line 1:** "The Sun just vanished. You won't know for 8 minutes."
2. **Twist:** "Even gravity takes 8 minutes to get the news."
3. **Escalation:**
   - For 8 min 20 s the sky still looks normal: that light already left the Sun.
   - Earth keeps orbiting nothing, because gravity's change travels at light speed too.
   - Then the lights go out and Earth flies off in a straight line into space.
4. **Payoff:** "The last sunset you ever see... was 8 minutes old." (Loop: a sunny picnic, normal.)
5. **Pinned question:** "Would you rather know or not know?"
6. **Title:** "What If the Sun Vanished? You'd Have 8 Minutes ☀️" (49 chars)
7. **Virality: 8/10.** Classic proven science question (Vsauce, Neil deGrasse Tyson talk about it). The "8 minutes"
   twist is the kind people share. Visual: the cast at a picnic, timer, then darkness.
8. **Sources:** [NRAO (National Radio Astronomy Observatory)](https://public.nrao.edu/?p=32404) ·
   [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/planet-earth/what-would-happen-to-earth-if-the-sun-suddenly-vanished) ·
   [Universe Today: how fast is gravity](https://www.universetoday.com/121284/how-fast-is-gravity/)
   **Softened:** 8 min 20 s is an average; the Sun can't actually vanish (say "imagine").
