# Sources: "What If You Fell Into a Black Hole?"

1. NASA Scientific Visualization Studio, "NASA Black Hole Visualization Takes Viewers Beyond the Brink" (ID 14576, May 6 2024).
   https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/14576
   - "The destination is a supermassive black hole with 4.3 million times the mass of our Sun, equivalent to the monster located at the center of our Milky Way galaxy."
   - "At 42 seconds, the camera crosses the event horizon" ... "After entering the event horizon, the camera would be destroyed by tidal forces 12.8 seconds later."
   -> supports: you can cross a supermassive black hole's horizon intact; "millions of suns heavy".
2. NASA Imagine the Universe, "Black Holes". https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/black_holes1.html
   - "The event horizon is the "point of no return" around the black hole."
   - stellar-mass: "masses about 5-20 times that of the sun"; supermassive: "masses millions to billions times that of the sun".
   - "When a star wanders too close to a black hole, intense tidal forces rip the star apart."
3. NASA Science, "Black Holes". https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/
   - Spaghettification: "A real term that describes what happens when matter gets too close to a black hole. It's squeezed horizontally and stretched vertically, resembling a noodle."
   - Sagittarius A* "is 4 million times the Sun's mass."
4. Wikipedia, "Spaghettification" (section "Relation to the event horizon"; cites Hawley & Holcomb 2005, Hobson et al. 2006, Kutner 2003).
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaghettification
   - "For a supermassive black hole ... this point lies within the event horizon. As a result, an astronaut may cross the event horizon without noticing any significant tidal forces."
   - "Stellar black holes have much higher spacetime curvature at their event horizon ... so the tidal forces would cause the astronaut to undergo spaghettification before the event horizon."

## Wording choices / softened
- "without even noticing" follows source 4 ("without noticing any significant tidal forces"); not "you survive forever" (source 1: destroyed 12.8 s later inside).
- "small black hole" = stellar-mass (5-20 Suns, source 2); the on-screen "10 SUNS" is an example inside that range.
- "feet harder than your head" = tidal force for a feet-first fall (sources 3, 4).
