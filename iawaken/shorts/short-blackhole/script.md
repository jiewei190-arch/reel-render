## Facts used (all in sources.md)
- Tidal force: gravity is stronger on the near end of a body than the far end, so a falling body is stretched (spaghettification).
- Near a stellar-mass black hole (about 5-20 Suns) this stretch tears you apart before the event horizon.
- For a supermassive black hole (millions to billions of Suns) the deadly stretch only happens inside the horizon, so you can
  cross the horizon ("point of no return") without noticing significant tidal forces.
- NASA's 2024 visualization: a 4.3-million-Sun black hole (like Sagittarius A*), the camera crosses the horizon intact and is
  only destroyed by tidal forces 12.8 s later.
