# Sources: pig-war (every spoken claim)
Checked Nov 8 2026. Primary source: National Park Service (NPS), San Juan Island National Historical Park.
- **NPS-1** The Pig War: https://www.nps.gov/sajh/learn/historyculture/the-pig-war.htm
- **NPS-2** Kaiser Wil-Ham statue: https://www.nps.gov/places/kaiser-wil-ham-statue.htm
- **NPS-3** News release, Oct 7 2022 (statue unveiled Sep 22 2022): https://www.nps.gov/sajh/learn/news/san-juan-island-nhp-american-camp-visitor-center-installs-public-art-and-announces-winter-hours.htm
- **WIKI** Pig War (1859), cross-check only: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_War_(1859)

| # | Line | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "America and Britain almost went to war over a pig." | A pig's shooting set off a US-British military standoff over the island. | NPS-1 |
| 2 | "The pig was the only casualty." | NPS: the "war" "in which the only casualty was a pig"; WIKI: "no human casualties on either side, only a pig". | NPS-1, WIKI |
| 3 | "It's 1859. Both countries claim one island." | Both the US and Britain claimed San Juan Island (unclear 1846 Oregon Treaty boundary); the incident is June 15, 1859. | NPS-1 |
| 4 | "A British company's pig eats an American farmer's potatoes." | NPS-1: Lyman Cutlar, an American, shot "a company pig rooting in his garden" (Hudson's Bay Company). NPS-2: the pig was "rooting in the small farm plot of American farmer Lyman Cutlar", "the potato filching pig"; NPS-3: "theft of potatoes from an American farmer". | NPS-1, NPS-2, NPS-3 |
| 5 | "So he shoots it." | "shot and killed a company pig". Shown off-screen: we only see the farmer and a comic "BANG!" card; the pig is never shown hurt. | NPS-1 |
| 6 | "Britain threatens to arrest him. The US Army lands. Britain sends warships." | British authorities threatened to arrest Cutlar; Capt. George Pickett's 64-man Company D, 9th US Infantry landed July 27, 1859; Capt. Geoffrey Hornby's HMS Tribune, joined by HMS Satellite and HMS Plumper. No troop/gun numbers are spoken (NPS and WIKI disagree: 52 vs 70 guns). | NPS-1 |
| 7 | "All over one pig." | Restatement of 1/4. | NPS-1 |
| 8 | "But a British admiral refuses to fight over a pig." | Rear Admiral Baynes would not "involve two great nations in a war over a squabble about a pig". | NPS-1, WIKI |
| 9 | "So both armies share the island. For twelve years." | "San Juan Island remained under joint military occupation for the next 12 years." | NPS-1 |
| 10 | "Then Germany's emperor settles it. The US gets the island." | Referred to Kaiser Wilhelm I of Germany; on Oct 21, 1872 "the commission, through the kaiser, ruled in favor of the United States". | NPS-1 |
| 11 | "And the pig gets a bronze statue. With a potato in its mouth." | NPS-3: "a life-size bronze statue of a Berkshire boar" that "depicts a pig with a potato in its mouth", by sculptor Robert MacDonald, at the American Camp Visitor Center; named Kaiser Wil-Ham (NPS-2). | NPS-2, NPS-3 |

**Legend / disputed parts:** none spoken. The famous Cutlar/Griffin "keep your potatoes out of my pig" exchange is called "likely apocryphal" by WIKI, so it is NOT used. Dollar amounts ($10 vs $100) are WIKI-only, NOT used.
**On-screen text** uses only the facts above (SAN JUAN ISLAND, 1859; 12 YEARS; 1872).
**Tone:** no gore; the shot happens off-screen (comic "BANG!" card, birds flap off); soldiers are comic, nobody is hurt.
