# Sources: What If the Earth Stopped Spinning for One Second?

| # | Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The ground (Earth's surface) moves east because Earth rotates west-to-east once a day; we don't feel it because everything around us moves with it at a steady speed. | Space Centre NZ, "How fast does the Earth rotate?" https://www.spacecentre.nz/resources/faq/solar-system/earth/rotation-speed.html ; ScienceABC, "Why don't we feel the Earth spin on its axis?" https://www.scienceabc.com/eyeopeners/why-dont-we-feel-the-earth-spin-on-its-axis.html |
| 2 | About 1,670 km/h (about 1,040 mph) at the equator; slower at higher latitudes (about 1,180 km/h at 45 deg, 0 at the poles). | Space Centre NZ (1,674.4 km/h at the equator), link above; WorldAtlas, "How fast is the Earth moving?" https://www.worldatlas.com/space/how-fast-is-the-earth-moving.html ; BBC Sky at Night Magazine, "How fast does the Earth spin?" https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/how-fast-does-earth-spin |
| 3 | If the rotation stopped suddenly, you, the air and the oceans would keep moving east at the old speed (inertia); everything not anchored to bedrock would be swept along. | Space.com, "What would happen if Earth suddenly stopped spinning?" https://www.space.com/what-if-earth-stopped-spinning.html ; BBC Sky at Night Magazine, "What would happen if Earth stopped spinning?" https://skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/what-happen-earth-stopped-rotating ; Science Notes, "What would happen if the Earth stopped rotating" https://sciencenotes.org/what-would-happen-if-the-earth-stopped-rotating/ (the "anchored to bedrock" wording is commonly attributed to NASA's Ask an Astrophysicist; attributed loosely here, not quoted). |
| 4 | "Faster than a jet": airliners cruise around 900 km/h (about Mach 0.8-0.85), well under 1,670 km/h. | Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Jet airliner / airplane: cruising speed" https://www.britannica.com/technology/airplane ; Boeing 787 cruise Mach 0.85 https://www.boeing.com/commercial/787 |

Softened / framing choices
- "About" 1,670 km/h and "at the equator" (the exact value depends on rounding and latitude; ~1,674 km/h is also quoted).
- The scenario is a thought experiment; the script does not claim specific death tolls, earthquakes or survival odds.
