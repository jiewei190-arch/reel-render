# Sources: "Australia Lost a War to Birds" (I.Awaken Short, emu-war)

Checked Nov 8 2026. Britannica (topic bank source) returned 403 to the fetcher, so claims are checked against National
Geographic, BBC Discover Wildlife and Wikipedia (which cites the academic source, Johnson 2006).

| Line | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Australia "declared war" on birds "and lost" | Popular framing of the 1932 "Emu War": the army's culling operation (Royal Australian Artillery, two Lewis guns) was called off without reducing the emus. Discover Wildlife headline: "declared war on 20,000 emus"; National Geographic: the government called off the operation. Comic hyperbole, not a formal war. |
| 3 | The army brought machine guns | National Geographic: "on November 2, 1932, three soldiers from the Royal Australian Artillery arrived with two Lewis light machine guns." Wikipedia: "two Lewis guns" and 10,000 rounds (Robin et al. 2009). |
| 4 | The emus brought... running | Comic line. Emus can run fast: National Geographic quotes truck drivers reporting birds "sprinting over uneven ground". |
| 5 | 1932, about 20,000 emus eating the wheat in Western Australia | Wikipedia: "the arrival of approximately 20,000 emus" (Gill 2007), wheat farmers in the Campion district, WA. Discover Wildlife: "20,000 emus turned up in the towns of Chandler and Walgoolan." National Geographic: "nearly 20,000 emus into the farming areas." |
| 6-7 | A gun was mounted on a truck; the emus were faster than the truck | Wikipedia, citing Johnson (2006): Meredith "mount[ed] one of the guns on a truck, a move that proved to be ineffective, as the truck was unable to gain on the birds, and the ride was so rough that the gunner was unable to fire any shots." Discover Wildlife: "Efforts to shoot them from the backs of moving trucks also failed." |
| 8 | Thousands of rounds later, **the commander reported 986 birds** (said as "reported", per the topic bank's "SAY according to" flag) | Wikipedia, citing Johnson, M. (2006), "'Feathered foes': soldier settlers and Western Australia's 'Emu War' of 1932", *Journal of Australian Studies* 30(88): Meredith "in his report ... claimed 986 confirmed kills with 9,860 rounds". Historian G. Beyer (2023): "highly disputed and ... likely to be very inflated." Other accounts differ (National Geographic: about 2,500; Discover Wildlife: "low hundreds" after six days). |
| 9 | Out of twenty thousand | ~20,000 (line 5). |
| 10 | The army went home. The emus stayed. | Wikipedia: Meredith was recalled on 10 December 1932 (a first withdrawal on 8 November, a second attempt from 13 November). National Geographic: "the government called off the operation." The emus remained a problem; later farmers used fences and a bounty system (Wikipedia). |
| 11 | "Final score: Emus, one. Army, zero." | Comic summary of the outcome above (the army withdrew without achieving its aim), not an official score. |

Extra (not spoken): Major G. P. W. Meredith later said emus "can face machine guns with the invulnerability of tanks" (Sunday Herald, Sydney, 5 July 1953, quoted by Wikipedia; also National Geographic and Discover Wildlife).

## What is shown (tone guards)
- No gore and no animal is ever hit: the emus run, dodge, eat and look smug; the gun never hits anything (the truck
  bounces so much the gunner can't fire, which is also the historical account). Soldiers are comic bean characters.
- All characters are original Toon 3D designs (bean soldiers, the commander, the smug emu "general"). The 1932 uniforms
  and slouch hats are simplified. The scoreboard and the emu's tiny cap are comic props.
- Cast cameo: BISCUIT the cat watches from a fence post (not historical).
