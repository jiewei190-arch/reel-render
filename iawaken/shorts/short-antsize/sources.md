# Sources: What If You Shrank to the Size of an Ant?

| # | Claim (as spoken / shown) | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Small animals fall slowly and survive long falls; insects "can fall without danger". Weight scales with volume (length^3), air resistance with surface (length^2). | J. B. S. Haldane, "On Being the Right Size" (1926/1928), https://www.marxists.org/archive/haldane/works/1920s/right-size.htm | "You can drop a mouse down a thousand-yard mine shaft ... it gets a slight shock and walks away." |
| 2 | An ant's terminal velocity in air is small, "no more than two meters per second" (~7 km/h, walking/jogging pace). | University of Illinois Physics, Ask the Van, "An ant Dropped off the Empire State Building", https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/2099 | Physicist's estimate; on screen as "~7 km/h", spoken as "walking speed" (softened). |
| 3 | Same scaling: gravity ~ length^3, air resistance ~ length^2, so the bigger body falls faster. | Same Illinois page, follow-up #1 | |
| 4 | A human in free fall reaches around 200 km/h (belly-down skydiver, ~120 mph / ~55 m/s). | NASA Glenn Research Center, "Terminal Velocity", https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/termv.html ; Encyclopaedia Britannica, "terminal velocity", https://www.britannica.com/science/terminal-velocity | Spoken as "around two hundred", shown "~200 km/h". |
| 5 | Surface tension is as formidable to an insect as gravity to a mammal; a wetted insect "is likely to remain so until it drowns"; a wet fly lifts many times its own weight in water. | Haldane, "On Being the Right Size" (as #1) | Basis for "one raindrop ... won't let go" and "water is scarier than gravity". |
| 6 | A raindrop can weigh ~50x a mosquito; insects hit in flight get carried on the front of the drop before breaking free. | Dickerson, Shankles, Madhavan & Hu, PNAS 109 (2012) 9822, "Mosquitoes survive raindrop collisions by virtue of their low mass"; Georgia Tech news release via https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/06/120604155558.htm | Supports the drop "landing on you and holding on" (visual); the impact itself is not called lethal. |

Softened: the ant fall speed (#2) is an estimate, spoken only as "walking speed"; "won't let go" / "scarier than gravity" are Haldane's surface-tension argument, not a measured survival rate.
