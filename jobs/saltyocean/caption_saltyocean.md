Why is the ocean salty when rain and rivers are fresh? 🌊 It starts with rain. Rain picks up a little carbon dioxide, so it's slightly acidic, and over time it wears down rock. Minerals dissolve as ions, and streams and rivers carry them to the sea. When the sun warms the sea, nearly pure water evaporates, but the salt stays behind, so it builds up over time. Hydrothermal vents and underwater volcanoes add more from below. Seawater is about 3.5% salt (35 parts per thousand), mostly sodium and chloride.

Sources: NOAA Ocean Service; USGS Water Science School; H. Swenson, "Why Is the Ocean Salty?" (USGS).
