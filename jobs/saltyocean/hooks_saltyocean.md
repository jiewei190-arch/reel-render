# saltyocean: opening lines (pick marked ★)

1. ★ "Why is the ocean salty, when rain and rivers are fresh?" (10 words: the search question plus the paradox). Frame 0: a huge ink-and-wash breaking wave fills the lower two-thirds with foam claws, and salt crystals tumble in its spray; "why is the sea salty?" is written in the sky in serif. At 1.8 s a fresh river spills in from the left, labelled "fresh". The but-turn at 4.0 s: "But that fresh water is the whole secret." The camera tilts up into the rain clouds.
2. "The sea is full of melted rock." (7 words). Punchy, but it's a stretch: the ions come from weathered rock, not melted rock.
3. "There's enough salt in the sea to bury the land." (10 words). A great fact, but it gives the act-4 reveal away and isn't the searched question.

Why ★1: it's the question people type, and the paradox (rain and rivers are fresh) makes the answer feel earned.
Re-hooks: 22.1 s "Here's the catch": water rises, the salt can't (one sodium bead jumps and falls back). 35.9 s "the sea is salted from below, too" (a dive to the sea floor: vents and ink plumes). 49.8 s "more than 500 feet" (the salt layer buries a house).
Payoff 54.7 s: "So seawater is about 3.5% salt, mostly sodium and chloride", then "Rain brings it in. The sun leaves it behind." Question: "Did you know the sea's salt came from rocks? Yes or no?"
