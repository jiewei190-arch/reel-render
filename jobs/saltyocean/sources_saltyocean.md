# saltyocean: sources

1. NOAA Ocean Service, "Why is the ocean salty?" https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/whysalty.html
   - Rain is slightly acidic and erodes rock. Streams and rivers carry the dissolved ions to the sea.
   - Marine organisms use some of the ions; others are left and build up over time.
   - Seawater seeps into cracks in the seafloor, is heated by magma, picks up minerals and comes back out of vents.
   - Underwater volcanic eruptions release minerals into the ocean.
   - Chloride and sodium are about 85% of the dissolved ions.
   - Average salinity is about 35 parts per thousand, about 3.5% of seawater's weight.
2. USGS Water Science School, "Why is the ocean salty?" https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/science/why-ocean-salty and USGS FAQ https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/why-ocean-salty
   - Rain picks up carbon dioxide, which makes it slightly acidic, so it breaks down rock and the ions run off to the ocean.
   - Salinity is about 35 parts per thousand.
   - Hydrothermal vents at oceanic ridges circulate seawater through the crust.
   - Spread over the land, the ocean's salt would make "a layer more than 500 feet (166 meters) thick".
   - Chloride and sodium are over 90% of the dissolved ions.
3. Herbert Swenson, "Why Is the Ocean Salty?", USGS general-interest publication (mirror: https://ponce.sdsu.edu/usgs_why_is_the_ocean_salty/usgs_why_is_the_ocean_salty.html)
   - Sunlight evaporates nearly pure water from the sea surface, and the dissolved salts are left behind.
   - River water carries only small amounts of salt, so rivers taste fresh.

Wording notes:
- "rain and rivers are fresh": rivers carry only small amounts of dissolved salt (source 3).
- "when the sun warms the sea, water rises into the air. The salt can't": evaporation removes nearly pure water (source 3).
- "Sea creatures use some of it. The rest builds up over time": source 1.
- "mostly sodium and chloride": NOAA says about 85% and USGS says over 90%, so the reel says "mostly".
- "the pair in table salt": table salt is sodium chloride.
