Why do you get carsick? 🚗 Your stomach isn't the problem. Your senses are arguing. Your inner ear's fluid-filled canals feel every turn and bump, so in the back seat it says "we're moving!", while your eyes, glued to a book or a phone, say "we're sitting still". Doctors call this sensory conflict, the most widely accepted explanation. Why it makes you feel sick is still debated: one theory says the brain treats the mix-up like poisoning. Kids get it most (it peaks around ages 7 to 12). The fix: sit up front, look at the horizon, put the phone down, lean into bends and keep your head still. And the more you ride, the more your brain gets used to it.

Sources: CDC Yellow Book, Motion Sickness; MedlinePlus (NIH); M. Treisman, Science (1977); Bárány Society consensus (2021).
