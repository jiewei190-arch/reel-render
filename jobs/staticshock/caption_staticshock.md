Why do you get more static shocks in winter? Every time your socks or shoes rub the carpet, tiny electrons hop from one material to the other, and your body piles up charge, up to thousands of volts (but only a tiny amount of charge, so it's just a sting). On humid days, a thin film of water on surfaces quietly drains that charge away. Cold winter air holds very little water, and heating it indoors doesn't add any, so the charge has nowhere to go... until you touch the doorknob. ⚡🚪

Sources: University of Kentucky Dept. of Physics & Astronomy; University of Wisconsin-Madison Physics ("Wonders of Physics"); University of Alaska Fairbanks Geophysical Institute (Alaska Science Forum); Northeastern University.
