# Sources: staticshock (why do you get more static shocks in winter?)

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| Rubbing two different materials (socks/shoes on carpet) moves electrons from one to the other, so your body builds up extra charge | University of Kentucky, Dept. of Physics & Astronomy, "Static Electricity" (SciWorks): https://www.pa.uky.edu/sciworks/THE/heat/static.htm [university]; University of Wisconsin-Madison, The Wonders of Physics, "What is electricity?": https://wonders.physics.wisc.edu/what-is-electricity/ [university] |
| You can be charged to thousands of volts, but the amount of charge is tiny, so it is only a sting | University of Kentucky, "Static electricity" (EM module): https://www.pa.uky.edu/sciworks/emmod/em/static.htm (voltages are very big while the charge is tiny; about 10,000 V makes a spark jump about 1 cm). The reel says "thousands of volts", with no exact number |
| Near metal, the charge jumps across the air as a spark | UW-Madison, Wonders of Physics (charge leaves you to the doorknob as a spark); UKY EM module |
| On humid days, surfaces carry a thin film of water that conducts the charge away | University of Kentucky, SciWorks "Static Electricity" |
| Cold air holds little water; heating it indoors doesn't add any, so indoor air is very dry | University of Kentucky, SciWorks "Static Electricity"; Larry Gedney, "The Wintertime Static Jump-Start", Alaska Science Forum, UAF Geophysical Institute (May 28 1985): https://www.gi.alaska.edu/alaska-science-forum/wintertime-static-jump-start ("the warmer air ... holds more moisture than does the cold air of winter"; "Dry air is such a poor conductor that a charge will build to very high levels") [university institute] |
| Tip: touch the metal with a key first, so the spark hits the key, not your finger | UAF Geophysical Institute, Alaska Science Forum (Gedney): "A good way to discharge yourself without making bodily contact is to carry a coin or a key around"; Northeastern University (Ivana Dimitrova, assistant professor of physics), Northeastern Global News, Jan 24 2025: https://news.northeastern.edu/?p=252933 ("You can use a key or a coin to touch a metal object") [university] |
| Pinned bonus: a humidifier helps | Northeastern Global News (Dimitrova): "adding humidifiers to your environment will add water molecules to the air"; UKY SciWorks (the water film) |
