How do glow-in-the-dark stickers glow? They don't make light; they give back light they stored. Light gives electrons a burst of energy. Normally they'd drop right back and the light would come out in a flash, but these stickers have traps, defects in the crystal that catch the excited electrons and hold them. In the dark, the room's warmth slowly shakes them loose, and each one that escapes gives off a tiny flash of green. That's phosphorescence. Older stickers used zinc sulfide with a pinch of copper and faded sooner; since the 1990s, strontium aluminate with europium (the green light) and dysprosium (the traps) can glow for hours. And it's not radioactive, unlike the radium paint on watch dials a century ago.
