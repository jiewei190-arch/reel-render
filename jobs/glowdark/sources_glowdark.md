# Sources: glowdark (How do glow-in-the-dark stickers glow?)
Researched Oct 28 2026. I found no ACS or RSC page on this in two searches; the mechanism is sourced from a 2020 Physical Review Letters study (via ESRF), NDSU and the Illinois Physics Van.

- ESRF (European Synchrotron) "Spotlight on Science", Jul 20 2020, on Joos J. J. et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. (2020): https://esrf.fr/home/news/spotlight/content-news/spotlight/spotlight365.html
- North Dakota State University News, Mar 2 2023 (persistent phosphor research; Nemoto's 1993 strontium aluminate): https://www.ndsu.edu/news/view/detail/72193
- University of Illinois Physics Van, "Making Glow-in-the-dark Powder" (2007): https://van.physics.illinois.edu/ask/listing/1908
- U.S. National Archives, "The Radium Girls at the National Archives" (Jan 2018): https://text-message.blogs.archives.gov/2018/01/04/the-radium-girls-at-the-national-archives

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| Light gives electrons a burst of energy (excited state) | ESRF: Eu2+ "absorbs blue or near-UV incident light", lifting an electron to an excited state |
| Normally an excited electron drops right back and the light comes out at once | ESRF: in a persistent phosphor the electron is transferred to a trap "before this radiative decay can occur" (i.e. normally it decays right away) |
| Traps: defects in the crystal catch and hold the excited electrons | ESRF ("transferred to a crystallographic defect, or a trap"); NDSU ("these defects trap the excited electrons") |
| In the dark, the room's warmth slowly shakes them loose | NDSU ("only release them gradually under thermal stimulation"); ESRF ("thermally-assisted release") |
| Each escape gives a little flash of green; many make the glow | NDSU ("europium ... is responsible for the emitted green light"); ESRF (emission follows the release) |
| Phosphorescence: light in, stored, light out | Illinois Physics Van ("phosphorescent" pigments); the trap mechanism above |
| Older stickers used zinc sulfide with a pinch of copper; they fade much sooner | Illinois Physics Van: ZnS with a little copper is a glow pigment; strontium aluminate "glows much longer than ZnS" |
| 1990s: strontium aluminate with europium and dysprosium | NDSU (developed 1993; "strontium aluminate with small amounts of ... europium and dysprosium"); ESRF ("mid 1990s") |
| Europium makes the green; dysprosium helps trap the electrons; glow can last hours | NDSU (europium → green); ESRF (dysprosium co-dopants "act as the trapping centre of the electron"; afterglow "tens of hours") |
| Not radioactive; a century ago dials used radium and many dial painters got very sick | ESRF (older self-luminous paints were powered by radioactive sources); National Archives (radium + zinc sulfide paint; the "Radium Girls") |
