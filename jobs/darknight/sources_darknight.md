# darknight: sources

1. NASA StarChild (Goddard Space Flight Center), "Why is the sky dark at night?" (Question of the Month, Dec 2002): https://starchild.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/StarChild/questions/question52.html
   - Olbers' paradox: if the universe were infinitely old and full of stars, the whole sky should be bright.
   - "The best solution at present is that the universe is not infinitely old"; "The light from stars farther away than that has not yet had time to reach us". (The page's old figure of ~15 billion years is superseded by WMAP/Planck; see 2.)
   - Light from stars moving away is shifted toward the red.
2. NASA, WMAP mission overview: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/wmap/wmap-overview/
   - "Determined the universe to be 13.77 billion years old to within a half percent." (Spoken as "about thirteen point eight billion".)
3. Penn State, ASTRO 801, "Olbers' Paradox": https://courses.ems.psu.edu/astro801/content/l10_p2.html
   - Dust is not the answer: in an infinite universe the light "would heat up the dust causing it to glow brightly enough to light up the night sky."
   - "Because the universe is not infinitely old ... we can see only those objects that are near enough to us that their light has reached us."
4. University of Oregon, ASTR 123, "Olbers's Paradox": https://pages.uoregon.edu/imamura/123/lecture-5/olbers.html
   - "The fact that the Universe has a finite age is the principal explanation of Olbers's Paradox."
   - "The expanding universe effects partially explain Olbers's Paradox": redshift stretches the light and lowers the energy that arrives, so distant sources look dimmer.
5. OpenStax Astronomy (Rice University), 29.4 "The Cosmic Microwave Background": https://openstax.org/books/astronomy/pages/29-4-the-cosmic-microwave-background
   - The leftover radiation from the hot early universe has been stretched by expansion into microwaves (about 2.7 K today), outside what our eyes can see.
6. Berkeley Lab, The Universe Adventure, "Solution to Olbers' Paradox: The Big Bang": https://universeadventure.org/big_bang/popups/conseq-dtrh-olbersbigbang.htm
   - "Since the speed of light is finite, we are only able to see as far as light has had time to travel (the age of the universe)."

Wording notes:
- "the universe has an age" / "only see as far as light has had time to travel": 1, 3, 4, 6. The finite age is the main reason (4).
- "a second reason: space is expanding ... shifts toward red and arrives weaker": 4 (expansion only partly explains it, so it is called the second reason).
- "Even the glow left over from the Big Bang has been stretched into microwaves": 5.
- The question says "a clue" the universe had a beginning, not "proof".
- The diagrams are schematic: the "light had time to get here" bubble is drawn as a map, not to scale.
