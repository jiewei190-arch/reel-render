Why is the night sky dark if there are so many stars? 🌌 If space went on forever, full of stars, every direction you look would end on a star and the whole sky would glow like the Sun. That puzzle is called Olbers' paradox. Dust can't fix it: dust soaking up all that light would heat up and glow too. The real answer: the universe has an age, about 13.8 billion years, and light travels at a fixed speed, so we only see as far as light has had time to travel. Light from farther stars simply hasn't reached us yet. Second, space is expanding, which stretches starlight toward red and makes it arrive weaker. Even the leftover glow of the Big Bang has been stretched into microwaves our eyes can't see. A dark night sky is a clue that the universe had a beginning.

Sources: NASA (StarChild, WMAP); Penn State ASTRO 801; University of Oregon ASTR 123; OpenStax Astronomy; Berkeley Lab.
