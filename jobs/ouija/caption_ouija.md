Rest your fingers on a Ouija pointer and it starts to move, but nobody means to push it. In 1853 Michael Faraday tested the same mystery with séance tables: he slipped a simple pointer between the sitters' hands and the table, and it showed their own hands were pushing, ever so slightly. Once they could watch it, the table stopped turning. It's the ideomotor effect: your brain expects a movement and your muscles make it, too small to notice. No ghosts needed. 👻

Sources: Faraday, letter to The Times (June 1853); Wikipedia "Ouija" and "Ideomotor phenomenon"; The Skeptic's Dictionary "ideomotor effect"; Gauchou, Rensink & Fels, Consciousness and Cognition (2012).
