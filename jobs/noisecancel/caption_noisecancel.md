How do noise-cancelling headphones work? They fight sound with more sound. Tiny microphones hear the noise, a chip flips that wave upside down, and the speaker plays this "anti-noise" with your music. When a squeeze meets a stretch, they cancel. It works best on slow, steady hums like a plane engine or an air conditioner (long waves, so a tiny delay barely matters). Voices and sudden sounds change too fast, so the earcup padding does that job. The idea was first patented in 1936.

Sources: Elliott & Nelson, Active Noise Control review (ISVR, University of Southampton); Sennheiser ANC notes.
