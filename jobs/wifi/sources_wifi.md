# Sources: wifi ("How WiFi passes through walls")

| Claim in the reel | Source |
|---|---|
| WiFi uses radio waves; 2.4 GHz ≈ 120 mm, 5 GHz ≈ 60 mm; also 6 GHz in newer generations | Wikipedia, "Wi-Fi" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi |
| Metal, rock (e.g. marble) and water strongly affect WiFi signals; walls reduce range | Wikipedia, "Wi-Fi" |
| 5 GHz is absorbed to a greater degree by building materials than 2.4 GHz, so it usually reaches less far (but carries more capacity) | Wikipedia, "Wi-Fi" |
| EU WiFi transmit power capped at 20 dBm = 100 mW (a tenth of a watt) | Wikipedia, "Wi-Fi" (regulatory section) |
| Microwave ovens run at about 2.45 GHz (12.2 cm), the same ISM band as 2.4 GHz WiFi; they heat by making polar molecules, mainly water, rotate (dielectric heating); household cooking power 600-1200 W | Wikipedia, "Microwave oven" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave_oven |
| Wavelength = speed of light / frequency (about 12.5 cm at 2.4 GHz, about 6 cm at 5 GHz) | Basic physics, c = f x wavelength (arithmetic checked) |
| Light/radio waves travel about 300,000 km/s; Earth's circumference is about 40,000 km, so about 7 laps per second | Basic physics / standard constants (arithmetic checked) |
| Building materials have measurable permittivity/conductivity that sets how radio waves pass or are absorbed | ITU-R P.2040 https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.2040/en (used as background only; no number from it is in the reel) |

## Dropped / unverified
- Specific "dB lost per wall" figures (drywall 3-5 dB, concrete 10-20 dB, 5 GHz 10-15 dB): consumer-blog sources disagreed and mix dB with dBm. **Dropped.** The wall transmission shown on screen is a qualitative illustration, not a measurement.
- "Water in the wall absorbs the most": stated only qualitatively (Wikipedia says water strongly affects the signal). No number given.
- The phone signal-bar drop (3 -> 1 bars) across three walls is illustrative.
- "Microwave ovens target water's resonance": a common myth; the Wikipedia article says all polar molecules heat, so the reel does not claim it.
