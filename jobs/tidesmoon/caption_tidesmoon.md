How does the Moon cause tides? The Moon's gravity pulls on all of Earth, but not evenly: hardest on the side facing it, weakest on the far side. On the near side the ocean is pulled toward the Moon; on the far side the water tends to keep going and lags behind, so there are two bulges. Earth spins through both, which is why most coasts get two high tides and two low tides a day, a little later each day as the Moon moves along its orbit. The Sun pulls too, about half as strongly. At new and full moon everything lines up and the pulls add up into extra-high spring tides (nothing to do with the season: the tide "springs forth", twice a month, all year). At right angles they partly cancel into neap tides. Coastlines and the seafloor shape the details, which is why tides differ from beach to beach.
