Cut a magnet in half and you never get a "north piece". You get two magnets, every time. Inside iron, the electrons act like teeny magnets, and they line up in zones called domains. A nearby magnet swings those domains into line, which is why a nail sticks. Heat iron past about 770 °C and the order falls apart. Fridge magnets hide stripes of alternating poles, so they only grip on one side. So could anything ever have just one pole?
