# Sources: window (why a plane window has a tiny hole)

| Claim in the reel | Source |
|---|---|
| The hole is called a breather hole or bleed hole and sits in the middle pane | Time, "Why airplane windows have that tiny hole" (quoting pilot Mark Vanhoenacker's Slate column) https://time.com/3903109/airplane-window-holes/ ; ScienceAlert https://www.sciencealert.com/here-s-why-there-s-a-tiny-hole-in-airplane-windows |
| Windows have three acrylic panes: inner scratch pane, middle pane with the hole, outer pane that bears most of the load | ScienceAlert (above) |
| At sea level pressure is about 14.6 psi; at 35,000 ft outside pressure is about 3.4 psi (less than a quarter); the cabin is pressurized so the big inside/outside difference falls mainly on the outer pane | ScienceAlert (above) |
| The hole lets pressure equilibrate between the cabin and the air gap between the middle and outer panes, so cabin pressure acts mainly on the outer pane | Marlowe Moncur, director of technology at GKN Aerospace, quoted via Slate/ScienceAlert; Time (above) |
| If the outer pane were damaged, the middle pane would still provide protection (backup) | ScienceAlert (above); "if a pane fails, it's the outer one that goes" (Time/Vanhoenacker) |
| The hole releases moisture from the gap, keeping the window mostly fog-free; fogging comes from the temperature difference between cabin and outside | Time (Boeing Commercial Airplanes engineer Bret Jensen cited in other coverage; Time's own text); ScienceAlert; AirHelp https://www.airhelp.com/en-int/blog/airplane-window-hole/ |

## Dropped / unverified
- No FAA regulation or advisory on the breather hole was found, so the reel makes no regulatory claim.
- Some sources describe the middle pane as an independent structural pane and others as a backup; the reel uses the common "backup" wording.
- ScienceAlert says the cabin is kept "similar to ground level"; actual cabin altitude is higher, so the reel does not state a cabin pressure figure.
- Diagram proportions (pane gaps, hole size) are illustrative.
