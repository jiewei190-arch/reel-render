How does a thermos keep drinks hot and cold? It has no idea which one it's holding. It doesn't make heat or cold; it just stops heat crossing, both ways. Heat travels by conduction, convection and radiation. The gap between a thermos's two walls is a near-vacuum, so heat can't conduct across and no air currents can carry it, and the silvered walls bounce infrared back. The weak point is the top, where the walls join, which is why the neck is narrow and the stopper is thick. James Dewar showed the idea in London on Christmas Day 1892, to keep liquid gases cold, and never patented it. Coffee or ice water in yours?
