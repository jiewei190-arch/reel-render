# Sources: submarine (How a submarine dives and surfaces)

| Claim in script | Source |
|---|---|
| Floating depends on the weight of the water pushed aside (Archimedes' principle): if it weighs more than you, you float; less, you sink | Wikipedia, "Archimedes' principle" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes%27_principle ; Wikipedia, "Buoyancy" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buoyancy |
| A steel sub weighing thousands of tonnes can still float | Wikipedia, "Virginia-class submarine": submerged displacement 7,900 t (Block I-IV), 10,200 t (Block V) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia-class_submarine |
| Ballast tanks hold air on the surface; filled with water to submerge, air to surface | Wikipedia, "Submarine": ballast tanks "can hold varying amounts of water and air", "filled with water to submerge or with air to surface." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submarine |
| To dive, the crew opens vents on top of the tanks; air escapes, seawater floods in through the bottom | Wikipedia, "Ballast tank": "The crew submerges the vessel by opening vents in the top of the main ballast tanks and opening valves at the bottom." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballast_tank |
| Heavier for its size (negative buoyancy), the sub slips under | Wikipedia, "Submarine": "To submerge hydrostatically, a ship must have negative buoyancy" |
| Trim tanks shift water until it neither rises nor sinks: neutral buoyancy | Wikipedia, "Ballast tank": "Ballast water can be transferred between trim tanks to balance the vessel in horizontal trim"; main tanks flooded "to achieve approximately neutral buoyancy"; Wikipedia, "Submarine" (neutral buoyancy, trim tanks) |
| Diving planes tilt it up or down as it moves | Wikipedia, "Submarine": "Hydrodynamic maneuvering is done by several control surfaces, collectively known as diving planes or hydroplanes" |
| To surface, the crew shuts the vents and blows compressed air into the tanks; the air pushes the water out the bottom | Wikipedia, "Ballast tank": "the crew shuts the vents in the top of the ballast tanks" and "releases compressed air into the tanks" |
| In an emergency, crews use every trick at once to surface fast | Wikipedia, "Submarine": "When a submarine performs an emergency surfacing, all depth and trim control methods are used simultaneously" |
| Many fish do a version of this with a gas-filled swim bladder | Wikipedia, "Swim bladder": "an internal gas-filled organ in bony fish that functions to modulate buoyancy" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swim_bladder |

## Softened / unverified
- "Many fish": only bony fish have swim bladders (sharks do not), hence "many", not "all".
- "Every trick at once" paraphrases "all depth and trim control methods are used simultaneously".
- Visual liberties: real submarines have no portholes; the glowing round windows and the lit tanks are the template's warm-window language, not hardware. Tank layout is schematic (two main tanks, two trim tanks).
