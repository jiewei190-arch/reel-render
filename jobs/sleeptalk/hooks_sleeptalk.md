# sleeptalk: 3 opening lines (template #1 paper-lantern)

Search question spoken in line 2: "But why do we talk in our sleep at all? Scientists don't fully know."

1. **PICK** 'In a sleep lab, the word people said most? "No."' (10 words; hedged from "The word people said most in their sleep?" per the main session, since the data is one lab study of mostly patients). This is pattern 3, the surprising number/fact. The source is Arnulf et al. 2017, Sleep 40 (883 episodes from 232 sleepers): the most frequent word was "No", and negations were 21.4% of clauses. Frame 0 shows the night bedroom with the sleeper, a glowing "No." speech bubble, the vertical SLEEP strip and the strip "the top word in a sleep lab".
2. "Most people talk in their sleep at some point." (9 words). Pattern 3, weaker; it is kept as the 7.8 s beat (AASM: 60-65% lifetime).
3. "Your sleep talk isn't a confession." (6 words). Pattern 7, myth correction; it is kept as the 57.5 s beat.

Why 1: it is a concrete, odd fact that begs "why?", and it pays off on screen at 35.7 s. The "but" lands at 3.6 s.
Re-hooks:
- 25.9 s "Here's the weird part": 883 episodes, more than half not words.
- 48.7 s "But here's the surprising part": grammar mostly normal, like one side of a conversation.
- 57.5 s "So is it a secret confession? Don't count on it." with the red NOT A TRUTH SERUM seal.

Hedges on screen and in the voice:
- 43.5 s: "Most of those sleepers had sleep disorders, so everyday sleep talk may be tamer." The Arnulf sample was 92% parasomnia patients and 15 healthy sleepers.
- "Scientists don't fully know" and "it's still unclear how closely it's tied to dreaming" (AASM).

Seal backing (NOT A TRUTH SERUM):
- Sleep Foundation (sleepfoundation.org/parasomnias/sleep-talking): "Research is mixed about the source of the content... Speech may not have any clear connection to a person's life, recent events, or prior conversations", and it is often gibberish or mumbling.
- AASM sleepeducation.org (reviewed by Hershner & Morse, 2020): the content "may make no sense", it tends to be harmless, and it may be vulgar.
