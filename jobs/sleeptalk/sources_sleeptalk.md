# Sources: sleeptalk ("Why do we talk in our sleep?")

| Claim in the script | Source | Type |
|---|---|---|
| It has a name, somniloquy; about 60-65% of people do it at some point | American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM), sleepeducation.org, "Sleep talking" (reviewed by Hershner & Morse, Sept 2020; https://sleepeducation.org/sleep-disorders/sleep-talking/): sleep talking (somniloquy) is a parasomnia; lifetime prevalence 60-65% | professional society |
| Reported in about half of young children, only about 5% of adults | AASM (same page): about 50% of young children; about 5% of adults | professional society |
| Any stage of sleep; unclear how closely it's tied to dreaming | AASM: can occur in any stage of sleep; it is "still unknown if" sleep talking is closely linked to dreaming | professional society |
| Lab study of 883 sleep-talk episodes; more than half weren't words (mumbles, whispers, shouts, laughs) | Arnulf I, Uguccioni G, Gay F, et al. "What does the sleeping brain say? Syntax and semantics of sleep talking in healthy subjects and in parasomnia patients." Sleep 40(11) (2017), doi:10.1093/sleep/zsx159: 232 adults, 883 speech episodes, 59% nonverbal (mumbles, shouts, whispers, laughs) | peer-reviewed |
| When the words were clear, the top one was "No"; about 1 in 5 clauses a negation | Arnulf 2017: "No" was the most frequent word; negations in 21.4% of clauses | peer-reviewed |
| About 1 in 10 had swearing | Arnulf 2017: profanity in 9.7% of clauses | peer-reviewed |
| Most of those sleepers had sleep disorders; everyday sleep talk may be tamer | Arnulf 2017 sample: mostly patients with parasomnias (REM sleep behaviour disorder, sleepwalking/night terrors), plus 15 healthy sleepers | peer-reviewed |
| Grammar mostly normal; like one side of a conversation, with pauses for replies | Arnulf 2017: sentences were mostly grammatical, and turn-taking gaps were respected, as in a conversation with "inaudible others"; "the sleeping brain can function at a high level" | peer-reviewed |
| Secret confession? Don't count on it (NOT A TRUTH SERUM seal) | Sleep Foundation, "Sleep Talking" (https://www.sleepfoundation.org/parasomnias/sleep-talking): "Research is mixed about the source of the content... Speech may not have any clear connection to a person's life, recent events, or prior conversations"; AASM: the content "may make no sense" | health nonprofit; professional society |
| Harmless on its own; see a doctor if it starts later in life or comes with other symptoms | AASM: sleep talking by itself is benign; see a provider if it starts at an older age, becomes more frequent, or comes with other symptoms | professional society |

## Softened / left out
- The Arnulf figures are shown as "in a lab study" and followed straight away by the caveat (43.5 s) that most sleepers had sleep disorders.
- "Scientists don't fully know" and "No one knows for sure" carry the open cause; no single brain mechanism is claimed.
- The stress and family-history triggers were dropped for length (both are on the AASM page).
