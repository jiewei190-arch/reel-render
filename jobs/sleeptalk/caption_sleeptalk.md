Why do we talk in our sleep? Scientists don't fully know, but it's common: about 60-65% of people do it at some point. It's reported in half of young children and only about 5% of adults, and it can happen in any stage of sleep. In a lab study of 883 sleep-talk episodes (mostly from people with sleep disorders), more than half weren't words at all: mumbles, whispers, shouts, laughs. When the words were clear, the top one was "No," and about 1 in 10 clauses had swearing. The grammar was mostly normal, like one side of a conversation. Is it a secret confession? Don't count on it: what people say may have no clear link to their real life. On its own it's harmless; if it starts later in life or comes with other symptoms, talk to a doctor. 💤

Sources: American Academy of Sleep Medicine (sleepeducation.org); Arnulf et al., Sleep (2017); Sleep Foundation.
