Why do bananas turn brown? It starts with a gas: bananas make their own ripening hormone, ethylene, and they're extremely sensitive to it (a trace as small as about 15 parts per billion can start ripening). Inside the peel, an enzyme (polyphenol oxidase) and plant compounds are packed apart. As the fruit ripens and ages, they meet, and the enzyme uses oxygen to turn mainly dopamine (yes, the same molecule your brain uses) into brown pigments, a kind of melanin. Bruises brown faster because crushed cells mix everything at once. And below about 13 °C (55 °F), the peel gets chill damage and darkens, but the fruit inside stays fresh: ripen on the counter, then refrigerate. 🍌

Sources: Chang et al., Frontiers in Plant Science (2022, University of Florida); Utah State University Extension; Scientific American.
