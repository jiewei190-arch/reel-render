Can you tell the temperature from cricket chirps? Roughly, yes, for the right cricket. NOAA's rule: count the chirps in 15 seconds and add 40 to get the temperature in °F. Physicist Amos Dolbear wrote the relationship down in 1897 (about 80 chirps a minute at 60 °F, 120 at 70 °F). The cricket isn't measuring anything: it's cold-blooded, so on a warm night its muscles work faster and its wings rub together faster. It works best for one species, probably the snowy tree cricket, nicknamed the thermometer cricket. Other crickets keep their own pace, and on cold nights they slow way down. 🦗🌡️

Sources: NOAA Office of Education; Dolbear, "The Cricket as a Thermometer," The American Naturalist (1897); Penn State Extension, Bug of the Month: Snowy tree cricket.
