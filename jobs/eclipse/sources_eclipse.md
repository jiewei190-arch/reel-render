# Sources: How a solar and lunar eclipse really work (shadow-puppet style)
| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Moon's shadow has a dark inner umbra (Sun fully blocked, total eclipse) and a fainter outer penumbra (partial) | NASA Science, Eclipse geometry: https://science.nasa.gov/eclipses/geometry/ |
| Sun is about 400x bigger than the Moon and about 400x farther away, so they look nearly the same size | NASA geometry page (above) |
| Typical umbral path is under 50 miles wide; totality at one place lasts only a few minutes or less | NASA geometry page (above) |
| The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5 degrees, so most new moons pass above or below the Sun; eclipse seasons happen about twice a year | NASA geometry page (above) |
| Totality: sky goes dark, corona visible, temperature drops | Eclipse viewing guide (Earth View Inc., 2017, adapted from Bryan Brewer's ECLIPSE; secondary): https://eclipse.aas.org/sites/eclipse.aas.org/files/Total-Solar-Eclipse-Viewing-Guide-Color-CMYK-vertical-format-2.4-MB-PDF.pdf |
| Never look at the Sun unprotected, even in a partial eclipse; use ISO 12312-2 eclipse glasses, regular sunglasses are not enough | Scientific American eclipse guide: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-scientific-american-guide-to-2026s-total-solar-eclipse/ |
| Lunar eclipse = Moon passes into Earth's shadow when Sun, Earth and Moon align; Earth's shadow has umbra (darkest part) and penumbra (outer, subtle dimming) | NASA Science, "What You Need To Know About the March 2025 Total Lunar Eclipse": https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/moon/what-you-need-to-know-about-the-march-2025-total-lunar-eclipse/ |
| Lunar eclipse happens at full Moon with Earth between Sun and Moon; shadow dims the Moon over a few hours; each lunar eclipse is visible from about half of Earth | NASA Science, Eclipses and the Moon: https://science.nasa.gov/moon/eclipses/ |
| The Moon looks dim red because Earth's atmosphere scatters away shorter blue wavelengths so longer red/orange light reaches the Moon | NASA Science, Eclipses and the Moon (above) |
| No special equipment needed to watch a lunar eclipse (binoculars/telescope only enhance) | NASA lunar eclipse article (March 2025, above) |

Caveats / not claimed:
- The reel says "no special glasses" for a lunar eclipse. NASA's article says no special equipment is needed; it doesn't use the word "safe". Wording kept to that.
- "most of the time it slips above or below the line" is NASA's solar-eclipse (new Moon) geometry; the same tilt applies to full Moons for lunar eclipses, which I inferred, not a quoted NASA line.
- Corona/thermometer/"air cools" detail comes from the secondary viewing guide above; no temperature number is stated in the reel.
- "400x" is NASA's rounded figure. The "same size" outcome is stated in the reel only through the 400x pairing.
- No eclipse dates are claimed.
- Stage frame, audience heads and the jointed watcher are decoration.
