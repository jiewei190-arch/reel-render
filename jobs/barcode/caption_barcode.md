A barcode doesn't hold the price or the name. All it holds is a number, and the store's computer looks up the rest. A scanner reads black bars (they soak up light) and white gaps (they bounce it back) as a signal, decodes seven-slice digits, and checks the last digit with a bit of math. The first item ever scanned was a pack of Juicy Fruit gum, in Troy, Ohio, on June 26, 1974.

Sources: GS1 (what a barcode encodes); Wikipedia "Universal Product Code" (structure, check digit, first scan, citing the Smithsonian and Wired); Scanbot and Triton (how laser scanners read bars).
