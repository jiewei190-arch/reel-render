How do cats always land on their feet? It's the righting reflex, and it puzzled physicists: a falling cat has nothing to push against, and you can't start spinning in midair from nothing (conservation of angular momentum). The trick is that a cat bends in the middle. The front half turns first while the back half twists a little the other way, then they swap, so the twists cancel and the total spin stays zero. Physicists think cats mix moves: the "bend and twist" and the "tuck and turn". But landing on their feet doesn't mean landing safe. In one vet-school study of 119 cats that fell from windows and balconies, nearly half had broken legs, and falls from the 7th floor or higher were worse. 🐈

Sources: Greg Gbur (UNC Charlotte), Falling Felines and Fundamental Physics (Yale, 2019); Kane & Scher (1969); Vnuk et al., University of Zagreb Veterinary Faculty, J Feline Med Surg (2004).
