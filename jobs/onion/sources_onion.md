# Sources: onion ("Why onions make you cry")

| Claim in the reel | Source |
|---|---|
| Cutting breaks open onion cells; enzymes (alliinase) turn amino-acid sulfoxides into sulfenic acid; lachrymatory-factor synthase then makes syn-propanethial-S-oxide; its vapours induce tearing | Wikipedia, "Lachrymatory factor" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachrymatory_factor |
| Enzymes are stored apart (in vacuoles) from the sulfur compounds and are released when cells are ripped open; the gas evaporates and drifts to the eyes | Discover Magazine, "Why Do Onions Make Us Cry?" https://www.discovermagazine.com/why-do-onions-make-us-cry-3138 ; The Conversation, "Why onions make us cry and why some don't" https://theconversation.com/why-onions-make-us-cry-and-why-some-dont-84486 |
| The gas dissolves in the water on the eye's surface and forms an irritant (sulfenic acid) in amounts that are "only irritating and not harmful" | The Conversation (same article) |
| Nerves in the cornea detect it and signal the central nervous system, which stimulates the lacrimal (tear) glands to flush it | Discover Magazine (same article) |
| It evolved as a defence against animals/pests | Discover Magazine; The Conversation |
| Two-enzyme model (alliinase, then LF synthase); Imai et al. 2002, Nature, "An onion enzyme that makes the eyes water" | The Conversation; Discover Magazine (cites Imai et al., Nature 2002) |
| Chilling can slow/damage the enzymes in theory, but no test results are given for the home tricks | The Conversation; Discover Magazine (which lists chilling as an unconfirmed home remedy) |
| In 2015, onions with the synthase gene suppressed reportedly made about 7.5 times less of the compound and caused no tearing in sensory panelists | Discover Magazine (reported result) |
| Garlic uses a different compound (alliin -> allicin), not the eye-irritating gas | The Conversation |

## Dropped / unverified
- "It becomes sulfuric acid in your eye": a popular claim, **dropped** (technical sources say sulfenic acid / a mild irritant; one blog claimed sulfuric acid).
- TRPA1 receptor details: not found in the sources checked. **Dropped.**
- "Use a sharp knife", goggles, a candle or cutting underwater: not confirmed by the sources read. **Dropped.** Chilling is mentioned only as "plausible, not well tested".
- The 2015 7x-less result is attributed as "one report" ("reportedly") because it is relayed by a magazine, not read in the original paper.
- The molecule drawing is simplified/schematic.
