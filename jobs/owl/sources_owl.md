# Sources: owl (How owls fly almost silently)

| Claim in script | Source |
|---|---|
| Owls fly almost silently and more slowly than other birds of prey | Wikipedia, "Owl": "Most owls share an innate ability to fly almost silently and also more slowly in comparison to other birds of prey." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owl |
| Much wing noise comes from the trailing (back) edge, where the airflow becomes turbulent; a sharp rigid edge scatters it | University of Cambridge, "The owl and the wind turbine" (Nigel Peake): noise comes from the trailing edge where flow becomes turbulent; the fringe is "a much softer 'sound scatterer' than a sharp rigid edge". https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/features/the-owl-and-the-wind-turbine-how-stealth-feathers-could-help-reduce-noise-pollution |
| Comb-like (serrated) front edge that researchers think smooths the incoming air | Wikipedia, "Owl": serrated edges on the remiges; "The serrations are more likely reducing aerodynamic disturbances, rather than simply reducing noise." |
| Soft, fringed back edge breaks up turbulence | Cambridge: "a flexible and porous fringe which reduces air turbulence by smoothing the passage of air" |
| Velvety surface soaks up the sound of the wing moving | Wikipedia, "Owl": "The surface of the flight feathers is covered with a velvety structure that absorbs the sound of the wing moving." |
| Together they make the owl quiet enough to hear its prey without the prey hearing it coming | University of Cambridge (9 Sep 2015): the features "enable owls to fly stealthily so that they can hear their prey without it hearing them." |
| It matters most for owls that hunt small animals by stealth | Cambridge: "not all species of owl but those species that rely on stealth in hunting small animals" |
| Fish-eating owls mostly lack these features | Cambridge: species "(such as fish owls) not requiring an acoustic stealth advantage do not possess them either"; Wikipedia, "Owl" (fish-eating owls lack the adaptation). Worded "mostly lack" to stay cautious. |
| Engineers copy the fringe to quiet wind turbines and fans | Cambridge: a prototype owl-inspired surface for wind turbines and fans |

## Softened / unverified
- The role of the leading-edge comb is debated (Wikipedia: "more likely reducing aerodynamic disturbances"; the Cambridge piece links it to steep dives), so the script says "which researchers think smooths the incoming air".
- The 2 kHz figure was dropped (main-session review); the hearing claim now follows Cambridge's wording.
- "Copying the fringe to quiet wind turbines and fans" refers to prototypes and research, not a claim that it is in wide use.
- No predation shown: the mouse just keeps eating (non-gory rule).
