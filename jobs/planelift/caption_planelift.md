How do planes actually stay in the air? Not because air on top has "farther to go": NASA calls the equal-transit idea one of the most widely circulated, incorrect explanations, and smoke tests show the top air reaches the back first. A curved, tilted wing turns a huge amount of air downward, and by Newton's third law the air pushes the wing up. Turning the flow also leaves lower pressure above the wing and higher below. Both descriptions are true at the same time. ✈️

Sources: NASA Glenn Research Center, Beginner's Guide to Aeronautics ("Equal Transit Theory", "What is Lift?"); University of Cambridge, Holger Babinsky (Physics Education 2003; "How wings really work", 2012); Scientific American (2020).
