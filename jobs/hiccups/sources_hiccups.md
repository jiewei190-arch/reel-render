# Sources: hiccups ("Why do we get hiccups?")

| # | Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A hiccup starts with the diaphragm (the dome of muscle under the lungs) suddenly contracting, and air rushes in | Howes D. (2012), "Hiccups: a new explanation for the mysterious reflex", BioEssays 34(6):451-453, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3504071/ ("the forceful contraction of the respiratory muscles"); NHS, "Hiccups" https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hiccups/ |
| 2 | About a thirtieth of a second later the vocal cords snap shut (the "hic") | Howes 2012 (above): hiccups are "the abrupt closure of the vocal cords approximately 35 milliseconds after the forceful contraction of the respiratory muscles" (35 ms ≈ 1/30 s) |
| 3 | It's a reflex loop running through nerves like the vagus and the phrenic | Howes 2012 (above): afferent signals "travel as part of the phrenic nerve, the vagus, and sympathetic (T6-T12) chain branches"; efferent from a medullary hiccup centre to the diaphragm |
| 4 | Nobody knows for sure what hiccups are for | Howes 2012 (above): the function is unknown; his hypothesis is "untested"; Straus et al. 2003 (below) call theirs a hypothesis |
| 5 | Newborns spend about 2.5% of their time hiccuping, and it starts in the womb | Howes 2012 (above): newborns may spend about 2.5% of their time hiccupping; hiccups seen on ultrasound in the fetus in utero |
| 6 | Idea 1: hiccups help babies burp, pulling swallowed air out of the stomach to make room for milk | Howes 2012 (above): proposes hiccups evolved to remove air from the stomachs of young suckling mammals (worded "one idea") |
| 7 | Idea 2 (scientists suspect): a leftover from ancestors that breathed with gills and lungs, like today's tadpoles; their gill-pumping rhythm also shuts the throat while breathing in | Straus C. et al. (2003), "A phylogenetic hypothesis for the origin of hiccough", BioEssays 25(2):182-188: hiccough as an expression of archaic motor patterns, "particularly the gill ventilation" of bimodal breathers such as most frogs (tadpoles), sharing "glottal closure during inspiration" (worded "scientists suspect") |
| 8 | Neither idea is proven yet | Howes 2012 ("There's not really any way to prove these kinds of things", The Globe and Mail); Allan Pack on Straus: "very tough to prove" (press coverage) |
| 9 | The cures: many people swear by them, but there's no proof they work for everyone | NHS (above): "Although many people find these things helpful for stopping hiccups, there's no evidence that they work for everyone." |
| 10 | If hiccups last more than two days, see a doctor | NHS (above): see a GP if "hiccups last longer than 48 hours" |

Visual notes: the cutaway, reflex loop, tadpole and the cure props are illustrations; the 1/30 s, 2.5% and 2-day numbers match the sources.
