Why do you get butterflies in your stomach? Before a big moment, your brain flips on fight-or-flight and adrenaline floods your body. Your heart speeds up and blood is sent to your muscles, and to do that adrenaline shunts blood away from your gut, so digestion slows. Physiologists think your stomach's own nerves notice that shortage, and that's the flutter. Your gut has its own nervous system, often called the "second brain", linked to your head by the vagus nerve; a Harvard gut doctor calls it "immense crosstalk". That's why stress can show up as nausea, cramps or butterflies. When the moment passes, rest-and-digest takes over and the flutter settles.

Sources: Bradley Elliott (University of Westminster) in The Conversation; Harvard Health Publishing; Harvard Medical School; Cleveland Clinic.
