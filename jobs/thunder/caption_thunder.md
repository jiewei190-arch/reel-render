Why does thunder rumble instead of just bang? A lightning bolt heats the air to about 50,000 °F, roughly five times hotter than the Sun's surface, so the air bursts outward and snaps back: that blast is thunder. But a bolt isn't a dot. It's a long, crooked channel, and every piece of it booms. Sound is slow (about 1 km every 3 seconds), so the boom from the closest part reaches you first and the farther parts arrive seconds later, smeared into one long rumble. Far away, the air soaks up the high notes too, so only the low rumble survives. Count from flash to bang: about 5 seconds per mile, 3 per kilometer. And if you can hear thunder, head indoors. ⚡
