# thunder: 3 opening lines (template #28 Sand art)

Search question spoken in line 2: "But why? Why does thunder rumble instead of just banging once?" (caption 3.8-7.1 s, the "but" at 3.8 s).

1. **PICK** "Crack! Then a long, low rumble rolls across the sky." (10 words) Pattern 1, the sound itself. Frame 0: a jagged bolt cleared through the dark storm sand to the glowing light table, striking beside a lit house; the screen shakes; the first sound ring leaves the bolt; "Crack! / then the long rumble".
2. "Thunder is one bang, heard from every point of a bolt." (11 words) Pattern 2, surprise. Frame 0: the long crooked bolt with rings from every point.
3. "Count from flash to bang, and you'll know how far it is." (12 words) Pattern 3, the tip first. Frame 0: the counter.

Why 1: it opens on the exact experience people search about, and the sand-art bolt is the strongest image. Line 2 spends the payoff; line 3 spends the practical tip at the end.
Open question: why does thunder rumble? The chain: the bolt heats the air to about 50,000 °F (about 5x the Sun's surface), the air bursts out and snaps back, and that blast is a sound wave. Twist at 23.7 s: a bolt is a long, crooked channel, and every piece booms. Sound is slow (1 km per 3 s), so the nearest part is heard first and the rest arrive seconds later: rings leave every point at the same speed and stack into a strip that smears into one rumble (the wow, 37-49 s). Re-hook at 49.5 s: distance also strips the high notes. Payoff at 59.1 s: "one bolt, heard from every point". Then flash to bang (5 s per mile, 3 s per km), head indoors, "Counting?", end card.
