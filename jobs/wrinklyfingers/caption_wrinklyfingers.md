Why do your fingers wrinkle in water? It's not just soggy skin: your nerves do it. Doctors noticed in the 1930s that fingers with a damaged median nerve stay smooth in water. In a warm soak, nerves make the tiny blood vessels under your fingertips narrow, the volume drops, and the skin on top is pulled into folds, only on hairless skin like fingertips, palms and soles. A 2025 study even found the same fingers wrinkle in the same pattern every time. Why? The leading idea is grip, but the studies disagree. Did you think it was just soggy skin?

Sources: Haseleu et al., PLoS ONE (2014); Wilder-Smith & Chow, Muscle & Nerve (2003); Kareklas, Nettle & Smulders, Biology Letters (2013); Binghamton University (2025).
