# Sources: goldfishmemory ("Do goldfish really have a 3-second memory?")

| # | Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | At the University of Plymouth, goldfish learned to press a lever for food; then it only worked one hour a day, at the same time; the fish got busy at the lever just before feeding time; after the food was switched off they kept the rhythm for a few more days | Gee P., Stephenson D. & Wright D.E. (1994), "Temporal discrimination learning of operant feeding in goldfish (Carassius auratus)", Journal of the Experimental Analysis of Behavior 62(1):1-13 (University of Plymouth research portal): 8 goldfish, lever-reinforced feeding reduced to 1 hr in every 24 at the same time each day, held 4 weeks; then 6 days of extinction; "the fish were able to exhibit temporal discrimination", responding in anticipation of feeding time, which persisted for a limited number of extinction days. (Popular sites date this to 2008 and claim "3 months": not supported by the paper, so no year or months are said) |
| 2 | In Israel, young fish trained to expect food at a sound were released into the sea; months later the sound reportedly still called them back (no number of months is spoken or shown; main-session edit) | Technion / Agricultural Engineering Institute team (Boaz Zion, Ilan Karplus, Assaf Barki), reported by The Jerusalem Post, "Fish have longer memories than previously thought, say Technion scientists" (Dec 31 2008) and Practical Fishkeeping, "Fish remember for four or five months, not three seconds". A conference presentation, not a peer-reviewed paper; species not stated in the reports, so "young fish" (not goldfish) and "reportedly" |
| 3 | In Japan, goldfish learned to tell Bach from Stravinsky, after lots of training | **Primary:** Shinozuka K., Ono H. & Watanabe S. (2013), "Reinforcing and discriminative stimulus properties of music in goldfish", Behavioural Processes 99:26-33 (Keio University): goldfish trained to discriminate Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D minor from Stravinsky's The Rite of Spring (Live Science, "Do Goldfish Prefer Bach or Stravinsky?"); it took many training sessions |
| 4 | Nobody knows where the 3-second idea came from; one fish researcher suspects it helped tiny bowls feel okay; he says goldfish memories can last weeks, months, even years | Prof. Culum Brown (Macquarie University), quoted in Live Science, "Do goldfish really have a 3-second memory?" https://www.livescience.com/goldfish-memory.html : goldfish memories span "weeks, months and even years"; he suspects the myth makes keeping goldfish in small bowls easier to accept (worded as his hunch) |

Visual notes: the 24-hour dial, lever, moon phases (months passing), music staves and the bead are schematic illustrations of the studies, not data.
