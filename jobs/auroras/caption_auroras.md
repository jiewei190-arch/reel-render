What causes the northern lights? The Sun is always blowing a wind of charged particles, and sometimes it hurls a giant cloud of them (a coronal mass ejection). Earth's magnetic field catches them and steers them toward the poles, where they crash into the air 50-300 miles up and make it glow. The color says which gas got hit: oxygen glows green or red, nitrogen blue and purple. The Sun hit the busy peak of its 11-year cycle in 2024, and the May 2024 storm, the strongest in two decades, pushed auroras over places that rarely see them. 🌌

Sources: NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center, "Aurora"; NASA Space Place; NASA/NOAA solar maximum announcement (Oct 2024); NASA Earth Observatory (May 2024 storm).
