What causes the northern lights? The Sun is always blowing a wind of charged particles, and sometimes it hurls a giant cloud of them (a coronal mass ejection). Earth's magnetic field catches them and steers them toward the poles, where they crash into the air about 80-500 km up and make it glow. The color says which gas got hit: oxygen glows green or red, nitrogen blue and purple. The Sun hit the peak of its 11-year cycle in 2024 (the May 2024 storm, the strongest in two decades, pushed auroras over places that rarely see them), and the years just after a peak still bring big storms.
