# Sources: airfryer (How an air fryer cooks)

| Claim in script | Source |
|---|---|
| An air fryer doesn't submerge food in oil; frying = immersion in hot oil | Wikipedia, "Air fryer": imitates deep frying "without submerging the food in oil"; heats "food coated with a thin layer of oil". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_fryer |
| It is a small countertop convection oven | Wikipedia, "Convection oven": an air fryer is "a small countertop convection oven that produces cooking temperatures similar to those of deep frying." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection_oven |
| A heating element and a fan; the fan circulates hot air at high speed | Wikipedia, "Air fryer": "A fan circulates hot air at a high speed, producing a crisp layer via browning reactions." |
| Air up to about 200 °C | Wikipedia, "Air fryer" / "Convection oven": "circulating air at up to 200 °C (392 °F)" |
| Still air forms a thin, cooler layer around food that slows heating; moving air thins it so cooking is faster | Wikipedia, "Convection oven": the fan reduces "the thickness of the stationary thermal boundary layer of cooler air" around food; "the layer is thinned, and cooking is faster." |
| Surface dries then browns: the Maillard reaction (amino acids + reducing sugars, browning) | Wikipedia, "Maillard reaction": "a chemical reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars" that gives browned food its flavor. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maillard_reaction |
| It speeds up from about 140 °C, the same zone as frying | Wikipedia, "Maillard reaction": "typically proceeds rapidly from around 140 to 165 °C"; Wikipedia, "Air fryer": conventional frying "at temperatures of 140 to 165 °C" |
| A thin coat of oil helps; food never swims in it | Wikipedia, "Air fryer" ("food coated with a thin layer of oil", "without submerging") |
| Many ovens have a fan; some reviewers say air frying is basically convection baking, just smaller | Wikipedia, "Air fryer": some reviewers say "air frying is essentially the same as convection baking"; air fryers "are smaller and give off less heat to the room". |
| Invented by Dutch engineer Fred van der Weij; Philips launched it in 2010 | Wikipedia, "Air fryer": "invented in 2006 by Dutch engineer Fred van der Weij" ... "Philips, who launched the Airfryer in 2010." |

## Softened / unverified
- "Up top: a heating element and a fan": layout varies by model; Wikipedia only says heating element + fan. The cutaway is schematic.
- "Thin, cooler layer ... like a blanket": the boundary layer explained as an analogy (source: Wikipedia, Convection oven); how much faster an air fryer is than an oven is disputed, so no speed number is given.
- "Some reviewers say": attributed opinion, not stated as fact.
- No health / fat-reduction claims are made.
- "Fred van der Weij" is spoken as "van der Vey" (Dutch pronunciation approximated for the TTS).
