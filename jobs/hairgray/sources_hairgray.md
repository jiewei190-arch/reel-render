| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| Hair colour comes from melanin, made by cells in the root called melanocytes | Cleveland Clinic |
| Each strand is coloured as it grows; once out, its colour is set ("grows in gray") | Cleveland Clinic: hair already has its color before it emerges |
| Gray or white hair has little or no melanin | Cleveland Clinic |
| The pigment cells are refilled by stem cells in each follicle | Sun 2023 via CBS (melanocyte stem cells in the follicle); Harvard (pigment-regenerating stem cells) |
| 2023 study in mice: stem cells normally shuttle between two spots | CBS: the cells "move between growth compartments"; they must be in the hair germ compartment to produce pigment |
| As hairs shed and regrow, more get stuck and stop making colour | CBS: the cells "get stuck as people age and lose their ability to mature and maintain hair color" (the study itself is in mice) |
| The hair keeps growing without its colour | Sun 2023 coverage: hair follicle cells keep growing while pigmentation fails |
| Aging follicles build up hydrogen peroxide, which blocks a key pigment enzyme | FASEB 2009 via ScienceDaily: peroxide builds up as catalase falls; it disrupts tyrosinase, which makes melanin |
| In mice, stress made nerves release noradrenaline | Harvard: sympathetic (fight-or-flight) nerves release norepinephrine (= noradrenaline) |
| It used up their pigment stem cells within days | Harvard: "After just a few days, all of the pigment-regenerating stem cells were lost" |
| Whether it works the same way in people is still being studied | Harvard (no human data); CBS (Ito: investigating "to what extent our theory is applicable to humans") |
| Genes matter most: they set when the first gray hairs show up | Cleveland Clinic: genetics strongly influences when you start graying |

## Softened / careful wording
1. The stuck-stem-cell and stress mechanisms are stated as mouse findings; human relevance is "still being studied".
2. "Noradrenaline" in the voice (the main session's word); the Harvard source says norepinephrine, the same molecule.
3. "Another clue" for the peroxide work (a contributor, not the whole story).
4. The paintings are shown only as images of grey and dark hair, with Met titles; "style of Rembrandt" is labelled as such. No claim is made about Rembrandt's biology.
