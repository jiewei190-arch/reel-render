# Sources: popcorn ("Why does popcorn pop?")

| # | Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The kernel's water is the secret; popcorn needs about 13 to 14.5% moisture | Purdue University Agriculture News, "What's under the shell of this popular snack?" (Chad Campbell, Feb 7 2020), quoting food scientist Bruce Hamaker: "Popcorn must have moisture inside the kernel of about 13 to 14.5 percent of its total weight." https://ag.purdue.edu/news/2020/02/whats-under-the-shell-of-this-popular-snack.html |
| 2 | A tough hull (pericarp) keeps the water in | Purdue (above): the best-popping kernels have sturdier outer shells that keep moisture in. Hoseney, Zeleznak & Abdelrahman (1983), "Mechanism of popcorn popping", Journal of Cereal Science 1:43-52: "the pericarp acts as a pressure vessel". |
| 3 | The water gets hotter than boiling and pressure builds | Hoseney et al. 1983: "Most of the water in the kernel is superheated at the moment of popping"; Purdue: the kernel works like a pressure cooker as its water heats and turns to vapor |
| 4 | The starch softens into a hot gel | Purdue/Hamaker and food-science summaries: heat and pressure gelatinize and soften the starchy endosperm (Mental Floss summary of Hamaker's work) |
| 5 | Popping at about 180 °C | Virot & Ponomarenko (2015), "Popcorn: critical temperature, jump and sound", J. R. Soc. Interface 12:20141247: a critical temperature of about 180 °C (only 34% popped at 170 °C), reported by ScienceAlert https://www.sciencealert.com/mystery-solved-how-popcorn-pops ; Hoseney 1983 gives about 177 °C |
| 6 | The pressure inside reaches around ten times normal air pressure | Press reports of Virot & Ponomarenko 2015 (AFP/Khaleej Times: "around 10 bar, or 10 times the atmosphere at sea level"); Hoseney 1983: about 135 psi inside the kernel (about 9-10 atmospheres) |
| 7 | The hull cracks; the steam bursts out and the gel puffs into a white foam, turning the kernel inside out | Purdue: pressure builds until "the outside layer of the kernel ruptures"; the starch expands quickly and the kernel turns inside out |
| 8 | The foam sets solid in a few hundredths of a second | ScienceAlert on Virot & Ponomarenko: the starch changes from liquid to solid "in the course of a few hundredths of a second" |
| 9 | The pop sound isn't the shell cracking; high-speed video shows it lines up with the steam rushing out | Virot & Ponomarenko 2015 (synchronised high-speed video and audio): the pop coincides with the release of water vapour, not the hull fracture (ScienceAlert; NBC News coverage) |
| 10 | The kernel jumps by pushing off with a little "leg" of starch | Virot & Ponomarenko 2015: a "starch leg" compressed on the ground propels the kernel |
| 11 | Kernels that never pop usually have a cracked or weak hull, so the steam leaks out first | Purdue: "Some kernels don't pop because they have cuts or weaknesses in the outside layer" that let moisture escape; 4-47% unpopped depending on variety |
