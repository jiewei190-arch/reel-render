How does a cucumber turn into a pickle? In classic fermented dills, most of the sour isn't poured in, it's grown. Cucumbers carry lactic acid bacteria on their skins. In salty brine those bacteria eat the cucumber's sugars and make lactic acid plus bubbles of carbon dioxide. Over about three weeks the brine gets more sour and the color and flavor change. Salt holds back many of the microbes that would rot the cucumber while the lactic acid bacteria do fine, and it draws water and sugar into the brine. Once it's sour enough, harmful bacteria can't grow, which is why tested recipes say never cut the salt in a fermented pickle. Quick pickles skip the bacteria: they're covered in vinegar, acid that's already made. Either way, the goal is enough acid.

Sources: National Center for Home Food Preservation (University of Georgia); University of Minnesota Extension. For home canning, follow a tested recipe.
