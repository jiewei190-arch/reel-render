# Sources: pickles (How does a cucumber turn into a pickle?)
Researched Oct 28 2026.

- National Center for Home Food Preservation (NCHFP, University of Georgia), "General Information on Pickling": https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/pickle/general-information-pickling/general-information-on-pickling
- NCHFP, "Dill Pickles" (fermented, tested recipe): https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/ferment/pickles/dill-pickles
- NCHFP, "General Information on Fermenting": https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/ferment/general-information-on-fermenting
- University of Minnesota Extension, "How to make fermented pickles": https://extension.umn.edu/preserving-and-preparing/how-make-fermented-pickles

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| The sour in a fermented pickle is made by bacteria | UMN: bacteria "convert sugars into lactic acid and release carbon dioxide"; the acid preserves the food |
| Fermented dills start with cucumbers, salt water and dill; most of the sour isn't poured in | NCHFP dill recipe: cucumbers, dill, salt, water (+ optional spices) and only 1/4 cup vinegar per gallon; UMN: in fermentation "an acidifying component, such as vinegar ... is not added" (so "most of", not "no" vinegar) |
| Lactic acid bacteria are already on the cucumbers | UMN/Extension: naturally present lactic acid bacteria on the vegetable's surface (also NMSU/extension guides) |
| In brine, they eat the sugars and make lactic acid plus CO2 bubbles | UMN ("convert sugars into lactic acid and release carbon dioxide"; bubbles are expected) |
| About three weeks; colors and flavors change, acidity rises | NCHFP: "fermented and cured for about 3 weeks ... colors and flavors change and acidity increases"; dill recipe 3-4 weeks at 70-75°F |
| Salt holds back rot microbes, lactic acid bacteria do fine | NCHFP: "salt favors the growth of desirable bacteria while inhibiting the growth of others" |
| Salt draws water and sugar into the brine | UMN: salt helps "move water, carbohydrates, and acid produced by the bacteria between the brine and inside the produce cells" |
| Once sour enough, harmful bacteria can't grow | NCHFP: a minimum, uniform acid level prevents the growth of botulinum bacteria; UMN: ready at pH 4.6 or lower |
| Tested recipes say never cut the salt in fermented pickles | NCHFP/extension: do not reduce the salt in fermented pickles |
| Quick pickles skip fermentation: vinegar, acid already made | NCHFP: "Fresh-pack or quick-process pickles are not fermented" ... covered with vinegar |
| Either way the goal is enough acid | NCHFP: "The level of acidity ... is as important to its safety as it is to taste and texture" |
