# Sources: sneeze (why do we sneeze?)

- Primary, institutional: M. Seijo-Martínez, A. Varela-Freijanes, J. Grandes, F. Vázquez, "Sneeze related area in the medulla: localisation of the human sneezing centre?", J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry 77(4):559-561 (2006): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2077498 (cited as Seijo-Martínez 2006).
- Primary: M. Wang, X. Sun, Y. Shi, X. Song, H. Mi, "A genome-wide association study on photic sneeze reflex in the Chinese population", Scientific Reports 9:4993 (2019): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6428856 (cited as Wang 2019).
- University medical centre: UAMS Health (University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences), "Does Your Heart Stop for an Instant When You Sneeze?", cardiologist Dr. David Rutlen: https://uamshealth.com/medical-myths/does-your-heart-stop-for-an-instant-when-you-sneeze/

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| A sneeze is a reflex | Seijo-Martínez 2006 (the sneeze reflex, with a nasal phase and a respiratory phase) |
| It starts when dust, pollen or a germ irritates the lining of the nose | Seijo-Martínez 2006: the nasal phase begins with "stimulation of the nasal mucosa by chemical or physical irritants" |
| The nerve endings there belong to the trigeminal nerve | Seijo-Martínez 2006: "irritation of the trigeminal nerve terminals in the nasal mucosa" |
| It signals the brainstem, where scientists think a sneeze centre sits | Seijo-Martínez 2006: input converges "in the putative 'sneezing centre' in the medulla"; "The existence of a sneezing centre has not been confirmed in humans" (hence "scientists think") |
| Eyes close, deep breath, pressure builds, a blast of air shoots out | Seijo-Martínez 2006: the respiratory phase "consists of eye closing, deep inspiration, and then a forced expiration" |
| ...washing the dust and germs out of the nose | Seijo-Martínez 2006: the sudden airflow is described as "washing out mucosal debris and irritants" |
| For about one in four people, bright light does it too: the photic sneeze reflex | Wang 2019: prevalence 25.6% in 3,417 people, "comparable to previously reported PSR prevalence in several western countries" (the plaque says "25.6% in a 2019 study") |
| Nobody knows exactly why; one idea: light signals leak into the trigeminal nerve | Wang 2019: "the biomedical mechanism of PSR remains largely unknown to this date"; the trigeminal nerves are named a "potential candidate" mediator between eyes and nose |
| Myth: your heart stops when you sneeze. No: chest pressure can briefly change the heart's rhythm, but its electrical signal keeps going | UAMS Health: "your heart doesn't exactly stop"; intrathoracic pressure "momentarily increases" and the heart adjusts; the heart's electrical activity continues |
