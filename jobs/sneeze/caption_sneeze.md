Why do we sneeze? It's a reflex. When dust, pollen or a germ irritates the lining of your nose, the trigeminal nerve signals your brainstem (scientists think a "sneeze centre" sits there), and the same routine runs every time: eyes shut, a deep breath, pressure, then a blast that washes the irritants out. About one in four people also sneeze in bright light (the photic sneeze reflex), and nobody knows exactly why. And no, your heart doesn't stop: the chest pressure can briefly change its rhythm, but the electrical signal keeps going. 🤧☀️

Sources: Seijo-Martínez et al., J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry (2006); Wang et al., Scientific Reports (2019); UAMS Health (University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences).
