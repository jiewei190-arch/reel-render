Why is ice slippery? Not because your weight melts it. The old "pressure melting" answer from 1886 only gets ice down to about -3.5 °C, yet people skate at -35 °C, and you can slip standing still. Friction heat melts a thin film while you move, and the surface of ice isn't quite solid: in 2018 researchers found loose top molecules rolling across it like tiny spheres, with ice slipperiest around -7 °C. A 2025 simulation suggests rubbing scrambles the surface without melting it. The exact mix is still debated. ⛸️

Sources: R. Rosenberg, "Why Is Ice Slippery?", Physics Today (2005); Weber et al., J. Phys. Chem. Lett. (2018) and University of Amsterdam news; Atila, Sukhomlinov & Müser, Phys. Rev. Lett. (2025).
