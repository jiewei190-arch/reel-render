# Sources: voicerecording (Why does your voice sound weird on recordings?)
Researched Oct 28 2026.
- Timothy E. Hullar, MD, otolaryngologist, Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis, answering "Why does my voice sound so different when it is recorded and played back?", Scientific American: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-does-my-voice-sound-different/
- P. Orepic, O. A. Kannape, N. Faivre, O. Blanke, "Bone conduction facilitates self-other voice discrimination", Royal Society Open Science 10(2):221561, 2023 (EPFL Laboratory of Cognitive Neuroscience, Geneva): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9929504
- Supporting: Wikipedia, "Bone conduction" ("one reason why a person's voice sounds different to them when it is recorded and played back").

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| Your voice reaches your ears by two paths | Hullar: "Sound can reach the inner ear by way of two separate paths" |
| One through the air, out of your mouth and round to your ears | Hullar (air-conducted sound) |
| The other inside: vibrations through the skull straight to the inner ear (bone conduction) | Hullar: "Bone-conducted sound reaches the cochlea directly through the tissues of the head" |
| Your head boosts the low, deep tones | Hullar: "the mechanical properties of your head enhance its deeper, lower-frequency vibrations"; Orepic et al.: bone conduction "is assumed to instantiate a low-pass filter" |
| To you, your voice sounds deeper and fuller | Orepic et al.: "Because of the low-frequency emphasis, we hear our voice as lower"; Wikipedia: people "perceive their own voices to be lower and fuller than others do" |
| A microphone only hears the air path; the recording sounds thinner and higher | Hullar: on a recording you hear "only the air-conducted component in unfamiliar isolation"; Wikipedia: a recording "frequently sounds higher than one expects" |
| That's the voice everyone else hears | Hullar (listeners hear the air-conducted voice) |
| It sounds like a stranger because you rarely hear it from outside | Orepic et al.: one's own voice "is perceived as strange when played back in a recording"; "a reduced ability to recognize ourselves in air-conducted recordings" |
| 2023 study: people picked out their own voice more easily through bone-conduction headphones | Orepic et al. 2023: self-other voice discrimination improved with bone conduction (Aftershokz bone-conduction headphones vs air-conduction loudspeakers/headphones; voice morphs between self and other) |

## Softened / careful wording
1. "Picked out their own voice more easily": the study measured self-other discrimination of voice morphs, not everyday recognition.
2. Holzman & Rousey (1966), suggested by the main session, could not be verified from accessible sources, so it is not cited.
3. "Brought back the voice they knew" is the reel's paraphrase of the study's interpretation (bone conduction restores the familiar own-voice cue).
