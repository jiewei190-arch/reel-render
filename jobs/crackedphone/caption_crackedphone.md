Why does a phone screen crack from a short drop? Glass is very strong when squeezed but much weaker when pulled, and every glass surface has tiny flaws. When the glass bends, the pull piles up at the tip of the deepest flaw and a crack runs (A. A. Griffith worked this out in 1920). So phone glass gets a hot salt bath: big potassium ions swap in for smaller sodium ions at the surface, which squeezes the surface tight, so small flaws stay shut. But that squeezed layer is only about as thick as a hair. Land face-down on pavement and a sharp grain can drive a flaw right through it. When Corning studied broken phones, sharp contact caused over 70% of the failures. Exposed edges are weak spots too. It's not the height, it's what the glass meets.

Sources: Corning (strengthening glass; Gorilla Glass field-failure study via Laser Focus World); Varshneya & Bihuniak, American Ceramic Society Bulletin (2017); Lehigh University glass-strength lecture.
