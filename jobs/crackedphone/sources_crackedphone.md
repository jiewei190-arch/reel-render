# Sources: crackedphone (Why does a phone screen crack from a short drop?)
Researched Oct 28 2026. corning.com returned 403 to the fetch tool, so Corning's own statements are cited from trade-press reports of Corning's releases.

- Corning, "How It Works: Strengthening Glass" (Science of Glass): https://www.corning.com/worldwide/en/innovation/the-glass-age/science-of-glass/how-it-works-strengthening-glass.html (seen through search snippets: ion exchange in a molten salt bath, potassium ions replace smaller sodium ions, forming a compressive stress layer; breaks start at surface flaws pulled into tension)
- Varshneya A. K., Bihuniak P. P., "Cover screens for personal electronic devices: Strengthened glass or sapphire?", Am. Ceram. Soc. Bulletin 96(5):20-25, June/July 2017: https://bulletin.ceramics.org/article/cover-screens-for-personal-electronic-devices-strengthened-glass-or-sapphire/
- Lehigh University, Optical/photonic glass course, Lecture 8 (glass strength, Griffith flaws): https://www.lehigh.edu/imi/teched/OPG/lecture8.pdf
- Laser Focus World, "Corning introduces Gorilla Glass 4" (Nov 2014, reporting Corning's field-failure study): https://www.laserfocusworld.com/articles/2014/11/corning-introduces-gorilla-glass-4-next-generation-of-chemically-strengthened-glass-for-displays-devices.html

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| Glass is far stronger in compression than in tension | Lehigh lecture 8 (Griffith: compressive strength ~8× tensile for sharp cracks); Corning (breaks grow when the glass is pulled into tension) |
| Every glass surface has tiny flaws from handling | Lehigh: "microscopic ... flaws ... at the surface ... usually the result of handling or abrasion" |
| Under bending, the stress concentrates at the flaw tip | Lehigh: flaws "act as stress concentrators" (Inglis 1913, Griffith 1920) |
| Griffith, 1920: the flaw focuses the force and the crack runs | Lehigh lecture 8 |
| Phone glass gets a molten salt bath; big potassium swaps for small sodium at the surface | ACerS Bulletin 2017 (Na+ out, larger K+ in, molten KNO3); Corning |
| The surface ends up squeezed (compressive layer) | ACerS Bulletin ("stuffing produces large surface compression"); Corning |
| A crack must beat that compression before it grows; small flaws stay shut | ACerS Bulletin ("must be overcome by an applied tensile stress"); Laser Focus World ("if a small flaw is formed, it does not propagate") |
| The squeezed layer is about as thick as a hair | ACerS Bulletin: depth of layer 25-60 µm typical; Gorilla Glass 4 ~90 µm (Laser Focus World); a human hair is roughly 50-100 µm thick |
| Face-down on pavement, a sharp grain can drive a flaw through it | Laser Focus World (Corning's drop test: face down onto a rough surface; sharp contact); ACerS ("falling on a non-smooth surface, such as pavement") |
| Corning: sharp contact caused over 70% of field failures it studied | Laser Focus World: "damage caused by sharp contact accounted for more than 70% of field failures" |
| Exposed edges are weak spots | ACerS Bulletin: "Drop breakage is surface- and edge-related"; thin designs with exposed edges are most likely to fracture |
