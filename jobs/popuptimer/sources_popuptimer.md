# Sources: popuptimer (How does a turkey pop-up timer know when it's done?)
Researched Oct 28 2026. Agency, institute and patent sources first.

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| No battery: a spring and a plug of soft metal (or, in newer timers, a wax-like material) | AIP Inside Science, "How pop-up turkey timers work": housing, rod, spring, and a pin of "thermoplastic substance" (metal or wax) chosen to melt at the cooking temperature https://www.aip.org/inside-science/brief-how-pop-up-turkey-timers-work ; Volk patent US 5,799,606 "Pop-up temperature indicating device" (coiled spring, fusible material holding the rod; alloys "increasingly being replaced by organic materials") https://patents.google.com/patent/US5799606 |
| A red stick sits on a squeezed spring; its tip is locked into the base | US 5,799,606 (rod held in a withdrawn position by the fusible material; spring around the rod) |
| At a set temperature the plug melts, the spring pushes the stick up | AIP Inside Science; US 5,799,606 ("the fusible material softens or melts, releasing" the rod) |
| The idea came from fire sprinklers (metal plug that melts in heat) | AIP Inside Science (turkey farmer George "Goldie" Kliewer drew the idea from fire sprinklers' melting alloy plugs) |
| California turkey growers got it patented in 1961 | AIP Inside Science (the California Turkey Producers Advisory Board wanted a done-ness gadget; original design patented in 1961) |
| In one 2016 test, Consumer Reports tried 21 timers; three popped too early, one at 139 °F | Consumer Reports, "Meat thermometer or pop-up timer for turkey?" (Diane Umansky, Nov 22 2016) https://www.consumerreports.org/meat-thermometers/meat-thermometer-or-pop-up-timer-for-turkey (21 timers; three popped below the safe zone, one as low as 139.5 °F) |
| USDA: a whole turkey is safe at 165 °F, measured with a food thermometer; check the innermost thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast (on screen: "165°F in the thickest part") | USDA FSIS / USDA blog "Turkey fundamentals" https://www.usda.gov/about-usda/news/blog/turkey-fundamentals-top-questions-cooking-turkey ; USDA myths post (pop-up is a good tool, but also check the three places) ; Michigan State University Extension https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/two_recommendations_for_your_thanksgiving_turkey |

## Softened / careful wording
1. No melting temperature is spoken or shown: sources disagree (about 165-185 °F; some say 178 °F), so the script says "at a set temperature".
2. "California turkey growers got it patented": the AIP piece credits a turkey farmer working with the California Turkey Producers Advisory Board; the single inventor is not named in the voiceover.
3. "In one 2016 test" (main-session review: it's an older test, dated from the article); CR's low reading was 139.5 °F, shown as 139 °F.
4. "It only feels one spot in the bird": a plain consequence of where it sits (the breast); the advice to also check the thigh and wing is USDA's and MSU Extension's.
5. "A handy hint": USDA itself calls the timers a useful tool, so the reel doesn't call them useless.
