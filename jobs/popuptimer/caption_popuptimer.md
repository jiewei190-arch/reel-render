How does a turkey pop-up timer know when the turkey's done? There's no battery inside: a red stick sits on a squeezed spring, and its tip is locked into the base by a plug of soft metal, or a wax-like material in newer timers. As the turkey cooks, the plug heats up. At a set temperature it melts, nothing holds the spring, and the stick pops up. The idea came from fire sprinklers, and the timer was patented in 1961. But it only feels one spot, and when Consumer Reports tested 21 timers, three popped too early. USDA says a turkey is safe at 165°F: check the thigh, the wing and the thickest part of the breast with a food thermometer. Does your family trust the pop-up?
