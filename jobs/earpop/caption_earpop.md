Why do your ears pop on a plane? That pop is a tiny bubble of air squeezing through your Eustachian tube, the pencil-lead-thin door to the air pocket behind your eardrum. Up high, the cabin is kept like a mountain town, so on the climb the air in your ear pushes out. On the way down the cabin pressure climbs back up, air has to get *in*, and the tube only opens when you swallow, yawn or chew. That's why landing is the hard part, and why babies cry near the end of a flight. ✈️ (Educational only, not medical advice.)
