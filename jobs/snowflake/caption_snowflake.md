Why do snowflakes have six sides? It starts with one water molecule: one oxygen and two hydrogens in a bent little V. When water freezes, hydrogen bonds lock the molecules into rings of six, ring after ring, a honeycomb of ice. So every snow crystal starts as a tiny six-sided prism, and as it grows, branches sprout from its six corners. The six arms match because they grow at the same time through the same air, and no two flakes fall the same path, so no two look exactly alike. ❄️
