# Sources: darkeyes (Why the dark gets less dark after a few minutes)

| Claim in script | Source |
|---|---|
| Two kinds of light cells: cones (bright light, colour) and rods | Wikipedia, "Rod cell": rods are the main source of night vision and "have little role in color vision". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_cell ; Wikipedia, "Adaptation (eye)" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptation_(eye) |
| One rod can respond to a single particle of light (photon) | Wikipedia, "Rod cell": "A rod cell is sensitive enough to respond to a single photon of light." |
| Around a hundred million rods, far more than cones | Wikipedia, "Rod cell": "approximately 92 million rod cells (vs ~4.6 million cones)"; elsewhere "about 120 million rod cells compared to 6 to 7 million cone cells" |
| Rods sit mostly around the edges of the retina | Wikipedia, "Rod cell": "Rods are usually found concentrated at the outer edges of the retina and are used in peripheral vision." |
| Bright light bleaches rhodopsin; in the dark it is rebuilt | Wikipedia, "Adaptation (eye)": rhodopsin "immediately photobleaches in response to light"; dark adaptation "requires the regeneration of the visual pigment from opsin and 11-cis retinal." |
| Cones recover first, in about ten minutes; then rods keep improving | Wikipedia, "Adaptation (eye)": "Cone cells are able to regain maximum retinal sensitivity in 9–10 minutes of darkness"; rods keep improving (maximum after about two hours) |
| After 20-30 minutes, up to a million times more sensitive | Wikipedia, "Adaptation (eye)": "approximately 20–30 minutes to fully adapt"; the eye "becomes 10,000 to 1,000,000 times more sensitive than at full daylight." |
| Pupils widen, but only about tenfold | Wikipedia, "Adaptation (eye)": the pupillary reflex is "a quick but minor mechanism", adjusting light "by about a factor of ten." |
| One bright flash bleaches it again | Wikipedia, "Adaptation (eye)" (rhodopsin photobleaches immediately in light) |
| Pilots and ship crews use red light at night; rods barely notice red | Wikipedia, "Adaptation (eye)": "rod cells are insensitive to long wavelengths"; aviators wear red-lensed goggles; red light on a ship's bridge at night; submarines "rigged for red". |
| Rods don't do colour, so night looks grey | Wikipedia, "Rod cell": rods "have little role in color vision, which is the main reason why colors are much less apparent in dim light." |

## Softened / unverified
- "Up to a million times" (source range 10,000 to 1,000,000), "around a hundred million" rods (sources give 92-120 million).
- "After twenty to thirty minutes": rods keep improving for up to ~2 hours; 20-30 min is the usual figure for "fully adapted".
- "Night looks grey" simplifies "colours are much less apparent in dim light".
- The retina "village" (cone towers in the centre, rod cottages at the edges) and the rhodopsin "windows" are metaphors, not anatomy.
