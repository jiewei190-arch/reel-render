# Sources: why the sky is blue, sunsets red, clouds white

| Claim in reel | Source |
|---|---|
| Sunlight looks white but is a mix of every colour | Met Office, optical effects: why is the sky blue https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/weather/learn-about/weather/optical-effects/why-is-the-sky-blue |
| Blue light has short waves (~400 nm), red long waves (~700 nm) | Met Office (same page) |
| Air molecules are much smaller than a light wave; light bounces off in every direction = scattering; blue scatters much more than red | Met Office; UCAR SciEd https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/atmosphere/why-sky-blue |
| Violet: sun sends less of it and our eyes see blue better (no claim about atmospheric absorption made) | Met Office; UCAR SciEd |
| The sky is not a reflection of the ocean (myth) | Royal Museums Greenwich https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/topics/why-is-sky-blue |
| Sunset: low sun, light crosses much more air, blue scattered out, reds/oranges left | Met Office; UCAR SciEd |
| Clouds: droplets much bigger, scatter all colours about equally, look white; thick rain clouds look darker as less light gets through | Met Office, why are clouds white https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/weather/learn-about/weather/types-of-weather/clouds |

## Dropped
- Any numeric scattering ratio (the "1/wavelength^4" figure) — not quoted, kept qualitative "far more".
- Any claim that violet is absorbed by the upper atmosphere.
