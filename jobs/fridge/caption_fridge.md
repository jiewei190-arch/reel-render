How does a refrigerator keep food cold? It doesn't make cold, it moves heat. A refrigerant runs round a loop: inside, it's cold and at low pressure, so it boils and soaks up heat from your food. The compressor squeezes it until it's hotter than your kitchen, and the coils on the back dump that heat into the room. Then it squeezes through a tiny valve, expands, and gets cold again. That's why the back of your fridge feels warm. Air conditioners and heat pumps use the same trick.

Sources: Penn State EGEE 102 "Refrigerators"; US EPA "Heat Pumps".
