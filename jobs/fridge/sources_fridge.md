# Sources: fridge (How does a refrigerator keep food cold?)
Researched Oct 28 2026.
- Penn State University, EGEE 102 "Energy Conservation for Environmental Protection", lesson 6.4 "Refrigerators": https://courses.ems.psu.edu/egee102/node/2402
- US EPA, Burn Wise "Heat Pumps": https://www.epa.gov/burnwise/heat-pumps (energy.gov pages tried returned 404, so the federal source is EPA)

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| The fridge doesn't make cold, it moves heat out of the box into the kitchen | Penn State: "Its job is to extract thermal energy from the cool interior and push it out into the warmer kitchen." |
| A refrigerant runs round a loop of pipes | Penn State (refrigerant cycle) |
| Inside, the refrigerant is cold and at low pressure, so it boils into a gas | Penn State: "The cold, low-pressure refrigerant flows through them and evaporates (turns from liquid to gas)." |
| Boiling takes energy, so it soaks up heat from the air and food | Penn State: "Evaporation requires energy." "The refrigerant steals that energy as heat from the air and food inside the fridge." |
| The compressor squeezes the gas, making it hot, hotter than the kitchen | Penn State: "The compressor squeezes the now-warm refrigerant gas, increasing its pressure and temperature dramatically." |
| In the coils on the back, heat flows out into the room | Penn State: "Because the refrigerant is now hotter than the kitchen air, heat naturally flows out of the coils and into the room." "...the black coils you might feel on the back or bottom of your fridge." |
| It turns back into a liquid | Penn State: "The refrigerant condenses back into a liquid, ready to repeat the cycle." |
| It squeezes through a tiny valve, expands and gets cold again | Penn State: "High-pressure liquid refrigerant squeezes through it and suddenly expands into a low-pressure zone"; rapid expansion cools the fluid |
| The back of the fridge feels warm: that's your food's heat | Penn State (heat from inside released at the condenser coils) |
| An open fridge can't cool the kitchen: the heat it pulls in comes out the back | Follows from the cycle above (Penn State); worded as heat going round, not as a temperature figure |
| Air conditioners and heat pumps use the same trick | EPA: "Similar to a refrigerator, heat pumps use electricity to transfer thermal energy"; "heat pumps don't actually create heat, they just transfer it from one place to another" |

## Softened / careful wording
1. "The motor at the bottom" for the compressor's usual position (some models differ).
2. No claim that an open fridge warms the kitchen (it does slightly, from the compressor's work), only that it can't cool it.
