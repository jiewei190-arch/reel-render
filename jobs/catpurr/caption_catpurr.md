How do cats purr? Scientists still argue about it. The classic answer: muscles in the larynx, the voice box, twitch about 25 to 30 times a second, so the vocal folds open and snap shut and chop the air into a rumble, breathing in and breathing out. But in 2023, researchers in Vienna blew air through cat voice boxes in the lab with no nerves or muscles working, and they still purred at 25 to 30 times a second. Soft pads of tissue inside the vocal folds make them heavy, so they vibrate slowly, which is how a small cat makes such a deep sound. Muscles or pads? Probably both. And cats don't only purr when happy: they purr at the vet, when hurt, while nursing, and some hide a cry inside the purr to ask for food. Is your cat a loud purrer?

Sources: Herbst et al. 2023 (Current Biology, purring without neural input); Remmers & Gautier 1972 (Respiration Physiology); McComb et al. 2009 (Current Biology, the cry embedded within the purr); University of Vienna.
