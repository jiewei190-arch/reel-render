# Sources: harvestmoon (why the October moon looks huge and orange)

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| Near the horizon the Moon looks more yellow/orange because its light crosses more atmosphere, which scatters away the shorter, bluer wavelengths | NASA Science, "The Moon Illusion: Why Does the Moon Look So Big Sometimes?": https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/moon/the-moon-illusion-why-does-the-moon-look-so-big-sometimes/ |
| Same reason sunsets look red (more air scatters blue away) | Sky at Night Magazine, "Why the Moon sometimes looks orange": https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/why-moon-sometimes-looks-orange ; NASA article above |
| Moonlight is sunlight reflected off the Moon | NASA article above (general); NASA Moon facts |
| Hold up a fingernail: it and the Moon are about the same size | NASA article above ("your fingernail and the Moon are about the same size") |
| Photos show the Moon is the same width near the horizon as high in the sky; the big horizon Moon is an illusion in the brain | NASA article above; NASA APOD 2009 June 16 "Moonrise over Turkey": https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2009-june-16-moonrise-over-turkey |
| One idea: foreground objects (hills, trees) make the horizon Moon seem farther away, so the brain reads it as bigger (Ponzo-type explanation) | NASA article above (Ponzo illusion); NASA APOD 2009 ("foreground objects make a horizon Moon seem farther in the distance") |
| Astronauts in orbit also see the Moon illusion, with no foreground objects | NASA article above |
| There is still no fully satisfying scientific explanation | NASA article above ("there's still not a satisfying scientific explanation") |
| The Hunter's Moon is the first full Moon after the Harvest Moon (the Harvest Moon is the full Moon closest to the autumn equinox) | StarDate (University of Texas McDonald Observatory), "Hunter's Moon": https://stardate.org/podcast/10-16-24 ; Old Farmer's Almanac: https://www.almanac.com/what-makes-hunters-moon-special |
| Name tradition: fields were bare after the harvest and hunters could track game by moonlight | StarDate; Old Farmer's Almanac (as above) |
