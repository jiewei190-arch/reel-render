Why does cracking your knuckles make a pop? Nothing breaks. Your knuckle is a capsule of joint fluid, and when you pull the bones apart the pressure drops, gas comes out of the fluid and a cavity snaps open. In 2015 researchers filmed it inside an MRI: the cavity formed at the moment of the crack and stayed afterwards. A 2018 model says a partial collapse could make the same sound, so the debate isn't settled. It takes about half an hour to crack the same knuckle again. And arthritis? One doctor cracked only his left hand for 60+ years and won an Ig Nobel Prize for finding no difference. 🖐️ (Educational only, not medical advice.)

Sources: Kawchuk et al., PLOS ONE (2015); Chandran Suja & Barakat, Scientific Reports (2018); Unger, Arthritis & Rheumatism (1998) / Ig Nobel 2009; Castellanos & Axelrod, Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases (1990); Arthritis UK.
