Why does the time change mess with your sleep? Your wall clock jumps an hour in a second, but your body's master clock (the SCN, a cluster of nerve cells in your brain) is set by light and shifts slowly. Left alone it runs a bit long, about 24 hours and 10 minutes, so shifting later (fall back) is easy and shifting earlier (spring forward) is hard. One study found sleep adjusted after fall back but not after spring forward, especially for night owls, and US fatal car crashes rose about 6% the week after spring forward, with no rise after fall back. 🕰️ (Educational only, not medical advice.)

Sources: NIH NIGMS "Circadian Rhythms"; Czeisler et al., Science (1999); Kantermann et al., Current Biology (2007); Fritz et al., Current Biology (2020); American Academy of Sleep Medicine position statement (2020).
