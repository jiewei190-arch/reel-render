When you sleep, your brain doesn't switch off. It runs through cycles of about 80 to 100 minutes, four to six times a night: deep slow-wave sleep early on, more REM later, when your eyes dart, your brain is about as active as when you're awake, and dreaming usually happens. Sleep helps form long-term memories, and in mice, researchers found the brain clears waste faster while asleep.

Sources: NHLBI/NIH (Stages of Sleep; Why Is Sleep Important?); Xie et al., Science 2013 (mouse study); Discover Magazine (why we dream is still debated).
