# Sources: sleep (what sleep and dreams actually do)

| Claim in the reel | Source |
|---|---|
| Sleep runs in cycles of non-REM and REM sleep lasting about 80 to 100 minutes; most people go through four to six cycles a night | NHLBI (NIH), "Stages of Sleep" https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/stages-of-sleep |
| Non-REM sleep has three stages: drowsiness, true sleep begins, deep slow-wave sleep; deep sleep occurs mostly in the first part of the night | NHLBI, "Stages of Sleep" (above) |
| REM: eyes move rapidly, brain about as active as when awake; dreaming usually happens during REM; muscles go limp so you don't act out dreams; REM occurs more often later in the night; newborns spend more time in REM | NHLBI, "Stages of Sleep" (above) |
| Blood pressure and heart rate fall during non-REM sleep | NHLBI, "Why Is Sleep Important?" https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/why-sleep-important |
| Sleep helps with learning and the formation of long-term memories; too little sleep makes it harder to focus and think clearly | NHLBI, "Why Is Sleep Important?" (above) |
| In mice, brain waste (including beta-amyloid) clearance increased during sleep: interstitial space expanded by about 60% and CSF-interstitial exchange rose | Xie et al., "Sleep drives metabolite clearance from the adult brain", Science 2013, mice; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3867735 ; NIH Office of Intramural Research lecture page https://oir.nih.gov/wals/2014-2015/nightlife-brain |
| Why we dream is still debated | Discover Magazine, "Why do we dream? Science offers a few possibilities" https://www.discovermagazine.com/why-do-we-dream-science-offers-a-few-possibilities-42073 ; International Journal of Dream Research commentary (2021) |

## Dropped / unverified
- Glymphatic-system clearance in humans: the reel says only "in mice" because the key study (Xie 2013) is a mouse study; no human claim is made.
- "REM consolidates memory" and "dreams process emotion": popular-site claims, treated as hypotheses and dropped.
- Exact hypnogram shape and REM block timing are illustrative; only "more deep sleep early, more REM later" is claimed (NHLBI).
- The NINDS "Brain Basics: Understanding Sleep" page returned 403 to the fetch tool, so NHLBI/NIH pages were used instead.
