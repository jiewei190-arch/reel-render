How does a candle actually burn? The wick isn't the fuel: the wax is, as a gas. The flame's heat melts a cup of wax at the top, the liquid climbs the wick like water up a towel, and near the flame it turns into vapour. That vapour is what burns. Michael Faraday showed it in his 1860 lectures for young people: blow a candle out, light the smoke, and a trail of fire runs down and relights the wick. The dark core of the flame is vapour that hasn't burned yet; the yellow glow is tiny specks of solid carbon, so hot they shine. It all ends up as water and carbon dioxide. Have you ever relit a candle through its smoke?

Source: Michael Faraday, The Chemical History of a Candle (Royal Institution lectures, 1860-61).
