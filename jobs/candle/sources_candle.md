# Sources: candle (How does a candle actually burn?)
Researched Oct 28 2026. Primary source: Michael Faraday, *The Chemical History of a Candle* (Christmas lectures for young people at the Royal Institution, 1860-61; ed. W. Crookes), Project Gutenberg #14474 https://www.gutenberg.org/files/14474/14474-0.txt

| Claim (script) | Source (Faraday, unless noted) |
|---|---|
| The wick isn't the fuel; the wax is, as a gas | Lecture II: "this is the vapour from the middle of the candle, produced by its own heat" (the vapour is the fuel that burns) |
| In 1860 Faraday asked the same question in a famous lecture for young people | The book is his 1860-61 Royal Institution Christmas lectures "to a juvenile auditory" (title page / preface) |
| The flame's heat melts the wax at the top into a little cup of liquid | Lecture I (the cup formed at the top of the candle by the melting fuel) |
| The liquid climbs the wick, like water up a towel | Lecture I: "the particles of melted tallow ascend the cotton and get to the top" (capillary attraction; Faraday's own comparison is a towel) |
| Near the flame the liquid turns into vapour, and the vapour is what burns | Lecture II (vapour drawn from the middle of the flame with a glass tube and lit at the other end) |
| Blow a candle out and light the smoke: a trail of fire runs down and relights the wick | Lecture I: "you will observe a train of fire going through the air till it reaches the candle" |
| A dark core of vapour that hasn't burned yet | Lecture II (the dark middle part of the flame is unburnt vapour; drawn off with a tube) |
| The yellow glow is tiny specks of solid carbon, so hot they shine | Lecture II: "it is to this presence of solid particles in the candle-flame that it owes its brilliancy" |
| The vapour burns with oxygen from the air into water and carbon dioxide | Lecture III: "that part of the product is just water—nothing but water"; Lecture V: carbonic acid (carbon dioxide) |
| Faraday caught the water condensing on a cold surface above the flame | Lecture III (a cold vessel held over the flame collects water) |

## Softened / careful wording
1. Hook reworded after review to "The wick isn't the fuel": the wick does char and slowly burn at its tip; the fuel burning in the flame is the wax vapour (Faraday).
2. "Carbon dioxide" for Faraday's "carbonic acid" (his 1860 name).
3. No flame temperatures or zone colours beyond what Faraday describes (the blue base is shown in the art but not claimed in the voiceover).
