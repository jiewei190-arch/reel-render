Why do we get goosebumps? Under every hair is a tiny muscle, the arrector pili. When you get cold, a nerve tells it to squeeze: the hair stands up and the skin bunches into a bump. On a furry animal that fluffs the coat and traps warm air; on us, with most of the fur gone, it's mostly a leftover. The twist: Harvard scientists found the same nerve also connects to the stem cells that grow hair, and in mice a long cold spell made it fire harder and grow fur faster. 🐈❄️

Sources: Shwartz et al. (Hsu lab, Harvard), Cell 2020; New York Stem Cell Foundation; ScienceAlert on the Harvard release; Science News Explores.
