# Sources: mpemba (does hot water really freeze faster than cold?)

Primary source: Henry C. Burridge and Paul F. Linden, "Questioning the Mpemba effect: hot water does not cool more quickly than cold", Scientific Reports 6:37665 (2016), open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5121640/ (cited below as B&L).

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| The idea is old: Aristotle, Bacon and Descartes wrote about it | B&L quote them: Aristotle, "The fact that the water has previously been warmed contributes to its freezing quickly"; Bacon, "slightly warm water will freeze more easily than water which is altogether cold"; Descartes, "water which has been kept hot for a long time freezes faster than any other sort" |
| Named after Erasto Mpemba, a Tanzanian schoolboy whose hot ice-cream mix froze before a classmate's cooler mix | B&L: Mpemba, a pupil in Tanzania, put a boiling milk-and-sugar mixture in the freezer while another pupil cooled his first, and the hot one froze first |
| In 1969 he and physicist Denis Osborne published tests where hot water started freezing first | E. B. Mpemba and D. G. Osborne, "Cool?", Physics Education 4(3):172-175 (1969) [primary]; as summarised by B&L (they documented "the time for water to start freezing"; e.g. 90 °C to freezing point in 30 min vs 100 min from 20 °C). The reel shows the curves qualitatively, with no minute numbers |
| In 2016, two scientists ran very careful tests and found no real evidence that hot water cools faster | B&L: "we ... carry out our own experiments by cooling water in carefully controlled conditions"; "there is no evidence to support meaningful observations of the Mpemba effect"; seven thermocouples at set heights in their tank experiments |
| Put the thermometer just 1 cm off and the result can flip | B&L: if "the vertical position of the temperature measurements were incorrect, by just 1 cm", results "could, quite incorrectly, be described as being observations of the Mpemba effect" |
| Possible reasons some see it: evaporation, dissolved gas, convection currents, supercooling | B&L, quoting the Royal Society of Chemistry competition entry: "(a) evaporation, (b) dissolved gases, (c) mixing by convective currents, and (d) supercooling" |
| The honest answer: sometimes, under special conditions, and scientists still argue | B&L find no meaningful evidence for water cooling; the reel presents the proposed mechanisms as "suspects" and the verdict as contested, not as settled either way |

Softened on purpose: no claim that the effect is real or fake for every freezer; no exact timings; "a physicist" for Osborne (as B&L describe him, a visiting lecturer); "two scientists" for Burridge and Linden, without naming their universities.
