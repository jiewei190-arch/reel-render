Does hot water really freeze faster than cold? The idea goes back to Aristotle, Bacon and Descartes, and it's named after Erasto Mpemba, a Tanzanian schoolboy whose hot ice-cream mix froze first. In 1969 he and physicist Denis Osborne published tests where hot water started freezing first. But careful tests in 2016 found no real evidence for it, and showed that moving a thermometer just 1 cm can flip the result. Evaporation, dissolved gas, currents and supercooling might matter in some freezers. Honest answer: sometimes, maybe, and scientists still argue. 🧊🔥

Sources: Burridge & Linden, "Questioning the Mpemba effect", Scientific Reports (2016); Mpemba & Osborne, "Cool?", Physics Education (1969).
