# runnynose: 3 opening lines (template #4 Crayon picture book)

Search question spoken in line 2: "So why does your nose run when it's cold?" (subtitle at 4.0-8.6 s, over the "not sick!" thermometer page).

1. **PICK** "Step outside on a freezing morning, and your nose starts to... drip." (12 words) Pattern 1, mid-action. Frame 0: a crayon kid in a red beanie and striped scarf under a cold watercolour sky with white-wax snow falling, a drop already swelling on the nose, "drip." written in red. The drop falls at about 1.7 s.
2. "Your nose makes extra water on purpose when it's cold." (10 words) Pattern 2, contradiction. Frame 0: the overflowing "more than it needs" cup.
3. "That winter drip isn't a cold. It's your nose working overtime." (11 words) Pattern 3, the result first. Frame 0: the crossed-out germ and the nose in a hard hat.

Why 1: everyone knows the feeling, and the drop moves from frame 0. Line 2 turns at once ("But you're not sick"). Line 3 gives the payoff away.
Open question: why does cold make your nose run? Built as two drips. Drip one (8-37 s): the nose is the lungs' air conditioner (warm + wet), cold air is dry, nerves raise the alarm, extra blood (re-hook at 25 s), glands make more mucus and water; the twist: more than it needs (31-35 s, wow), and it drips out. Drip two (37-50 s): warm, wet breath hits the cold tip and condenses, like a foggy window (re-hook at 48 s). Then the name (cold-induced rhinitis / skier's nose), "5 to 9 out of 10", and the payoff: "not a germ, your nose working overtime".
