Why does your nose run when it's cold? Your nose is the air conditioner for your lungs: it warms and wets every breath. Cold winter air is usually very dry, so nerves in your nose raise the alarm, your brain sends extra blood to warm the air, and tiny glands pump out more mucus and water, often more than it needs, so the extra drips out. A second drip comes from your warm, wet breath hitting the cold tip of your nose and condensing, like breath fogging a cold window. Doctors call it cold-induced rhinitis, or "skier's nose". 🤧❄️
