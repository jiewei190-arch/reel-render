# Sources: zipper ("How a zipper locks")

| Claim in the reel | Source |
|---|---|
| Two rows of metal or plastic teeth ("elements") interlock to join two fabric edges | Wikipedia, "Zipper" https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipper |
| A slider with a Y-shaped channel meshes the teeth together or separates them depending on direction; it acts on the teeth one at a time | Wikipedia, "Zipper" |
| Each tooth has a rounded projection that snaps into a recess on the opposing tooth; teeth deform elastically and spring back, so the locked state is stable | Wikipedia, "Zipper" |
| Unlike a drawstring (which relies on tension), a zipper is a lock-and-key design | Wikipedia, "Zipper" |
| 1893: Whitcomb Judson patented a "clasp locker" shoe fastener shown at the Chicago World's Fair; little commercial success, not used in clothing | Wikipedia, "Zipper" |
| Gideon Sundbäck redesigned it; the zipper first went on sale in 1913 | Wikipedia, "Zipper" |
| 1923: B.F. Goodrich used it on rubber galoshes and called it the "zipper"; the name stuck | Wikipedia, "Zipper" |
