Why do your ears ring after a concert? 🎸 It isn't sound stuck in your ears. Deep inside, the cochlea is lined with tiny hair cells that sway with sound and send signals to your brain. A concert can hit 94 to 110 decibels, and every 10 dB more is 10 times the intensity, so those hair cells get pushed too hard for too long. The ringing (tinnitus) is your hearing reacting to the strain; muffled hearing often recovers within a day or two. But human hair cells don't grow back (birds and frogs can regrow theirs), and long or repeated sound at 85 dB or more can cause lasting hearing loss. The good news: it's completely preventable. Earplugs, and stand back from the speakers.

Sources: NIDCD (NIH), Noise-Induced Hearing Loss; CDC/NIOSH, Noise and Hearing Loss Prevention.
