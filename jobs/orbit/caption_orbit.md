To reach orbit, a rocket doesn't just go up. It goes sideways. The space station moves at about 17,500 miles per hour, fast enough that as it falls toward Earth, the ground curves away beneath it. Orbit isn't escaping gravity: at the station's height, gravity is still about 90 percent as strong. It's falling and missing the ground, which is why astronauts float.

Sources: NASA (What Is Microgravity?; Space Station Facts and Figures; Basics of Space Flight, Gravity & Mechanics; Technical Memorandum TM X-52396); NOAA SciJinks.
