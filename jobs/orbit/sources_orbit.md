# Sources: orbit (how a rocket stays in orbit)

| Claim in the reel | Source |
|---|---|
| A launch vehicle starts vertically then tips over; for a circular orbit the burnout attitude is horizontal and the real requirement is a very large horizontal velocity (about 25,750 ft/s at 100 miles in the memo); early thrust mostly gains altitude | NASA Technical Memorandum TM X-52396, https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19680008648/downloads/19680008648.pdf |
| A spacecraft must speed to the neighbourhood of 30,000 km/h to keep falling around Earth; with enough launch speed Newton's cannonball "falls completely around Earth! We can say it has achieved orbit" | NASA Science, Basics of Space Flight ch. 3 "Gravity & Mechanics", https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter3-4/ |
| Slow shot drops, faster shot lands farther, fast enough and it never lands (Newton's cannon) | NASA Science ch. 3 (above); Bucknell Astronomy 101 "Newton's Cannonball" https://eg.bucknell.edu/physics/astronomy/astr101/specials/newtscannon.html |
| The ISS moves at 17,500 miles per hour | NASA, "What Is Microgravity? (Grades 5-8)" https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-is-microgravity-grades-5-8/ |
| The station travels at five miles per second; orbits Earth about every 90 minutes; 16 orbits in 24 hours | NASA, Space Station Facts and Figures https://www.nasa.gov/international-space-station/space-station-facts-and-figures/ |
| At 200-250 miles up, gravity is about 90 percent of surface gravity | NASA, "What Is Microgravity? (Grades 5-8)" (above) |
| Astronauts float because they are in free fall: the spacecraft, crew and objects are "falling toward but around Earth" | NASA, "What Is Microgravity? (Grades 5-8)" (above) |
| Thin upper atmosphere gives a little drag in low Earth orbit; satellites need occasional rocket thrusts or the orbit decays | NASA Science ch. 3 (above); NOAA SciJinks "Why don't satellites fall out of the sky?" https://scijinks.gov/about/k-12-education/satellites-technology/why-dont-satellites-fall-out-of-the-sky |

## Dropped / unverified
- "Most of a rocket's energy goes to sideways speed": not stated by any source found, so the script only says the real job is building sideways speed (supported by the NASA memo).
- Exact ISS altitude: not stated on the NASA facts page, so none is given (NASA gives 200-250 miles in the microgravity article for the gravity figure).
- Earth, rocket, cannon and station models are illustrative, not to scale; the cannon trajectories are a real inverse-square simulation with illustrative numbers.
