How does your phone's GPS know where you are? About thirty GPS satellites circle the Earth twice a day, 20,200 km up, each broadcasting where it is and what time it is. Your phone times how long each signal takes to arrive, turns that into a distance, and with at least four satellites pins your spot (trilateration). The twist: the timing has to be perfect, so each satellite carries an atomic clock, and relativity makes those clocks gain about 38 microseconds a day. Without correcting for it, your position would drift by about 10 km a day.

Sources: GPS.gov (Space Segment); Richard Pogge, Ohio State University, "GPS and Relativity".
