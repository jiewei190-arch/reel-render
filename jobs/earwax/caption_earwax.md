What is earwax actually for? 👂 It isn't dirt, it's your ear's built-in cleaner. Glands in the ear canal make a waxy oil called cerumen. It traps dust and germs, keeps the skin moist and even fights bacteria. Old wax rides out on a conveyor belt: the skin of the canal slowly moves outward from the eardrum, chewing and talking help it along, and at the opening it dries, flakes and falls out. That's why ear doctors say to skip the cotton swabs, which mostly push wax deeper. Sticky vs flaky wax comes down to one letter in one gene, ABCC11, and dry wax is most common in East Asia.

Sources: AAO-HNS Clinical Practice Guideline: Earwax (2017); AAO-HNS ENT Health; MedlinePlus; Yoshiura et al., Nature Genetics (2006).
