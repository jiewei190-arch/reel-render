# Sources: handwarmer (how do hand warmers get hot with no battery?)

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| The recipe: iron powder 50-60%, water 30-40%, activated charcoal 5-10%, vermiculite 5-10%, sodium chloride (salt) 1-5%, water-absorbent resin 1-5% (by weight), inside a non-woven fabric | Kobayashi Healthcare International, Safety Data Sheet "Air-activated heating packs of various styles" (rev. Dec 8 2021), Section 3, via School Specialty: https://cdn.schoolspecialty.com/cc9ed102-f795-4ca9-9463-b0f100b7fa9d/1530365_SDS_010224_Original%20file.pdf [manufacturer, primary] |
| Opening the pack lets air (oxygen) in through the porous cloth; the iron reacts with oxygen to make iron oxide, i.e. rust; the reaction is exothermic (releases heat) | Dr. Marie Pomije, Minnesota State University, Mankato, via KTTC (2025): https://www.kttc.com/2025/02/23/looking-into-science-how-hand-warmers-work/ ; University of Silesia, Faculty of Science and Technology, Prof. Robert Musioł, "Chemistry for frosty days" (2024): https://us.edu.pl/wydzial/wnst/en/2024/01/10/chemia-na-mrozne-dni-prof-robert-musiol/ [university] |
| Ordinary rusting gives off the same kind of heat, but too slowly to notice | HowStuffWorks, "How do disposable hand warmers work?": https://science.howstuffworks.com/disposable-hand-warmers.htm ; Duke University CHEM 110 (LibreTexts), "Thermochemistry of Hand Warmers": https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/Duke_University/CHEM_110_Honors_Writing_Projects/Duke_CHEM_110_Fall_2022:_Cox_and_Shorb/Thermochemistry_of_Hand_Warmers_in_Everyday_Life |
| Fine powder (high fragmentation) and salt make the rusting much faster | University of Silesia (Musioł): "Due to the addition of salt and the high degree of fragmentation, this reaction takes place much faster" |
| Charcoal spreads the heat; vermiculite controls/holds the water | Dr. Pomije via KTTC (activated carbon spreads the heat evenly; vermiculite controls the amount of water) |
| Warm hands for hours | HotHands packaging/retail listings ("up to 10 hours"), e.g. https://groceries.morrisons.com/products/hot-hands-hand-warmers/107851180 ; worded only as "hours" |
| Once started, it can't be switched off | University of Silesia (Musioł): "when the reaction is initiated it cannot be stopped or reversed" |
| The click kind: a supersaturated sodium acetate solution (more dissolved than it could normally hold); bending the metal piece starts crystals forming; crystallising releases the stored heat; boiling it re-liquefies the crystals | BBC Science Focus, "How do re-usable hand warmers work?": https://www.sciencefocus.com/science/how-do-re-usable-hand-warmers-work ; University of Silesia (Musioł) (click, crystallises, re-activated in hot water) |
