# fog: sources

1. NOAA NESDIS (SciJinks K-12 education), "What's the difference between fog and clouds?": https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/about/k-12-education/atmosphere/whats-the-difference-between-fog-and-clouds
   - "Fog is a kind of cloud that touches the ground."
   - "Fog forms when the air near the ground cools enough to turn its water vapor into liquid water or ice."
2. National Weather Service, Louisville, "Fog tutorial": https://www.weather.gov/lmk/fog_tutorial
   - Radiation fog: "This type of fog forms at night under clear skies with calm winds"; the ground loses the day's heat, the air at the surface cools, "the humidity will reach 100% and fog will form".
   - "Radiation fog varies in depth from 3 feet to about 1,000 feet"; it can cut visibility to near zero; valley fog (a radiation fog) usually burns off once the sun comes up and warms the air.
   - Advection fog "is also the result of condensation", caused by "the horizontal movement of warm moist air over a cold surface" (e.g. snow); its sideways movement sets it apart from radiation fog.
3. National Weather Service, Lake Charles, "Fog types": https://www.weather.gov/lch/fogres2
   - Radiation fog "does not move much and develops from ground up"; "It is never very deep which means it burns off faster."
4. National Weather Service, "Fog safety" / Dense Fog Advisory: https://www.weather.gov/safety/fog-ww
   - Dense fog: "visibilities frequently drop to one-quarter of a mile or less."
5. NASA Earth Observatory, "Another Foggy Day in San Francisco": https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/another-foggy-day-in-san-francisco-79119
   - In summer the California Current's surface water is pushed offshore and "deeper, colder water rises up to replace it, a process called upwelling."
   - "When sea breezes blow over this cold water, water vapor is forced to condense out of the air, forming advection fog."

Wording notes:
- "Dense fog can cut how far you see to a quarter of a mile or less": source 4 (the NWS dense fog advisory threshold).
- "3 feet deep, or about 1,000": source 2.
- "usually burns off": sources 2 and 3 ("usually", because widespread or deep fog can last).
