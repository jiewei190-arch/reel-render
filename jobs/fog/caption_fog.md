Walked through fog? Then you've walked through a cloud. ☁️ Fog is a cloud that touches the ground (NOAA). It forms when the air near the ground cools enough that its invisible water vapor turns into tiny droplets, and dense fog can cut visibility to a quarter of a mile or less. There are two main ways to cool the air that much. Radiation fog: on a clear, calm night the ground gives off the day's heat, the air touching it cools until the humidity hits 100%, and fog grows from the ground up, anywhere from 3 feet to about 1,000 feet deep. When the sun comes up and warms the air, it usually burns off. Advection fog: wind carries warm, moist air over a cold surface, like cold water or snow, so the air is chilled from below and the fog drifts in sideways. San Francisco's summer fog forms this way, as sea breezes blow over cold water that wells up from the deep Pacific.

Sources: NOAA NESDIS (SciJinks); National Weather Service (Louisville, Lake Charles, fog safety); NASA Earth Observatory.
