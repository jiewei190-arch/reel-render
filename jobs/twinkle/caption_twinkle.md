Why do stars twinkle, but planets don't? ✨ The stars aren't flickering; our air is. Starlight crosses space perfectly steady, then in its last split second it hits Earth's atmosphere, full of moving pockets of warm and cool air that each bend light a tiny bit. A star is so far away it's just a point, one thin beam, so when the air bends it the star jumps, dims and flashes colours. A planet is close enough to be a tiny disk: its light comes from many points that wobble differently and average out, so it shines steady. Near the horizon, light crosses much more air, so even planets can twinkle there.

Sources: NASA APOD, "Scintillating" (2011); StarDate, McDonald Observatory, The University of Texas at Austin.
