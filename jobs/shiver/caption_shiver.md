Why do we shiver when we're cold? Because shaking makes heat. Your hypothalamus works like a thermostat: when it senses you're getting too cold, it can switch on shivering, fast cycles of muscles tightening and relaxing. Muscles never turn fuel into motion perfectly, and shivering does lots of muscle work with no real movement, so the energy ends up warming you. In one study, people shoulder-deep in 18 °C water shivered their metabolism to about 3.5 times its resting rate, burning the sugar stored in their muscles. Your body also has a quieter heater, brown fat, and shivering can also show up when a fever is on the way. 🥶

Sources: MedlinePlus (NIH), "Chills"; OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology 2e (Rice University); Martineau & Jacobs, J Appl Physiol (1988); Haman & Blondin, Temperature (2017).
