# Sources: sodabubbles (Why do soda bubbles rise in lines?)
Researched Oct 28 2026. The close-up work is on champagne; the same team's model covers "champagne and carbonated beverages" (J. Phys. Chem. B 2006), so it is applied to soda here.

- Liger-Belair G., "The Physics of Champagne", Scientific American (June 2015): https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-physics-of-champagne/
- Liger-Belair G., Jeandet P., "Effervescence in a glass of champagne: A bubble story", Europhysics News 33(1), 2002: https://www.europhysicsnews.org/articles/epn/pdf/2002/01/epn02103.pdf
- Liger-Belair G., Marchal R., Jeandet P., "Close-up on Bubble Nucleation in a Glass of Champagne", Am. J. Enol. Vitic. 53:151 (2002), DOI 10.5344/ajev.2002.53.2.151
- Liger-Belair G., Topgaard D., Voisin C., Jeandet P., "Is the Wall of a Cellulose Fiber Saturated with Liquid Whether or Not Permeable with CO2 Dissolved Molecules? Application to Bubble Nucleation in Champagne Wines", Langmuir 20:4132 (2004, ACS), DOI 10.1021/la049960f
- Liger-Belair G., Parmentier M., Jeandet P., "Modeling the Kinetics of Bubble Nucleation in Champagne and Carbonated Beverages", J. Phys. Chem. B 110:21145 (2006, ACS), DOI 10.1021/jp0640427
- Liger-Belair G. et al., "Artificial bubble nucleation in engraved champagne glasses", Journal of Visualization (2006), DOI 10.1007/BF03182193
- Zenit R., Rodríguez-Rodríguez J., "The fluid mechanics of bubbly drinks", Physics Today, Nov 2018: https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/the-fluid-mechanics-of-bubbly-drinks

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| Soda holds CO2 dissolved under pressure; opening drops the pressure, the drink is supersaturated | Physics Today 2018; SciAm 2015 |
| A brand-new bubble is too small to survive; it needs a pre-existing gas pocket (critical radius ~0.2 µm) | SciAm 2015 ("critical radius ... around 200 nanometers"); EPN 2002 ("pre-existing gas cavities with radii of curvature greater than the critical radius") |
| "You'd guess scratches" / the glass's own bumps are far too small | EPN 2002: "Contrary to a generally accepted idea, nucleation sites are not located on irregularities of the glass itself ... far below the critical radius"; SciAm 2015 |
| Liger-Belair filmed the sites up close: mostly hollow cellulose fibers from the air or from wiping, stuck to the wall | SciAm 2015 ("Most are hollow cellulose fibers that fell out of the air or were left behind as the glass was wiped dry"); AJEV 2002 (high-speed close-ups); Langmuir 2004 |
| On pouring, each fiber traps a gas pocket | EPN 2002 ("able to entrap gas pockets during the filling") |
| Gas diffuses in, pocket grows, bubble breaks off, part of the pocket stays and repeats | EPN 2002; Physics Today 2018 ("leaving behind a smaller cavity to repeat the cycle ... the trail of bubbles") |
| Up to about thirty bubbles a second | EPN 2002 ("up to 30 bubbles per second"); SciAm 2015 |
| Bubbles grow as they rise, gaps widen | EPN 2002 ("bubbles grow during ascent ... the distance between two successive bubbles increases"); SciAm 2015; Physics Today 2018 |
| Some glasses are etched on purpose | J. Visualization 2006 (engraved glasses) |
| Hedge: other sites exist (crevices, tartrate crystals, ice, poured-in air) | Physics Today 2018; "most" and "mostly" in the script |
