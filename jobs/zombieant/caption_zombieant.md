A real Halloween story: in tropical rainforests, the fungus Ophiocordyceps takes over carpenter ants. An infected ant leaves its nest high in the trees, wanders in a "drunken walk", then around noon bites the vein under a leaf about 25 cm above the ground, in humid air the fungus likes. Its jaw muscles waste away, so it stays put. Surprise: in 2017 scientists found the fungus wrapped around the ant's muscles, not in its brain. Days later a stalk grows from the back of its head and releases spores to infect more ants. Fossil leaves show the same bite marks 48 million years ago. And no, it can't infect you.
