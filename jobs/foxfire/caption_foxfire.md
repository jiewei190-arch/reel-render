Some mushrooms glow in the dark, with no battery and no fire. People call it foxfire: Aristotle wrote about its cold light, and it even lit the compass of the Turtle submarine in the 1770s. Inside the fungus, a fuel called luciferin reacts with oxygen, helped by an enzyme, and the energy comes out as green light. But why spend energy on light? A 2015 study in Brazil set out fake glowing mushrooms, and they drew far more insects than dark ones. Those insects may carry the spores to new wood. 🍄✨

Sources: Oliveira et al., Current Biology (2015); Kaskova et al., Science Advances (2017); Dartmouth Geisel School of Medicine; Wikipedia ("Foxfire", "Turtle (submersible)").
