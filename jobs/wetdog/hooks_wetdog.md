# wetdog: opening lines (pick marked ★)

1. ★ "Why do wet dogs smell so bad?" (7 words, the exact search question). Frame 0: a sketchbook page in the park, ink only, except the one coloured thing. A wet ochre dog shakes by the pond, with droplets, motion arcs and green-dabbed ink curls of smell drifting to a sitter on the bench, who says "ew!". By the but-turn (2.7 s, "But the smell isn't really the dog. It's who lives on it.") the camera has pushed in on the dog's back and a pen circle asks "who lives on it?".
2. "Your dog doesn't stink. Its microbes do." (7 words). Strong, but it gives the payoff away and skips the search question.
3. "That wet dog smell isn't really the dog." (8 words). Close to ★1's but-turn, but on its own it isn't the question people type.

Why ★1: it's the question people actually type, and "it's who lives on it" is the twist by 3 s.
Re-hooks: 28.3-37.5 s water arrives as colour over the fur, molecules knocked loose, then "up to your nose" (the sitter goes "ew!" again); 38.2 s "So how many smells are in that cloud?"; 47.6-52.7 s mushrooms, fruity, floral... "and some smell like... poop."
Payoff 65.3 s: "So wet dog smell is microbe molecules, set free by water, drifting to your nose." The colour arrives over the whole park (lawn, trees, skyline). Question: "Does yours stink when wet? Yes or no?" (ticks in the margin). No "Good ___." sign-off.
