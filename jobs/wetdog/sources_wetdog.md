# wetdog: sources

1. American Chemical Society, Reactions: "Why Do Wet Dogs Stink? (And Other Canine Chemistry)", Season 2 Ep. 28, released June 1, 2015 (PBS Digital Studios). The wet-dog smell comes from microorganisms such as yeast and bacteria that live in a dog's fur and excrete smelly volatile compounds. When the dog gets wet, the water displaces and frees these molecules from the fur; as it evaporates it carries them to your nose. The video also explains why a dog still smells after a bath. https://www.pbs.org/video/why-do-wet-dogs-stink-other-canine-chemistry-c8n6zg ; coverage quoting it: https://salon.com/2015/06/01/heres_why_wet_dogs_smell_so_terrible_its_science ; EurekAlert release: https://www.eurekalert.org/multimedia/719436
2. Rodrigues Hoffmann A. et al., "The Skin Microbiome in Healthy and Allergic Dogs", PLoS ONE 9(1): e83197 (2014). Texas A&M University College of Veterinary Medicine & Biomedical Sciences (plus NC State CVM); 12 healthy dogs, 12 skin sites. "Higher species richness and microbial diversity were observed in the samples from haired skin when compared to mucosal surfaces or mucocutaneous junctions." The authors conclude that dog skin carries much richer, more diverse microbial communities than culture-based methods had shown. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0083197
3. Young L., Pollien P., Roberts D., Cline J., "Compounds Responsible for the Odor of Dog Hair Coat", WSAVA 2002 Congress (Nestlé Purina PTC / Nestlé Research Centre), hosted on VIN. A pilot study (GC-olfactometry on 1 dog, quantification on 6 dogs). "Sixteen compounds were found to be important odorants in dry dog hair coat vs. 22 in wet dog hair coat." Benzaldehyde, phenylacetaldehyde, acetaldehyde, phenol and 2-methylbutanal rose most when wet, "32, 6, 5, 5 and 4 times the dry hair value". Odor notes: 1-octen-3-ol mushroom-like, 2-nonanone fruity, β-damascenone floral, p-cresol fecal. https://www.vin.com/doc/?id=3846472
4. Jennifer Schissler, DVM (veterinary dermatologist, Colorado State University Veterinary Teaching Hospital), quoted in Dogster: the smell comes from normal microorganisms on healthy dog skin and hair, which make volatile byproducts that have little scent when dry. https://www.dogster.com/lifestyle/why-do-dogs-stink-when-wet

Wording notes:
- "In one small pilot study" = the Purina pilot study (source 3); "32 times higher" = benzaldehyde, named on screen.
- "On a dry dog, they mostly stay put, and you barely notice" follows sources 1 and 4 (little scent when dry; water releases the compounds).
- "a bath can make it worse at first: you just added water" follows source 1 (wetting releases the molecules; the bath itself isn't the cause).
- The bacteria/yeast doodles and the smell icons are illustrations, not specific species.
