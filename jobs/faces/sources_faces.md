# Sources: faces (Why you see faces in pumpkins, clouds and outlets)
Researched Oct 28 2026. Papers/pages opened where possible; secondary coverage noted.

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| Seeing faces in objects is called (face) pareidolia; illusory faces in clouds, outlets, everyday objects | Wardle, Taubert, Teichmann & Baker, "Rapid and dynamic processing of face pareidolia in the human brain", Nature Communications 11:4518 (2020). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7481186/ |

## Softened / careful wording
1. "about a tenth of a second" refers to the fastest eye movements toward faces in a lab task (Crouzet et al. 2010), not to every glance.
2. "handled by the brain's face areas, like real faces" simplifies "represented in face-selective cortex (FFA, OFA), initially more similar to real faces than to matched objects".
3. The womb study is attributed ("In one study"); it shows a preference for a face-like dot pattern, not face recognition.
4. "Missing a real face could cost you" is the researchers' evolutionary explanation (Alais/Sydney 2021, Wardle), presented as a reason, not a proven fact; the voiceover frames the tuning as "Scientists describe it as...".
5. The "ghost in the curtains" line is a Halloween framing of pareidolia, not a study claim.
