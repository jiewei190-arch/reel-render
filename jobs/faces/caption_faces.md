Two dots and a line, and your brain yells: face! That's pareidolia. Your brain runs an always-on face detector: your eyes can start moving toward a face in about a tenth of a second, and a 2020 brain-scan study found face-like objects handled by the brain's face areas, before being sorted back into plain objects within about a quarter of a second. Monkeys do it too, and in one study babies still in the womb turned toward a face-shaped pattern of three dots. Scientists describe the detector as tuned to never miss, even at the cost of false alarms. So that ghost in the curtains? Your face detector, doing its job.
