# Sources: mirrorflip (Why do mirrors flip left and right but not up and down?)
Researched Oct 28 2026. Institutional sources only (no Q&A forums). The Feynman Lectures discuss mirror symmetry (Vol. I ch. 52) but not this everyday puzzle directly, so they are not cited.

- Harrison, David M., "Mirror Symmetry", Department of Physics, University of Toronto (v1.15, 2007): https://faraday.physics.utoronto.ca/PVB/Harrison/Parity/Parity.html
- University of Sydney, School of Physics, "Mirrors and Reflections: Coordinate Systems" (physics activity): https://www.physics.usyd.edu.au/super/physics_tut/activities/Mechanics/Mirrors_and_Reflections.pdf

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| Raise your right hand: the hand in the mirror is on the right side too | Harrison: "hold up your right hand. The hand that is raised in the mirror image of you is also on your right side" |
| Up stays up | Sydney: up and down are not reversed |
| A mirror reverses only front and back (the direction into the glass) | Harrison: "it is front and back that are being reversed by the mirror" |
| Each point appears as far behind the mirror as it is in front | Standard plane-mirror optics (image distance = object distance); consistent with Harrison's front-back reversal |
| We call it a left-right flip because we picture the image as a real person who walked around to face us | Harrison: "if the person you see in the mirror were real, you would say that the person is raising their left hand" |
| Left and right are defined relative to ourselves; up and down are set by the ground | Sydney: "we define left and right as relative to ourselves ... [up and down] are defined externally, usually relative to the ground, hence are not reversed" |
| Nobody imagines turning around head over heels | The reasoning above (we picture a turn about the vertical axis, so only left/right appear swapped) |
