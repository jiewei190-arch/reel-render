Why do mirrors flip left and right but not up and down? They don't flip left and right at all. Raise your right hand: the hand in the mirror is on the right side too. Point up, and the image points up; point at the glass, and it points straight back at you. The only thing a mirror reverses is the direction into the glass: front and back. The "left-right" part comes from us: we picture the image as a real person who walked around to face us, and that person's right hand would be on our left. Left and right are defined relative to ourselves, while up and down are set by the ground, and nobody imagines turning around head over heels. As University of Toronto physicist David Harrison puts it, it's front and back that are being reversed.

Sources: D. M. Harrison, "Mirror Symmetry", University of Toronto Physics; University of Sydney School of Physics, "Mirrors and Reflections".
