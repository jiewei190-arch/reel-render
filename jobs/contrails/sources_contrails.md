# contrails: sources

1. EPA, FAA and NOAA, "Contrails Fact Sheet" (joint fact sheet, faa.gov/contrails and epa.gov/contrails; 2025 revision). Contrails are line-shaped clouds of mostly ice crystals. They form when hot, humid exhaust mixes with cold upper air, and the vapor condenses on fine particles and freezes. Air at those levels can be "as cold as -70 °F". The fact sheet compares this to seeing "your breath on a cold day". Contrails evaporate within minutes in low humidity and persist for hours in high humidity. Persistent contrails can spread across the sky with the winds (wind shear) and become contrail cirrus. Engine exhaust is mostly carbon dioxide and water vapor. "It has been estimated that in certain flight corridors, cloud cover has increased by as much as 20 percent." https://www.faa.gov/contrails/fact-sheet
2. NASA, "Contrails" Museum in a Box (K-12 educator guide). Short-lived contrails form in dry air and last a few minutes at most. Persistent contrails need a humid atmosphere. Spreading happens when the air is unstable or turbulent. https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/contrails-k-12.pdf
3. NOAA, National Weather Service Flagstaff, "Clouds: Contrails" (the fact sheet cites it for the three contrail types). https://www.weather.gov/fgz/CloudsContrails
4. Shearer, West, Caldeira and Davis, "Quantifying expert consensus against the existence of a secret, large-scale atmospheric spraying program", Environmental Research Letters (2016). 76 of 77 atmospheric chemists and geochemists surveyed had found no evidence of a secret spraying program. Press releases: UC Irvine and Carnegie Institution for Science, Aug 2016. https://news.uci.edu/2016/08/12/surveyed-scientists-debunk-chemtrails-conspiracy-theory
   (The one "yes" respondent cited unusually high barium in soil in one area; the reel says "76 of 77 ... saw no evidence", which matches.)

Wording notes:
- "Up to 20 percent" is the fact sheet's estimate for certain corridors; it is spoken as "is estimated to be up by as much as twenty percent".
- "as cold as minus 70 Fahrenheit" is quoted as an upper-air extreme ("can be as cold as").
- Engine exhaust: the reel says "carbon dioxide and lots of water vapor" and does not quote the fact sheet's 71/28 percent split.
