Why do planes leave white trails in the sky? ✈️ It isn't smoke: the plane is making a cloud. Burning jet fuel makes carbon dioxide and lots of water vapor. At cruising height the air can be as cold as -70 °F, so the vapor condenses on tiny specks and freezes into ice crystals, like your breath on a cold day. Whether a trail lasts depends on the sky, not the plane: in dry air the ice evaporates within seconds to minutes, while in humid air the crystals keep growing and the trail can last for hours or spread into thin cirrus cloud. And no, they're not "chemtrails": in a 2016 survey, 76 of 77 atmospheric scientists had seen no evidence of secret spraying.

Sources: EPA/FAA/NOAA Contrails Fact Sheet; NASA contrails educator guide; Shearer et al., Environmental Research Letters (2016), UC Irvine & Carnegie Institution.
