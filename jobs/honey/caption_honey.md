How do bees turn nectar into honey? 🍯 Two changes. First, enzymes: a forager sips nectar into her honey crop and passes it mouth to mouth to other bees, and they add invertase, which splits the nectar's sucrose into glucose and fructose. Second, drying: nectar is far too watery to keep, so bees dry it. It starts on the flight home (nectar in a forager's crop is already about twice as sugary as in the flower), then bees push tiny droplets out on their tongues and suck them back in, again and again, and lots of bees fan their wings to move air through the hive. When the honey is down to about 18% water (US honey grades allow no more than 18.6%), the bees seal each cell with a wax cap.

Sources: Nicolson, Human & Pirk, Scientific Reports (2022); NC State University; Texas A&M AgriLife Extension Entomology; USDA AMS honey grade standards.
