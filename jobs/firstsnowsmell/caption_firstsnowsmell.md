Can you really smell snow coming? Sort of. Snow is frozen water, and pure water has no smell. Cold air slows odor molecules, so there are simply fewer smells around (Monell scientist Pamela Dalton). Humid air before a storm may help your nose, though lab studies disagree. The strongest clue is a feeling, not a smell: nerve endings in your nose carry a cold sensor (TRPM8, the one menthol switches on), and Monell researchers found the fresh feeling of breathing comes mostly from your nose lining cooling down. ❄️

Sources: Monell Chemical Senses Center (Dalton, via Discovery News; Zhao et al., PLoS One 2011); Kuehn et al. (2008) and Drews et al. (2021), Eur Arch Otorhinolaryngol; Ordás et al., J Comp Neurol (2021).
