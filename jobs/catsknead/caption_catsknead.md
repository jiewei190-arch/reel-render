Why do cats knead? It starts in the first days of life: a nursing kitten presses its mother's belly, which helps release oxytocin and get the milk flowing. Push, milk, warmth and safety all arrive together, and grown-up cats keep the habit (scientists call that neoteny). The leading theory: kneading still feels like that comfort, which is why it so often comes with purring. Theory two: cats have scent glands in their paw pads, so each press may leave an invisible "this one's mine". Theory three: wild cats trampled grass into a soft bed. If the kneading turns frantic or nonstop, ask your vet. 🐈🍪

Sources: Hazel & Henning (School of Animal and Veterinary Science, University of Adelaide), "Why do cats knead?", The Conversation; VCA Animal Hospitals; Vetstreet.
