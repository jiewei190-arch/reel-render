Why is food cold in the middle after microwaving? Microwaves don't cook from the inside out: they only reach about an inch (2.5 cm) into food, where they make water molecules jiggle and heat up. Everything deeper waits for that heat to creep in by conduction, slowly. Frozen food is worse, because ice barely absorbs microwaves: thawed edges soak up the energy and overheat while the icy middle lags. The USDA warns that cold spots can let harmful bacteria survive. Three fixes: stir or rotate halfway, use medium power for big portions, and let it stand, because standing time completes the cooking. 🍲

Sources: USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, "Cooking Safely in the Microwave Oven"; University of Illinois Extension (2014); University of Kiel, materials science course notes, "Cooking with Microwaves".
