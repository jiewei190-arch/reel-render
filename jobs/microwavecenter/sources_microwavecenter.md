# Sources: microwavecenter ("Why is food cold in the middle after microwaving?")

| Claim in the script | Source | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Microwaves don't cook from the inside out; they only reach about an inch into food | University of Illinois Extension, Jenna Smith, "What cooks better: the microwave...?" (17 Apr 2014, https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/simply-nutritious-quick-and-delicious/2014-04-17-what-cooks-better-microwave): "microwaves can only penetrate about 1 inch in depth" | university (extension) |
| They make water molecules jiggle, and the motion becomes heat | University of Kiel, Faculty of Engineering, materials science course, "Cooking with Microwaves" (https://www.tf.uni-kiel.de/matwis/amat/elmat_en/kap_3/advanced/t3_4_1.html): heating happens as the water's dipoles absorb the 2.45 GHz field | university |
| Everything deeper waits for heat to creep inward, slowly, by conduction | Illinois Extension (same page): "The heat created is then slowly conducted to the center." | university |
| Ice barely absorbs microwaves | Kiel (same page): "The DK of ice at the microwave frequency is only about 3, and little energy is absorbed"; frozen food takes long to start thawing, then turns mushy fast | university |
| Thawed edges get hotter while the icy middle hardly warms | Kiel (same page); USDA FSIS "Cooking Safely in the Microwave Oven" (below): "some areas of the frozen food may begin to cook during the defrosting time" | university; government |
| Microwaves cook unevenly; cold spots can let harmful bacteria survive | USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service, "Cooking Safely in the Microwave Oven" (rev. June 2011; govinfo copy https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-A110-PURL-gpo127402/pdf/GOVPUB-A110-PURL-gpo127402.pdf): "Microwave ovens can cook unevenly and leave 'cold spots,' where harmful bacteria can survive." | government |
| Stir or rotate halfway | USDA FSIS (same): "Stir or rotate food midway through the microwaving time to eliminate cold spots where harmful bacteria can survive, and for more even cooking." | government |
| Medium power for big portions, so the heat can reach the center | USDA FSIS (same): cook large cuts on medium power (50%) for a longer time; "This allows heat to reach the center without overcooking outer areas." | government |
| Let it stand; standing time completes the cooking | USDA FSIS (same): "Always allow standing time, which completes the cooking" | government |
| Bonus (pinned): leftovers to 165 °F after standing time | USDA FSIS (same): "After reheating foods in the microwave oven, allow standing time. Then, use a clean food thermometer to check that food has reached 165 °F." | government |

## Softened / left out
- "About an inch" is the extension figure; real depths vary with the food (water-rich foods less, dry foods more). The bowl's band is schematic.
- "Medium power for big portions": FSIS says it about large cuts of meat; the script keeps "big portions" general and adds no percentages or times.
- No standing-wave / mesh / turntable story (microwavelight, EP 53). The turntable appears only as art in the oven window.
- The fsis.usda.gov page itself returned 403 to the fetcher; quotes come from the FSIS PDF (govinfo copy) and are worded as in it.
