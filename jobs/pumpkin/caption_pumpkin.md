Carve a pumpkin and in a few days its face starts to sag. An uncut pumpkin has a hard, waxy rind that keeps water in and germs out, and a pumpkin is about 90% water. Carving cuts right through that seal: water escapes, the flesh dries and softens, and airborne mould spores like Penicillium and Aspergillus move in with bacteria. A real candle's heat makes it worse. To slow it down, scoop out all the insides, keep it cool and out of the sun, put petroleum jelly on the cuts and use an LED light. 🎃

Sources: Utah State University Extension (2020); K-State Research and Extension (2024); Mississippi State University Extension; University of Illinois Extension.
