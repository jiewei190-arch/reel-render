Why do leaves change color in the fall? All summer, leaves are full of green chlorophyll, which turns sunlight into food. As nights get longer, trees stop making it and the green breaks down, revealing yellow and orange carotenoids that were there all along. Reds are different: most anthocyanins are made fresh in the fall, when a cork-like layer at the leaf stem traps sugar and bright sun helps turn it red. That's why warm sunny days and cool nights give the brightest reds. Brown tannins come last, then the leaf lets go. Red or gold?
