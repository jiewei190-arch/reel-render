# Sources: freezerburn (What is freezer burn?)
Researched Oct 28 2026.
- Tong (Toni) Wang, professor and food scientist, University of Tennessee, "Freezer burn is a serious problem..." (The Conversation): https://theconversation.com/freezer-burn-is-a-serious-problem-preventing-ice-recrystallization-may-alleviate-it-164989
- Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, "What is freezer burn?" (E. Rodriguez): https://agrilifeextension.tamu.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Freezer-Burn.pdf
- NDSU Extension, Julie Garden-Robinson, "Prairie Fare: your freezer questions answered" (cites USDA FSIS on safety): https://www.ag.ndsu.edu/news/columns/prairie-fare/prairie-fare-your-freezer-questions-answered
- (USDA FSIS "Freezing and Food Safety" returned 403 to the fetcher; its safety line is cited via NDSU.)

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| Frozen food holds tiny ice crystals; surface ice turns straight into vapour without melting (sublimation) | Tennessee (Wang): "tiny ice crystals on the food's surface evaporate directly into vapor"; sublimation, skipping the liquid stage |
| Freezer air is very dry (moisture leaves the food) | Tennessee (moisture lost from the food's surface); NDSU: "Freezer burn is a form of dehydration" |
| The surface dries into tough, leathery patches, often grayish or brown | Texas A&M: meat may develop "dark brown or grayish-white leathery areas"; NDSU: grayish meat is most likely freezer-burned |
| As water leaves, air creeps in, changing colour and flavour | Texas A&M: water molecules escape, "which allows oxygen molecules to seep in"; oxygen changes colour and flavour |
| Safe to eat; a quality problem, not a safety one | Texas A&M: "Food that has freezer burn is safe to eat"; NDSU citing FSIS: the quality loss "doesn't necessarily mean the food is unsafe to eat" |
| It cooks up dry and tough | Texas A&M: "When cooked, the texture may be dry and tough." |
| Ice cream loses its creaminess and grows ice crystals | Texas A&M: "When ice cream gets freezer burn, it loses its creaminess and gains ice crystals instead." |
| Keep the air out: wrap tightly, squeeze the air out of bags, use small containers | Texas A&M: wrap meat in freezer paper or plastic wrap, then foil, then a freezer bag; "Remove as much air as possible"; "use small containers"; NDSU: moisture/vapour-proof packaging |
| Keep the freezer at 0 °F or colder | Texas A&M: "keeping your freezer at 0°F or lower" |
| For ice cream, press plastic wrap onto the top before the lid | Texas A&M: "cover the top of ice cream with freezer paper or plastic wrap before replacing the lid" |

## Softened / careful wording
1. "Often grayish or brown" (colours vary by food).
2. Trimming and frost-on-the-package are not claimed (the pages fetched did not state them).
