Why does your stomach growl? It can growl even when you're not hungry. It's your gut squeezing: muscle waves called peristalsis push food, fluid and gas through your stomach and small intestine, and the gas and fluid gurgle as they're squeezed through. The ancient Greeks called it borborygmi, "rumbling". It's often loudest when you're empty, because there's no food to muffle it. Once your stomach is empty, a cleaning wave sweeps through, roughly every couple of hours while you fast, driven by a hormone called motilin, which some researchers link to hunger. Its cousin ghrelin rises right before meals. Does yours growl at the worst moment?
