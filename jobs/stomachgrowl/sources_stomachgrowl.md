# Sources: stomachgrowl (Why does your stomach growl?)
Researched Oct 28 2026. Reviews and institutional pages first.

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| A growl doesn't need hunger; it's the gut squeezing contents along | Scientific American, "Why does your stomach growl when you are hungry?" https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-does-your-stomach-gro/ ; Wikipedia "Stomach rumble" (peristalsis of the small intestine; most noise comes from the intestines) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_rumble |
| Peristalsis pushes food, fluid and gas; gas and fluid squeezed through make the gurgle | Same two sources |
| The Greek word borborygmi = rumbling (onomatopoeic) | Wikipedia "Stomach rumble" (Ancient Greek βορβορυγμός, probably onomatopoeic) |
| Often loudest when empty: no food to muffle it | Scientific American (food dampens the sound; an empty gut is louder) |
| Between meals a "cleaning wave" (migrating motor complex, phase III) sweeps from stomach to gut | Takahashi 2012, "Mechanism of interdigestive migrating motor complex", J Neurogastroenterol Motil https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3400812/ |
| Repeats about every 90-120 minutes while fasting | Takahashi 2012 (above); Takahashi 2014 review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5137267 |
| Motilin drives those waves | Ohno et al. 2010 review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2925306 (plasma motilin rises cyclically every 90-120 min, tracks gastric phase III) |
| Some researchers link the waves to hunger | Tack, Deloose et al. 2016, Gut 65:214-224, "Motilin-induced gastric contractions signal hunger in man" https://gut.bmj.com/content/65/2/214 ; Frontiers in Endocrinology 2021 motilin review https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/endocrinology/articles/10.3389/fendo.2021.700884 |
| Ghrelin (motilin's cousin, same peptide family) rises right before meals and helps make you want to eat | Cummings et al. 2001, Diabetes 50:1714-1719, "A preprandial rise in plasma ghrelin levels suggests a role in meal initiation in humans"; family relation: Ohno 2010 |

## Softened / careful wording
1. Hunger link is "Partly, maybe" and "some researchers link them to hunger": the motilin-hunger work is a small number of studies from one group (Leuven).
2. Ghrelin "helps make you want to eat" (not "causes hunger"); its pre-meal rise may partly reflect meal anticipation (noted by later work).
3. "About two hours after your stomach empties": approximate. The fasting MMC pattern returns once the meal has cleared (the fed pattern ends), typically within a few hours of eating; no source gives an exact delay, so the caption says "about".
4. "Most of the sound comes from the intestines": the script says "your gut" rather than "your stomach" for the squeezing.
5. No medical advice; no claim that a silent gut is dangerous.
