# Sources: hurricanewc (How a hurricane forms, watercolor)
Same voiceover script as `hurricane` (identical spec phrases). Fact-checked Oct 27 2026.

| Claim (script) | Source |
|---|---|
| Fuel is warm ocean water; sea surface at least about 27 °C / 80 °F | NOAA "Hurricanes" https://www.noaa.gov/education/resource-collections/weather-atmosphere/hurricanes ; NOAA JetStream https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/tropical/tropical-cyclone-introduction (27 °C to about 150 ft depth) |
| Warm moist air rises, cools, condenses into clouds and rain; condensation releases the storm's energy | NOAA JetStream "Tropical Cyclone Introduction" (heat released by condensation) |

| Almost never form within about 5° of the equator, where the Coriolis effect is too weak | NOAA JetStream "Tropical Cyclone Introduction"; Hong Kong Observatory https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/education/tropical-cyclone/intensity/00150-why-do-tropical-cyclones-always-form-more-than-5-degrees-of-latitude-away-from-the-equator.html |
| Need moist air and low wind shear | NOAA "Hurricanes" |
| 74 mph makes it a hurricane | NHC Saffir-Simpson scale https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/aboutsshws.php (Category 1 = 74-95 mph sustained) |
| Eye: sinking air, calm and clear; eyewall has the strongest winds | NOAA JetStream "Tropical Cyclone Structure" https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/tropical/tropical-cyclone-introduction/tropical-cyclone-structure ("usually the strongest winds") |
| Over land it loses its warm ocean fuel and weakens | NOAA JetStream "Tropical Cyclone Introduction" |

## Softened / flagged claims
1. SPOKEN, NOT FIXED: "Once it moves over land, it loses its warm ocean fuel, and friction slows it." NOAA JetStream says storms weaken over land "not because of friction" but from losing heat and moisture (friction only lowers surface winds). Correct wording would be: "Once it moves over land, it loses its warm, moist ocean fuel, so it weakens." Needs a re-voice of that one phrase.
2. SPOKEN, minor: "When the winds reach seventy-four miles per hour" - the threshold is for sustained winds; caption says "sustained".
3. "Hurricane" is the Atlantic / NE Pacific name for the same storm (typhoon, cyclone elsewhere); not stated in the reel, fine to leave.
4. Wikipedia "Tropical cyclone" (cited in the old hurricane caption) replaced by NOAA/NHC pages.
