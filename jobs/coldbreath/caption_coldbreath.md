Why can you see your breath when it's cold? Your breath is always full of water, as invisible water vapor. Warm air can hold lots of it, but air at freezing holds only about a tenth as much as air as warm as your body. So when your warm, wet breath hits the cold, it cools past its dew point, and the vapor sticks together into tiny droplets of liquid water: a little cloud. Real clouds form the same way, just way bigger. A second later it mixes with more dry air, and the droplets evaporate again. ☁️❄️

Sources: University of Washington, Dept. of Atmospheric Sciences; NOAA National Weather Service (dew point, JetStream "How clouds form"); Library of Congress, Everyday Mysteries.
