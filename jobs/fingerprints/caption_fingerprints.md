Why do we have fingerprints? 🔍 Not for the reason you'd guess. In a 2009 experiment, scientists pulled a strip of plastic across a fingertip and measured the friction: the ridges cut the contact area by about a third, so on smooth surfaces fingerprints actually lower grip. So what are they for? The leading ideas: better grip on rough or wet surfaces, and touch. When a ridged sensor was slid over rough surfaces, the ridges turned fine textures into vibrations right in the range our deep touch receptors (Pacinian corpuscles) feel best. Your prints form before birth: the ridges start in the third month of pregnancy and are set by the sixth. Genes shape the general pattern, the womb shapes the details, which is why even identical twins have different fingerprints.

Sources: Warman & Ennos, Journal of Experimental Biology (2009); Scheibert et al., Science (2009); MedlinePlus Genetics (US National Library of Medicine).
