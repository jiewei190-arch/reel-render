Why does spicy food feel hot? 🌶️ A chili isn't hot at all. It's tricking your nerves. Capsaicin, the chemical in chilies, switches on TRPV1, a sensor in your nerve endings that normally fires above about 43°C, hot enough to hurt. So your nerves send the same alarm they'd send for a burn: a false fire alarm. David Julius found this sensor using capsaicin and shared the 2021 Nobel Prize. The fix: capsaicin is oily and doesn't mix with water, but milk's protein casein grabs it like soap on grease. Even skim milk works.

Sources: Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2021 (via NIPS, Japan); D. Eldridge, Swinburne University (The Conversation); Cleveland Clinic.
