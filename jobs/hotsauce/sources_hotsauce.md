# hotsauce: sources

1. The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine 2021 (David Julius and Ardem Patapoutian, "for their discoveries of receptors for temperature and touch"), press release: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/2021/press-release/
   - Julius used capsaicin to identify a heat-sensing receptor in nerve endings: TRPV1.
   - nobelprize.org returns 403 to this session's fetcher; the facts were checked via source 2.
2. National Institute for Physiological Sciences (Japan), press note on the 2021 Nobel Prize: https://www.nips.ac.jp/eng/release/2021/10/david_juliusardem_patapoutian.html
   - The prize was awarded jointly to David Julius and Ardem Patapoutian for discovering receptors for temperature and touch.
   - Julius's group isolated the gene for the capsaicin receptor TRPV1 in 1997.
   - TRPV1 is activated by a "noxious heat stimulus with a temperature over 43oC".
3. Daniel Eldridge (Senior Lecturer in Chemistry, Swinburne University of Technology), "Why doesn't water help with spicy food? What about milk or beer?", The Conversation: https://theconversation.com/why-doesnt-water-help-with-spicy-food-what-about-milk-or-beer-226624
   - "capsaicin is a hydrophobic molecule"; "hydrophilic and hydrophobic substances don't mix", so water is a poor rinse.
   - Casein is an emulsifier that binds capsaicin.
4. Cleveland Clinic, "Does Milk Help With Spicy Food?": https://health.clevelandclinic.org/does-milk-help-with-spicy-food/
   - Capsaicin doesn't dissolve in water.
   - Casein breaks it up "much like dish soap breaks down grease".
   - Skim and whole milk performed about the same (a 2019 study).
   - Plant milks lack casein.

Wording notes:
- "with no heat at all" / "false alarm": capsaicin activates the same receptor heat does (sources 1-2); the chili itself is room temperature.
- "Capsaicin is oily": hydrophobic (source 3) and dissolves in fats.
- "Even skim milk works": source 4.
- "shared the 2021 Nobel Prize": shared with Ardem Patapoutian (touch), named on the plaque in the reel.
