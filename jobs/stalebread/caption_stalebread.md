Why does bread go stale? Not mainly because it dries out: even a loaf sealed airtight goes stale. In the oven the starch soaks up water and loosens into a soft gel; as the loaf cools and sits, that starch slowly locks back into stiff crystals (retrogradation) and pulls water in, away from the soft parts. The fridge makes it worse: in one test, bread staled as much in one day in the fridge as in six days on the counter. The freezer nearly pauses it. And warming it up melts the crystals back into gel, so stale bread goes soft again, for a while. 🍞

Sources: Indiana University, A Moment of Science; Gray & BeMiller, Comprehensive Reviews in Food Science and Food Safety (2003); Fadda et al., CRFSFS (2014); America's Test Kitchen.
