# Sources: skeleton ("What your skeleton really does all day")

| # | Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bone is living tissue | Wikipedia, "Bone": "Bones consist of living cells (osteoblasts and osteocytes) embedded in a mineralized organic matrix." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone |
| 2 | You were born with around 300 bones | Healthline, "How Many Bones Does a Baby Have?" (medically reviewed by Karen Gill, M.D., 2019): "Babies are born with around 300 bones, but some of these will fuse together with age." https://www.healthline.com/health/how-many-bones-does-a-baby-have . Wikipedia, "Bone": "approximately 300 bones are present" at birth. |
| 3 | Many fuse as you grow; adults end up with 206 | Healthline (above): "Adults, on the other hand, have 206 bones"; Wikipedia, "Bone": "a total of 206 separate bones in the adult". (Cleveland Clinic gives "between 206 and 213", since some people have extra small bones; 206 is the standard count.) |
| 4 | Job one: support, carrying the body's weight | Cleveland Clinic, "Skeletal System": "Your skeleton supports the weight of your body." https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/21048-skeletal-system |
| 5 | Job two: the skull guards the brain; the ribs shield the heart and lungs | Cleveland Clinic: "Your skull protects your brain, your ribs shield your heart and lungs" |
| 6 | Job three: movement; muscles pull on bones, joints let them bend | Cleveland Clinic: "Your joints, connective tissue and muscles all work together to push and pull parts of your body every time you move." |
| 7 | Soft, spongy marrow inside some bones | MedlinePlus, "Bone Marrow Diseases": bone marrow is "the spongy tissue inside some of your bones, such as your hip and thigh bones." https://medlineplus.gov/bonemarrowdiseases.html |
| 8 | Marrow makes red blood cells, white cells that fight germs (infection), and platelets that help blood clot | MedlinePlus (above): marrow stem cells develop into red blood cells, white blood cells that fight infection, and platelets that help with clotting. Cleveland Clinic: "Bone marrow at the center of some of your bones produces new red blood cells". Wikipedia, "Bone": "Bone marrow produces blood cells in a process called hematopoiesis." |
| 9 | Bones store calcium and other minerals | Cleveland Clinic: "Your bones hold your body's supply of important minerals like calcium"; Wikipedia, "Bone": bones are "reserves of minerals important for the body, most notably calcium and phosphorus." |
| 10 | Its cells keep tearing down old bone and building new | Wikipedia, "Bone remodeling": "a lifelong process where mature bone tissue is removed from the skeleton" and new bone is formed (osteoclasts remove, osteoblasts build). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_remodeling |
| 11 | In adults, about 10% of the skeleton is rebuilt every year | Wikipedia, "Bone remodeling": "In adults, remodeling proceeds at about 10% per year." |
| 12 | The rebuilding helps keep the right amount of calcium in the blood | Wikipedia, "Bone remodeling": through remodeling "the body is able to maintain proper levels of calcium required for physiological processes." |

Visual-only elements: the purple "chomper" (osteoclast) and the green "builder" with a hard hat (osteoblast) are cartoon stand-ins; the "BONE BANK" and "MARROW factory" are metaphors.
