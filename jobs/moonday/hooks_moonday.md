# moonday: 3 opening lines (template #22 paper stop-motion ledger)

Search question spoken in line 2: "But isn't the Moon a night thing? Why can you see it during the day?" (caption 3.8-6.9 s, the "but" at 3.8 s).

1. **PICK** "There's the Moon, hanging in a bright blue afternoon sky." (10 words) Pattern 1, the thing itself. Frame 0: a big pencil half Moon (lit on the right, first quarter) and the Sun in a blue-hatched afternoon sky over a pencil horizon, a typed "3:30 pm" slip, "the Moon?" in blue pencil, a stick figure pointing up.
2. "The Moon spends almost as much time in daylight as at night." (12 words) Pattern 2, surprise. Frame 0: the day/night bars.
3. "Want to see the Moon at lunch? Wait for first quarter." (11 words) Pattern 3, the tip first. Frame 0: the first quarter slip.

Why 1: everyone has seen this exact sky and wondered; line 2 turns it into the search question. Lines 2 and 3 spend the payoffs (the 39.5 s fact and the 51.5 s tip) at frame 0.
Open question: why is the Moon out in the day? The chain: the Moon reflects sunlight and is bright enough to shine through the blue sky, while the glare hides the stars (the demo's thermometer becomes a brightness gauge: Moon > day sky > stars). Then the twist at 25.1 s: its timing is tied to its orbit, not to night. A typed moonrise log shows it rising about 50 minutes later each day, and a 24-hour clock shows the rise time sliding around the clock, so it spends almost as much time in the day sky as at night. Re-hook at 44.7 s: "some days are much better" (four phase slips). Near new Moon it is lost in the glare; the first quarter rises around noon and glows all afternoon (the wow); the third quarter hangs in the morning sky. Payoff at 62.3 s: "it keeps its own schedule, not the Sun's". Then "look up", "Spotted?" and the end card.
