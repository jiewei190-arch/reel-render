Why can you see the Moon during the day? The Moon makes no light of its own; it reflects sunlight, and it's bright enough to shine through the blue daytime sky (the stars are up there too, but the glare hides them). Its timing isn't tied to night at all: each day it rises about 50 minutes later, so over a month its rise time slides all the way around the clock, and it spends almost as much time in the daytime sky as at night. Best bets: the first quarter Moon in the afternoon, about a week after new Moon, and the third quarter Moon in the morning, about a week after full. 🌓☀️
