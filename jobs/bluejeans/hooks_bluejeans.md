# bluejeans: opening lines (pick marked ★)

1. ★ "Why do jeans have that tiny pocket, and those little rivets?" (11 words, the search question). Frame 0: a graphite drawing of a pair of waist overalls on pale ledger paper, the tiny pocket already ringed in yellow marker and "that tiny pocket?" written in blue ballpoint. Red arrows then point at each copper rivet ("rivets?"). At the but-turn (4.4 s, "But they weren't made for style. They were made for work.") a typed PURPOSE slip strikes "style" in red and ticks "work".
2. "That tiny pocket on your jeans has a job." (9 words). Intriguing, but it skips the rivets half of the question.
3. "Your jeans are patented." (4 words). Strong, but it gives away the patent re-hook at 26 s.

Why ★1: it's the question people type, and "made for work" is the turn by 5 s.
Re-hooks: 25.9 s the U.S. PATENT OFFICE slip types out No. 139,121; 35.3 s "And that tiny pocket?" (zoom on the ringed pocket, then the pocket watch slides in); 50.3 s WWII (the pocket's rivets are erased to save metal).
Payoff 60.7 s: "So the rivets are armor for weak seams, and the tiny pocket is a watch pocket." Question: "Have you ever used it? Yes or no?"
