Why do jeans have that tiny pocket and those little rivets? 👖 They were made for work, not style. A woman asked a tailor named Jacob Davis for pants for her husband that wouldn't fall apart, and the weak spots were the pocket corners, so Davis pinned the points of strain with metal rivets. He teamed up with his cloth supplier, Levi Strauss, and on May 20, 1873 they got patent No. 139,121 for "an improvement in fastening pocket-openings". The tiny pocket was on the very first pair: it was made for a pocket watch. They were called "waist overalls" until 1960, the watch pocket lost its rivets in WWII to save metal, and once the patent ran out in 1890, rivets showed up on everyone's work pants.

Sources: Levi Strauss & Co. history + archives (Tracey Panek); U.S. National Archives; US Patent 139,121.
