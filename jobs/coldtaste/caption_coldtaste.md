Why can't you taste food when you have a cold? Your tongue works fine: it only senses a few basic tastes, like sweet, salty, sour, bitter and umami. Everything else, strawberry, coffee, garlic, is smell, drifting up a channel at the back of your mouth into your nose (retronasal smell). A stuffy nose blocks that channel, so food tastes bland. Try the jellybean test: pinch your nose and chew, it's just sweet. Let go, and suddenly it's cherry. Have you tried the nose-pinch test?

Sources: NIH / NIDCD taste and smell disorder fact sheets; Monell Chemical Senses Center.
