# Sources: Did Cavemen Have Pets?
Line numbers = narration line order in script.json (= shot order in shots.json). 21 lines, 168 words.

- L1-2, 5-11 (Bonn-Oberkassel puppy: ~14,000 years, buried with a man and a woman (and a second, older dog), died at ~27-28 weeks, probable canine distemper diagnosed from tooth-enamel damage, 2-3 bouts of illness, survived ~8 weeks, "without adequate care, a dog with a serious case of distemper will die in less than three weeks", could not have been a working animal while sick): Janssens, Giemsch, Schmitz, Street, Van Dongen & Crombe 2018, "A new look at an old dog: Bonn-Oberkassel reconsidered", Journal of Archaeological Science 92: 126-138, doi:10.1016/j.jas.2018.01.004. Leiden University press release (Feb 2018): https://www.universiteitleiden.nl/en/news/2018/02/emotional-bond-between-humans-and-dogs-goes-back-14000-years ; UCL: https://www.ucl.ac.uk/human-evolution/news/2018/feb/archaeologists-re-examine-famous-14000-year-old-dog-bonn-oberkassel-germany ; Smithsonian: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/14000-year-old-puppy-may-have-been-cared-paleolithic-humans-180968282/ ; National Geographic: https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/ancient-pet-puppy-oberkassel-stone-age-dog . Distemper is a probable (not DNA-confirmed) diagnosis; the care is the authors' inference, hence "Experts think" (L9) and "probably loved" (L11). L1 "nursed ... for weeks" = the paper's central inference (~8 weeks of survival).
- L12-13 (dogs = oldest domestic animal; "at least fifteen thousand years"): two Nature papers, March 2026, identified a ~15,800-year-old genetically confirmed dog at Pinarbasi, Turkiye (oldest genetically confirmed), plus a ~14,200-year-old dog at Kesslerloch, Switzerland: https://www.heritagedaily.com/2026/06/earliest-known-domesticated-dogs-identified-at-prehistoric-sites-in-turkiye/158285 ; https://www.cp24.com/news/2026/03/25/genetic-study-identifies-earliest-known-dog-dating-to-15800-years-ago/ (Reuters). Bergstrom et al. 2020, Science 370: 557-564 (five dog lineages by 11,000 years ago; start of domestication unresolved). Start of domestication often put at ~20,000-40,000 years ago but uncertain, so the script uses only the safe floor "at least fifteen thousand years".
- L14-16 (fox buried with a person, Jordan, ~16,500 years): Maher, Stock, Finney, Heywood, Miracle & Banning 2011, "A Unique Human-Fox Burial from a Pre-Natufian Cemetery in the Levant (Jordan)", PLoS ONE 6(1): e15815, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0015815 (site Uyun al-Hammam). Cambridge press release: https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/was-the-fox-prehistoric-mans-best-friend ; ScienceDaily: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/02/110202132609.htm . Banning: the "pet" idea is only one hypothesis -> "A pet? Maybe. Scientists aren't sure."
- L17 (Ain Mallaha / Eynan, Israel: woman buried with her hand on a puppy, ~12,000 years, Natufian; puppy ~3-5 months): Davis & Valla 1978, "Evidence for domestication of the dog 12,000 years ago in the Natufian of Israel", Nature 276: 608-610, doi:10.1038/276608a0 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CA%BFAin_Mallaha . "About twelve thousand years" used (dates for the site span ~14,300-12,000 cal BP; species once debated wolf vs dog, so script just says "puppy").
- L3-4, 18-21: question / Moss / summary / joke / outro; no new claims ("furry friends" covers dogs and the possible fox).

## Softened
- "Cared for" stated via "Experts think" / "probably loved" (inference, not proof); distemper not called certain in sources.md.
- Dog domestication: no single start date; only "at least fifteen thousand years".
- Fox: explicitly "maybe" a pet.

## Dropped
- Exact week numbers of illness bouts (outlets disagree: 19/21/23 vs 22/24 weeks).
- Claims that dogs were domesticated 30,000-40,000 years ago (e.g. Goyet, Razboinichya "dogs"): contested, omitted.
- Other "pets" (cats etc.): cat taming is Neolithic (~9,500 yrs, Cyprus) and not "caveman"; omitted for focus and word limit.
