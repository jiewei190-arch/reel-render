# Sources: How Did Cavemen Stay Warm?
Line numbers = narration line order in script.json (= shot order in shots.json). 22 lines, 168 words.

- L1-2, 5-8 (lice date clothing; body/clothing lice live in clothes and split from head lice at least ~83,000 years ago, possibly ~170,000): Toups, Kitchen, Light & Reed 2011, "Origin of Clothing Lice Indicates Early Clothing Use by Anatomically Modern Humans in Africa", Molecular Biology and Evolution 28(1): 29-32, doi:10.1093/molbev/msq234, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3002236 (posterior mode 83 ka, median 170 ka, 95% HPD 29-691 ka). Discover: https://www.discovermagazine.com/lice-dna-reveals-when-humans-first-clothed-their-nakedness-13627 . Script: "suggests ... at least eighty-three thousand years ago. Maybe earlier." (statistical estimate, so "suggests").
- L9 (skins and fur): early clothing made from animal hides, evidenced by hide scrapers (Gilligan, Univ. of Sydney, 2024: https://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2024/06/29/the-beginnings-of-fashion.html ). Hedged "likely".
- L10-13 (bone eyed needles; oldest from Siberia, ~40,000 years, maybe more): Gilligan, d'Errico, Doyon, Wang & Kuzmin 2024, "Paleolithic eyed needles and the evolution of dress", Science Advances 10: eadp2887 ("earliest known eyed needles appeared approximately 40,000 years ago in Siberia", Sydney release above). Denisova Cave (Siberia) bird-bone needle with an eye, ~7.6 cm, reported as ~50,000 years old (2016): https://www.archaeology.org/news/4784-160823-denisova-cave-needle . Because ages differ (40k vs 50k) and the Denisova date/maker are uncertain, script says "about forty thousand years old, maybe more" and does not name a maker.
- L14-15 (needles may mean layered clothing; underwear): Gilligan et al. 2024 (above): eyed needles harder to make than awls (awls already enough for fitted clothes), so may reflect "more complex, layered clothing" and decoration; the paper raises the emergence of underwear within layered garment sets as a possibility while noting limited evidence -> "may" / "Some scientists even wonder". https://www.sci.news/archaeology/eyed-needle-invention-13057.html
- L16-19 (Sungir, Russia: three people buried with more than 13,000 ivory beads, over 30,000 years ago; beads sewn on clothes/caps; thousands of hours of work): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sungir (calibrated dates 32,050-28,550 BCE, i.e. ~34,000-30,500 years ago; >13,000 beads; ~10,000 hours); Dobrovolskaya, Richards & Trinkaus 2011 (direct radiocarbon dates); Trinkaus & Buzhilova 2018, Antiquity 92: 7-21; Cambridge Radiocarbon supplement (beads "sewn on to the clothes and headgear"): https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn:cambridge.org:id:article:S0033822222000613/resource/name/S0033822222000613sup001.pdf ; Don's Maps summary of White's bead experiments (~45 min to over 1 hour per bead): https://donsmaps.com/sungaea.html . Labour estimates range ~2,500-10,000 hours, so script says only "thousands of hours". "Many seem to have been sewn" (attachment inferred from bead rows).
- L21 (fur, fire and sewing): summary; fire for warmth is general knowledge (see fire reel sources). No new claims.
- L3-4, 20, 22: question / Moss / joke / outro.

## Softened
- Lice date: "suggests ... at least 83,000 ... maybe earlier" (not the press-favoured flat "170,000").
- Oldest needle: "about forty thousand years old, maybe more" instead of "50,000-year-old Denisovan needle".
- Underwear: framed as scientists wondering.
- Sungir: "over thirty thousand years"; "thousands of hours" instead of a precise figure.

## Dropped
- "The Denisova needle was made by Denisovans": attribution uncertain.
- Possible ~61,000-year-old needle from Sibudu, South Africa: contested, omitted.
- Exact per-person bead counts (sources disagree) and the "10,000 hours" figure.
