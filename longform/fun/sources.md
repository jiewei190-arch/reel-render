# Sources: What Did Cavemen Do For Fun?
Line numbers = narration line order in script.json (= shot order in shots.json). 24 lines, 171 words.

- L1-2, 5-6 (Hohle Fels flute: griffon vulture wing bone (radius), five finger holes, at least ~35,000 years): Conard, Malina & Munzel 2009, "New flutes document the earliest musical tradition in southwestern Germany", Nature 460(7256): 737-740, doi:10.1038/nature08169. University of Tubingen press release (24 June 2009): https://uni-tuebingen.de/uploads/media/09-06-24FloeteHohleFels.pdf ; assembled from 12 pieces, flutes predate 35,000 calendar years ago: https://phys.org/news/2009-06-prehistoric-flute-germany-oldest.html ; https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/35000-year-old-german-flutes-display-excellent-kraftwerk . Some later estimates put it nearer 40,000 years; script says "at least thirty-five thousand" (safe floor).
- L7-8 (playable replica): experimental archaeologist Wulf Hein carved a replica from griffon vulture bone and recorded a tune on it (NPR, 24 June 2009, "A little flute music to warm the cave": https://www.npr.org/2009/06/24/105823127/a-little-flute-music-to-warm-the-cave ; Scientific American blog https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/news-blog/stone-age-jams-humans-playing-the-f-2009-06-24/ ). Exact scale/tune claims (pentatonic, "Star-Spangled Banner") NOT used: inconsistent/unverified.
- L9-12 (Rouffignac finger flutings by children, lifted up): Sharpe & Van Gelder 2006, Rock Art Research 23(2) (https://rockartresearch.com/index.php/rock/article/view/432): many flutings in Chamber A1 likely by children aged ~2-5 "possibly held aloft to touch the ceiling"; low ceiling areas children could reach alone show few flutings. Van Gelder & Cooney 2011 follow-up (children ~3-7; flutings ~13,000 years old, Magdalenian): https://www.npr.org/sections/13.7/2011/10/06/141042561/the-child-artists-of-prehistory ; https://cavingnews.com/20111003-new-research-finds-french-cave-artists-were-children ; Science News https://www.sciencenews.org/article/children-prehistory (dates 27,000-13,000 years ago; "Adults must have hoisted children on their shoulders"; Bednarik dissents, suggesting floors were higher). Hence softened to "Scientists think" and "over thirteen thousand years ago". The surface is a soft clay/mud film (moonmilk) -> "soft clay".
- L13-16 (children's hand stencils): Fernandez-Navarro, Camaros & Garate 2022, "Visualizing childhood in Upper Palaeolithic societies: Experimental and archaeological approach to artists' age estimation through cave art hand stencils", Journal of Archaeological Science 140: 105574. 180 stencils in five Spanish caves; roughly a fifth to a quarter estimated to be children's (~2-12 yrs); pigment blown around the hand: https://phys.org/news/2022-03-ancient-handprints-cave-walls-spain.html ; https://www.archaeology.org/news/10419-220322-spain-hand-prints . Hedged as "maybe a quarter". "Selfie" = playful comparison, not a claim.
- L17-20 (fireside stories, singing, dancing; modern clue): Wiessner 2014, "Embers of society: Firelight talk among the Ju/'hoansi Bushmen", PNAS 111(39): 14027-14035, doi:10.1073/pnas.1404212111, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4191796 : "81% of lengthy conversations involving many people [at night] were devoted to stories"; night activities turn to "singing, dancing, religious ceremonies, and enthralling stories". Explicitly framed as a modern clue (L20), not direct Stone Age evidence. Ju/'hoansi live in the Kalahari (Botswana/Namibia) -> "Kalahari hunter-gatherers" (easier for TTS).
- L21-24: summary / joke / outro; no new claims.
