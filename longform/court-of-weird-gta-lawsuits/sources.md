# Sources and fact check: "10 Real GTA Lawsuits and How They Ended"

Fact-checked Nov 24, 2026 with live reads. Two cases rest on the court opinion itself (the strip club; the Hot Coffee
class action). The rest rest on major-outlet or trade reporting read live, because the opinions could not be opened
(nycourts.gov, Justia, CourtHouse News, GameSpot, ftc.gov and Reuters refused automated reads or returned 404).
Wikipedia was read for dates and is marked "secondary" wherever it is the only support. Stories about what courts
decided. Not legal advice. Not affiliated with Rockstar Games or Take-Two.

### 9. The strip club (opinion: https://static.case.law/f3d/547/cases/1095-01.json)
Every spoken claim is in the opinion: the Play Pen "on the eastern edge of downtown Los Angeles"; the "Pig Pen" in East Los Santos; artists "visited Los Angeles to take reference photographs"; suit filed April 22, 2005; "far-fetched"; "A reasonable consumer would not think a company that owns one strip club in East Los Angeles ... also produces a technologically sophisticated video game"; "fans can spend all nine innings of a baseball game at the hot dog stand; that hardly makes Dodger Stadium a butcher's shop"; "protected by the First Amendment"; "AFFIRMED" (Nov. 5, 2008). "Copying its sign" is plain English for the logo and trade dress claims. Fair to the club: the court accepted the artists took "some inspiration" from photos of it.

### 5. Washington v. Take-Two
Sources: Loeb & Loeb case summary (Oct. 31, 2012 decision); Game Developer, Nov. 2012.
Spoken claims: a model, credited in the game (both); met developers in 2003 and gave photos (his account, Game Developer; spoken as "says"); sued two years before the 2012 ruling for $250 million (Game Developer); trial court struck the suit (anti-SLAPP); the appeals court assumed use of his likeness and found it transformative, CJ "not a 'literal depiction'", no unique shared traits, situations with "no demonstrable analogue" to his life (Loeb). Not said: any band affiliation (disputed in the reporting). The opinion itself was not read.

### 4. Rockstar against the BBC
Source: PC Gamer, May 2015 (https://www.pcgamer.com/rockstar-files-suit-against-bbc-over-upcoming-drama/): the 90-minute film; the statement "Our goal is to ensure that our trademarks are not misused in the BBC's pursuit of an unofficial depiction of purported events related to Rockstar Games"; attempts to resolve it first. Air date and final title: Wikipedia, The Gamechangers (BBC Two, 15 September 2015; secondary). **The outcome of the suit could not be confirmed and the narrator says so.**

### 3. The fans who rebuilt the game
Sources: TorrentFreak, Sept. 3, 2021; PC Gamer, April 7, 2023; Wikipedia, Grand Theft Auto modding (the 19 February 2021 takedown).
Spoken claims: the projects and what they did; takedown, counter notice, restoration (TorrentFreak); suit in September 2021 with statutory damages of up to $150,000 per work (TorrentFreak); the fans' side, from the project leader's own statement: "our project falls under fair use ... you still need the original game ... fixing and improving it"; dismissal with prejudice against the four named defendants after "word of a potential settlement" (PC Gamer). The developers are not named. "Line by line" is a figure of speech for a reverse-engineered source recreation.

### 2. Benzies v. Take-Two
Sources: PC Gamer, April 9, 2018 ("no language mandating equal payments"); search-result reporting of the 2016 complaint (defendants, the 2009 plan, the counter-suit); Wikipedia, Leslie Benzies (sabbatical from 1 September 2014; suit filed 12 April 2016; 12 of 18 claims dismissed 29 March 2018 with "remains entitled to receive certain royalties"; confidential settlement 7 February 2019).
Hedges: "He alleges he was pushed out" is his allegation. "Reportedly ended in early 2019 with a confidential settlement" rests on Wikipedia and one trade site. Unrelated later allegations about him that appear on Wikipedia are not part of this story and are not mentioned.
