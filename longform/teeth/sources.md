# Sources: How Did Ancient Humans Take Care of Their Teeth?
- Hunter-gatherer decay rates (0-14.3% of teeth) and Taforalt acorns/cavities, ~15,000-13,700 yrs: Humphrey et al. 2014, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.1318176111; NHM release https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2014/january/ancient-hunter-gatherers-diet-toothache.html ; "almost every adult" = 49 of 52 adults (Archaeology magazine https://archaeology.org/news/2014/01/07/140107-morocco-teeth-decay/).
- Grit wearing teeth down: Humphrey (same NHM release).
- Toothpick grooves back ~1.6-1.9 million years (Homo habilis); Krapina Neanderthal (~130,000 yrs), 6 grooves, crooked P4 / impacted M3: Frayer et al. 2017, Bull. Int. Assoc. Paleodontology; https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/neanderthals-used-toothpicks-treat-aching-teeth-180963883/ ; https://cosmosmagazine.com/history/palaeontology/neanderthal-dentistry-used-tools-to-treat-toothache-130000-years-ago
- El Sidron calculus DNA, abscess, poplar (salicylic acid), Penicillium; "can't prove" caveat: Weyrich et al. 2017, Nature; https://www.dental-tribune.com/news/dna-from-dental-calculus-shows-neanderthal-used-natural-analgesics/ ; https://cosmosmagazine.com/science/biology/neanderthals-used-aspirins-ancestor
- Villabruna ~14,000 yrs, flint scratching/levering of a molar cavity ante-mortem: Oxilia et al. 2015, Scientific Reports; https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/earliest-evidence-of-proto-dental-treatment-in-the-late-upper-pal/
- Riparo Fredian (near Lucca) ~13,000 yrs, bitumen + plant fibers + possible hairs in two upper front teeth: Oxilia et al. 2017, AJPA; https://www.snexplores.org/article/stone-age-dentists-treated-cavities-tar
- Mehrgarh 7,500-9,000 yrs, 11 drilled molars, 9 adults, flint bow drill < 1 min in tests, living patients, one person with 3 drilled teeth: Coppa et al. 2006, Nature 440:755; https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna12168308
- Lonche, Slovenia ~6,500 yrs, man 24-30, cracked left canine, beeswax filling, could not rule out post-mortem: Bernardini et al. 2012, PLoS ONE 7(9) e44904.
Softened: "close to two million years"; "may be one of the earliest signs"; "one of the earliest fillings we know of"; farming "in several parts of the world"; poplar use stated as unproven.
