# Sources and fact check: "14 Real Lawsuits That Sounded Like Jokes"

Fact-checked Nov 24, 2026. For the nine cases new to this video, the court opinion itself was downloaded and read live
(Harvard Caselaw Access Project, `static.case.law`), except Liebeck, which has no published opinion (jury trial, then a
confidential settlement) and rests on news and secondary sources, named below. For the five cases with existing
research (Pearson, cash, Katko, Carlill, Lucy) the facts and hedges come from the channel's own `sources.md` files,
which were checked against the opinions on Nov 23, 2026; they were re-read, not re-fetched.
Stories about what courts decided. Not legal advice.

### 11. Naruto v. Slater (https://static.case.law/f3d/888/cases/0418-01.json)

| Spoken claim | Basis |
|---|---|
| Indonesia, 2011; Slater left a camera unattended in a reserve | "In 2011, a wildlife photographer, David Slater, left his camera unattended in the reserve" (Sulawesi). |
| Naruto allegedly took several photos of himself; they ended up in Slater's book | "Naruto allegedly took several photographs of himself"; published in a book in December 2014. "Allegedly" is the court's word and is kept. |
| 2015: PETA sued in the monkey's name, claiming the copyright | Complaint for copyright infringement "as Next Friends on behalf of Naruto" (with Dr. Antje Engelhardt, who later withdrew). |
| Trial court dismissed; PETA appealed, then settled; the monkey was not a party to the settlement | "Naruto was 'not a party to the settlement'"; the court declined to dismiss the appeal. |

Fair to both sides: the court also held the monkey had constitutional (Article III) standing under circuit precedent; the video only says he could not sue under the Copyright Act. PETA's side (it obtained a pledge of 25% of the book's future earnings for habitat charities) is in the opinion and can go in a pinned comment.

### 2. Liebeck v. McDonald's Restaurants
No published opinion exists. Sources read live: Retro Report documentary transcript (https://www.retroreport.org/video/the-misunderstood-mcdonalds-hot-coffee-lawsuit/), American Museum of Tort Law (https://www.tortmuseum.org/liebeck-v-mcdonalds/), Consumer Attorneys of California (https://www.caoc.org/?pg=facts), Vox, Dec. 16, 2016, and Wikipedia (secondary). The NYT, Britannica and AP pages refused automated reads (HTTP 403). Two of these sources (the tort museum, the trial lawyers' group) favour the plaintiff's side; where sources differ the cautious figure is used.

| Spoken claim | Basis |
|---|---|
| Albuquerque, 1992; she was 79; the passenger; the car was parked | Retro Report: "a 79-year-old widow sitting in the passenger seat of a parked car ... February 27th, 1992"; tort museum; Vox. |
| Cup between her knees, adding cream and sugar | Tort museum; CAOC. |
| Coffee kept at 180 to 190 degrees | Retro Report: "McDonald's policy was to serve coffee between 180 and 190 degrees"; CAOC. |
| Third-degree burns; eight days in hospital; skin grafts | Tort museum; Retro Report (grafts); Wikipedia. Burn percentages differ between sources (6% vs 16%), so none is spoken. |
| She asked about $20,000; the offer was $800 | Tort museum; CAOC; Vox; Retro Report ($800). |
| Roughly 700 earlier burn reports | "nearly 700" (Retro Report), "700" (tort museum, Vox), "more than 700" (CAOC, Wikipedia): spoken as "roughly seven hundred". |
| McDonald's side: she spilled it herself; customers want coffee hot | Vox ("claimed customers wanted the coffee this hot"); Wikipedia (the commuter argument; the cup carried a warning). |
| 20% of the blame on her; $160,000; $2.7 million punitive, about two days of coffee sales | Tort museum; Retro Report; Wikipedia ($200,000 reduced by 20%). |
| The judge cut it to $480,000 | Tort museum; Wikipedia (total $640,000). |
| Settled for an amount never made public; reportedly less than $600,000 | Confidential (tort museum, CAOC). Vox: "less than $600,000"; Retro Report and tort museum: "less than $500,000" per a source. Spoken with "reportedly" and the higher bound. |
